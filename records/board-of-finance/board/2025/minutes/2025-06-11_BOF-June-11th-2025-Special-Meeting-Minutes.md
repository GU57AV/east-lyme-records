      Note Mr. Cunningham provided an update on the search for a new police chief:
     They have received approximately 15 resumes, with applications closing around June 16th.
     The selection process will involve four tiers-
     1. The HR Director and the Police Commission will review applications, eliminating incomplete or clearly
     unqualified candidates.
     2. The remaining resumes will be reviewed by the Connecticut Police Chiefs Association, which offers its
     services for free as the town is a member.
     3. The East Lyme Police Commission will then review the candidates passed on by the Association.
     4. Finalists will be interviewed by a five-person panel, expected to include Mr. Cunningham, Dan Price
     (Chairman of the Police Commission), two members from the Board of Selectmen, and one outside
     individual. This diverse panel aims to ensure a comprehensive selection process.
     Their aim is to offer a competitive salary to attract the best candidates.

  4-2. Finance Director

      Note Mr. Gervais presented several updates:
     *For the 2022-2023 fiscal year, tax collections were at 99.26%.
     *For the current 2023-2024 fiscal year, with 19 days of collections remaining, the rate stands at 99.17%,
     closely tracking last year's performance.
     This is slightly above the budgeted rate of 99.1%, potentially leading to a slight over-collection.

      Note *The town is approximately three weeks away from finalizing the implementation of ADP for payroll
     and HR.
     *The Water and Sewer Department, in conjunction with the Finance office, is implementing Munis utility
     billing to streamline software packages town wide.
     Both are expected to roll out by July 1.

      Note *The Finance office is preparing the offering statement for the Bond package, which will be issued in
     the coming weeks.
     *Short-term borrowing covers upfront costs, which convert to long-term notes once projects are completed.

      Note *The Treasurer has spearheaded an RFP for banking services, with a meeting scheduled to review
     the proposals.

      Note Regarding the health insurance premium issue for approximately 50 employees who were incorrectly
     paying post-tax instead of pre-tax-
     *The 2021 tax returns were amended earlier this year, and stipends were provided to employees for re-
     filing.
     *The Finance office aims to fix the 2022 and 2023 issues, totaling about $50,000 annually, before June
     30th.
     *An appropriation to contingency later in the meeting will address this.

      Note Fiscal Year 2023 Audit-
     *The largest outstanding item, the sewer assessment issue, appears to have reached a resolution with the
     auditors.
     *This was a multi-month process, and the proposed number seems acceptable to both parties.

      Note *The Board of Selectmen passed the establishment of the Coastal Resiliency Fund.
     *After June 16th, budgeted money will be transferred to the fund.

6. Public Discussion

  Note There were no members of the public present to address the Board.

7. Board Comments

  Note Mr. Steel suggested that future meetings with many new business items could benefit from a consent
 agenda, provided detailed explanations of transfers are distributed in advance. He also recommended
 providing materials further in advance, potentially with color-coding for clarity, to assist new Board members in
 their understanding of the transfers.

  Note Mr. Steel expressed hope for future contract negotiations to provide more flexibility with staff-related
 fixed costs that heavily constrain the Town's and Board of Education's budgets.

  Note Mr. Steel also voiced concern about the potential impact of federal spending on local budgets. He noted
 significant federal expenditures (e.g., sending Marines to Los Angeles, Army celebration parade) and
 questioned how much federal funding the town receives directly or indirectly through the State, urging caution
 due to potential impacts on local finances.
