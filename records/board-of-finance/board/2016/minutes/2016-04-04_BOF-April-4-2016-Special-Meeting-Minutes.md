Luane Lange, 160 Pennsylvania Ave, Chair of the Historic Properties Commission said that when she had
spoken previously at the other meetings that she had spoken about the various historic homes. She wanted
them to know that the Samuel Smith House came to them and to their attention because of the 65 letters that
were sent out by the Natural Resources Commission asking the owners of these properties in the watershed
area should become available that the Town may have an interest in them. She said that the letter was
signed by more than one person and that the previous First Selectman had signed it. She provided the
timeline regarding the Samuel Smith Property (copy attached). She mentioned that they had done a number
of things to the House since acquired and that the ADA restroom would cost around $6500 to do.

Mark Christiansen, 66 Grassy Hill Road said that he is the VP of the Samuel Smith House Committee and
noted that the purchase originated with the Natural Resources Commission. That was then and moving
forward the work that they have done with many volunteer hours amounted to a cost of $3000 however if the
man hours were figured they received over $200,000 worth of work. The inmates have done a lot of the yard
work; Guy's removed the oil in the tank and paid them for it; the Rotary also donated funds and helped with
the garden and they were able to donate items form the garden to the soup kitchen. He noted the various
fund raising that they have done and that Mr. Nickerson had come up with the idea of the biking event and
that he has been generous with his personal donations. He also suggested possibly holding a dance on the
House grounds in the late summer and while all of this is nice, this is a Town property and they should have
a budget and fund of their own.

Alex Argeris, 17 South Beechwood Rd. said that he is a concerned parent of a school child and asked that
they consider that the current proposal on LB Haynes and Flanders School is being rushed and not all of
their questions were answered. At the Finance meeting of March 30, 2016 Mr. Nickerson said that it would
not come forward for any vote until May or June so there is plenty of time for questions and more details to
be worked out. While he realizes that their job is to do what the Town can afford — if they were to find this
project as proposed affordable — it would not meet the needs of the educational system. He said that he is
assuming that when the proposal goes to the Board of Selectmen that the outcome would be the same as
with the BOE so he is asking them to vote it down when it comes here so that is can go back out to the

people.

Ms. Alberti said that regarding the BOE budget that she was in favor of it as presented. She has also spoken
to many people who have expressed their views and concepts against it however she remains in favor of it.

reduced their own budget by 1% before presenting it here, they face unfunded mandates and she feels they
have reduced staffing with the enrollment reduction. They have made some hard decisions with regard to the
elementary schools and have considered the needs of the Town. She would not cut their budget further.

For General Government she said the goal was to try to keep the mil rate the same and she was prepared to
cut the budget to a level where it could be achieved but that would require a cut of $1M and the requested
increase is $900,000 so to keep it the same the budget would be less than the current budget.

Board has repeatedly asked to be in the union negotiations but they have not been there. The non-union
positions have not proven that the increases are necessary and it is also not necessary to hire extra people
when the work load increases a bit when you have had them there when it was not as busy.

She would put Town Capital projects on hold until the school project is decided.

She also said that when the Gateway came to Town that assertions were made that the additional taxes
would help and would create additional revenues — she would like to see the BOE and General Government
manage themselves better to stay in line with the revenues.

researching what is happening in the surrounding Towns she found that Groton is searching for millions in

1.97%; Montville 1.9% and Waterford declined increases for it’s officials. Our projection of 3.74% is the
highest of all of the surrounding communities.

She said that the First Selectman is the CEO and he should make the cuts as he works with the people
everyday. She echoed Ms. Hogan’s suggestions and comments on a subcommittee to review salaries and
raises.

Pay increases of 3% to 6% are unacceptable whether you call them step or something else — they need to
change.

There are several department heads who receive over 5% and that has to change.

The larger budgets can be reviewed for some efficiencies — a requested increase of 7.7% is too high.

The Counselor position suggested for Parks & Rec is not the right fit.

She could see a $40,000 cut to Public works Storm Materials.
