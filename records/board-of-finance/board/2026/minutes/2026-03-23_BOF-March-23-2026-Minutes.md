      Note Dr. McCallister asked about class sizes and what the district considers ideal. Mr. Newton said they do
     not believe class sizes are unusually low. He said middle school classes are generally in the low-to-mid
     20s and close to classroom capacity. At the elementary level, the district guidelines are about 18 students
     per class in grades K-2 and about 20 in grades 3-4, though some classes can run slightly above or below.
     He explained staffing is harder to adjust when a grade only has two or three teachers, since small
     enrollment changes can cause big swings in class size, and he said the district is looking at redistricting or
     reorganization to balance classes. At the high school, he noted positions were cut last year and class sizes
     vary depending on course level and student needs.

      Note Ms. McNamara asked if the district has looked at sharing even more services with Salem, including
     whether deeper administrative sharing or regionalization is possible. Mr. Newton said they are always
     looking for options and have a meeting planned in May with Waterford and Lyme-Old Lyme to explore
     more shared course offerings. On administrative sharing with Salem, he said it is difficult because East
     Lyme already has limited administrative capacity.

      Note He then shared state data showing East Lyme is on the low end for administrators compared to
     similar districts. East Lyme is at about 16.5 administrators, and most comparable districts have more. He
     also noted that two roles (Security Director and Food Services Director) are not counted in that state
     administrator figure because they are non-certified positions. He added that they already reduced an
     Assistant Principal role from 12 months to 10 months.

      Note Ms. McNamara asked if there is a system to look for grants beyond the Pathways program to offset
     costs. Mr. Newton said the district does not have a dedicated grant writer because that would add cost and
     create ongoing administrative work to maintain grants. He said their part-time Pathways Coordinator has
     been doing much of the current grant and donation work.

      Note Ms. McNamara asked why the Niantic Center budget went down compared to other schools and
     whether there were lessons to apply elsewhere. The response was that it was mainly due to staffing
     changes (including turnover from a higher-salary teacher to a lower-salary teacher) and a cut to the
     secretary position (from full-time to part-time). It was also noted that Niantic Center had lower repair and
     maintenance needs and requested less in some supply lines.

      Note Ms. Kalajainen asked about the $97,000 increase and whether it reflects new staffing. Mr. Newton
     explained that the increase is tied to the district’s move toward an armed security model, including higher
     hourly rates and the addition of one full-time position. Ms. Kalajainen noted the armed security plan is
     included in the Board of Education adopted budget, but the program itself is still a recommendation moving
     through the approval process.

      Note She also inquired why the budget includes $675,000 for substitutes and what is causing it, and
     potential ways to bring that cost down.

      Note Mr. Newton said that number matches what they have actually been spending lately (about $650,000
     to $671,000). Most day-to-day substitutes are provided through Kelly Services (custodians are handled
     separately). The cost is driven by staff being out for absences and leave time, including several people on
     FMLA, and that Connecticut leave rules also affect some non-certified staff. He doesn't have an exact
     breakdown, but he can provide more detail in the coming days.

      Note Dr. McCallister left the meeting.


      Note Ms. Kalajainen said it is a big expense and should be actively addressed. Mr. Newton replied that
     staff burnout and absences are happening statewide and warned that cutting the budget further could make
     staffing strain worse and increase the need for substitutes.

     *Driver shortages are pushing costs up.
     *Special education transportation is very expensive when students are placed out of district; one student’s

     breaks down), so it is hard to cut routes based only on who usually rides.

     is mostly a budget re-categorizing change, not a big new spend. Costs that used to be listed under
     textbooks and equipment were moved into supplies because they were being coded in the wrong place.
