   4-3. Finance Director

      Note Mr. Gervais reported that the town will likely move money around in the budget to cover extra costs,
     especially firefighter overtime (about $500,000 over budget) and legal expenses. The town is also working
     on its audit and expects a draft soon.

      Note He added that even with a spending freeze, the town plans to hire an Accounting Coordinator to help
     with payroll, since current staff are being stretched too thin.

      Note Mr. Gervais briefly walked the Board through the Fire Department budget using the model they did,
     and some of the following was highlighted:
     *Right now, the Department has 12 firefighters, but 4 are out (military leave, injury, FMLA etc.)
     *Because of this, many shifts are being filled with overtime, which is driving costs way up- about $1 million
     in overtime this year.
     *Firefighters work a 24-hours-on, 48-hours-off schedule, and due to staffing shortages and limited part-time
     help, the town often has to pay overtime or even double-time to cover shifts.

      Note He presented a proposed solution- increase staffing to 15 firefighters (5 per shift instead of 4):
     *This would increase regular salary costs but reduce overtime significantly.
     *It would also improve service, since more firefighters are available each shift.
     *The goal is to find a balance between staffing levels and overtime costs.

      Note Mr. Cunningham compared the situation to buying in bulk. You might spend more upfront on staff, but
     you get better value and service instead of overspending on overtime with fewer people. He said the BOS
     believes adding firefighters is the better long-term approach.

      Note Commission Members asked how the extra (5th) firefighter would be assigned. The plan is likely to
     place them at a station that currently has EMTs but no full-time firefighter. Some concern was raised about
     having only one firefighter at a station, but it was clarified that EMTs are always present there, so the
     firefighter wouldn’t be alone. Final decisions will be made by the Fire Chief.

      Note Mr. Gervais added that the CIP Committee will be looking at a fire response analysis study as part of
     its long-term Capital Improvement Plan. This study will look at whether fire stations are in the right
     locations, staffing levels, and response times. This will help determine the best long-term setup for fire
     services.

      Note Mr. Sheckley noticed $284,000 increase in wages, which seems good, but also saw a $200,000
     decrease in part-time firefighters and a $200,000 increase in overtime. He said if we’re hiring more full-time
     staff, shouldn’t overtime go down instead of up?

      Note Mr. Gervais explained that essentially, they’re paying the equivalent of 6 firefighters in overtime to
     cover 4 missing positions. Even though the new budget shows $700,000 for overtime, that’s actually a
     $300,000 reduction from what’s really being spent now. Hiring more full-time firefighters should reduce
     overtime over time and provide a buffer so shifts don’t always require overtime coverage.

      Note Mr. Gervais added that the model was wrong last year (first year using new schedule system) and
     didn’t account for staffing shortages. The new budget adjusts for real-world overtime costs and adds
     salaries for new hires. Savings also come from reducing part-time staff.

      Note Mr. Steel explained that the salary amounts shown in the budget are not the full true cost.
     Some of the salary expenses are set aside in a contingency fund instead of being listed directly.
     This is because the final amounts depend on ongoing contract negotiations.

   4-4. New Business- Review of FY25/26 Budget & FY26/27 Budget

      Note Mr. Cunningham reviewed key points from the FY25/26 budget:
     *The town hired its 12th firefighter on July 1st, 2025.
     *The 13th firefighter was planned for January 1st, 2026.
     *A staffing study identified an ideal level of 18 firefighters total- 6 per shift, with 2 firefighters at each of the
     3 stations, covering 24/7 operations.

      Note *The goal was (and remains) to reduce borrowing by using more cash for capital expenses.
     *Last year’s proposal included $1.35 million in cash capital, but it was reduced to $92,000- still higher than
     prior years.
     *The long‑term target is $2 million per year in cash capital, which would allow the town to pay down more
     debt than it takes on.
     *The town is not there yet, but increasing cash spending is the only way to break the debt cycle.

      Note *Emphasis continues to be on maintaining buildings, vehicles, and equipment, such as Public Works
     washing and maintaining vehicles after storms to extend their lifespan.
     *All Departments are expected to follow this maintenance‑first approach.
     *The focus was/is on needs over wants.

      Note *The town must be cautious about adding new projects or infrastructure, since they require more
     maintenance, more staff, and higher long‑term costs (salaries, benefits, etc.)
     *The original goal of reaching 6 firefighters per shift in one budget cycle was likely too ambitious.

      Note *After reviewing options, the BOS decided to hire 3 firefighters starting July 1st, 2026.
     *This gradual approach allows the town to increase staffing steadily and test whether adding one firefighter
     per shift reduces overtime.

      Note *Minimum staffing remains 4 firefighters per shift.
     *The additional firefighter is intended to reduce overtime, not permanently raise baseline staffing to 5 per
     shift.
     *If the town staffs 5 firefighters every shift, the savings disappear and the overtime problem repeats.

      Note *Recently, the Department was down four firefighters due to deployment, medical leave, and family
     leave, creating heavy reliance on overtime.
     *Without additional staffing, the town remains vulnerable to high overtime costs.
     *The plan is to hire 3 firefighters now, monitor the results, and re‑evaluate next year.

   4-19. Capital Projects Overview

      Note *Departments originally requested about $12 million in capital projects.
     *The CIP Committee set a target of about $7 million.
     *Setting clear targets helped bring costs down.
     *Even with this plan, the Town is still in a debt spiral.

      Note *Cash capital is $1.055 million and only $250,000 is true cash.
     *The rest comes from other sources.
     *Borrowing bonds of $6 million.
     *Debt being paid off (principal) is $4.9 million.

      Note *Essentially, the town is borrowing more than it is paying off and going deeper into debt by roughly $1
     million per year.
     *Even though the town is increasing cash capital, it is not yet reversing the trend of taking on more debt
     than it eliminates.

      Note *Many capital projects were cut or scaled back, with Parks & Recreation taking the biggest hit.
     *An example being the removal of the $1.1 million park facility/bathroom project.

      Note *The town prioritized essential needs over discretionary or nice‑to‑have projects.
     *Fixing a critical piece of equipment (such as a broken loader needed for operations)

     *The focus is on replacing failing equipment and supporting basic operations not expanding facilities or
     adding new amenities.
