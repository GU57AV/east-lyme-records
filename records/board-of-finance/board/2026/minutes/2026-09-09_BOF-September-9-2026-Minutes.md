5. Finance Director Report

  Note Mr. Gervais provided a detailed financial report.

  5-1. Tax Collector Report

     Note Collections through August 31st stand at 55.3%, compared to 53.95% at the same point last year.
     He described this as a positive sign given the town’s historically high collection rate of approximately 99%.

  5-2. Fund Balance & Budget

      Note The fund balance currently sits at roughly 22%. Long‑range projections show a potential decline to
     around 15.5% by FY2027, he stressed that this projection assumes the full use of $1 million in fund
     balance, which he does not expect will be necessary.

  5-3. 2026 Budget Performance

      Note The town budgeted around $96.6 million in both revenues and expenditures. Actual expenditures
     totaled about $95.5 million, while revenues reached roughly $96.5 million, leaving the town with a positive
     margin of about $1 million.

      Note Mr. Gervais that more than half of this surplus (roughly $550,000) resulted from a budgeting error in
     which sewer bond payments were inadvertently double‑counted in both the general fund and the sewer
     fund. He also noted that several departments returned funds due to turnover and last year’s spending
     freeze, including the Police Department, Fire Department, and retirement benefits accounts.

      Note He expects to have a fully reconciled year‑end report next month.

6. Old Business
  6-1. FY2028 Budget Projection Proposal

      Note see attachment.
             0013_001.pdf

      Note see attachment.
             0013_001_(1).pdf

      Note Mr. Sheckley presented the FY2028 Budget Projection Proposal, the result of nearly a year of
     research, collaboration, and analysis. The proposal is not a budget itself but a process for developing early
     projections using a consistent set of assumptions.

      Note Mr. Steel joined the meeting via Zoom.


      Note He reviewed twelve core assumptions, including stable interest rates, known wage increases from
     collective bargaining agreements, predictable benefit and pension costs, inflation‑based increases for
     operating expenses, differentiated inflation rates for capital and technology costs, flat intergovernmental
     revenue, maintaining a 17% fund balance target, and historical Board of Education budget trends.

      Note He also reviewed historical grand list increases during revaluation years, noting an average increase
     of about 22%.

      Note Mr. Sheckley explained the proposed fiscal “guardrail,” developed through extensive analysis and
     discussion with other Board members. By comparing historical budget increases to inflation, he determined
     that the average difference between the two is 2.75%. He proposed that future budget growth be capped at
     inflation plus 2.75%, creating a predictable and sustainable guideline. He emphasized that this approach
     mirrors the successful CIP process, where departments work within a defined target.

      Note The Board discussed the proposal at length, including staffing assumptions, inflation projections, and
     the need for departmental performance metrics.
