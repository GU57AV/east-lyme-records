1. Call Meeting to Order

   Note Chair McCallister called the April 23rd, 2026, Public Hearing of the Board of Finance to order at 5:30
  p.m., led those assembled in the Pledge of Allegiance; a quorum was present.

  Note He noted that public comment sign‑up sheets were available and welcomed attendees to the first Public
  Hearing on the FY 2026-2027 East Lyme budget.


2. Comments by First Selectman

   Note First Selectman Dan Cunningham opened by explaining that this was the first of two public presentations
  on the FY 2026-2027 budget, with a second session scheduled for the following week at the high school
  auditorium.

   Note He began by outlining the budget development process, noting that each Department Head submits a
  proposed budget to him. He and the Finance Director then meet with every department to understand their
  needs, identify what is essential, and determine what can be reduced or eliminated. After these meetings, he
  prepares a proposed budget and submits it to the Board of Selectmen, who conduct their own review, question
  departments, and sometimes make additional reductions- or restore items they believe should remain.

   Note He described the work of the Capital Improvement Committee, which spent many hours reviewing
  long‑term capital needs. Capital items, he explained, are durable goods such as lawnmowers, beach
  groomers, plows, trucks, roofs, HVAC systems, and buildings. He emphasized the importance of determining
  which items are truly necessary and ensuring the town does not defer critical equipment. He gave the example
  of the beach groomer, describing how it removes debris and prepares the beach, and how the town had to
  decide whether to replace it this year or postpone the purchase.

  Note He reviewed last year’s initiatives, including hiring the 12th Firefighter toward the long‑term goal of 18
  firefighters- six per shift, two per station. The town also purchased a new rescue boat and repowered a Boston
  Whaler, which made a successful rescue within six hours of being deployed. Additional accomplishments
  included creating a Fair Rent Commission, expanding Youth and Family Services, and increasing Senior
  Center transportation, which he noted is essential for many seniors who rely on it for medical appointments
  and errands.

   Note Mr. Cunningham detailed the town’s debt burden. Last year, the town allocated $990,000 in cash capital,
  which reduces reliance on borrowing. He explained the importance of reaching an “inflection point” where the
  town pays down more debt than it borrows, allowing long‑term debt service to decrease. The goal is to
  eventually reach $2-3 million in annual cash capital, though this must be done gradually.

   Note He also highlighted the creation of a Coastal Resiliency Fund, which bond counsel now expects coastal
  towns to maintain. Even though the fund is small, consistently contributing to it improves the town’s financial
  standing when borrowing.
  He also discussed improvements to cybersecurity and communications infrastructure, including expanding the
  town’s dedicated dark fiber network connecting police, fire, and public safety buildings. He noted the growing
  threat of ransomware attacks on municipalities and emphasized the town’s efforts to strengthen protections.

   Note Mr. Cunningham stated that the proposed budget includes three new Firefighters and two new Police
  Officers, with staggered hiring to reduce immediate budget impact. This will increase Firefighter staffing from
  four to five per shift. He stressed the importance of maintaining town buildings, purchasing only necessary
  capital items, and protecting long‑term investments- such as switching dump truck bodies to stainless steel to
  prevent rust and extend lifespan.

   Note He then presented a broad overview of the budget:
  *Town operations total $27,828,450 (a 10.38% increase).
  *Debt service is $7,266,120 (a 7.2% increase).
  *Cash capital is $1,055,571 (a 5.68% increase).
  *The subtotal for town operations is $36,150,141, an 8.15% increase.
  *The Board of Education budget is $65,926,685, a 4.26% increase.
  *The total proposed budget is $102,077,026, representing a 5.61% overall increase.

   Note Mr. Cunningham then identified the major budget drivers:
  *Employee benefits account for 48.1% of the total increase.
  *Salary contingency is necessary because the town is currently negotiating contracts with Fire, Police, and
  Dispatch Unions.
  *Other increases include IT services (+5.1%), legal services (+8%), Police staffing (+4.3%), three new
  Firefighters (+9.7%), the library (+1.5%), Parks & Recreation (+3.1%), the Registrar of Voters (+2.3%), and
  Public Works (+6.7%).

   Note He emphasized the impact of unfunded state mandates, which the town cannot avoid, and the strain
  placed on local services by state‑owned facilities such as the prison, Rocky Neck State Park, Camp Nett. He
  noted that the ambulance service responds to the correctional facility multiple times per day, which indirectly
  affects town resources even though the ambulance service is now a separate nonprofit.

   Note He stressed that the town is trying to protect essential services such as trash pickup, road maintenance,
  snow plowing, while keeping the budget at a level residents can bear.

   Note Chairman McCallister began by introducing the Board Members and explaining that the Board had held
  nine meetings since receiving the Board of Selectmen’s budget on March 11th. He emphasized that the Board
  approached the budget as a work process, not a political one.

  Note The Board sought to balance three priorities- strong schools, reliable town services, and a sustainable
  tax burden.

   Note He reiterated the budget pressures, noting that increases in health insurance, pension obligations,
  staffing needs, and inflation are not optional and cannot be cut away and these pressures are inherent to
  maintaining a functioning town.

  Note He also echoed the concerns about long‑term financial stability, pointing out that the town’s fund balance
  is trending toward the lower end of the target range and that East Lyme carries over $80 million in debt. He
  stressed that these realities shape what is responsible in both the near and long term.

  Note Dr. McCallister said that the Board of Finance has made $1.45 million in reductions to the proposed
  budget, which lowered the mill rate from where it otherwise would have been. The BOF reduced the BOE
  request, but the BOE budget increase is still 4.26%. He emphasized that the Board did not cut core services,
  did not reduce education funding year‑over‑year, and did not defer critical public safety or infrastructure needs.

  Note He noted that the Board is listening to residents, will take notes during both Public Hearings, and will
  meet again after the second hearing to finalize the budget.


5. Public Discussion - Comments from Public
   5-1. Patricia Whitaker of 190 Upper Pattagansett Road

      Note Ms. Whitaker's remarks highlighted the following:
     *She's concerned that meetings are not well advertised.
     *She is on a fixed income and is increasingly worried about her ability to afford rising property taxes.
     *The town is approving too many residential developments without ensuring that commercial or industrial
     growth keeps pace.
     *New housing brings additional demands on schools, public safety, and infrastructure, but does not
     generate enough revenue to offset those costs.
     *East Lyme should explore regionalizing certain services such as Police and Fire, with Old Lyme or other
     neighboring towns to reduce expenses.
     *Budget increases should be tied more closely to inflation.
     *The current rate of growth is unsustainable for many residents.

   5-2. Tim Dowd of 66 East Pattagansett Road

      Note Mr. Dowd offered some of the following remarks:
     *He's deeply concerned about the removal of funding for the Armed Security Officer (ASO) position at the

     than relying on the Board of Education to fund security positions.
     *Schools have been forced to compensate for what he sees as insufficient Police staffing.
     *Cutting or shifting responsibility for school security could create long‑term safety issues.
     *Decisions made to save money in the short term often lead to larger, more expensive problems later.
     *He urges the Board of Finance to reconsider the reduction and to prioritize student safety.
