      Note Mr. Gervais reviewed the newly ratified Firefighters’ and Dispatchers’ union contracts. Key points for
     Firefighters included the following:
     *3% wage increase each year for three years.
     *Saturday pay reduced from double‑time to time‑and‑a‑half, which will create major overtime savings for
     the Town.
     *Health insurance cost‑share increased for employees.
     *Up to $20,000 of overtime can now count toward retirement earnings for firefighters in the defined
     contribution plan.
     *Only one firefighter remains in the old defined benefit pension; all others are in the 401(a)‑style plan.
     *$1,000 Class A uniform allowance added for each firefighter.
     *Union agreed to switch to bi‑weekly payroll.

      Note Key points for Dispatchers included the following:
     *3% wage increase each year for three years.
     *Higher health and dental cost‑share for employees.
     *Dispatchers will move from a defined benefit pension to a defined contribution plan effective July 1st,
     2026.
     *Retirement contributions- employees pay 3%, town pays 6%.
     *Shift differentials increased- second shift is $0.60/hour, and third shift is $0.80/hour.

      Note Mr. Gervais confirmed that the cost impacts of both contracts have already been modeled with the
     town’s labor attorney and incorporated into the salary contingency.
     Actuarial review will determine the pension savings from Dispatchers moving to a defined contribution plan.

9. Board Comments

  Note Dr. McCallister reminded the Board that the Town budget referendum was underway and that residents
  could vote at the Community Center until 8:00 PM. Depending on the results, the Board will likely meet next
  week- if the referendum passes, the Board must convene briefly to set the new mill rate; if it fails, the Board will
  need to reconvene for further budget deliberations. Results are expected later in the evening.

  Note Mr. Steel suggested the Board of Selectmen consider updating senior tax‑relief programs, such as
  raising income limits and possibly capping taxes for eligible seniors. He noted East Lyme income levels and
  said many residents have expressed affordability concerns.
  He also recommended doing post‑budget review work in the coming months to improve efficiency, limit future
  increases where possible, and explore using AI tools to support analysis and planning.

   Note Mr. Sheckley made the following recommendations:
  *Setting some basic budget assumptions ahead of time (inflation, revenues, salary increases) so the Board
  has a starting target for the FY27-28 budget, such as aiming for about a 3% increase.
  *Having each department list what they accomplished this year and what they plan to do next year to make the
  budget more transparent.
  * Adding a short explanation for each line item in department budgets so it’s clear why each request is needed.
  *Creating simple guidelines that spell out what counts as a capital improvement.

   Note Mr. Sheckley suggested putting these ideas under New Business at an upcoming meeting so the Board
  can talk them through.

   Note Dr. McCallister asked Mr. Gervais to share this information with Department Heads so they can be
  prepared to share this information for the next budget cycle.
