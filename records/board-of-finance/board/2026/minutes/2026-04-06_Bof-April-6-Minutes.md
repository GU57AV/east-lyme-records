     *Registrar supplies are not election‑specific (not isolated to Election Day, primaries, or referenda) but are
     used across all voting events and ongoing operations.
     *The supplies line is intentionally general because many materials are purchased in advance, shared
     across multiple election types, and reused when legally allowed.
     * Recent changes to state election laws have made elections more complicated to run.
     *These changes include more days of early voting, additional paperwork, and new procedures that require
     more supplies and advance preparation.
     *Separating supplies into very specific categories (such as only for Election Day or only for primaries)
     would limit flexibility and could cause shortages or inefficiencies during busy election periods.
     *Keeping supplies in one general line allows her office to adjust as needed for different types of elections,
     including primaries, referenda, and municipal, state, and federal elections.

   3-1. Town Hall Security Cameras

      Note Dr. McCallister questioned the need for the proposed Town Hall security cameras and asked whether

     Note MOTION (1)

     Ms. McNamara seconded the motion.

      Note Dan Cleary, the IT Director, explained the project would install around 18 cameras inside and outside
     Town Hall, along with a server that would allow police dispatch to view footage in real time. The cameras
     were proposed due to recent vehicle thefts in the Town Hall parking lot and safety concerns in departments
     such as the Tax Collector and Town Clerk offices.

      Note Tax Collector Christine Dixon emphasized that security in her office is currently very limited. She
     stated that the Tax Collector’s Office processes approximately $79 million in tax payments each year and
     becomes especially busy and stressful during peak collection periods.
     Ms. Dixon explained that members of the public can walk directly into her office without physical barriers,
     and interactions can occasionally become heated. She stated she would prefer to have security measures
     in place before an incident occurs rather than after and expressed concern about staff safety during tax
     season. While panic buttons exist, she noted their effectiveness is limited without additional security
     support.

      Note During discussion of the Town Hall security cameras, Ms. Sims, one of the Registrar of Voters, noted
     that new state election requirements mandate a camera be positioned on the ballot box when absentee
     ballots are issued, and that video must be retained for an extended period. She explained that this added a
     compliance-related need for reliable cameras and sufficient storage capacity, beyond general building or
     parking lot security.

      Note The cost of the project is largely driven by the server and required software licenses. Board Members
     discussed whether fewer cameras, shorter video retention periods, or alternative equipment sizing could
     reduce costs.

      Note Board Members requested additional information, including review by the Police Department
     regarding camera placement and necessity.
