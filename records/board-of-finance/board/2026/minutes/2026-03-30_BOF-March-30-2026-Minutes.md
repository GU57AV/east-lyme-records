      Note He reported on multiple emergency medical incidents, including a cardiac arrest response on
     Thanksgiving Day, in which four on-duty Firefighters responded and successfully provided life-saving
     intervention. The patient later survived, and the Department received follow-up communication from the
     family.
     He also described a downtown cardiac arrest incident near the theater where Firefighters arrived before
     EMS and began life-saving measures. In addition, he referenced a maternity-related call where crews
     assisted with a home birth, noting the wide range of medical-related calls handled by fire personnel.

      Note Chief Bundy reported that the initial response included four Firefighters on duty, and it took around 12
     minutes to assemble the minimum staffing needed for interior fire suppression under NFPA 2-in / 2-out
     requirements. Additional personnel were brought in through callback procedures and mutual aid from
     neighboring towns to safely manage the incident.

      Note NFPA guidelines call for roughly 10 Firefighters within 10 minutes to safely conduct interior fire
     operations, and that this level of staffing was not reached within the initial response time. As a result,
     mutual aid was required to support operations.

      Note It was further noted that response times are affected by geography and infrastructure, including the
     distance between stations and ongoing road construction. He mentioned that I-95 construction and related
     closures can slow or disrupt travel, sometimes forcing units to pass an empty firehouse while responding
     from another station.

   3-5. Staffing & Budget Request

      Note *The ideal staffing target is 18 Firefighters (3 shifts of 6).
     *He requested additional Firefighters (initially 6, reduced to 3 in budget discussion).
     *Small increases help but do not fully solve overtime or coverage gaps.
     *Mutual aid is essential but also limited because neighbors are also strained.

      Note Chief Bundy explained that the town’s ISO insurance rating is based on several factors, including
     staffing levels, available water supply, and response times. He stated that the town is likely not meeting key
     staffing benchmarks, specifically the standard of having approximately 10 firefighters on scene within 10
     minutes, which can impact compliance and overall rating.

      Note *Training and certification requirements are a significant budget driver.
     *Fire certification classes typically cost between $750 and $1,000 per person, while hazmat training can
     range from $1,200 to $1,500 per person.
     *OSHA and other regulations require mandatory ongoing and annual training.
     *That these costs are required for compliance and are not discretionary.

      Note Chief Bundy briefly discussed capital equipment needs, noting that a new ladder truck can cost
     around $2-3 million. Refurbishing existing apparatus at an estimated cost of around $500,000 is a more
     cost-effective option to extend service life. It takes a long time to buy new fire trucks, and supply chain
     problems are still making it hard to replace them or get them on time.

   3-6. Board Member Questions

      Note Dr. McCallister noted that historically there were no Firefighter hires between 2004 and 2017 and
     asked how that occurred.

      Note Chief Bundy replied that he could not explain the 2004-2017 hiring gap because he joined in 2023,
     but he had the same question when he arrived. He explained that the Department has been playing
     catch‑up because staffing levels have not kept pace with modern demands.

      Note Dr. McCallister asked what could be done locally to increase volunteer participation.


      Note Chief Bundy highlighted the following in his response:
     *Declining volunteerism is a nationwide issue.
     *The Department recently met with the high school, which has a strong EMS program, to explore creating
     a similar fire‑side program to attract younger volunteers.
     *This will not solve short‑term staffing needs because training takes time.

      Note *Historically, volunteers often get certified and then leave for paid departments.
     *Collective bargaining rules in some departments prohibit or discourage their members from volunteering
     elsewhere.
     *The town’s existing tax‑abatement incentive program helps, and he encourages continuing to explore
     incentives.
     *Volunteers face the same training and professional standards as career Firefighters, which makes
     balancing life and service difficult.

      Note Dr. McCallister asked whether the Department could realistically hire three new firefighters if funding
     is approved, if certified candidates are already available, or if academy programs would make those hires
     feasible.

      Note Chief Bundy offered the following response:
     *He originally requested six new firefighters but reduced the request to three for the budget proposal.
     *Ideally, the town would hire applicants who already hold required certifications (Firefighter II and EMT).
     *Competition is intense because all municipalities are trying to hire from the same limited pool.

     He asked how staffing works if fewer than three positions are funded.

      Note Chief explained staffing must increase in multiples of three, so each shift gains one firefighter; adding
     only one position does not create a usable crew or reduce overtime. Call volume is unpredictable; major
     incidents occur at all hours.

      Note Mr. Steel asked about the leadership structure and Chief detailed the following:
     *The Deputy Fire Chief position was eliminated last year.
     *Funding was redistributed to support three shift captains and the Deputy Fire Marshal.
     *Fire inspectors and the Deputy Fire Marshal handle code enforcement and investigations, not operations.

      Note Mr. Steel asked how captains were selected and Chief explained that an independent oral board of
     outside Fire Chiefs ranked candidates; the top three were chosen.

      Note Mr. Steel asked whether the National Guard could assist when members are deployed. Chief said he
     has never heard of that being done but is willing to look into it.

   Note Dan Cleary, the IT Director came forward and presented an overview of town-wide IT operations:
  *The Department supports over 120 software applications, approximately 32 network environments (including
  VLANs and segmented systems), and roughly 19-34 town departments, depending on classification.
  *IT functions as a centralized support service for all departments, reporting administratively to the First
  Selectman while providing direct technical support across the entire organization.
  *Each Department Head is responsible for their own operations, while IT is responsible for maintaining and
  supporting all technology systems used by those departments.

   Note *The IT department provides network and server administration, application support, cybersecurity
  protection, 24/7 emergency response for critical systems.
  *Public Safety Departments (Police, Fire, Dispatch) are the highest priority due to their need for continuous
  system availability.
  *Public safety technology relies on 30-40 interconnected applications, including 911 call‑recording systems
  (required by law), Dispatch and communication platforms, vehicle tracking systems, state and federal database
  connections.

  Note *These systems must operate together continuously to support Police, Fire, and EMS.
  System Availability (Uptime)
  *2024- 96.8% uptime (6 days unplanned downtime).
  *2025- 99.9% uptime (9 hours downtime).
  *Current projection- 99.8% uptime (17 hours downtime).
  *Scheduled maintenance is not included in these totals.
  Causes of Downtime
  *Cyberattack attempts targeting government systems (no successful breaches).
  *Network disruption attempts.
  *A major incident involving failure of the backup power/battery system at the Public Safety facility, causing 45
  minutes of outage before power was manually rerouted and systems restored.

  Note *The current IT workload includes approximately 22 support tickets per day, about 7 quick-response
  issues (password resets, minor fixes), and approximately 15 more complex technical issues per day.
  *The Department has centralized software purchasing to reduce costs and improve efficiency including
  consolidation of licenses (such as Adobe and Microsoft products), elimination of unused or redundant software
  subscriptions, and Department-wide licensing agreements for cost savings.

  Note *The town uses a 5-year computer replacement cycle, though it has been delayed in recent years due to
  budget constraints.
  *Replacement decisions are based on device age, security risk, and operating system support status.
  *Hardware costs are rising- previously, approximately $1,200 per device, the current estimate is approximately
  $1,800, and the future projected cost is approximately $2,200.
  *Delays in replacement result in increased maintenance needs and reduced performance.

   Note *Copier systems are being centralized and optimized to reduce printing costs.
  *Benefits include reduced toner and maintenance costs, replacement of multiple standalone printers, and
  extended use of leased equipment where feasible.

   Note *The Department adjusts contract timing to take advantage of lower pricing cycles, such as end-of-year
  vendor discounts.
  *Some contracts reflect higher short-term costs but are expected to reduce long-term expenditures by
  approximately 30-40% in certain categories.
