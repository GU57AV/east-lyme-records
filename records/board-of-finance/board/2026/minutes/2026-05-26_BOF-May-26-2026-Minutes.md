2. Public Comments
   2-1. Dave Swinburne, President of East Lyme Firefighters Union

       Note Mr. Swinburne expressed concern about the proposed delay in hiring firefighters and the impact on
      staffing levels, safety, and response times. He emphasized that the Department is already stretched thin
      and that delaying hires would worsen coverage gaps, particularly during daytime hours. He urged the
      Board to maintain the originally proposed firefighter staffing plan.

   2-2. Chief William Bundy, East Lyme Fire Department

       Note Chief Bundy explained that the Department’s staffing model relies heavily on the timing of new hires
      and that delaying the three firefighter positions until January would significantly affect station coverage,
      overtime, and the Department’s ability to meet contractual staffing requirements.
      He noted that the Department has been working to stabilize staffing after years of shortages and asked the
      Board to consider the operational impacts of any reductions.

   2-3. Deborah Stewart-Van Orden of 21 Hillwood

       Note Ms. Stewart-Van Orden stated that the voters had already sent a clear message when they rejected
      the first budget, and she believed the message was not that the schools should be cut further. She
      emphasized that many residents- including those without children in the schools, value the quality of East
      Lyme’s education system and see it as central to the town’s identity, property values, and long‑term
      stability.

      Note She expressed concern that the Board was focusing too heavily on reductions to the Board of
     Education budget rather than addressing structural issues on the Town side. She warned that if the Board
     continued to reduce the school budget, the revised budget would likely fail again, because the community
     has repeatedly shown strong support for maintaining educational quality.

      Note Mr. Dowd addressed the Board with strong concerns about the direction of the budget process

     residents did not want cuts to the schools, and the referendum results reflected that sentiment. He
     emphasized that the Board of Education budget is already lean and that further reductions would directly
     harm students, teachers, and classroom resources.

      Note He urged the Board of Finance to work collaboratively with the Board of Education rather than
     repeatedly reducing their allocation. He stressed that the schools are a core part of what makes East Lyme

     property values. He also expressed frustration that the Board appeared to be focusing reductions
     disproportionately on education rather than addressing Town‑side spending growth.

      Note Mr. Dowd said he wants the Town to clearly explain to the public what changes are being made

     including him, don’t know where that money went. He asked the Board to make sure voters understand the
     decisions made at Thursday’s meeting, so the Town doesn’t end up repeating this process again next
     week.

  2-5. Kevin Glenn, Chief of Police

      Note Police Chief Kevin Glenn said he wanted to get straight to the point. He understands the referendum
     failed and that the Board of Finance now has to make cuts, and he wants to cooperate. He explained that
     the easiest thing to cut might seem like the two half‑year police officer positions in the proposed budget,
     which cost $66,000. But if those positions are removed, the Department would need an extra $20,000 in
     overtime for each one ($40,000 total), so the real savings would only be $26,000.

      Note He said many people don’t realize how new staffing reduces overtime, so he wanted to make that
     clear. To help the Board reach the same $26,000 reduction without cutting the two positions, he suggested
     cutting $14,000 from part‑time police staffing (bringing it to zero) and cutting $12,000 from overtime, spread
     across boat patrol, summer walking beats, and general enforcement.

      Note He asked the Board to focus on the actual dollars saved rather than the perception that keeping the
     positions means adding staff, since his proposal achieves the same financial reduction.

  4-1. Cash Capital

      Note Dr. McCallister’s gave an explanation of the current and prior year cash capital amounts. Cash capital
     stands at $1,362,951 this year, compared to $998,000 last year, an increase of roughly $364,000. Dr.
     McCallister proposed reducing cash capital by $262,951, bringing the new total to $1.1 million.

      Note MOTION (11)
     Mr. Steel moved to reduce the cash capital outlay by $262,951, lowering the total from $1,362,951 to $1.1
     million for the upcoming fiscal year.
     Ms. McNamara seconded the motion.

      Note Ms. Kalajainen asked how much money the Board had actually removed from the capital budget,
     since the proposed cut to cash capital didn’t seem to match the total reductions. Mr. Gervais explained that
     the Board had cut about $745,000 in capital items, leaving only the $75,000 ELCC ADA project. After
     adding back $45,000 later for groomer and bleacher repairs, the net capital reduction was about $700,000.
     He then explained that reducing cash capital by $262,951 meant that the Town would need to shift that
     amount into five‑year bonding, because the Board of Education’s capital projects could not be fully funded
     in cash.

      Note He noted that some smaller items could be bonded, but the biggest issue was the $350,000 school
     technology line, which includes devices that only last four years. Ms. Kalajainen pointed out that it makes
     no financial sense to bond something for five years when it only lasts four, and asked whether the Town
     had any option other than a five‑year bond.

      Note Mr. Gervais explained that the Town cannot issue bonds for less than five years, so five years is the
     standard term. He noted that many of the capital cuts the Board made were items that would normally fall
     into the five‑year bonding category, such as the beach groomer. He said he would support reducing cash
     capital only to the point where the Town is not forced to bond items that do not have a five‑year lifespan.

   emphasized that the Board should avoid bonding anything that depreciates faster than the bond term. Mr.
   Gervais added that the Town should not be paying for a loan on something we no longer have.

    Note The goal of cash capital is to avoid the Town’s past pattern of bonding short‑lived items, which
   created a debt service spiral. Cash capital should be used for items with shorter lifespans, while larger,
   longer‑lasting capital investments should be bonded.

    Note Mr. Gervais identified three projects that could be moved into bonding because they each have a
   lifespan of at least five years: $20,000 for HVAC work, $55,000 for patrol rifles, and $20,000 for regional
   tactical team equipment, totaling about $75,000. To reach the full $262,951 needed to shift out of cash
   capital, he explained that the Town would also need to bond a portion of the Board of Education
   technology request.

    Note The ClearTouch boards about $83,304 for 26 units qualify for bonding because they last five years or
   more. The teacher laptops, estimated at roughly $100,000 for 100 elementary teachers, could also be
   bonded if the school district commits to using them for at least five years. Mr. Gervais calculated that
   shifting approximately $200,000 of the school technology line into bonding, combined with the HVAC and

   $18,249 remaining in cash, bringing cash capital to $1.1 million. By moving HVAC, patrol rifles, tactical
   equipment, and the long‑life technology items into bonding, the Town’s total bonding level dropped from
   $4.8 million at the start of the meeting to $4.3 million, while cash capital still increased by about 10%. He
   noted that the Town would now be bonding less than its annual debt service, which was a major financial
   goal.

    Note Because these adjustments were made internally, the Board did not need to change the motion. The
   original motion, to reduce cash capital by $262,951, could stand as written. Board Members agreed,
   provided that the school district confirms that both the elementary teacher laptops and the ClearTouch
   boards will be used for at least five years.
