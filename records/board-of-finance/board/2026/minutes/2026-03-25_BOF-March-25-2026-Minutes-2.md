      Note *Radio communication tower (separate but major capital item).
     *A townwide radio tower project is critical for communications across many departments (Police, Fire,
     EMS, schools, Public Works, Senior Center, Water & Sewer, Parks & Rec, and others).
     *The original request was $1.5 million, reduced by the CIP Committee to $1.365 million.
     *The goal is to fix significant coverage gaps and connect more effectively to the state’s core system, also
     benefiting from Waterford coverage overlap.
     *Future needs may include portable/mobile radios for some departments.
     *The Police Department currently does not need new radios because equipment bought in 2021
     (previously considered incompatible) is now being used and can access the state system.

      Note *Each department will decide later whether it needs to buy new portable/mobile radios if the tower
     project moves forward.
     *Dispatch will need new equipment (consoles) to fully connect to the state’s core radio system, and the
     town recently received a $300,000 grant to support that part.
     *The current system properly covers less than half the town for in-home and on-road communications.

      Note *Coverage will improve to roughly 90% to 95%, which is a major safety improvement.
     *The tower project includes a 100-foot monopole, site work and permitting, antennas/microwave
     dishes/cabling, software and programming, a secure equipment bunker, and a generator plus battery
     backup to avoid reboot interruptions during outages.
     *The current preferred site also requires extending power roughly half a mile, adding cost.

      Note *The total cost for the site is now estimated at $2.8 million.
     *The amount needed in the upcoming fiscal year is only the first phase, 48% upon deliverables, which is
     about $1.344 million.
     *This fits under the current CIP request amount of $1.365 million, leaving a small contingency.

      Note Ms. Kalajainen asked how the tower design was developed and whether the town obtained
     competitive bids.

      Note Chief provided the following background:
     *East Lyme currently relies on an older, town-owned radio system supported by four existing towers, and
     that system has had serious coverage and reliability problems for years.
     *The town essentially had two paths- rebuild and operate its own modern town-run radio system (which
     could cost roughly $5 to $10 million and would leave the town responsible for ongoing maintenance and
     liability) or connect to Connecticut’s statewide “core” radio system.
     *Police currently have access to the state system, but because of local coverage gaps they still rely on a
     temporary workaround that uses both systems, while other departments are not fully positioned to do that
     yet.

      Note *Moving onto the state core system requires following state specifications and vendor requirements.
     *The state uses Motorola equipment and an approved Motorola contractor (Norcom) for implementation.
     *The town uses state contract pricing and works with the state communications division to review the
     project documents before the town’s own legal review.
     *Because it is under the state’s vendor structure, he said the town does not have a typical competitive
     bidding option for this work.

      Note The Chief said Waterford is moving onto the state’s core radio system, which is also what most
     Connecticut towns use. When asked if any towns do not use the state system, he gave North Haven as an
     example and said it built its own Motorola system in 2018 because it was more cost-effective for that town
     at the time. Based on East Lyme’s current situation, he said creating a brand-new town-run radio system
     from scratch would not be realistic, so joining the state core system is the most practical option.

      Note Staffing justification-
     *Chief originally requested four new officers, but the request in front of the Board is now two, one starting
     in July and one in January.
     *While the Department has 29 total officers serving a population of about 19,000, that headcount does not
     reflect how many Officers are actually available on patrol at any given time.
     *Many positions are supervisory, investigative, administrative, or required for compliance and accreditation,
     leaving 18 patrol officers spread across three shifts.
     *With rotating schedules, days off, training, and leave time, there can be only a few Patrol Officers on duty
     at a time.

      Note Chief said the Department is understaffed and pointed to a 1970s roll-call sheet showing more
     officers per shift than can be staffed today; he also clarified that officers seen at the I-95/Exit 74
     construction site are working paid details funded by the contractor and do not add to regular patrol
     coverage.
     He presented a goal of 41 officers (up from 29), increasing staffing from about 1.5 to about 2.2 officers per
     10,000 residents, and said this would strengthen administrative oversight, potentially add school resource
     officer coverage, create a small traffic unit, and raise patrol staffing from 18 to 24 (roughly two more per
     shift after schedules). He supported this with comparisons showing East Lyme is low relative to peer towns
     by population, shoreline communities, land area, and grand list metrics, and said reaching the goal could
     take about six years at two officers per year or about four years with a faster ramp-up.

      Note Chief said that while the town has invested strongly in education, other town services, including
     policing, have not kept pace as East Lyme has grown. He argued that underinvestment creates real costs
     and risks, citing more than $220,000 spent in 2025 on outside legal support and internal investigations, and
     noting additional potential expenses tied to required decertification actions and ongoing litigation, including
     two pending lawsuits and possible settlements.

      Note He said current staffing levels limit patrol visibility and can contribute to longer response times, and
     he noted frequent requests from residents and Main Street businesses for more presence, such as walking
     beats and additional community programming.
     Increased staffing would allow the Department to add capacity where it is currently stretched, including
     more Detectives for complex investigations, School Resource Officer coverage (ideally at least one next
     school year, potentially two), stronger command-level oversight to reduce liability, and a dedicated traffic
     unit to address speeding and other traffic-related quality-of-life issues.
     He reiterated that while he originally requested four additional officers, the current proposal in front of the
     Board is smaller, about 1.5 funded positions.

      Note Board Questions:
     Ms. McNamara asked if adding officers would help reduce the town’s liability by improving supervision and
     oversight. Chief said yes, and explained that additional command-level support outside the union (such as
     a captain or assistant chief type role) would strengthen internal affairs work, accountability, and day-to-day
     management. More staffing, including additional detectives and a dedicated traffic unit, would also help
     reduce risk by improving how investigations and enforcement are handled.
     Ms. McNamara also asked whether calls for service are increasing; Chief said they are and cited the
     annual report, estimating a 20% to 22% increase from 2024 to 2025.

     stronger communication and coordination, increase day-to-day Police presence in schools, and can
