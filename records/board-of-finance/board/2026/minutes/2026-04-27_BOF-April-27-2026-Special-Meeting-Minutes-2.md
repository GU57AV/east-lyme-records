   3-3. Technology & Other Capital Items

      Note Ms. Kalajainen raised a concern about the technology line in the Capital Improvements Plan. She
     questioned why the town was putting technology purchases into a five‑year bond when the devices don’t
     even last that long. She said the Board needs to know exactly what devices are included, how often they
     need to be replaced, and why this is being treated like a long‑term capital project

      Note MOTION (10)
     Ms. Kalajainen moved to move $350,000 for Technology & Other Capital from the 5‑year Bond to Cash
     Capital.
     Ms. Bullock seconded the motion.

      Note The comments led Dr. McCallister to call Facilities Director, Mr. Lund up to explain the details.


      Note Mr. Lund provided an in‑depth presentation on the District’s technology system and replacement
     needs. Some of the following was highlighted:
     *The technology system is huge, aging, and absolutely essential.
     *It includes student Chromebooks and laptops, staff laptops, ClearTouch boards, the network,
     testing‑ready devices, and specialized tools for STEM, art, and special education.

      Note *All of this equipment together is worth about $3.8 million, and the District must keep replacing
     devices on a regular schedule to prevent major system failures.

      Note Mr. Lund explained that the District replaces all student and staff devices every 4 years for several
     important reasons:
     *After 4 years, Chromebooks stop getting ChromeOS updates, which creates security risks.
     *Older devices can’t run state testing software reliably.
     *Batteries wear out badly after year 3.
     *Failure rates jump in years 4 and 5.
     *New instructional software often won’t run on old hardware.

      Note Mr. Lund warned that if the District doesn’t replace devices on time, whole grade levels won’t be able
     to take state tests. Old devices that are out of warranty or no longer get updates can’t run SBAC or NGSS.




Page | 5                                                                 Minutes created with
      Note He added that that the District’s ClearTouch interactive boards last 7-10 years, depending on the
     model, and they are used every single school day in almost every classroom. Because the warranty only
     covers 3 years, any failures after that become expensive for the District.
     The District has been replacing these boards slowly and, on a schedule, so they don’t end up with a huge
     wave of failures all at once. Spacing out replacements avoids a future pile‑up where dozens of boards
     break at the same time and overwhelm the budget.

      Note Chromebook prices have gone up 20-30% since COVID because of supply‑chain problems.
     Meanwhile, laptop prices have gone down, which makes them a better choice for high school students.
     Replacement parts for older devices- like screens, keyboards, and batteries, are now more expensive and
     harder to find. And the District can only buy devices that are approved for state testing, which means they
     can’t switch to cheaper models that don’t meet requirements.

      Note Mr. Lund agreed with the concern about bonding technology. He said devices only last 4 years, so
     putting them in a 5‑year bond means the town would still be paying for them after they’re already thrown
     out. Bonding also adds interest costs and doesn’t make the equipment last any longer.

      Note Cash capital is the right way to fund technology because it matches the 4‑year replacement cycle,
     avoids paying interest, and keeps the District’s technology plan steady and predictable. It also prevents big
     budget spikes in future years and ensures the District can always meet state testing requirements.

      Note The Board and Mr. Lund had a detailed conversation about all of these technology issues.


      Note Mr. Steel asked whether paying off existing leases early would save money and Mr. Gervais

      Note Mr. Gervais agreed with the shift to Cash Capital. He explained that in past years the District paid for
     devices through lease payments, but Ms. Kalajainen's motion moves this funding into cash, which means
     the town won’t pay interest and won’t get stuck in a 5‑year bond for devices that only last 4 years.
