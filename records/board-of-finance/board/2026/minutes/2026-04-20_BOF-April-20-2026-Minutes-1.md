3-7. Capital Budget

   Note MOTION (9)
   Ms. Kalajainen moved to reduce paving the allocation from $750,000 to $650,000.
   Ms. Bullock seconded the motion.

    Note Ms. Kalajainen asked Mr. Gervais to clarify the real financial impact of reducing the paving line when
   the project is funded through a five‑year bond. She noted that although the cut appears as $100,000 on
   paper, the actual impact depends on how the debt is amortized.

    Note Mr. Gervais explained that the $750,000 paving allocation is bonded over five years and reducing it
   to $650,000 lowers the principal by roughly $107,333. The first‑year savings would be modest- only $5,000
   to $7,000 because the reduction affects a bonded capital item rather than the operating budget.

    Note Ms. Kalajainen emphasized that while the line‑item cut is $100,000, it does not translate into a
   $100,000 savings in the FY27 operating year, since bonded capital reductions do not produce immediate
   tax relief.

    Note Mr. Bragaw came forward and explained that the paving budget had already been cut by $100,000
   earlier in the process, so this additional reduction effectively becomes a $200,000 total cut.
   He highlighted the following:
   *The town’s pavement management plan relies on doing full drainage inspection and repair before paving.
   *With reduced funding, the department would be forced to do surface‑only work, skipping drainage repairs.
   *The Black Point area is overdue for work (15–20 years) and a cut may mean only partial completion.
   *East Lyme has 118 miles of road; reduced funding slows progress.
   *The town has been completing 4–5 miles per year, which is considered good; cuts will reduce that.
   *Delaying maintenance increases long‑term costs: untreated roads may cost three times more to repair
   later.
   *Several major roads still needing reconstruction, including Drabik Road and Grassy Hill Road.

    Note Ms. Kalajainen asked how much of Public Works overtime is contractual and unavoidable.
