    Note The review is being driven by several factors, including declining enrollment, excess capacity at some
   elementary schools, differences in class sizes among schools, concerns regarding the transition from
   fourth to fifth grade, and long-term financial sustainability.
   Enrollment has declined by 637 students since the 2010-2011 school year and enrollment projections
   indicate relatively stable student populations over the next decade, though future hiring growth at Electric
   Boat remains an unknown factor that could affect enrollment levels.

    Note Mr. Bauman said the Board of Education is focused on two main goals: improving student learning
   and managing costs over the long term. He noted that Niantic Center School currently has the most
   unused space among the district's schools.

    Note He reviewed several options being considered, including adjusting school boundaries, consolidating
   students into fewer buildings, and repurposing school space for district or community use. Some options
   have already been removed from consideration due to space or operational concerns.

    Note Mr. Bauman said the Board of Education will continue gathering public feedback and reviewing the
   remaining options before making any decisions.

    Note The Board will also consider student impact, class size balance, building use, transportation, staffing,
   financial sustainability, and community feedback. Public forums, surveys, public comments, and a
   dedicated email address are being used to gather input from residents.

    Note Additional analysis of projected cost savings will be presented to the Board of Education in
   December. He noted that any potential savings would need to be weighed against one-time implementation
   costs, such as facility modifications, office relocations, or building reconfigurations.

    Note Dr. McCallister requested that the Board of Education return in December to present additional
   information regarding projected cost savings and enrollment projections and Mr. Bauman agreed to return
   with their findings.

    Note Ms. Kalajainen suggested that future presentations include educational research supporting the
   various grade-level and school configuration options under consideration. Discussion included the cost of
   out-of-district placements for special education students and whether additional analysis should be
   conducted to determine if some services could be provided locally while still meeting student needs.

    Note Ms. Kalajainen asked if future analyses could include information on building conditions, long-term
   capital needs, and how facility investments may influence reorganization decisions.

    Note Discussion also focused on the financial impacts of the various options. Mr. Bauman explained that
   the Board of Education is evaluating both potential long-term savings and any one-time implementation
   costs. Additional cost-savings information will be presented in December, and the Board of Education
   intends to keep the Board of Finance informed throughout the process.

    Note Mr. Sheckley stressed the importance of demonstrating how any proposed changes would improve
   student outcomes and achievement. Mr. Bauman responded that student achievement remains a primary
   consideration in the evaluation process and noted that additional information would be provided as the
   study progresses.

    Note Ms. Bullock expressed appreciation for the presentation and the Board of Education's efforts to
   explore options that could address future educational and financial challenges while keeping the
   community informed.

     increased demands on town services and illustrates the need to continue monitoring service levels.

     that additional discussion would be held at a future meeting.

6. Old Business- FY2028 Budget Projection

  Note The Board discussed establishing a 4.97% budget increase target for the FY 2027-2028 Town and
 Board of Education operating budgets. The target was developed using projected costs such as labor
 contracts, employee benefits, pensions, inflation, and other known expenses.

  Note MOTION (3)
 Mr. Sheckley moved that following the 2027-2028 budget projection procedures discussed at the October
 Board of Finance meeting; the Board of Finance sets an expenditure target for the respective Town and Board
 of Education 2027-2028 operating budgets of 4.97% above the respective 2026-2027 operating budgets
 approved for the Town and the Board of Education.
 Ms. Bullock seconded the motion.

  Note Dr. McCallister thanked Mr. Sheckley and Mr. Gervais for the significant work that went into developing
 the budget model. While he would have preferred a lower number, he feels the proposed 4.97% target is a
 reasonable place to start and is supported by available data. He noted that setting the target too low could
 result in departments returning later in the budget process requesting additional funding.

  Note Ms. Kalajainen requested additional information regarding future costs that are already committed
 through labor contracts, benefits, and pension obligations. She also raised concerns about the impact of
 revaluation and future tax increases, noting that residents could still see higher tax bills regardless of the
 budget target. She questioned how the proposed increase would affect the mill rate, reassessments aside.

  Note Dr. McCallister responded that the Board of Finance's primary responsibility is reviewing and controlling
 expenditures and that factors such as property assessments, revaluation, and Grand List growth are largely
 outside the Board's control. He stated that the purpose of the expenditure target was to provide departments
 with a realistic guideline as they begin developing their budgets. Mr. Sheckley echoed those comments, noting
 that the Board has more direct control over spending than revenue-related factors.

  Note Ms. Kalajainen stated that while she believed Mr. Sheckley's analysis was thorough and well supported,
 she did not believe the proposal addressed the broader issue of reducing the Town's overall spending. She
 expressed concern that the 4.97% target would not place enough pressure on departments to identify
 efficiencies, eliminate waste, redesign processes, and reduce non-salary expenses. She noted that residents
 had clearly expressed a desire to see lower spending and indicated she would oppose the proposal.

  Decision MOTION (3) Vote- Mr. Sheckley restated the motion: Following the 2027-2028 budget projection
 procedures discussed at the October Board of Finance meeting, the Board of Finance sets an expenditure
 target for the respective Town and Board of Education 2027-2028 operating budgets of 4.97% above the
 respective 2026-2027 operating budgets approved for the Town and the Board of Education.
