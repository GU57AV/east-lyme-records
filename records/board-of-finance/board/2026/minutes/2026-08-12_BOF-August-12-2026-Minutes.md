     *He attended a statewide seminar in Milford on Flock cameras and ALPR (automatic license plate readers).

     *The Board of Selectmen already approved the speed camera contract.
     *The State must now determine where cameras can legally be placed based on traffic and speeding data.
     *After that, there will be a public hearing.

      Note *Speed cameras and Flock cameras are separate and distinct.
     *Speed cameras enforce speeding violations.
     *Flock cameras (ALPR) capture license plates and vehicle data.
     *The major difference is what data is stored, how long it’s stored, and how metadata is handled (GPS,
     time, location, etc.).
     *Flock is a brand name.
     *There is pending legislation, and the town will need a broader discussion before moving forward.

      Note Dr. McCallister asked about project costs for the old Police building.

     and around the Old Police Station:
     *Asbestos present in multiple areas.
     *Lead was detected in building materials.
     *PCBs were found in certain components.

      Note Mr. Cunningham explained that abatement alone is estimated at roughly $300,000, even if the
     building is ultimately demolished. Phase 2 will include core sampling of the parking lot to determine the
     extent of contamination. The town may qualify for a State brownfield grant, which could significantly reduce
     the local financial impact.

   5-1. Tax Collector Report

      Note Mr. Gervais reported that tax collections are ahead of last year.

     *As of August 5th, this year, 54.4% collected

   5-2. Fund Balance & Budget

      Note The FY24 draft fund balance is approximately $18.7 million, representing 22% of total revenues.
     The town originally expected to use $1 million of fund balance to close the fiscal year.
     Updated projections show the town may need only about $100,000, and the town may instead add roughly
     $1 million back to the fund balance.

      Note Revenue Summary-
     *Budgeted revenues: $96.66 million.
     *Collected $96.55 million.
     *Shortfall is around $133,000, effectively on target.

      Note Major revenue over‑performance included-
     *Interest income was budgeted at $1.25 million and collected $1.75 million.

     * The Fire Department achieved savings despite overtime costs.
     *The Highway Department saved about $50,000 due to vacancies.

   5-3. FY2024 Financial Statement Audit Update

      Note Mr. Gervais reported that past town audits have taken between 379 and 650 days to complete.
     The goal is to reduce the audit timeline to under 365 days, with a long‑term target of 180 days.

      Note Mr. Gervais noted that more timely audits strengthen the town’s bond rating.


      Note Dr. McCallister asked why past audits were slow.
     Mr. Gervais explained delays stemmed from outdated systems, staffing gaps, and unimplemented
     software. The town had been paying for Munis since 2018 but did not implement it until 2024. Payroll and
     time‑and‑attendance systems were also outdated.

   6-1. FY2028 Budget Projection Proposal

      Note see attachment.
           0013_001.pdf

      Note Mr. Sheckley presented a proposal to modernize and streamline the FY28 budget process, focusing
     on clearer expectations, better data, and improved coordination. Key elements include:
     *Early assumptions- establish core assumptions at the start of the cycle (inflation, interest rates, grand list
     growth, Mill rate stability, Employee wages, benefits, and pensions, operating cost inflation, capital project
     inflation- construction vs. technology, Board of Education expenditure trends, Fund balance policy, Coastal
     resiliency and other BOS‑driven project needs.)
     *Budget guardrails- set clear guidelines for departments before they begin preparing requests.
     *Use actuals as baselines- shift from prior‑year budgets to actual spending data when determining starting
     points.
     *Operational metrics- require departments to include measurable performance indicators.
     *Public communication- strengthen how budget information is shared with residents.
     *Joint meetings- consider periodic joint sessions with the BOE and BOS to improve alignment.

      Note *Build a FY28 projection model using actual data from Finance, Assessor, and Treasurer.
     *Provide both Town and BOE with starting budget figures early in the process.
     *Departments would allocate within those figures and return to the BOF only if additional funding is
     required.

      Note Board members expressed strong support for the proposed reforms.


      Note Dr. McCallister expressed strong support for proactive budgeting but cautioned that the BOF should
     not advise the Board of Selectmen on departmental management, since the BOF’s role is financial
     oversight.

      Note Ms. Kalajainen praised Mr. Sheckley's modeling framework and recommended using prior‑year
     actuals rather than prior budgets as baselines. She also encouraged each department to provide key
     operating metrics to support their budget requests.

     Works, Parks & Recreation, IT, Board of Education) to improve communication before budget season.

      Note Each BOF member would serve as a liaison to one department.The liaison would act as a
     communication link between the Board and the department head. Liaisons would provide brief updates at
     BOF meetings about department needs, issues, or upcoming budget impacts.
     The goal is to build ongoing relationships, identify concerns early, and make the budget process smoother.
