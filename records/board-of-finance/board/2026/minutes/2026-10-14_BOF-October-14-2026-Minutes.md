2. Discussion and Possible Action - Review of Short-Term Investment Policy

  Note see attachment.
       Short_Term_IP.pdf

  Note Deputy Treasurer Hall came forward and explained that earlier in the year the Town completed an RFP
  for banking services. During that process, they determined it would be beneficial to create a formal short-term
  investment policy to provide clear guidance to the Finance Department regarding excess cash balances.

   Note The policy was developed using sample policies from CCM and GFOA and tailored to East Lyme's
  needs. The policy is intended to provide written instructions for how Town funds can be invested while
  complying with Connecticut law.

  Note She explained that Connecticut General Statutes limit the types of investments municipalities may use
  with general fund money. Since tax revenues are collected throughout the year and then spent to operate the
  Town, investments must remain safe and easily converted to cash without risking principal.

  Note Permitted investments generally include:
  *Certificates of Deposit (CDs).
  *U.S. Treasury securities.
  *Government-backed money market mutual funds.
  *Repurchase agreements backed by government securities.

  Note Ms. Hall emphasized that the Town's primary goal is to preserve principal and maintain liquidity, rather
 than seek higher returns through riskier investments such as stocks or long-term bonds. She also
 recommended annual reviews of the policy to ensure continued compliance with state regulations.

  Note Board members asked questions about the proposed investment policy, including who would serve as
 the Town's designated Investment Officer and whether the Finance Director should automatically hold that role.
 Ms. Hall explained that the Finance Director would typically be designated by the First Selectman, while Mr.
 Cunningham noted that the current language intentionally provides flexibility for future circumstances.

  Note Board Members also requested clarification on the policy's provisions regarding interest rate risk. Ms.
 Hall explained that the policy limits investment maturities to reduce risk and ensure funds remain readily
 available when needed. She noted that the policy applies only to short-term investments of general fund
 money and does not apply to long-term investment programs such as pension funds.

  Note It was questioned whether the authority to designate the Investment Officer should belong to the Board
 of Selectmen or solely to the First Selectman. Ms. Hall indicated that flexibility could be appropriate but
 acknowledged the issue was ultimately for Town officials to determine.

  Note Board Members discussed governance and oversight concerns, including whether requiring approval by
 the full Board of Selectmen would provide greater accountability than leaving the decision to a single
 individual. It was noted that Town Counsel had reviewed the policy and that it was believed to be consistent
 with Town Charter requirements.

  Note Mr. Steel pointed out that the policy does not create new investment practices, but instead formally
 documents procedures already being followed by the Finance Department. Ms. Hall agreed, explaining that the
 policy simply establishes written guidelines for managing short-term municipal funds through low-risk
 investments.

 Decision MOTION (2)
 Ms. Kalajainen moved to approve the Short-Term Investment Policy.
 Ms. McNamara seconded the motion.
 Motion carried, 5-0-0.

6. Finance Director Report- Mr. Gervais provided an updated report on the current status of
the budget.

  Note He reported that firefighter overtime remains a significant budget concern. Current overtime costs are
 averaging approximately $17,000 per week, compared to the budgeted level of about $12,500 per week. If this
 continues, overtime expenses could exceed the budget by approximately $240,000 to $375,000 by year-end.

  Note Recent overtime costs had begun to improve, dropping from $19,000 per week to about $13,000 per
 week. He attributed some previous increases to storm-related staffing needs and personnel shortages caused
 by military leave and injuries.

  7-1. FY2028 Budget Projection

      Note Mr. Sheckley presented a summary of the budget forecasting model he developed with assistance
     from Mr. Gervais. The model is intended to establish budget targets and improve long-term financial
     planning.

      Note see attachment.
             Scanned_20261109-1412.pdf

      Note Mr. Sheckley explained that analysis of prior budgets showed Town spending growth has historically
     exceeded inflation by approximately 2.75%. Based on that history, the proposed budgeting framework uses
     inflation plus 2.75% as a maximum budget growth guideline.

      Note Using current assumptions, the projection estimates:
     *Town operations increase: approximately 4.1%.
     *Board of Education increase: approximately 5.0%.
     *Overall expenditure increase: approximately 4.97%.

      Note Mr. Sheckley explained that these figures would serve as targets to guide departments as they
     prepare budget requests. The process would also include accountability measures requiring departments
     to establish goals and performance indicators.

      Note Board Members supported the new budgeting process and praised the work that went into developing
     the model. Several Members expressed concern that a projected budget increase of nearly 5% could face
     strong opposition from taxpayers.

      Note Board discussion focused on balancing fiscal realities, contractual obligations, inflation, and taxpayer
     affordability. Some Board Members favored setting a lower target to encourage departments to identify
     efficiencies and cost savings before requesting additional funding. Other Members cautioned that
     unrealistically low targets could undermine their credibility if they prove unattainable.

      Note Mr. Gervais explained that many projected increases are driven by fixed costs such as union
     contracts, pension obligations, health insurance, debt service, and previously approved staffing additions.
     Expenditure growth and tax increases are separate issues that should be evaluated independently.

      Decision The Board agreed to continue refining the budgeting framework and requested additional
     information regarding fixed contractual expenses before establishing final budget targets.

      Note A suggestion was made to form a small working group or subcommittee to continue discussions
     outside of regular meetings.
