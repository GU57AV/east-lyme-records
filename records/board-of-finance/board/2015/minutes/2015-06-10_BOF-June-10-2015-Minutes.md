James Levandoski, Flanders Fire Chief said that he had Dan Somers, Deputy Chief and Bill Rix, Assistant
Chief with him this evening to do a PowerPoint presentation and to help answer any questions they might

Mr. Somers narrated the PowerPoint presentation for the Tower Ladder Replacement Proposal (Copy
attached) He said that they are looking to replace the rear mount 1989 truck with a mid mount new one.
He explained that it failed testing this year as there is a visible twist in the ladder.

Mr. Rix explained that the mid mount can be set up anywhere and could also go under the low bridge going
into Black Point and Crescent Beach. They currently have a three section rear mount and are looking at a
five section mid mount. This is not necessarily just vertical reach as the horizontal reach is also important as
there are many houses in town that are 60’ from the road. They have met with two vendors, Ferrara and
Smeal and they have the specifications and feel that they are ready to go to bid.

Mr. Harney said that it takes about a year before they would have the new one built and asked what they
planned on doing in the meantime with the repairs that need to be made to the truck they have now.

Mr. Somers said that they have discussed this and that they can do some lower-cost repairs such as welding
for $3000 and replacing hydraulic hose lines for up to $1000. They are guessing that the worst case for the
repairs that they would do would cost from $5000 to $10,000.

Mr. Kelley asked if the visible twist of the ladder out of alignment could cause it to get stuck.
Mr. Levandoski said perhaps but that they hoped not even though the aerial cannot be certified.

Mr. Carpenteri asked what the possibility of the ladder truck failing was.

Mr. Somers said that it passed the 800 Ib. load test hanging at the end of the bucket. The failing was due to
some engineer somewhere deciding that % inch variance was the number and that is what the standard is
and the reason why they failed.

REASONS FOR THIS PROJECT
FUEL TANKS

The existing fuel tanks at 12 Roxbury are over 26 years old while their useful life is 30 years. At this
point, our insurance carrier won’t cover underground storage tanks (UST’s) older than 25 years so it will
be problematic to insure them.

When UST’s get within 3 years of their end life, the testing requirements start getting much more costly.
Most of the Highway/Sanitation and Parks and Recreation equipment are at the FSB so we would like to
have the fueling station closer to our operation.

Understanding that the school does not want to have the buses go on the highway and/or drive 5 miles to
fill up, we were still looking to install a new above ground diesel tank at 12 Roxbury for their
convenience

The new fueling station at FSB would have a canopy and a new fuel management system. The existing
system is archaic and runs only with a very old computer with a dot matrix printer. We would like the
ability to have remote access to the fuel system to improve the efficiency of the billing process.

very hard to get more salt. This proposed salt dome would have a capacity over 3,000 tons or 250%
