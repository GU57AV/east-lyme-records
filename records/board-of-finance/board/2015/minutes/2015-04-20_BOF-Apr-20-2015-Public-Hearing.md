EAST LYME BOARD OF FINANCE
PUBLIC HEARING MINUTES
FY 2015-2016 PROPOSED BUDGET
Monday, APRIL 20th, 2015

A. Call Public Hearing to Order

Chairman Hamey called this Public Hearing on the FY 2015-2016 Proposed Budget of the East Lyme Board
of Finance to order at 7:00 PM. He noted that the Public Hearing notice was published in the Day newspaper
on April 16, 2015.

B. Pledge of Allegiance
The Pledge was observed.

Mr. Harney welcomed everyone, and said that they would first hear some comments from the Board of
Education Chairman, Tim Hagen and then from the First Selectman, Mark Nickerson.

C. Comments by Board of Education Chairman

Tim Hagen, Board of Ed Chairman thanked everyone for coming and said that while the 2/9% increase is not
what they would have liked that it is what they will work with. He said that he would like to introduce the new
Superintendent of Schools, Jeffrey Newton.

Jeffrey Newtown, Superintendent of Schools said that he has been working to get up to speed and thanked
everyone for coming this evening.

D. Comments by First Selectman

First Selectman, Mark Nickerson also thanked everyone for coming. He said that just after he took over that
the meetings with the departments on the budgets started. There are no new positions being added here.
The major driver is wages and benefits — especially health insurance which has been budgeted for a 10%
increase and that may not even be enough as the costs keep going up. The cost of insurance for the
infrastructure in Town has risen by 15%. They are always looking to try to make these numbers come down
and they have worked hard on this budget.

E. Board of Finance Presentation of Proposed Budget for FY 2015-2016

Mr. Harney thanked everyone for coming, introduced the Board members and said that they all work to
provide services to the Town while trying to maintain affordability. He synopsized the events that brought
them to tonight noting that they have held many budget meetings prior to this evening. He further explained
that they do not have any control over how the BOE allocates their money and that since the budget is not
bifurcated; it gets voted on together with the Towns’.

In a Power Point presentation (attached at end of Minutes) he noted some of the major projects completed
this year:
Completion of Roof replacements at town Hall and Central Office
Completion of the Rest Room Facilities at Vet’s Memorial Park

Partial Implementation of Mandatory Teacher Evaluations

Completion of Regional Water Interconnection Project

Commencement of boardwalk construction

District-wide Safety and Technology Infrastructure Upgrades in our Schools
Acquisition Program — BOE Technology and Town Vehicles and Equipment
Replacement of High School Tennis Courts

= Mr. Harney gave a summary of the budget process and noted that the current budget is $65,113,598
and the budget being submitted this evening for comment is $67,170,328 — a 3.16% increase. They
estimate that it would represent around a .67 mill increase.

F. Public Discussion
Mr. Harney called for public comments and asked that speakers state their name and address for the record.

Karen Rak, 27 Black Point Road said that she has been coming to these meetings for years and that she
opposes the proposed budget due to the current economic indicators - such as: 50% of renters (48% in east
Lyme) spend more than one-third of their pay on rent; cost of ground beef has doubled since 2009; after
adjusting for inflation median household income has fallen nearly $5000 since 2007 (Federal data); 62% of
all Americans live paycheck to paycheck and 43 million Americans have unpaid medical debt (Attached at
end of Minutes). Further, the annual performance reports for the schools are contrary to the reports of ‘losing
our edge’ that we are being told as we aren't and that term is being used to manipulate people. People are
paying more in taxes than ever before. May 13 is tax day in CT — this is tied with New Jersey for having to
work this long into the year to cover their tax burden. This economy is still down by 5.9M jobs and despite the
continued increase in education spending over the last 50 years, according to NCES there is little to no
correlation between education spending and achievement. Over a five year period the US Dept. of Education
increased education spending by $1.6B. The myth of budget cuts is just that, a myth. We do not go into the
voting booth to vote on a department, specific budget or line item therefore, she asked that they present a
flat or reduced spending budget.

Ron Rando, 194 Boston Post Road said that he has lived here for 79 years — a lot longer than a lot of the
people. He recently heard that one of the former chairs of the finance board who educated his kids here has
now moved to Waterford. He asked about the tennis courts and the final cost and if they fixed the river that
runs underneath them as if not, it will happen again.

Mr. Nickerson said that they cost $750,000 and that when they dug in, they did find the water and they fixed
it -in fixing that — the lights did not happen as there was no money left for them.

Mr. Rando said that we increase our budget every year and people can't afford to pay their taxes or bills. He
said that he has noticed that they do not show any of the Town or Board of Ed salaries in the budget and
asked for a figure on the salaries. He said that they give the Board of Ed a bottom line figure and let them
figure it out as to how they spend it. He hopes that the new superintendent will be more mindful of the people
who live here. Further, they have two fire departments in the Niantic section of Town and they do not need
them; there should only be one; they should also sell the water & sewer department to a private organization
and regionalize the school systems and make the superintendent work for his money. He said that he was
surprised with Beth Hogan as he had backed her as First Selectman but now has a totally different opinion of
her. There are 1500 houses for sale in East Lyme — people are moving out as they cannot afford to live here.
Also — the State is $144M in the red so far this year.

Sarah Beaudin, 61 Terrace Ave. said that what concemed her was something in the newspaper today about
the sports programs. She said she saw that a $250,000 cut was being made and asked who will be cut from
this sports program. She would like some information on it. She said that she was an educator in New
London for many years and loved working there.

Mr. Newton said that he is not aware of the article and that nothing has come across his desk — he asked
that she share the article with him.

Joe Mingo, 397 Boston Post Road said that what concerns him is that the Waterford School budget came in
at 1.66%. He has lost count of the number of administrators here in our schools. They also have a number of
unfunded liabilities that have to be taken care of and the condition of our roads is of concern to him. Any
increase in the mill rate more than eats up his Social Security increase.

Maia Vargus, 18 Pontiac Drive spoke in favor of the BOE budget and asked that they not cut it any further.
She said that she is an educator elsewhere and that education is not where cuts should be made. The
educators here know where to make the cuts in their budget.

Gill Gallant, 167 Boston Post Road said that he is the president of the BOE Teachers Association and wants
them to restore dollars to the BOE budget just as Mr. Lombardo and the BOE have said as they are the most
important thing in Town and the next cuts they will make will be to the Police and Fire. The teachers need to
make a decent wage and it is their position to fund education. He said that he was a teacher in Town when
Beth Hogan was in office. The 2.9% increase in the budget is not enough.

Mike Schulz, Lovers Lane said that he wanted to thank the Recording Secretary for the minutes that she
does as he has been reading them on-line and does not think she is given enough credit. He also thanked
the Board of Finance and BOE members for their hard work and time on this budget. He wanted to speak
about sports first as he has advocated that sports should be ‘pay for play’ and speaking of which — some kids
knocked on his door with coupons for a $20 donation — this, he said, gives him the option to pay for what he
believes in. He said that parents need to get a better hold on sports activities and the associated costs as it
is not the general taxpayer's responsibility to pay for them.

He said that he reviewed the teacher contracts and from what he saw no teacher will move a step — they get
COLA - if they had a step — they would have had an 8% increase. The number of administrators has
increased along with their salary increases — in 2014-2015 it was 1.75%; for 2015-2016 — it is 3% and 2016-
2017 it’s 3.25% - the cost of living is no where near this. If you are not satisfied or happy with this location -
then move — but do not take it out on this Town and its’ people.

Jim Lombardo, 4 Maple Street said that he has lived in three states and worked in five different school
districts over the past 30 years and the taxes here are the lowest of all and the services are the best — they
have an excellent educational program for very low taxes, He thanked the Board of Finance and the teachers
who have been very entrepreneurial over the years with programs. The budget has increased over his time
of service here some 10% and not the 45% Ms. Rak spoke of. Right now East Lyme starting salaries are low
in comparison to the same type of areas. When he was superintendent he said that he would hire people at
lower levels than where they should be or what they were qualified for due to the cuts. He also spoke about
the Chinese program explaining that it is a 6 year program with years 4, 5, and 6 bringing 10 Chinese
students here with the revenues coming in to cover the cost of the language teachers necessary for the
program. He said that he thinks that the Town and school system need to work out some sort of plan on this
as if it goes in as revenues to the Town there will have to be another line to take it back out for use for the
program and they should not be penalized for having a good program. He also advocated to the Board of
Finance that the school board had 7 meetings on the budget going line by line and they only had one
meeting with the BOE and then made cuts. He appreciates that they say that they cannot affect line items.

Karen Rak, 27 Black Point Road said that she would like to comment on what Mr. Gallant has said -

The Board of Finance has not been decimating the BOE budget and she further finds it disgusting that he
stands up and advocates for his own salary. The BOE has been getting increases and she would like him to
provide facts to back up the statements such as that which he has made.

Ron Rando, 194 Boston Post Road said that he had a few questions. He asked about the school

enrollment this year versus last year.
Mr. Newton said that the projection is for them to be down by 88 students; they are down by 25 this year.

Mr. Rando said that if they are going to be down by 88 students — what happens to the money that you
would have used to educate them.
Mr. Newton said that it is getting absorbed across the rest of the district for the 2800 other students as they

will not lost all 88 students in any particular classroom - it will be spread out.

Board of Finance Public Hearing Minutes - FY 2015-2016 Proposed Budget — April 20, 2015 3
Mr. Rando asked how they are going to pay for the whole Resident Trooper salary as the State has been
talking about.

Mr. Nickerson said that they now pay $155,000 and to get to 100% it will be $45,000 more. They have
accounted for the potential for it in the budget and if it is not needed then it will be in the undesignated fund
balance.

Mr. Rando asked if they know what they are going to get from the State.

Mr. Nickerson said that right now it looks like it will remain flat — there is a bit of a decrease in LoCIP and
PILOT. He said that the CCM group meets and that they are still not sure of what the State will do. Some
of the State proposals are very scary.

Barbara Senges, 17 Green Valley Lakes Road said that she has been on the BOE for one year and has lived
here for 25 years. The BOE did a demographic study and there is a drop in enrollment in this State due to
the aging population. She thinks that they are being short-sighted about the elderly ability to pay as the grand
list is up; there is building going on and there are houses being built in her neighborhood that are costing
$350,000 to $450,000 and people are coming here with kids that will go to the East Lyme schools. The grand
list is growing because of these people coming here. She said that the BOE had said that a 3.2% increase
would be a flat budget and they gave 2.97%. Taxes are a bargain — they were $3000 per year 25 years ago
for her and they are now $5000 per year and that is a good bargain. We have an aging population because
the young people are moving away. She said that she wants some money put back in the BOE budget.

Bill Derry, 212R Pennsylvania Ave. said that he is speaking in support of the BOE budget. He thanked Mr.
Lombardo for sharing the problems that he said that he has had with hiring. He teaches in another Town and
they had to hire under the same issues but they got a new superintendent and things changed. He said that
the school system that he works for is a good one. The Town cannot afford to lost good teachers and the
teachers need to feel that the Town supports them. He thinks that this Town has the potential to fix this
problem.

Maia Vargus, 18 Pontiac Drive said that the BOE has meetings all the time and people do not come to them
— they should so that they can be involved in the process. She also said that the parents are paying extra to
support the kids and doing fundraising. She noted that she has been at BOE meetings where she has been
the only one there.

Mr. Harney thanked everyone for all of their comments.

G. Board Comments
Mr. Harney called for any comments from the Board.

Ms. Hogan said that efficiencies in the budget has taken a toll and that she thinks that when the BOE builds
it's budget that it should be from the bottom up and not the top down as the biggest discrepancy year after
year is the same dialogue. They need to stop talking about teachers not being paid enough and have a
dialogue to work on that. She said that she would vote for a restoration of some funds but they would have to
say that then the next year they will no longer hear about the inequity in teachers pay — that would have to be
their priority. She said that she does not think that the restoration of $100,000 is going to change anything
and that the power is on the BOE side to change this inequity.

Mr. Nickerson said that the inequity in pay is not just within the BOE but across the line in Town - among all
workers — however, when you compare to Waterford — they have a $3B grand list and all sorts of associated
commercial areas; which is $1B more than ours and they can afford to pay out more. So — when comparing
at least hear the facts as most of the taxes here come from us and not a Dominion etc.

Ms. Alberti thanked everyone for coming and said that she knows that most will not be satisfied with any
outcome - they have to make some hard choices and they also did not just take one meeting to come up
with decisions.

Mr, Harney thanked the Board members and everyone for their comments. He thanked the BOE members
and said that they are all in a difficult position and in a County that is having great difficulty with economic
recovery.

H. Adjournment
Mr. Harney called for a motion to close the Public Hearing.

“*MOTION (1)

Budget at 8:40 PM.

Ms. Picarazzi seconded the motion.

Vote: 6-0-0. Motion passed.

Mr. Hamey said that they would now go to the INTV Room and commence their Special Meeting and that
