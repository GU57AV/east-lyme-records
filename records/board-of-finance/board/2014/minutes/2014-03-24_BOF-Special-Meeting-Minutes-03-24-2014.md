BUDGET REVIEWS — FY 2014-2015

A. Call Special Meeting to Order
Chairman Kelley called this Special Meeting of the East Lyme Board of Finance to order at 6:30 PM.

B. Pledge of Allegiance
The Pledge was observed.

C. Delegations
Mr. Kelley called for Delegations.
There were none.

D. New Business

a. Budget Reviews — FY 2014 - 2015

¢ Planning Department & Commission (117) - Gary Goeschel, Planning Director

Mr. Formica recalled that they had combined the combined Planning Department and Commission and the
increase has to do with the salary. He explained that this is a four day position and that Mr. Goeschel is the
Inland Wetlands Officer to complete the week. He said that Mr. Goeschel was present if they had any
questions.

Ms. Hogan asked if the COLA was being put into the figure.
Mr. Formica sald that Ms. Johnson puts it in during the last quarter and that the 6.31% is not accurate.

Mr. Kelley asked about the $61,019.

Ms. Johnson said that the actual figure falls somewhere in-between the two numbers that they see.

Mr. Formica said that he thinks that they should do the salary transfer so that the figures in the books are
correct as the 6.31% is confusing. He noted that there is a COLA every year with a step increase added to

that every other year.

¢ Inland Wetlands Agency (132) - Gary Goeschel, IWA Officer

Mr. Formica explained that Mr. Goeschel is also the Wetlands Officer and that this is 20% of his salary. They
were requesting an increase however he reversed it back as he could not justify increasing one person when
he felt that all of them deserved an increase.
Mr. Kelley said that the budget after the adjustment is up by $150.

¢ Zoning Commission (116) - Marc Salerno, Zoning Commission Chairman, William Mulholland,
Zoning Official

Mr. Formica noted that the salary adjustment would be the same here and added that Mr. Salerno may be

here to discuss the new Consultants line item that the Board of Selectmen had reduced from $4000 to

$2000. He added that so far they have not had to use it. Last year they took it out and this year they left it at

the $2000.

Mr. Salerno said that he thinks that the $2000 should meet their needs. This is an ‘in case’ item as he thinks
that at some point they may need to have a traffic study done for one of the applications that they receive.

¢ Ledge Light Heaith District (108) - Steve Mansfield, Deputy Director LLHD & Paul Formica

Mr. Formica explained that this is based per capita and that it went up by 10 cents per person to $6.98 per
person from the previous $6.88. The population figure of 18,892 includes the prison population. He added
that Ledge Light pays the Town for a room downstairs in the Land Use area and that we provide office
support for them there.

Ms. Hogan asked for an update on what they have been doing.

Mr. Mansfield said that the regional health district includes New London, Groton, Ledyard, Waterford and
East Lyme. They have been involved with communicable disease education amongst a number of other
interesting areas. Baker Salisbury, Director recently did a Lyme disease presentation.

Ms. Picarazzi asked if the per capita rate is the same for every Town.
Mr. Mansfield said yes.

¢ Niantic Fire Department (217) — John McDonald, Niantic Chief
Mr. Formica noted that the increase in the salary line item is not posted as they are in labor negotiations. He
noted that the cost for fuels is being offset through the generosity of the Ambulance Association.

Mr. Kelley said that the PT and Overtime line items are about 27% of the regular salary line and they are
increasing next year by $12,000 without the negotiated raises — he asked when it becomes more cost
effective to increase the FT so that the PT and Overtime line items come down.

Chief McDonald said that right now they do stagger personnel and shifts. The FT people work a nine hour
day so they get one hour of overtime. The overtime line item is for call backs for fires so this really is the ‘luck
of the draw’ so to speak. The PT’s fill in for the FT people and they also work nights from 5 to 11PM and
weekends 8AM to 3PM and 3PM to 11PM so that there is coverage for vehicles to get right out the door for
calls. If they were to hire a FT person, they could not cover all of the stations with people.

Ms. Picarazzi thanked them for all that they do and asked what percentage of Overtime is contractual.

Mr. McDonald said that there is an hour each day and also when they add an extra shift — that has to go to
the FT’s first.

Ms. Picarazzi said that she echoed Mr. Kelley's comments as the Overtime rate is 1.5 times the regular rate.
Mr. Formica said that they would look at this during contract negotiations.

Mr. Kelley thanked them for all that they do to keep the Town safe.

¢ Flanders Fire Department (218) - Jim Levandoski, Flanders Fire Chief
Mr. Formica said that this is much the same situation as the Niantic Fire Department.

Chief Levandoski said that he thinks that when you compare the pay and full benefit package of the FT’s and
if you look at if you want someone coming from their homes for an emergency rather than from the fire
station to answer that emergency call that this seems the better way at this time.

Mr. Kelley said that the question was when it becomes more cost effective.

Mr. Levandoski said that it is difficult to answer and noted that Sgt. Blanchette also could not answer that
question when asked. They are all in the same situation.

Ms. Hogan asked about the radios as she had thought that Emergency Management was taking care of that
system.

Mr. Levandoski said that it is a battery issue — they are all five years old and they are all going and need to
be replaced.

Ms. Alberti asked about training.

Mr. Levandoski said that he tries to cover it as he can with the PT and FT people.

Mr. Formica said that they built the meeting room at the Field Services building so that they could host some
of those sessions here and get one free from time to time.

Mr. Kelley thanked them for all that they do for the Town.

¢# Emergency Management (224) — Dick Morris, Fire Marshal
Mr. Formica noted that he had removed the fifth dispatcher as the negotiations are moving forward with the
three Towns. Those numbers are adjusted. The rest of the increases are for phones and training.

Ms. Alberti asked Mr. Morris and Ms. Johnson about the 5% increase for the Director and it is the same issue
of not being moved.
Ms. Johnson said yes.

Ms. Alberti asked how that could be fixed to reflect the accurate amount.
Ms. Johnson said if they did the increases before the fiscal year starts or if the transfers were cone before
the budget session starts it would be reflected correctly.

Ms. Picarazzi asked about the Overtime line item showing an increase of 21.63%.

Mr. Morris said that it is contractual and that when people take their time off that they have to fill it with a FT
person - which is overtime. He said that he had projected a need for 28 days and that it went over. The
contract calling for this to be done this way has another two years to it.

Ms. Picarazzi asked if it would be worth doing some cost benefit analysis for some of these budgets with the
overtime and the way that it is handled due to contract.

Mr. Harney asked about training and miscellaneous supplies.

Mr. Morris said that training is $150 per person and they send three people for certification which includes
mileage which gets costly. There is also Fire Marshal certification and he has a few going to 911 training
school. The miscellaneous supplies are for paper, printer cartridges, computers and data.

Mr. Kelley thanked them for all that they do.

¢ Animal Control (226) - presented by Paul Formica

Mr. Formica explained that this budget represents 50% of what it costs for the Animal Control Agent who is a
police officer in Waterford. This person may or may not be in this position as they are a 20 year person and
they may retire and come back as other than a police officer. They are also looking at a new facility. East
Lyme currently provides the van and Waterford the facility. Dominion has been very kind in providing
$25,000 to offset the cost of the van. Waterford has raised over $120,000 for a new facility and they have
been working between Waterford and New London as New London had a tree come through their facility in a
storm. He noted that they approached Corrigan in Montville about building a new facility on the outskirts of
their facility by the inmates there as they have the talents to do that type of work. The facility would then be
maintained by those same inmates with each Town also having their own animal control officer to bring the
animals in and to do the processing and to also work with the public. If this were to go forward the $120,000
would go far as the labor would not be the issue. They already have an extensive animal rehabilitation
program there. They have been involved in discussions on this and if it were to go forward — it would be an
opportunity to stabilize the costs. For now, the figure that they are seeing here is what it is. He said that he
would keep them posted as discussions continue.
