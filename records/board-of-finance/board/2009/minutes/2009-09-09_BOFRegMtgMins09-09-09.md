b.   Discussion and possible action to approve Appropriation for Acquisition of Properties and
     to Issue bonds
Mr. Larcen asked Mr. Formica to comment.
Mr. Formica noted that the Board of Selectmen had moved to send this forward to them in a unanimous
vote earlier this evening. He synopsized the Colton Road and Capitol Drive property noting that many of
them had been on the tour in late August of the facilities and equipment that would benefit from the
purchase of this property. The Board of Selectmen had discussed this in Executive Session and
determined that they wanted more information on the retrofit aspect. He went over some of the numbers
and said that it seems that they have the perfect storm of events here – between a bad economy, our
own debt reduction, the low cost of borrowing money and our working together and good fiscal
management to be able to do this.

Mr. Giannattasio explained that they had looked at this building before and that when it truly became
available, they had Mr. Alberti from Weston & Sampson take a look at it over the last few days. He said
that Jeff has been in the business for 17 years and has worked for many Towns doing exactly what he
was asked to do here. He will present a Power Point presentation on this building and his findings and
answer any questions they might have.

Jeff Alberti, Weston & Sampson said that he has worked in this field for many years and greatly enjoys
evaluating and retro-fitting buildings for the various needs of the Towns that they are asked to service.
He went through the Power Point presentation (copy attached at end of minutes) explaining that he first
determined what the Town Department of Public Works does, what they have now and what their needs
are. He noted, among other things that pre-engineered metal buildings are cost-effective and that this
building has the advantage of having a fully insulated metal siding wall and roof system with interior


Board of Finance Regular Meeting Minutes – September 9, 2009                                               4
liner panel and adequate interior height for mezzanine and vehicle lifting. With respect to some
disadvantages he cited the interior columns pose potential conflict, no internal floor drains and the 6”
slab-on-grade with heavy equipment traffic may result in a reduced service life of the slab. He explained
that the cost of purchasing property and constructing a new building would highly exceed the cost of
this building and its retrofit and added that many Towns do exactly what they are considering here.

Mr. Taylor asked about the slab floor maintenance and when and if it would be necessary to cut out
areas in the future if there would be problems with the adjacent areas.
Mr. Alberti said that he does not anticipate replacing certain areas being any problem and added that
with the boring samplings they could determine the PSI and characteristics of the concrete and that he
would suggest having it done at the outset if they decide to purchase the building.

Mr. Larcen asked what type of numbers they would be talking if they found that the base is
substandard.
Mr. Alberti said that they would have to determine from the borings what needed to be done and go
from there.
Mr. Giannattasio noted that they would know this prior to any purchase.

Mr. Larcen asked about the aisles.
Mr. Giannattasio said that the aisle width is approximately 6000 sq. ft. and takes up a little over 20% of
the space. The Town owns approximately 100 pieces of equipment and they would need to park 40 in
this building and it can park 42 vehicles.

Mr. Larcen asked the type of vehicles that would be parked in this building.
Mr. Giannattasio said it would take the pick-ups, trailers, dump trucks and plows, mid-size trucks,
sweeper and the automated garbage trucks. The old garage would be used for other storage and for
the old backload garbage trucks.
Mr. Larcen said that he is impressed that they can get 40 vehicles into the facility. He then asked if the
‘wash area’ is the same area where they proposed storing the automated garbage trucks.
Mr. Giannattasio said yes.

Mr. Jones asked if the salt shed is staying where it is.
Mr. Giannattasio said that he thought so along with the current fuel facilities as that is an expensive
project.

Mr. Birmingham asked how they would protect the columns from being hit.
Mr. Alberti said that with 6’ to 8’ bollards that are affixed to the floor. He noted that typical stalls are 12’
which includes the vehicle and walk path. He said that of the facilities that he has seen that this is
certainly one of the better ones.

Mr. Taylor asked Mr. Alberti if new buildings of this size would be $6M.
Mr. Alberti said yes they would without the land and that with the land they would be around $8M.

Mr. Larcen asked about the roof repair on the old facility.
Mr. Giannattasio said that they would defer that for right now.

Mr. Langan asked for a comparison of the anticipated operation costs including the operational
expenses of the old facility.
Mr. Alberti said that there are a number of factors that they would have to have in order to project this.
Mr. Giannattasio noted that they have been shown operational savings with the cost of fuel in the start
ups of the vehicles.

Mr. Birmingham asked if the Board of Ed rental of trailers would go away.
Mr. Larcen said that he thought that was unclear since the Board of Ed sent mixed messages on what
they actually use them for as some are for off season storage of program equipment and supplies.
