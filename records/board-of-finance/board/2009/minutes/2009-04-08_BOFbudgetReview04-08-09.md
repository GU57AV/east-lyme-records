 A. Call Meeting to Order
 Chairman Larcen called this Regular Meeting of the East Lyme Board of Finance to order at 7:00 PM.

 B. Pledge of Allegiance

 C. Delegations
 Mr. Larcen called for delegations.

 Robert Gadbois, 358 Boston Post Road said that he sat here when the Board of Ed met with them two
 nights ago and that he was shocked when they said that they need 19 substitutes per day and that they
 also have a paid person who calls the substitutes each day. When he worked as a pipe fitter, if you were
 out over periods of time repeatedly, the boss would tell you that you were gone. He also said that since
 they have offices with secretaries working at each of the schools – those secretaries could call the
 substitutes for their schools instead of having a separate person doing it. He said that he finds that paying
 for a math coordinator is unforgivable to the taxpayers of the Town. If the teachers cannot teach math –
 then they should not be teaching at all. He lastly said that Mr. Taylor had asked about the givebacks from
 the school teachers and administrators/personnel and that they have known about it for a good two months
 and have not bothered to come back with anything.

 Mike Schulz, Lovers Lane said that as they are going to begin discussion on deliberations tonight that he
 has brought some information that was in the newspapers regarding other Towns – 14 teachers were cut in
 Stonington; 21.5 positions were cut in Norwich; the Waterford budget is under discussion with a
 recommendation on freezes; in Florida, 522 first and second year teachers contracts were not renewed;
 additionally he has read and outlined for them, the Town contracts and cited that the increases range from
 6% to 16% and from $3700 to $7000. The Town department heads have given back COLA’s and the
 Superintendent and Asst. Superintendent have given back theirs. What about the rest of the union people
 and the school administrators who have not been approached for concessions. They should be
 approached. Many of the Town department line items have been cut. The school budget needs to be cut.
 What about the taxpayers who are paying for these wages – how about giving back 3% - that would still
 leave 3% for an increase which is more than fair in this economy. If they do not agree to this – then lay
 them off. The unions have become a detriment to our Town and to the economy – showing up for work is
 not a reason to get an increase and the step increases have to go as they are killing everything.

 G. Old Business
 ♦ Discussion / Deliberations – Proposed 2009/2010 Budget
 Mr. Larcen suggested that they review the revenue picture as it has changed. He noted that they had
 copies of Option A – Governor Rell’s projection and Option B – the Legislature projection and that there are
 few changes between them. The differences would be (worst case scenario for right now) $26,958 less in
 TAR; $100,000 less in PILOT; $55,000 less projected in Building fees and Conveyance taxes and $45,000
 less in landfill deposit projections.
 The consensus of the Board was to err on the side of caution because they do not know where the State
 will end up and they do not want to over-project revenues and end up very short. They agreed that without
 including any fund balance monies that they would need to find $1,748,091 to balance the budget. It was
 also agreed to go with a 98.4% tax collection rate due to the state of the economy, and by recommendation
 of the Tax Collector.

 Ms. Carini asked if they knew what the lowest collection rate figure was that they had calculated at.
 Mr. Jones said that he recollected that the lowest was in the high 80’s percentile quite some time ago.
 Mr. Taylor said that it was before Ruth Ames time.

 Mr. Formica said that he would agree with the sentiment of the Board to take a conservative approach
 regarding any revenues from the State as they do not have any figures from them that they can ‘take to the
 bank’ and probably won’t for some time. Should they get more than they are figuring at this time, they can
 certainly add it to revenues later.

 Mr. Larcen asked about any potential activity that might be in the pipeline for 2010.
 Mr. Formica said that there may be some small projects but nothing like what they have seen.

 Mr. Birmingham asked about the CVS and McDonald’s at Flanders four corners and the effect they have on
 the Town.
 Mr. Formica said that the permits generate income for the Town but the buildings themselves generate
 rental income for the property owners.

 trimmed pretty well already as he had scrutinized them and found them to be running rather tight.
 Mr. Formica explained the thinking regarding the trial of buying supplies in bulk from a particular supplier
 and seeing how they do with that in an attempt to save money.
