 Ai''|ffi*: Chairman Hall introduced the Board of Finance Members, discussed the budget process, and said some
 of the following:
 Overall, the Board considers the proposed revenues, expenses, capital and what it would ultimately mean to
 the mill rate, and to people's taxes.
 The budget was submitted to the Board of Finance on March 8th, 2023, with an overall expenditure increase
 of 8.77'/" and a revenue increase of 8.27%" from the fiscalyear 2022-23 budget.
 This would have resulted in a mill rate increase to 25.45.
 They held 6 meetings to review and deliberate on the proposed budget and met with Department Heads and
 with the Board of Education.
 The BOF proposed reductions that would have resulted in a mill rate of 24.98.
 The Board of Finance will meet immediately after tonight's Public Hearing to further finalize the budget, which
 will subsequently be voted upon at the Town Meeting on May 8th, 2023, followed by the Annual Budget
 Referendum on May 18th, 2023.
 In terms of budgetary goals, they have been working to increase the undesignated fund balance to get to 15%
 of total expenditures in the General Fund.
 Financially, they want the town to be healthy and have the ability to withstand any unforeseen events that could
 take place throughout the year.
 Their aim is 15% and they've seen other towns that are both higher and lower.
 They went through a risk analysis using a template from CCM, and they recommend that we have greater than
  16%.
 They want to reduce our bonded debt.
 That's another metric that when the rating agencies look at us; they decide how much debt per capita each
 town has.
 That goes into their determination of what our rating should be.
 The higher rated you are, the lower the interest rate assigned to those bonds.
 They want to keep some of these factors in mind so that we can hopefully get to a point where we could get a
 rating upgrade.
 They're also always looking to limit the burden on our taxpayers.
 Some of the challenges as Mr. Seery alluded to is the debt service is large.
 We have to make sure we fund our pension for our retirees.
 They're looking at possibly, over the next 5 years, having to put 2 million ayear into the pension.
 These are some of the challenges we face.
 We were lucky to have a reval last year and see really good growth of the Grand List.
 But history has shown us that that that doesn't keep going in that direction.
 lf we are entering into a continued economic recessionary mode, then the ability for continued growth of the
 Grand List can be constrained somewhat.
 These are some of the things that we're looking at as we look to put the budget together while trying to
 balance all of these issues.
 Our total proposed operating budget is $86,015,876, which is an increase of 7.16%.
 Our mil rate will be 24.98.
 The assessment of one's home is 70% of the home value.
