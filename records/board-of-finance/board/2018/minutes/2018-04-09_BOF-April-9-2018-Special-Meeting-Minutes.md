A. Call Special Meeting to Order
Chairman Weber called this Special Meeting of the East Lyme Board of Finance to order at 6:31 PM.

B. Pledge of Allegiance
The Pledge was observed.

C. Delegations
Mr. Weber called for Delegations.

Jamie Barr Shelbum, 31 McElaney Drive, read from her prepared statement (not submitted). She asked for
their support for the BOE budget and the hiring of two social workers and a psychologist. She said that she
feels that we are having a mental health crisis in the country and the schools. The State has cut mental
health and other services so the Towns have had to pick it up. She asked that they fund these necessary
items.

Lori Singer, 31 South Ridge Road spoke about the budget and the science standards at the schools. She
said that they are supposed to have kids acting as ‘scientists’ in the classroom but the budget is bare bones
and they need supplies. She asked that they not cut the budget as if they do they will decimate the science
classes. She said that they don't want to fall backwards. They cannot afford a cut.

Robin Levine, 130 Black Point Road said that she has lived in Town for over 20 years and that she also
works for the BOE as a Para-professional. The Town will not be great educationally if the budget is cut. The
blue ribbon schools will become average and good teachers will leave. They must keep our education as it
is. They also must take care of the social/mental health programs. If the budget is cut — it will have a ripple
effect. She said that she too, moved here for the schools.

Victoria Thompson, 51 Plants Dam Road said that she is a parent and a teacher at the High School. She is
looking at classroom sizes pf 25 and It makes her look at Magnate schools for her kids as she wants her kids
to have the best resources. In order to feel confident, they need the resources. Kids have a lot of academic
pressure today. She asked that they support this budget.

E. New Business

a. Historic Properties (139) - Barbara Low Johnson; Benn Bullock

Mr. Nickerson said that Ms. Johnson took over after the untimely passing of Luane Lange last year. She has

worked hard and is here to answer any questions that they might have.

Ms. Johnson thanked Mr. Nickerson and said that she would like to introduce Benn Bullock, the President of

the Samuel Smith House and property to speak regarding what they do.

Mr. Bullock said that it is a 1685 house and that they are strictly a volunteer group. They do the best that they
can to raise funds and give of their time to maintain the house. They also offer programs for the kids on what
it was like to live during 1685.

Mr. Birmingham asked what the plans are to bring the house up to par as it suffers from some disrepair.

Mr. Bullock said that it really is not in great disrepair as there was a family living in there 20 years ago. They
are residing the house and they have a five year plan which includes the roof. The house is not going to fall
down - it is structurally sound.

Mr. Birmingham asked what the goal for the property was. He noted that he has been to see it.

Mr. Bullock said that they plan to continue to work to bring it back as best as possible to what it was during
its time in history. It has an apple orchard and it was a working farm in that time frame.

Ms. Picarazzi asked the estimated cost of the five year plan and if the dollars they receive from the Town
keep the property up.

Mr. Bullock said yes, what they receive is adequate and the provisions were to maintain the house.

Ms. Johnson said that it is difficult to answer that question — the dollars they receive will keep it up.

Mr. Bullock noted that, as with any home that as you do repairs you may find other things that may also need
to be done. He said that he does not see any problems at this time.

Ms. Johnson said that all three of the houses are working together to raise funds — the Lee House, Samuel
Smith and Smith Harris.

Mr. Nickerson said that they have the revenue information and noted that the State is still working on the
budget. He recalled to them that at the end even after their fiscal year had started that the Governor came

$7.1M. At this point in time, it is looking like they will receive some $400,000 less from the State.

that are all a best guess right now — they will have to wait and see.

of last year — so they did not have to use the entire $675,000. He asked if that could happen again.

Mr. Nickerson said that based upon the mid-year cuts that they took that it is a bare bones situation. He
added that Moody's would like to see 12-15% in the fund balance.

Ms. Picarazzi asked Ms. Johnson if she was concemed with the school bonding coming on.

Ms. Johnson said that they would go to Standard & Poor as they should have a better change at keeping up
our rating.
