C. Delegations
Mr. Kleinhans called for Delegations.

Becky Miller, 7 River Road said that she had some to voice her concern about the Board of Ed process. She
explained that her mother was the first class to graduate from ELHS in 1969 and that she now teaches at the
High School herself and was saddened to hear that at least 20 teachers received pink slips. She said that
she heard this information because she is a teacher and that she wants the public to know so that they can
make an informed decision on the budget. She wants the Board of Finance and the Board of Ed to make the
information available so that the taxpayers can speak to this at the Public Hearing on April 25, 2011.

Jim Lombardo, Maple Street – Saunders Point, Niantic, CT said that he was speaking as a resident and not
as the Superintendent of Schools. He said that as a resident of the area without public sewers and one who
lives on a street where water collects when it rains heavy that he thought that he heard about a $900,000
sidewalk that will run along the road for walkers, etc and he feels that the money would be better spent on
neighborhoods such as his that do not have sewers, getting them.

Karen Rak, 27 Black Point Road said that concerning Saunders Point; to Mr. Lombardo that he had just
missed a Water & Sewer meeting where they spoke on the items that he was talking about. She encouraged
him to go speak there as that is where they fund such projects and not here. As far as the Board of Ed
budget goes, the Board of Finance only has total figure control – they do not have the purview of line item
control. Also, they are charged with deciding what the entire Town and all the taxpayers can afford and to
just a certain group. She further encourages people to go to the Public Hearing and speak about how they
want the Board of Ed to make cuts as they have the line item control over their budget. Those who want to
see specific Board of Ed cuts to specific line items need to voice their concerns to the Board of Ed and make
them aware of what they want.

Robin Soule, 14 McElaney Drive said that she is the parent of students in the schools and she feels that the
success of the schools has to do with the smaller class sizes and the teachers. She said that she teaches
and that the classes are much different now than when she went to school. She urged them to look long and
hard at the cuts and to not limit today’s youth.

Madeline Miller, 28 Bush Hill Drive thanked the Town for putting up the Town budgets and detail on the
website and said that she wanted the Board of Ed to put all of their info on their website for all the people of
the Town to be able to see. She said that she had seen two layoffs and six retirements and they are stating
that there are 20 layoffs – she asked why they are not offering the OHIO plan for retirements. She said that
she is concerned with two of the people who received layoff notices – Jim Littlefield and Rose Ann Hardy –
both are part time staff (.20) and both provide invaluable hands on instruction, one in anthropology and the
other in contemporary issues. Jim Littlefield has his students of anthropology working on a dig site. Rose Ann
Hardy has an honors contemporary issues class that is involved in many civic organizations. There is a great
amount of personal integrity that these people bring to these programs. The proposed budget does have
some layoffs in it and does allow the Superintendent to re-allocate dollars – but until they actually bring forth
where – she said that she could not support the Board of Ed budget as requested.

Bob Gadbois, 358 Boston Post Road said that he supports what Ms. Rak had to say and added that he
thinks that both budgets should come in with zero increases. The seniors are hurting very badly and
everyone has to tighten their belts. The seniors and retirees have to pay the same as others who are working
and the prices for gas and oil are increasing.

Mr. Formica noted that the refunding that had taken place last week and that there would be a savings of

Mr. Formica said that they are not sure what will happen with PILOT with Gates closing however he has
spoken with Montville and was told that they do fund regularly until the property value just goes down –
basically from the property not being maintained. He also said that if the State sales tax goes to 6.3% that
there could be something there.

Mr. Larcen suggested that they could increase the Building permit line item. He also asked if anything had
come out of the talks on the Conveyance Taxes.
