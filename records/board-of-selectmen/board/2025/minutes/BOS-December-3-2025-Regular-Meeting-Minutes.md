2a. Automated Ticketing Ordinance
DISCUSSION: Some of the comments and concerns brought up by the Selectmen and discussed
with Chief Glenn are as follows:
    o Q-What amount will the town actually receive as revenue from this program, A-100o/o of
       the fine paid will go to the Town. Administrative costs are covered by the "administrative
       fees" line on the ticket, which is in addition to the fine itself.
    . Q-What can the money received by used for? A-The use of these funds is very specific but
       funding for things like traffic calming rneasures or installation of sidewalks.
    o Q-Are there any type of incentives from the State if the town implements this system. A-
       this program is a town initiative governed by an ordinance that is very specific in its
       guidelines. There are no incentives to be had with this program.
    o Q-Once installed, what will prevent future advancement of the technology beyond what is
       approved today. And if the information is kept by the vendor, the Town can't really
       confirm what they will actually do with the information. A-the system does not collect
       identifying information, only the registered owner of the vehicle by the plate scan. The
       ordinance and subsequent contract with the vendor will outline how the gathered
       information is handled.
   .   If someone moves or something else legitimate causing them not to receive the ticket in
       the mail it could cause more problems for that person,
   r   Ms. Cicchiello reported that at the annual CCM (CT Council of Municipalities) this year, it
       was reported that speeding was the number one problem in every one of Connecticut's
       169 towns. She is in favor of this ordinance,
   o   Q-How does it compare with a police officer issuing tickets? A-with the automated
       ticketing, it will only require a staff person to review the infractions. With an officer
       posted doing radar, they would then have to get in their vehicle and pull the driver over
       immediately. These normal traffic stops take at least 15-20 minutes for that officer to
       complete the transaction. This is time better spent on programs that work with the public
       in a positive way.
   o   Q-Do the Public Works speed signs, like the one near Lillie B. Haynes, collect any data. A-
       Yes, they collect a lot of data and can be utilized to identify possible locations for the
       proposed ticketing equipment,
   r   Q-How will people pay the citations? A-The accepted payment methods are customizable.
   o   Q-How long will data be saved by the vendor? A-Collected data will be stored for a year as
       there is an additional penalty for a second offense within a year of the first offense. There
       is also an appeal process, and that information will be on the ticket and easy to read and
       understand. This program can be tailored to fit specific needs, should these arise.
   r   Mr. Deeble feels that it takes humanity out of policing and could cause unintended
       consequences. He believes there is bias built into such equipment.
   .   Ms. Cicchiello feels like the camera takes away the risk of bias because it only knows if
       you are speeding and then only take a picture of the rear plate once the vehicle goes past.
       She supports this ordinance and would like to see them definitely by the schools and high
       pedestrian areas,
   .   Ms. Carlson is in favor and will support this ordinance.
   .   Ms. Hardy inquired if the program could be started with the existing administrative staff
       and the answer was yes.
   .   Mr. Cunningham spoke with the vendor at the annual CCM conference on Tuesday, and it
       was explained to him that information is kept for the 30 day appeal time frame, then it is
       wiped. He feels it is important to note that the Board of Selectmen can tailor the
       ordinance to East Lyme's needs. He affirmed that we have had a couple vehicle vs.
       pedestrian accidents and the office receives many complaints about speeding all around
       town. He does not feel that this equipment can be biased as it will only ticket those who
       are speeding. He is in favor of this ordinance.

Chief Glenn stated that the first step is for the Board to adopt the ordinance. Once the ordinance
is adopted and in force, the process will begin to try to figure out where in town this equipment
will be useful, and that will be done via a speed study, which will be performed by the vendor.

It was decided that the Board would review the proposed ordinance and Mr. Cunningham will
review it with Town Counsel, and that this matter will be tabled until the next meeting,
