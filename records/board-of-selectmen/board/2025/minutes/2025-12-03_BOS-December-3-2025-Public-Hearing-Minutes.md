Lisa McGowan, 33 Spinnaker, inquired about where the data is going and who it will be
shared with; she is concerned about privacy and profiling. She requested the Board
take a pause before adopting the ordinance. Chief Glenn stated that this proposed
equipment is not part of the State's license reader program, and he noted that the
proposed speed camera program is backed by the State and ACLU and does not collect
or store any identifying information. He acknowledged that he understands her
concerns.

Joshua Caske, One Grooms Way stated that he is generally in favor of the ordinance as
these devices have been proven to reduce speeding and fatal crashes in the areas that
they are installed. He also feels that a program like this overall reduces the risk of bias
because there is no human interaction. He stated that the revenue generated should
be used for installing sidewalks and traffic calming measures in the worst areas of town.

Chris Hanney, 124 N. Bride Brook Road, asked where the cameras would be installed.
Chief Glenn stated that the ordinance needs to be adopted before they can decide on
possible locations, but the areas surrounding the school are priority. Once the
ordinance is adopted, locations will be considered, and public hearings will be scheduled
for the public to weigh in on where they should be placed. Mr. Hanney is concerned
about how having the equipment near the schools and the senior center might
inadvertently penalize a vulnerable portion of the community, including penalizing the
parent who is the registered owner of the vehicle. Mr. Hanney asked how much of the
revenue the State will receive. Chief Glenn stated that the Town receives 100o/o of the
revenue as the administrative fees are built into the infraction. Chief Glenn stated that
the offender would not receive an infraction, but simply a Town fine. He stated that the
fines are not meant to bring in revenue or penalize a person specifically but is intended
to be a tool to promote safety in those areas. Mr. Hanney asked what would happen if
the individual does not pay for the ticket. Chief Glenn stated that it would be up to the
Board of Selectman and included in the ordinance, so he is not yet sure, but that other
municipalities use tools such as debt collection and civil court to recoup these unpaid
fines.

Jim Craddock, 31 Pattagansett Drive, stated that he is in favor of this ordinance as
speeding in town, especially on Boston Post Road, is very dangerous.

Jaime Barr Shelburn, 31 McElaney Drive, inquired as to who will be calibrating the
equipment and how often it will be done to ensure accuracy. She noted that the one by
Lillie B. Haynes is often way off on the speed. Chief Glenn stated that the vendor will
be calibrating the equipment on a regular basis and noted that the electronic sign by
the school is owned by the Town and does not collect any data and simply shows
speed, but that the Public Works department calibrates that equipment. She noted that
she appreciates the effort to slow down speeders, but she feels like this could be just a
mechanism to bring in revenue, citing the recent article in The Day stating an average
income of $1,000,000 per week, Chief Glenn stated that he is not sure where the
repofter came up with that number, and to remember that the goal of this program is
not to generate revenue but to promote safety. The vendor has supplied statistics
showing that within a year, approximately 90o/o of the municipalities are reporting a
drastic decrease in the number of speeding violations and accidents in the areas that
they are installed.
