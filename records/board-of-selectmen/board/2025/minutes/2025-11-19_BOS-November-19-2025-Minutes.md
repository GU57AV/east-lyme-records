2b.   Automated Ticketing Ordinance
DISCUSSION: Chief Glenn was present to answer questions and discuss this proposed ordinance.
He reported that the number one complaint from our residents is speeding throughout the entire
town. In2023, the State gave the Municipalities authority to institute their own individual
ordinances to address individual town's needs. Some of the biggest questions:

a.      Is data compiled through ticketing shared with individuals insurance company or the DMV?
A.      No. An outside vendor collects the data and sends the infractions directly to the
registration holder, and then also collects the fees on the town's behalf. ACH payment will then
be sent to finance directly once the agreed upon payment schedule is decided. No personal or
identifying information on the individual driving the registered vehicle is known.

a.      Is there a cost to the town and taxpayers?
A.      No actual cost to the town or taxpayers, but it will use up manpower and hours of an on-
duty officer to review each instance of ticketing before approving the vendor to invoice. This is
done to ensure that no error was made, as technology requires a human to make a final decision
on if the ticket is warranted. The vendor will assist the PD with implementation of the system,
Should additional staffing be required, this is something that will need to be addressed in the
future and something that might be eligible for reimbursement on the state program.

a.     Is there an appeal process?
A,     Yes. The instructions on how to appeal will be included on the citation. The town already
has a mechanism in place for enforcement in its general government ordinance S 150.036.

a.     How will the town determine placement of said cameras?
A.     They will first concentrate on school zones before moving to other areas of town. A public
hearing will be held in advance of instituting any equipment so that the public is well aware of
where the ticketing equipment is located and when it will become active,

a,     Will this be a big revenue stream for the town?
A,     This program is not meant to bring in revenue but is meant to slow traffic down in areas
of town where the police receive almost daily complaints of speeding and reckless driving.

a      Will there be adequate signage to alert people of the cameras?
A      Yes. Proper signage is mandatory.

a.     Can the equipment be moved?
A.     No; the equipment will be permanently installed in the locations agreed upon.

The Chief further explained that Marlborough was the first to institute an ordinance; they are
currently in use in Fairfield, Milford, Hamden and Middletown; and New Haven has just adopted
their ordinance so installation of said equipment is in the works. For the overall safety of the
public, he anticipates that each town will eventually institute some level of ordinance for their
own municipality. Much research of crash data analysis and speed studies have been done on
this concerning topic which is a problem all throughout Connecticut, not just in East Lyme.

3c,     Proposed Cell Tower at 306 Flanders Road
DISCUSSION: Attorney Collins explained that the Board has several options to take this evening:
a) seek intervenor status for the Town in order to fully participate at the public hearing before the
Connecticut Siting Council on the application; b) enter a limited appearance on behalf of the
Town that includes submission of written comments at any time up to 30 days after the public
hearing before the Siting Council on the application; or c) attend the hearing before the Siting
Council to make oral comments during the public comment session of the public hearing. She
stated that the Siting Council has complete jurisdiction over this project, and local planning and
zoning regulations are not factored into their final decision making on the application. It was
suggested that a survey be put on the website to allow residents to weigh in on the application
before the scheduled Siting Council public hearing in January 2026.
MOrroN (7)
Ms. Cicchiello MOVED to authorize the First Selectman to direct Counsel to enter a limited
