3a. Discussion – Ordinance Establishing an Agricultural Commission
Mr. Formica read a letter from Dick Waterman into the record (identified as Attachment
A).

Mr. Hogan said that this matter had been discussed extensively for well over a year and
he supported the adoption of the ordinance establishing an advisory commission. He said
that the POCD endorsed the idea, the Planning Commission, and the Harbor Management
Commission.

Mr. Wilson said “when townspeople come together for a reason, we have to take that into
account and listen and that we would be better off having it than not.” He said he
supported the ordinance.

Mr. Birmingham said that he had heard both sides and stressed that everyone wants to
preserve farmland and open space. He said that there already are a lot of local resources
to protect the farming; Planning, Conservation and the Zoning Commissions. He said
there are also state and federal sites available. He suggested space on the Town’s website
to promote agriculture and having a booth at Celebrate East Lyme. He reviewed the
supplied list of farms in town. He questioned if all the farms on the list were viable since
even he was on the list. He noted he had two horses that were largely pets and several
others had “back yard horses”. He said that ten of the farms produce flowers and produce
and of that, 4-5 are of one family. He said that the 3-6 other farmers raising flowers and
produce had not come before this board to support the ordinance. He questioned if we
really need it.

Ms. Hardy said that this has been discussed for well over a year and it came about as a
result of a grass roots citizen request and noted that considerable interest had been
expressed. She said that we have a number of citizens who have come to the Board of
Selectmen requesting help to establish a commission for something positive in town. She
quoted Senator Graham “Be a player, not a spectator” and stressed we should listen to
this. She said that she supported this ordinance and said that passions should not carry a
negative bias.

Mr. Nickerson said that this community of farmers does not need a municipal group or
agency to be successful, they already are. He said the statutory requirement to publicize
the draft ordinance had already cost the town $1000 and if this ordinance is adopted, they

will have to comply with FOI requirements and any regulations or public hearings would
also have to be publicized. He asked what staff member would help this group write
grants. He suggested setting up an East Lyme Agricultural Association and appoint
leadership modeled after Save the River, Save the Hills or the Friends of Oswegatchie
Hills who have both educated and influenced the town commissions. He said this
commission would not be formulated to preserve farms and open space since if a farmer
wanted to sell and develop his land he can if he chooses. He said he respected those in
favor of this, but he would not be voting to approve. He said he favored a subcommittee
which would report to the Conservation of Natural Resources Commission. He added that
subcommittees do not have to be temporary.

Mr. Formica said that he appreciated all the effort expended but said that this is a solution
in search of a problem He said that East Lyme is a farm friendly town. He said he was
fundamentally opposed to growing town government in this environment. He said that no
farm problem has ever come before this Board and that the Conservation Commission
was split to make the Conservation Commission regulatory in nature and the
Conservation of Natural Resources to study other issues. He said that some have said the
Conservation of Natural Resources is too busy to accommodate this project. He said we
do nothing to discourage farming and everything to encourage it. He stressed that a
commission is not needed to pursue grants. He suggested that interested farmers could
provide education and a subcommittee of the Conservation of Natural Resources would
fulfill their needs and he did not believe an agricultural commission was needed. He said
that a subcommittee should be formed and he would ask the Selectmen to add $250.00 to
the budget of the Conservation of Natural Resources for an agricultural subcommittee.
He said if interest remains next year, he would personally support the formation of an
agricultural commission. He said he would be voting against the proposed ordinance.

MOTION (10)
Ms. Hardy moved that the Board of Selectmen adopt the ordinance establishing an
Agricultural Commission.

Seconded by Mr. Wilson.

Aye: Ms. Hardy, Mr. Wilson, Mr. Hogan.
Nay: Mr. Formica, Mr. Nickerson, Mr. Birmingham. Motion failed 3-3.

The consensus of the Board was for the First Selectman to write a letter to the
Commission of Natural Resources urging it to create a subcommittee to address
agricultural matters.

Mr. Schutt thanked the board for considering the ordinance and said he would not turn
down the challenge to help the Commission of Natural Resources to create a
subcommittee.

3b. Discussion – Ordinance Regulating the Use of Golf Carts

Mr. Hogan noted that he had created a draft ordinance to get things moving and that the
Town Attorney had made some suggestions and another draft. He said that he drove all of
the roads when he was drafting his and suggested a map with highlighted areas be used
instead of street names. He noted the section of the Attorney version calls for seatbelts
and passenger safety restraints and said that state statutes do not require seatbelt
restraints. He said that he had added restraints for rear facing passengers. He suggested
deleting section 2.1 regarding seatbelts. He said Section 2.3 of the Attorney version
should end after the word cart. He said section 3.3 talks about a dollar amount and he was
suggesting $25.00.

Mr. Wilson inquired if this would be just a beach issue. He said there are other areas in
town under the speed limits of what is suggested and inquired if they would be able to
use carts in their areas.

Mr. Hogan said that Sgt. Blanchette was against carts anywhere with speed limits above
25mph. He said that any other community could come to petition to be included.

Mr. Nickerson suggested keeping it simple – it was a beach issue and if others came
forward, the ordinance could be amended. He questioned the expiration date of the permit
and suggested that it go in line with beach passes.

Mr. Birmingham inquired if a beach area with its own security would have to enforce
this.

Mr. Formica said no, they would call the police department. He suggested adding a map
of highlighted areas and suggested sending a letter to the Council of Beaches with the
draft ordinance to get their comments.

The consensus of the Board was to alter the draft as follows:
       -section 2.3 period after the words golf cart.
       -section 3 – date should be consistent with beach passes – Memorial Day to
               Memorial Day.
       -Wording stating that at no time would these vehicles be allowed on any other
               roads than those specified.
       -section 3.3 a permit fee of $25.00.
The First Selectman will write a letter to the Counsel of Beaches and send a draft of the
amended ordinance for their input. Mr. Nickerson will check on insurance.
