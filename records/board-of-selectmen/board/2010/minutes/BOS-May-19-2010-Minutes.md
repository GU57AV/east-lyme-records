3a. Discussion - Golf Cart Ordinance
Attorney O’Connell noted the new draft regulation dated May 17th and reviewed the
changes with the Board.

Mr. Hogan reviewed the draft and noted that the enabling state statute had been written
for beach communities and suggested that this regulation begin this way. He said if other
areas were interested, they could petition the Selectmen.

Mr. Wilson said he thought the insurance coverage was a good idea. He noted that
Section 4.1 states a CT drivers license and said that many operators might be holding

another state’s license since they reside in another state most of the year. He suggested
the section just say “valid license”.

Attorney O’Connell said that the enabling act 14-300g, section 4 says “CT License”. He
added that our hands are tied since this is state law.

Mr. Birmingham said that registering the carts was a good idea so that we know how
many are out there and that insurance coverage is necessary since they would not be
covered under any other policy. He agreed to start this regulation with the beach
communities and see what happens.

Mr. Nickerson agreed noting that someone’s auto policy or homeowners would not cover
the golf cart. He said the registration would start a database and that the fee for
registering was not that important, the communication was necessary. He said insurance
was definitely needed.

Ms. Hardy inquired about windshields and rear view mirrors. She agreed with keeping
the insurance and registration requirement.

Mr. Formica said that the mirror could be mounted on the dashboard or side to get a rear
view.

Mr. Formica inquired about liability for the Town and the pros and cons of adopting a
regulation.

Attorney O’Connell responded that the State Legislature said that it was appropriate to let
a community opt to allow use on certain roads in their community.

Mr. Wilson said no public hearing was necessary since this topic had been reviewed at
several meetings and a public informational meeting had been held at the last meeting
where residents offered comment.

The consensus of the Board was that there had been enough review and an opportunity
for public input and they were ready to move on it.

Attorney O’Connell suggested building a record by including Sgt. Blanchette’s letter
regarding golf cart usage in Town and to get a letter from the Director of Public Works
with his views. He said he would have a clean copy of the regulation to accompany these
documents along with the minutes from the public informational hearing at the next
meeting and the Selectmen should be ready to vote on this item then

5a. Ex-Officio
Mr. Birmingham attended the Harbor Management Commission meeting and reviewed a
letter by Chair Don Landers to the Coast Guard (identified as Attachment A). He said
their main concern was boating safety while the new railroad bridge was being built.

Ms. Hardy said that Mr. Nickerson had attended the Zoning Commission meeting in her
stead. She said there is another meeting tomorrow evening and there is a proposed change
of zoning regulations to allow reduced minimum lot size for affordable housing in RU40
zones. She said that the Planning Commission had voted against this proposal because of
the inclusion of the RU 40 zone. She inquired about the affordable housing report that
was to have been delivered to the Board and requested a copy. She said she has concerns
with this proposal since the majority of the Town was RU 40.

Mr. Formica stated that Ms. Hardy was confusing two separate and distinct items. He
said that Attorney Harris is not available for the meeting tomorrow evening therefore, the
public hearing on his text amendment reducing the minimum lot size for RU 40 zones
will be continued. He said the second item has nothing to do with incentive housing
zones. He added he will be attending the Zoning meeting and will present draft
regulations for incentive housing zones drafted by Jim Butler of the Council of
Governments. He said the incentive housing zones that are being identified are
downtown Niantic and the Midway Mall area. He said that incentive housing allows the
Town to suggest a more appropriate place for workforce housing and that the State will
reimburse $2000 for every unit planned and an additional $2000 for every unit built. He
said the Zoning Commission will be handling this decision.

Ms. Hardy requested a copy of the report listing the appropriate areas for affordable
housing.

Mr. Nickerson said he attended the Board of Education meeting and they were starting a
comprehensive study on capital needs and facilities review. He said they were moving
forward with the sports complex architectural study.

Mr. Formica noted a bill was passed allowing the town to reduce the budgets of Board of
Education by $3000 per student but that the bill was only good for this year.

Mr. Formica attended the Leo leadership session and the Rocky Neck / Department of
Environmental Protection Annual Park Pursuit. He said that the Regional Water
committee had met and he would be hosting a Regional Water Stakeholders meeting on
Tuesday, May 25th at 2:00 p.m. at Nett Hall, Camp Rell.
