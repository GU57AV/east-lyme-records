                 EAST LYME BOARD OF SELECTMEN
                           MARCH 3, 2010
   PUBLIC HEARING RE: ORDINANCE ESTABLISHING AN AGRICULTURAL
                           COMMISSION
                            MINUTES

PRESENT: Paul Formica, Mark Nickerson, John Birmingham, Rob Wilson, Rose Ann
                  Hardy.

ALSO PRESENT: Town Attorney Ed O’Connell.

Mr. Formica called the meeting to order at 7:11 p.m. and led the Pledge.

Attorney O’Connell reviewed the proposed ordinance noting that this was the end result
of 2-3 meetings where the Board of Selectmen had considered format and content.

Joe Mingo of 397 Boston Post Road said that farming is the only vocation that can be
practiced anywhere in town, where other businesses cannot. He read Attachment A into
the record.

LuAnne Lange of 160 Pennsylvania Avenue read Attachment B into the record.

Fred Grimsey of Waterford and President of Save the River, Save the Hills said that his
Board believes strongly in the freedom to farm, but not the freedom to pollute. He
stressed that the Board of Selectmen should pay attention to the best management
practices regarding farming. He said he has been told that farms are exempt from inland
wetland regulations and did not know if this was true.

Wayne Budney from the New London County Farm Bureau said he was surprised that it
has taken over two years and there is still no decision. He said an agriculture commission
contributes to the town regulatory commissions.

Ron Rando of 194 Boston Post Road said this was an expansion of government and that
the State takes care of the farmers and we do not need this commission.

John Drabik of 18 Drabik Road said he has been involved with farming for 20-30 years
and if he thought this commission would be a good idea, he would be involved. He said
you already have the Department of Agriculture on a federal level, the CT Department of
Agriculture on a state level and they both defend the right to farm. He said that East
Lyme is user friendly and that you do not need a commission to solve a problem that does
not exist.

Marvin Schutt of 29 N. Edgewood said he was testifying in favor of an Agricultural
Commission even though he sensed that the members of the Board of Selectmen were not
enthusiastic about the idea. He read Attachment C into the record.

William Sawicky of 424 Drozdyk Drive, Groton said he has a vested interest in farmland
and he is a taxpayer of the town. He recommended that the Selectmen put off the idea of
a commission and form a committee to see how that works. He read Attachment D into
the record.

Nancy Kalal of 80 Grassy Hill Road said that farmers have come forward asking for an
ag commission and there is no conflict of interest because no one is on a commission.
She said that page 59 of the town’s POCD recommends the formation of an Agricultural
Commission. She stressed that this is a commission to preserve a way of life and is
strictly advisory not regulatory.

Maureen Bell of 18 Upper Pattagansett Road asked how an agricultural commission
would be a detriment to the Town. She said nothing has been said about it and she has
only heard benefits and therefore supports the ordinance.

Cheryl Lozanov of 9 W. Society Road told of her agricultural / horticulture degree and
said as long as best management practices are used she is in favor of supporting the
commission.

Bill Wernau of 13 Jean Drive said he is the volunteer coordinator for Heifer International
which promotes urban farming and said that locally grown food is really healthy and we
need to encourage the growing of it.

Alicia Geer of 86 Grassy Hill Road said there were farmers here, long before there was
industry. She said farmers are being pushed away as being less and less important.

Joan Nichols works in government relations for the CT Farm Bureau said that an
agricultural commission can only do good things for the Town of East Lyme and that
there is nothing to fear. She agreed that there were only 9-11 commissions in CT, but
more in Massachusetts. She asked the Selectmen to support this ordinance.

Mr. Birmingham said that most towns have their Conservation Commission perform the
work that an agricultural commission might do.

LuAnne Lange said that the Conservation Commission supported this ordinance because
they do not have the time to handle this subject. She inquired what they are so busy
doing?

Mr. Sawicky said that there appears to be some dialogue needed and said with a
commission, there will be no dialogue. He stressed the need for a committee instead.

Mark Christensen of 66 Grassy Hill Road said he was speaking in favor of this
commission and he wanted to correct the inaccuracies from the Board of Selectmen
meeting of February 3rd. He read Attachment E into the record.

MOTION (1)
Mr. Wilson moved to close the Public Hearing at 8:57 p.m.
