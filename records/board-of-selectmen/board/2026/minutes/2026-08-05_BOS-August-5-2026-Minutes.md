Addressing Privacy, Data Control, and Legal Implications of Surveillance Technology

The use of automated surveillance technologies, such as speed cameras, raises significant privacy
concerns. As investigative reports have revealed, the companies that provide these camera
systems have experienced security vulnerabilities, and their data-sharing practices have drawn
scrutiny. The ACLU and EFF have raised serious questions about the widespread collection of
data from these cameras, which can be used to track the movements of ordinary citizens and is
often shared with a vast network of law enforcement agencies across the country, including
federal agencies like ICE. This raises concerns about the potential for this data to be used for
purposes far beyond traffic enforcement. If you neglected to read fully the reports footnoted
above. then you should at a minimum review the video report entitled "This Flock Camera
Release is like Netflix for Stalkers." a

security vulnerabilities in Flock Safety's surveillance cameras. If you don't wish to spend the
time to watch his report, I've taken the opportunity here to summarize some of the key points

Pre-Implementation and Baseline Statistics:

      a   Data-Driven Site Selection: The selection of camera locations should be based on a

      a   Public Engagement and Education: The community must be informed and engaged in

          program's goals, and a well-defined warning period before any tickets are issued. Data

      a   Establishing Baseline Metrics: A comprehensive set of baseline statistics should be

By adopting a framework that prioritizes privacy, transparency, and data-driven decision-
making, we can ensure that any implementation of speed camera technology serves the best

obvious that automated traffic enforcement requires a significant cost in both personnel time and

                       School Reorganization Update (30 Min)


l. Why This Conversation, Why Now

East Lyme's elementary schools are out of balance. Enrollment has shifted significantly across
Flanders, Haynes, and Niantic Center
                                        - and projections show that gap widening. At the same
time, the 2025-26 budget cycle made clear that the district faces real fiscal pressure: declining
enrollment means fewer students, fewer sections, and harder budget math. These two
pressures together
                     - equity of access and long-term sustainability - led the Board to form a
school reorganization committee in early 2026.



ll. What the Committee Was Asked to Do


The committee's charge was to ensure that class sizes and teaching staff are appropriately
aligned across grade levels
                              - maximizing use of existing building space, streamlining fiscal
obligations, strengthening student transitions, and continuing to meet the academic and
social-emotional needs of students and families. This was not a charge to make a
recommendation
                  - it was a charge to gather data and bring options back to the full board

lll. What We Learned


The committee engaged MP Planning Group to analyze enrollment trends, building capacities,
staffing structures, and grade configurations. Key findings: current utilization is uneven across
buildings; demographic trends do not reverse on their own; and the district has more
elementary school capacity than its enrollment requires. The committee also explored grade
configuration - specifically, whether moving to a different K-8 structure could address
enrollment imbalance and ease fiscal pressure at the same time.
