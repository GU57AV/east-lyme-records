To the Members of the Office of State Traffic Administration


I am writing to submit formal technical feedback regarding the Town of East Lyme's proposed
Automated Traffic Enforcement Safety Device (ATESD) plan utilizing speed-enforcement
cameras. While I am aware that East Lyme's official plan has not yet been formally
submitted to OSTA for revieq the town has recently authorized a vendor contract, and
it is my understanding that the final application is likely to be submitted in the near
future. I am providing this data and notice proactively to ensure it is included in the town's
official file and reviewed the moment their application arrives.

As OSTA prepares to conduct its statutory review of East Lyme's upcoming submission, I urge
the Administration to deny iBploval of this plan. Based directly on the Connecticut
Department of Transpoftation's own data repository (a copy of which was presented at the
initial approval meeting and which is attached hereto), the town of East Lyme completely fails
to meet the state-mandated substantive engineering threshold required to justify automated
speed-ticketing cameras. Under Connecticut's established standard of review, an ATESD plan
must demonstrate a documented safety justification rooted in an active, severe speed-related
crash profile. The state's own data proves that no such speed profile exists on East Lyme's
local roads.

An analysis of the Connecticut Crash Data Repository (compiled locally with the assistance
of CTDOT representatives) highlights why East Lyme's plan fails to meet OSTA's engineering
benchmarks:

1. East Lyme Ranks at the Bottom of SECOG for Speed-Related Crashes
OSTA guidelines dictate that speed cameras must be reserved for areas with
disproportionately high speed-related risk. However, when evaluating the Southeastern
Connecticut Council of Governments (SECOG) region, East Lyme is remarkably safe regarding
speed.

   . Out of the 16 SECOG municipalities, East Lyme ranks in the bottom half for speeding-
       related crashes per million Vehicles Miles Traveled (VMT).
   .   East Lyme's speeding-related crash rate per million VMT sits at a low 0.200. For context,
       it ranks far below neighboring municipalities such as Salem (0.331), Voluntown (0.253),
       and Norwich (0.297).
   .   The data demonstrates that East Lyme is already highly successful at controlling traffic
       speeds through traditional means, rendering the intrusion of an automated vendor
         unprincipled and unnecessary.

2. Aggression vs. Speeding


OSTA's standard of review requires a rational connection between the device deployed and
the hozord identified. East Lyme has mistakenly authorized speed cameras to solve a problem
that the data proves is actually an aggressive driving problem.

   . According to the Crash Data Repository, there were 2,219 aggressive driving
         crashes in East Lyme between 201 5 and 2024, compared to just 428 speeding crashes.
   . On East Lyme's roads, crashes caused by aggressive drivers outnumber speed-related
      crashes by an overwhelming 5-to-1 ratio.
   . East Lyme climbs to the top three in all of SECOG for aggressive driving crashes per
      million VMT (1.480), sitting abnormally high for its population bracket.

Per the East Lyme Police Depaftment's own definitions, aggressive driving encompasses
distracted driving (cell phones, eating), tailgating, improper lane changes, and running
yellow/red lights. The speed-radar cameras under review do not capture or deter these
behaviors. Deploying automated speed cameras will do nothing to mitigate the actual 5-to-1
threat driving East Lyme's accident metrics.

3. Separation of Non-Jurisdictional Highway Data


A significant portion of East Lyme's raw crash volume is concentrated on lnterstate 95, over
which the local Board of Selectmen has zero statutory authority. Between 2015 and 2024,1-95
accounted for 1,675 total crashes and 1,030 aggressive driving incidents in the town's
geographic footprint. When this non-jurisdictional highway data is disaggregated from local
traffic maps, the justification for speed cameras on town-controlled roads collapses entirely.

Conclusion: Failure to Meet the Substantive Data Threshold OSTA's regulatory mandate is to
ensure that automated enforcement is deployed strictly as a data-backed safety intervention
rather than a predatory municipal revenue stream. lf a municipality lacks a statistically
significant speeding crisis, approving an ATESD contract represents a clear violation of state
policy goals.

The data proves that East Lyme does well at controlling speed without automated
intervention. Because the town's proposed initiative lacks an empirical safety justification for
speed cameras, we respectfully request that OSTA deny approval to East Lyme's plan when it
is submitted, and direct the municipality to focus its public resources on targeted law
enforcement actions that address driver aggression.

Thank you for your rigorous, data-driven stewardship of Connecticut's roadways.

Sincerely,


Gustav Schmidt
