Memo

To: East Lyme Board of Selectman

From: Ben North, Chief Operating Officer, East Lyme Water and Sewer

Date:2/17/2026

RE: Capital Request for Installation of Additional Inigation Pumping Systems at Gorto4lPond


Esteemed Board of Selectman members:

It has come to my attention that there is a currently a capital request by the East Lyme Parks Department to
obtain $50,000 for the purpose of installing additional pumping systems in Gorton Pond to assist with inigating
the athletic fields between the East Lyme Middle School and Lilly B Haynes Elementary School. I would

formally like to object to this capital request for the following reasons:


East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission currently operate two major wells in close proximity to not only the

Parks Department's proposed new pumping system, but also to the current Parks Department pumping system

that is already in use. Our municipal wells can produce about 1.5 million gallons of clean, safe drinking water
per day, providing nearly half of the entire demand of the East Lyme system, but are restricted during dry

periods of the summer months due to Connecticut DEEP requirements to maintain adequate stream flow levels

East Lyme's water deficits in the summer months should come as no surprise to the Board of Selectman, as it

has been a perennial issue for years, and was the main driver for the town spending roughly $10 million 13

years ago to create an interconnect with New London to make up for this summer-time shortfall.



These wells are extremely important to the viability of the East Lyme Water System. The water they produce

serves homes and businesses that create a thriving community and support critical infrastructure such as fire

protection. But when the stream levels run low in Gorton Pond, this critical infrastructure must be shut down to
comply with state regulations. In competition with this resource, the Parks department is operating an un-
                                                                       I




regulated pumping operation that takes water directly from the site at which our ability to withdrawal water is

affected. I have already advised the Parks Department that the current operation must operate under 50,000
gallons per day threshold to stay under the requirement for a DEEP diversion permit, but given water is already
withdrawn by two major wells in the area, the operation should be reviewed for compliance considering all
withdrawals and their cumulative impacts on streamflow. The current Parks system has no flow meter, or logs
of daily totals of operation. It is operated under a series of guesses and assumptions. To allow the current
system to continue in its current form is problematic, and to allow an increase in this activity is an active

subversion to the East Lyme Water System and the rate payers who rely on it.



Consider a scenario where Southeastern Connecticut is in a drought, just as it was last summer. The stream
gauge installed in Gorton Pond drops below safe levels. The municipal wells that homes and businesses rely on

are shut down to conserve stream flows in Gorton Pond. And yet at the same time, water is pumped, unmetered

and unregulated, from the very same tributary that the Water Department is trying to protect so that athletic

fields can stay lush and green. At the same time that water demand throughout the system is at its highest and
regulated production is taken offline, water is continued to be withdrawn for non-potable, non-necessary uses.

Not only does this practice deprive residents of the ability to use their water through mandatory water
restrictions, it also undermines the regulations that protect environmental sustainability of the Gorton Pond area.
And by the Parks department continuing to pump water from a source already under stress to irrigate fields, it
funher exacerbates and delays the recharging of the aquifer to allow municipal water production to resume.


The proper action moving forward demands that the Parks Department, and any other entities operating un-
permitted and unregulated pumping systems, cease the withdrawals from the Gorton Pond area and connect to
the East Lyme Water System where it can be properly metered and regulated. Instead of increasing production

and further circumventing regulations and its own water department, the Town should be a model citizen and

abide by state regulations and support protecting the integrity of the East Lyme water system and maintaining

environmental stewardship of our natural resources.
Thank You for your consideration of this very important matter.
