2a. Amendment to Automated Traffic Enforcement Safety Devices Ordinance
DISCUSSION: Town Attorney Tim Bleasdale was present and stated the following:
   . The citation process relates to the town ordinance section 150.036, which follows
          Connecticut General Statute 7-t52C. Our ordinance needs to be amended so that it
          includes the citation hearing officer along with the blight hearing officer,
      .   State Statute dictates the time period for appeal, so we cannot change it at the
          municipal level.
      .   The Citation Hearing Officer must be separate from the police department and will be
          appointed by the First Selectman.
      .   See the attached Exhibit #B for an example of what the Notice of Violation will look like;
          please take the time to read the information on how to pay the infraction and time limit
          to do so, as well as on the appeals process and timeline.
      .   The proposed changes to the ordinance are simple housekeeping matters to include the
          new ordinance regarding automated ticketing.
      .   Chief Glenn stated that he has been working on a privacy policy and will share that as
          soon as it is complete.
      .   Chief Glenn suggests that we put an RFP/RFQ out to hire a firm to handle all appeals, as
          being done by both Fairfield and Milford with success'
      .   There are a number of steps to go before we are ready to launch this program.

I'm here tonight to share my perspective on the school rebalancing initiative. I'd like to start by
recognizing there are and have been fiscal pressures that our town has faced and I support
responsible financial management. I also believe strongly that investing in our public schools
should be one of our community's highest priorities. The Board of Education's proposed
budget was not fully funded, and I find that concerning. We should be looking for
opportunities to strengthen our schools and improve educational outcomes, not continually
asking our district to do more with less.

I also recognize that funding constraints create pressure to find efficiencies and generate

savings wherever possible. I respect that instinct, but I worry that financial pressure may be
influencing how we're approaching rebalancing-and potentially leading us to solve the wrong
problem.

We started with a clearly defined challenge: an imbalance in student enrollment across
our three elementary schools. Yet three of the four scenarios go well beyond addressing that
inrbalance, introducing significant structural changes designed, in part, to generate financial
savings.

While I understand the appeal
                                - we risk turning a legitimate enrollment challenge into a
cost-saving opportunity simply because the district is under financial pressure.

My concern isn't that we're looking for savings. lt's that we're evaluating these scenarios without
answering two fundamental questions: First, what are we trying to accomplish with the
savings? We haven't seen a clear strategy for how those funds would be invested to improve
our schools and student outcomes. And second, what is the true cost of generating those
savings? Not just the financial costs, but the impact on our students, their educational
experience, and our broader community. Without answering both questions, how can we
determine whether these scenarios actually deliver greater value?

I believe we should approach this from the opposite direction. First, establish how much of the
projected savings would be needed to address existing budget shortfalls, and whether any
would be available to reinvest in improving our schools and student outcomes.

Then identify where additional investment could make the greatest difference, what resources
would be required, and how best to fund those priorities.
That should include a comprehensive review of district spending and potential efficiencies,
rather than focusing primarily on the savings associated with closing a school.

Glosing a school may be one of the most straightforward ways to generate significant
savings, but that doesn't necessarily make it the most financially responsible or
strategically sound.

I don't believe the current timeline allows sufficient opportunity to fully evaluate the financial,
operational, and educational implications of these proposals. That's why I support redrawing
attendance boundaries across our three existing elementary schools as the most
responsible near-term solution. lt addresses the original enrollment challenge, preserves
flexibility, and allows time to develop a comprehensive financial and educational strategy.

I'm not asking this Board to make educational decisions. I simply want our town's leadership to
understand that my concerns extend well beyond the future of any one school.

It's about ensuring that financial pressure doesn't drive permanent structural changes
before we've defined what we're trying to achieve for our students.
