The First Selectmen met with several residents on September 1,2026 to discuss issues related
to surveillance technology within the Town. Among the several matters discussed, two specific
matters were discussed in-depth:

    L. Based on the Flock Safety Contract entered into in 2023 and discovered via a FOIA
       request to the Town, it appeared that the expenditure may have violated the Town
       Charter based on the fact that it exceeded the Special Appropriation limit of 510,000

    2. lt appeared that the cameras installed pursuant to the aforementioned contract were
       installed within the state's right-of-way along two highways in the Town, apparently
       without the required approval from the CTDOT under C.G.S. 5 L3a-247.

lf either or both are true, the question arose as to who would have the authority to order the
cameras removed under the Town Charter and municipal code. The following is a brief attempt
as an answer to that specific question based on a review of the relevant information.


Executive Summary

Under the East Lyme Town Charter and Code of Ordinances, municipal power is intentionally
decentralized. The Board of Selectmen ("BOS") cannot simply issue a direct administrative
directive to the Police Department to physically dismantle equipment. Howevel if a budget
violation or policy conflict occurs, specific administrative, legislative, fiscal, and legal pathways
exist across different town entities to force the decommissioning, deactivation, or physical
removal of the surveillance system.

Option 1: Direct Administrative Action by the Board of Police Commissioners

   a   Analysis: Under East Lyme Code 5 32.L7, the Board of Police Commissioners is explicitly
       granted the "general management and supervision of the Police Department of the town
       and of the property and equipment used in connection therewith. " Because the cameras
       were purchased out of a police lT budget, they are legally classified as police property
       and operational equipment.

   a   Mechanism: lf the Police Commission determines that the cameras are a liability, create
       privacy concerns, or were procured under fractured budgetary processes, they can vote
       to adjust department policy. They have the direct authority to order the Chief of Police
       to physically take down the cameras or permanently cut their network feeds, This is the
       cleanest, most immediate method of removal, requiring no secondary legislative steps.

Option 2: Legislative Intervention by the Board of Selectmen

   a   Analysis: The Board of Selectmen functions as the town's legislative body. While they
       cannot meddle in day-to-day police operations or command police staff directly, their
       legislative authority is superior to the internal regulations of any individual board or
       commission. Section 3.4 of the Town Charter gives the BOS the power to pass
       ordinances for the peace, health, and safety of the town.

   a   Mechanism: The BOS can draft and pass a generaltown ordinance that regulates or
       outright bans public surveillance systems or automated monitoring equipment within
       public right-of-ways. Because police department policies cannot be "contrary to law,"
       any ordinance passed by the BOS completely supersedes the Police Commission's
       authority. The Police Commission would be legally forced to comply and order the
       removal of the cameras.

Option 3: Fiscal Controls by the Board of Finance and Board of Selectmen

    a   Analysis: Modern surveillance systems (such as automated license plate readers or
        cloud-connected security cameras) are not static, one-time expenses. They rely on
        recurring operational expenditures, including software licensing fees, data storage
        subscriptions, cellular connectivity, and municipal electricity. Charter Section 6.3
        establishes strict rules regarding appropriations and transfers.

    a   Mechanism: lf it is discovered that the original capital expenditure violated the Charter
        (by exceeding thresholds without being a distinct line item or going to a town meeting),
        the Board of Finance and the BOS can immediately refuse to authorize any subsequent
        operational transfers. By freezing or defunding the lT operational lines tethered to the
        cameras, the town forces a functional deactivation. The cameras may physically remain
        on poles, but they become legally and operationally dark.

Option 4: Formal Remedy Directed via Town Counsel

    a   Analysis: lf the Selectmen or Finance Board discover a definitive Charter violation
        regarding the procurement threshold, they can formally request a binding legal opinion
        from the Town Attorney.

        Mechanism: lf the Town Attorney issues a formal finding that public funds were spent in
        direct contravention of Charter Section 5.3, the expenditure is rendered unlawful. Town
        Counsel would advise the Police Commission that maintaining and operating equipment
        acquired through an illegal diversion of town funds exposes both the department and
        the town to severe civil liability and audit penalties. Faced with an officialfinding of
        unlawfulness, the Police Commission would likely vote to decommission the hardware to
        self-correct and avoid litigation.

Option 5: Taxpayer-Driven J udicia I I njunction

   a    Analysis: lf the municipal boards reach a political stalemate-for instance, if the Board
        of Selectmen and Board of Finance refuse to act against a Charter violation committed
        by the Police Department-the remedy shifts to the public sphere. Connecticut law
        grants municipal taxpayers legal standing to sue town officials if public funds are being
        used or maintained illegally.

   a    Mechansim: East Lyme residents can file a lawsuit in Connecticut Superior Court seeking
        a temporary and permanent injunction. By proving that the cameras were acquired via
        an illegal budgetary process that bypassed mandatory Charter transparency thresholds,
        a judge can order the town to cease spending public money to operate the system and
        mandate the physical removal of the illegally obtained property.
