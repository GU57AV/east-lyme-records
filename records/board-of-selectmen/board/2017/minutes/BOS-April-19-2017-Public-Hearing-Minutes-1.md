PRESENT: Mark Nickerson, Kevin Scery, Marc Salerno, Rose Ann Hardy, Paul Dagle and Dan

ALSO PRESENT: Attorney Tracy Collins, Town Attorney; and Dave Putnam, Director of Parks
& Recreation.

Mr. Nickerson called the Public Hearing to consider a proposed amendment to the Ordinance
entitled “Ordinance Concerning Areas Under Jurisdiction of Parks & Recreation Commission” to
order at 7:15 p.m. and stated that the Pledge of Allegiance was done at the start of the Town
Meeting at 7:00 p.m.

Mr. Nickerson stated that he caused to have published the Notice of Public Hearing in The Day
on April 7, 2017, and then he invited Parks & Recreation Director, Dave Putnam, to provide
background information on this matter.

Mr. Putnam explained that over the winter the boardwalk remained very busy and utilized; and
there were quite a number of complaints concerning dogs on the boardwalk. He explained that
although the no dogs rule has been in place for quite a while, it has been difficult to enforce
without a fine in place. The amendment to the said ordinance will now allow our Police Officers
to fine violators of this rule. Also added to this ordinance is the rule that no dogs shall be
allowed on town owned beaches and McCook Park between Memorial Day and Labor. Both of
these amendments do not apply to service dogs. Mr. Nickerson made clear that these rules are
made by the Park & Recreation Commission, and this ordinance gives the EL Police Department
the authority to enforce these rules. In addition, the Department of Public Health’s regulations
require municipalities and other entities (except the state) operating or maintaining a public
swimming area to prohibit domestic animals from being in the water or on the immediate
shoreline when the swimming area is open for use (Conn. Agencies Regs. 19a-36-B61, effective
March 17, 2014). Mr. Putnam stated that at Peretz Park and Darrow Pond, there are beautiful
walking trails and dogs are allowed all year round; and at Peretz Park, there are doggie bag
stations throughout the trails.

Ms. Donna Palumbo, 133 Black Point Road, Chairwoman of the newly formed Dog Park
Committee, reported that her and the Co-Chairman, Paul Kramm, are in favor of this ordinance
and would like the opportunity to speak with anyone who opposes the ordinance so that she can
introduce them what this committee has been working on. She reported that there are
approximately 20-30 people active in this group already. Anyone interested in contacting Ms.
Palumbo may call the First Selectman’s office for contact information. Mr. Nickerson reiterated
that this is a grass roots effort and not a Town committee; and he stated that this committee is
comprised of very passionate dog owners who look forward to a pleasant, safe place to bring
their dogs. Primary funding for this project will be through fundraising efforts.

Ms. Dottie Verkade, 3 Amberly Lane, stated that she is in favor of this amended ordinance, and
suggested clearer signage around town outlining the rules of this ordinance; the way it is posted
now is very confusing. Mr. Putnam stated that new signage was discussed at the last Park &
Recreation Commission meeting, and that will be happening soon.

Ms. Kimberly Rider, 73 Riverview Road, stated that she is not in favor of imposing fines on
citizens; she feels it is an undue burden on people that have limited income to begin with. She
feels as though a fine is unnecessary as dog owners understand the liability they carry when they
take their dogs out into the public. She stated that she feels our rules are far more restrictive than
surrounding beaches, and she does not like the anti-dog sentiment that this ordinance portrays to
her.

Mr. Alan Alfano, 3 Amberly Lane, stated that he is in favor of the amended ordinance and in
favor of the fine. He stated that he is more concerned with the health and safety issues
associated with dogs defecating on the boardwalk than being bitten.

Mr. Charlie Fenick, 78 Riverview Road, stated that he is a member of the Park & Recreation
Commission and that he is in favor of the amended ordinance and the fines associated. He stated
that no one likes to pay fines, but it is necessary to enforce the rules. There are a lot of public
trails in Town that allow dogs and are a nice alternative to the boardwalk.

Ms. Judy Lanza, 23 Spinnaker Drive, stated that she loves dogs, but not on the boardwalk. She
walks quite a bit and there are a lot of people walking on the boardwalk with canes, walkers,
baby carriages, etc., and that these people should have a safe place to walk and get fresh air. She
stated that it’s really the only place in town that restricts animals, and it is only a one mile
stretch. Ms. Lanza stated that there are so many more miles of places that you can walk your
dog in East Lyme.

Attorney Tracy Collins explained that legally, according to the Americans with Disability Act
(ADA), you can only ask two questions; is the animal required because of a disability, and what
work or task the animal has been trained to perform. Connecticut has an additional licensing
process for the blind, deaf or mobility challenged persons, which is Sec. 22-345 and is attached
hereto as Exhibit 1.

Mr. Seery stated that also important to consider is that if a child, or anyone for that matter, gets
bit by a dog on the boardwalk, this will expose the Town to possible lawsuits. Attorney Collins
answer affirmative, that the Town is at risk if someone were to get bit by a dog on the
boardwalk.

Ms. Hardy inquired as to what if a person has an animal besides a dog that they consider a
service pet. Attorney Collins responded that beginning on March 15, 2011, only dogs are
recognized as service animals under titles II and III of the ADA. Mr. Putnam stated that the
Department of Public Health’s regulations require municipalities and other entities (except the
state) operating or maintaining a public swimming area to prohibit domestic animals from being
in the water or on the immediate shoreline when the swimming area is open for use (Conn.
Agencies Regs. 19a-36-B61, effective March 17, 2014).
Ms. Sheri DiGiovanna, One Old Oak Lane, stated that she agrees with the fines and also agrees
that the signage needs to be clearer; however she feels that dogs should not be allowed on the
beach or boardwalk any time of year.

Mr. Alan Alfano, 3 Amberly Lane, reiterated his earlier statement that the boardwalk is a
pedestrian zone, and he agrees that new signage is needed.

MOTION (1)

Mr. Seery moved to adjourn the April 19" Public Hearing at 7:49 p.m.
Seconded by Mr. Salerno. Motion Passed 6-0
