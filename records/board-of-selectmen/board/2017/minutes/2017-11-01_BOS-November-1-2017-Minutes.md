EAST LYME BOARD OF SELECTMEN
REGULAR MEETING OF NOVEMBER 1, 2017
MINUTES

PRESENT: Mark Nickerson, Marc Salerno, Rose Ann Hardy, Dan Cunningham and Paul Dagle
EXCUSED: Kevin Seery

Also present was State Representative Holly Cheeseman

Mr. Nickerson called the meeting to order at 7:31 p.m. and led the Pledge of Allegiance.

Ib. Additional Agenda & Consent Calendar Items

Ie, Delegations

Ms. Holly Cheeseman, State Representative, 16 Mitchell Drive, Niantic, gave an update on the
budget situation. She reported that this is a bipartisan budget, and that there is equal support from
both Republicans and Democrats. She stated that this is not a cure all, and remember that this did
not happen overnight and it will take a while to get Connecticut in a better place and moving
forward. Ms. Cheeseman briefly touched on a number of items that did and did not pass in this new
budget, and stated that anyone who would like more information or further details to please contact
her office to set up a time to talk. Her door is always open.

Mr. Ron Rando, 194 Boston Post Road, East Lyme, stated that he has spoken out on many matters
regarding the Town over the years, and that he has spoken the truth. He expressed his support for
Wes Firmin, his grandson and Independent candidate for First Selectman; and stated that he has not
assisted his grandson with his campaign race and that Mr. Firmin has taken on this endeavor on his
own based on his desire to see a change. Mr. Rando expressed his concern for the recently passed
budget up on Hartford, the Town’s Moody Rating review, the way the Town handled the new water
meters, the hold up with the Costco project, and tax hike on tobacco products, which increases the
tax burden on lower income residents. He expressed concern with the way the jet ski event was
handled at McCooks Point, and does not feel the band shell is a legal addition to the park.
Regarding the property up on Darrow’s Ridge, Mr. Rando noted that the town spent $4.3 million on
the land in order to put up a water tower, and he is not pleased that that project never happened and
he does not think spending that kind of money to in turn use two acres out of 300 to build a dog
park is not reasonable.

Mr. Joe Mingo, 397 Boston Post Road, East Lyme, read into record an email exchange from
November 2016. Mr. Mingo expressed concern with The Day blocking his access to the opinion
section of the online site, his previous involvement with the Inland Wetlands Commission, and his
former position as the tree warden and said duties of such position.

6a. Ex-Officio

Mr. Salerno reported that at the last Zoning Commission meeting, they voted to opt out of the
proposed temporary health care structures program. This decision will now be forwarded to the
Board of Selectman and an upcoming meeting for their review and decision as to whether or not the
Zoning Commission’s decision to opt out will be upheld. He attended the Town Building
Committee meeting and they discussed the schematic design estimates, asbestos abatement, and
that they have run into issues with the alarm systems that need to be addressed. He noted that the
project is still under budget, and that a report is being prepared and will be submitted shortly to
outline progress.

Mr. Cunningham reported that he attended the WELSCO meeting on October 19" at which they
reviewed their appointed duties and powers as a commission. They also discussed the proposed
shellfish farm and he noted that there are residents weighing in from both Waterford and East Lyme
on this issue. He suggests that anyone interested should please review the previous meetings
minutes as well as attend future meetings on this matter as there is a lot of discussion and a lot of
information. The Historic Properties Commission held their annual Bike & BBQ event and they
brought in approximately $10,000; what a great event! Holding the event at the Smith Harris
House turned out to be a great venue and will be considered for the location next year.
Mr. Dagle reported that the Inland Wetlands Commission reviewed a cease and desist case
regarding dock encroachment on the 4 Mile River. Afier hearing from the property owner, the
commission decided to give the owner more time to work on this issue and rectify it; this will be
heard again at a future meeting. He reported that at the EL Harbor Management & Shellfish
Commission meeting they heard a presentation from Mr. TJ Londregan. Although the general
consensus is that we are in support of this project, because it is in Waterford waters and there is
discussion with the State over jurisdiction, it was decided that this commission would not send a
letter to the State at this time in support of the project.

6b. First Selectman’s Report

Mr. Nickerson reported on the recent storm that we had, and made note of the progress made over
the last couple of days in getting our residents power back on and debris/trees cleaned up. He
stated that Eversource was caught off guard by this storm and was slow to respond, put out a lot of
misinformation to customers, and provided less than adequate customer service to its customers.
He stated that he would be filing a report, as the municipalities do after each such event, and
express our concerns and experience during this event. Election Day is Tuesday, November 7
from 6am until 8pm. Residents north of route 95 are District | and will vote at the High School;
residents south of 95 to the beaches are Districts 2 and 3 and will vote at the Community Center.
Should you need to register to vote, you can do so up to the day of election at the Registrar’s Office
located in the Community Center.

7. Public Comment

Mr. Ron Rando, 194 Boston Post Road, East Lyme spoke regarding the proposed shellfish
farm in the bay, and stated that he supports Mr. Londergan’s efforts and feels that we should be in
support of bringing scallops back into the bay. Mr. Rando noted beach neighbors that were
supposed to get sewers as well as the removal of eel grass in the bay as possible reasons that the
scallop population dwindled from years ago when it was abundant. Mr. Rando stated that he
supports Mr. Trump and believes that he is making good decisions and doing a good job.

Mr. John Drabik, 18 Drabik Road, East Lyme stated that the next budget season is fast
approaching, and he is suggesting that future budgets be either flat or reduced; stating that he
believes that the State and the Town are double taxing residents in the water bills, and stated that
people are leaving our town and the state so there are less taxpayers to shoulder the burden. Mr.
Drabik also expressed his concern with the State work around town that is disrupting traffic and
putting a burden on business owners in the North end of town.

8. Selectman’s Response

Mr. Nickerson stated that the town of East Lyme has every intention of supporting Mr.
Londregan’s endeavors in building up a shellfish farm in the bay. There is a conflict in duties
between the state and the towns, and WELSCO is working with the State on rectifying this. He
reported that up on Darrow’s ridge there are 2 acres that have been set aside for the community
endeavor to build a dog park; 2 acres out of 300. 100 acres are set aside as open space, and the
remaining 200 acres are for town use. Currently we are installing the new communications tower
that will service our police and emergency responders; there are proposed plans in the works for a
possible water tower. Mr. Nickerson reported that regarding the Moody Rating review is
happening to 35 towns in Connecticut; any town whose fund balance is lower than the State fund
balance has been put into the review status. Moody would like to see $12 million and we only have
$5 million set aside in the rainy day fund. Regarding the double billing, we inquired about this
matter and the CT Department of Health determined that customers are not being billed twice. He
reminded everyone that the decision to purchase the new meters under this plan was agreed to by a
10 person panel, with a vote in favor of 9-1. Regarding the jet ski event, he agreed that things did
not play out as planned and the Park and Recreation department is addressing this and will have
clearer rules and regulations in place should this event come back to our bay next year. The band
shell is a recreational facility and does not make any money from any of the events.
