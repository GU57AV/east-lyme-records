3a. Review and approve updated Workplace Harassment and Workplace Violence policies, and
designate an EEO Grievance Officer

DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that the Human Resources department is continually
reviewing policies and making updates as needed, and it was lime that these policies were reviewed

include all employees as well as members of Town of East Lyme boards and commissions.

3b. Review and approve the Zoning Commission’s recommendation to opt out of temporary

health care structures

DISCUSSION: Attorney O’Connell addressed the Board of Selectmen and gave a brief review of
Public Act 17-155 and the applicable statutes relating to his recommendation to opt out of this

public act, specifically Section 1, subsections a3 and j. Based on his review of this public act, and

the Zoning Commission’s decision at a Public Hearing held on October 5, 2017, these are the

reasons he recommends opting out of Public Act 17-155. The Board of Selectmen agreed that they
understood the reason for such a Public Act; however, the act does not allow for the Town to easily

keep track and police these structures. It was discussed that the Town would look into a similar

way to institute a Town policy that would put the Town Zoning department in control.

MOTION (4)

Mr. Seery moved that WHEREAS, the East Lyme Board of Selectmen (“Board”) has received and
reviewed a resolution from the East Lyme Zoning Commission (“Commission”) titled “Resolution to
Opt Out of the Requirements of Public Act 17-155;” and,

WHEREAS, the Commission’s resolution recommends to the Board that the Town “opt out” of the
requirements of Public Act 17-155 in accordance with the Act’s opt out provisions; and

WHEREAS, the Commission’s resolution sets forth the reasons for its recommendation; and
WHEREAS, the Board, having reviewed the Commission’s reasons and recommendations, agrees with
the Commission’s conclusion that opting out of the requirements of Public Act 17-155 is in the best
interests of the Town at the present time;

IT [S RESOLVED, that the East Lyme Board of Selectmen hereby affirmatively opts out of the
provisions of Public Act 17-155.

Seconded by Mr. Salerno. Motion passed 4-0.

6a. Ex-Officio

Mr. Salerno reported that he attended the Library Board of Trustees meeting and that the budget
line item for books has been cut, and this past year donations have been lower than normal. He
reported that the Library is again participating with other area libraries in the Lion Delivery club
which shares books; and stated that initial reporting shows that we lend books to other libraries
more than our patrons borrow books from the other libraries.

Mr. Seery reported that he attended the Park & Recreation Commission’s meeting, and it
was reported that the summer ended positively all around: good revenue and the parks are in good
shape. They began discussing possible changes in the rates for next season, and expect to set the
rates for next year at the December meeting.

6b. First Selectman’s Report

Mr. Nickerson stated that December 9"" is going to be a very busy day in our Town, The
annual Jingle Bell 5K hosted by the Brian T. Dagle Foundation will be happening in the morning;
Smith Harris House will have an open house from | lam-4pm with Bellsnickel arriving at 2pm; and
the annual Light Parade will be held in the evening. Costco held a press conference and announced
that they are pulling out of the deal to come to East Lyme, but the project itself is not dead and it is
still a viable location for number of different purposes; if not a big box store then possible office
space. It does not necessarily have to be 100 percent retail; the developer continues to pursue other
projects. Mr. Nickerson also noted that Costco also pulled out of two other locations, not just the
East Lyme project. The Miracle League unveiled its proposal to bring a field to East Lyme, and is
considering the small field behind Flanders School. There is one other Miracle Field in
Connecticut, located in West Hartford, so this is an honor to have East Lyme chosen for the second
location in our state. This project is a big deal and will bring people and business from all over the
region. The field itself will provide a safe and productive environment for children with disability
to play a wide array of sports, including baseball and soccer. It was noted that the plan will be for
our varsity sports teams to participate in teaching and helping the children at the sports events at
this field. Please watch for the video for more information and to learn about this amazing project.

On Veterans Day there were numerous events around town that Mr. Nickerson attended,
including events at the Town Green, the Middle School, the High School, and the Rotary Club. On
Monday, the Senior Center hosted a breakfast for Veterans and it was very well attended and a very
nice honor to our community’s veterans and their families. He attended a small business forum,
and it was reported that local businesses are doing well and working together as a community to
continue to make this town great and getting better every day. On Saturday night the Niantic Main
Street group held their annual Beers for Beauty fundraiser, which is how they fund the beautiful
flowers and plants along Main Street. Mr. Nickerson noted that the planting of flowers and the
upkeep from spring until fall is completely done by volunteers with funds raised at this annual
event. He also reported that Sue Kumro of Mermaid Liquors was honored for all of her time and
hard work spent volunteering and organizing fundraisers to make our Main Street and town as a
whole beautiful. The Board of Education has begun working on the Long Range Plan and is
working to get everyone on the same page. At an event at Secor Subaru, they donated a vehicle to
our TVCCA program to use to deliver meals to those homebound. Prior to tonight's Board of
Selectmen meeting, Mr. Nickerson presented information to a group of Girl Scouts to assist them in
finding and participating in community service. Mr. Nickerson reported that the band shell had
been vandalized late Saturday night into Sunday morning, but that between the members of the East
Lyme Police Department, the juvenile vandals were caught within a couple of hours of the start of
the investigation. Also, the Parks & Recreation Department, together with volunteers, cleaned up
the graffiti and all was as it should be by Monday morning. The cost of cleaning and repairing the
damage done was approximately $6,100.00, and it is Mr. Nickerson’s hope that the culprits
responsible be made to pay that money back to the town,
