Mr. Formica introduced Mike Silvestrini, President of Greenskies.

Mr. Silvestini reviewed the proposed solar project at the north end of town. He said that
his company’s primary focus is on large commercial businesses where they place solar
panels on the roofs of these buildings, but that at the end of 2011, a request was put out
for projects generating power. He said they had been chosen since they were more cost
effective than their competitors and the location for the program would be on a parcel
owned by partner Bob Landino on the corner of Walnut Hill Road and Grassy Hill Road.
He added that the solar farm’s panels would not be seen from either road and in lieu of a
residential development on this land, they were proposing to place 17,000 modules which
would generate approximately 14% of East Lyme’s homes.

Mr. Nickerson inquired about the initial proposal including a relationship with the
schools.

Mr. Silvestrini responded that the legislation as drafted was not applicable to virtual net
metering and value therefore; there would be no relationship with the schools at present.
He added that they had to go before the CT Siting Council for their permitting process.
He said the project would include a 25 acre fenced in parcel with access only by those
who worked there. He said the panels would be 8-12 feet off the ground and the only
building would be a storage barn. He noted that there would be a number of public
hearings and meetings and this was just the beginning of the process. He urged the Board
to view their website www.greenskies.com. for more information.

Ms. Hardy said that she had viewed their website and it was very well done. She inquired
if this was the first project on the ground that they would construct.

Mr. Silvestrini responded in the negative noting that the closest one would be in
Springfield Massachusetts. He said that in order to get the site ready for this project, the
area would need to be leveled out, but it would be very quick construction lasting
approximately 3-4 months and that no noise is generated by this solar farm.

Ms. Hardy inquired about water run off from the panels and the life span of the
equipment.

He replied that the equipment is warranteed for 25 years with a 20 year contract with
CL&P. He added that the equipment is highly recyclable.

2c. SEAT Resolution

Mr. Formica noted that the fuel spill and problems of SEAT had been the subject of
numerous articles in the newspaper. He said that the member municipalities of SEAT
were potentially trying to improve the regional network over a period of time under
different management. He added that the next step was for the Board members of SEAT
to vote to accept this resolution after the nine member towns had passed it. He added that
we were the last town to approve the resolution. He added that this is the only transit
district where member towns do the funding. He noted that they want to make sure that
this grows to be a regional transit district while removing us from liability and with the
CT DOT funding 100% cost of operation.

MOTION (11)
Mr. Nickerson moved the following:
CERTIFICATE OF AUTHORITY

WHEREAS, the Southeastern Area Transit (SEAT) district was formed by the action of
the original eight member municipalities in 1975; and

WHEREAS, in April of 2011, the nine current SEAT member municipal chief elected
officials first learned of the fuel release that had been discovered by SEAT in August
2010 at the SEAT facility in Preston; and

WHEREAS, nine current SEAT member municipal chief elected officials request the
assistance of the State of Connecticut, Department of Transportation in responding to this
fuel release, and specifically, in payment for the cost of cleanup, including past, present
and future expenses, in return for SEAT withdrawing its related claim against the State of
Connecticut in the development of the new operations and management model for the
provision of transit in Southeastern Connecticut; and

WHEREAS, the nine current SEAT member municipal chief elected officials wish to
see a better future for, and provision and growth of quality transit service in their
municipality and throughout southeastern Connecticut; and

WHEREAS, the nine current SEAT member municipal chief elected officials, along
with the SEAT Board, will undertake a full evaluation of the management and operations
of SEAT and the facility in Preston, including but not limited to, standard operating
procedures, fuel delivery, storage, and dispensing procedures, inventory controls, testing
protocols, employee training and insurance issues;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE BOARD OF SELECTMEN OF
THE TOWN OF EAST LYME that the nine municipal chief elected officials and their
signature below respectfully requests that the Connecticut Department of Transportation
take on the responsibility for the cleanup of the fuel release and it's full associated costs;

East Lyme Board of Selectmen Meeting of December 5, 2012 minutes

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the chief elected officials from SEAT’S nine
member municipalities hereby request and express their support for the Connecticut
Department of Transportation to assume responsibility for the operation and management
of transit service in the Southeastern Connecticut region, and the SEAT Board of
Directors be replaced by an advisory panel comprised of the Chief Elected Officials from
the municipalities or their designee, acting by and through Southeastern Connecticut
Council of Governments, whose staff will provide administrative and technical support as
needed to the Connecticut Department of Transportation.

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that the First Selectman is authorized to affix his
signature to a resolution of the chief elected officials of the nine member municipalities
that is substantially similar to this resolution.
