Mr, Formica called the Public Informational Meeting to order at 6:15 p.m. The
Pledge of Allegiance was observed.

1. Potential Purchase of Samuel Smith Property, 82 Plants Dam Road, Assessor’s
Map 19, Lot 36. Mr. Formica stated this property has been discussed for 1% years, The
two parcels being considered for purchase are three acres with a home and 14 4 acres
surrounding it.

The Board of Selectmen authorized Mr. Formica to develop an option to purchase
the property from the Hubers’ for $425,000, He had discussions with the Sportsman
Club and asked if they would be interested in purchasing part of the property. It
benefited them to have ownership of land adjacent to their club and it would be a buffer
for them. The Town would have an opportunity to reduce the price and it still could be a
Museum. A committed group wanted the Town to purchase it. The Board of Directors of
the Sportsman’s Club approved the purchase of the 9.5 acres at a cost of $125,000,
pending approval by the Town boards and residents. The deed would note no buildings
would be built on the property. It would be deeded as open space. The Sportsman’s
Club also agreed to allow the Friends of the Samuel Smith House to have activities on the
property. The Sportsman’s Club has no desire to build on the property. The Sportsman’s
Club membership approved the purchase of the 9.5 acres at a cost of $125,000.

On Monday evening Mr. Formica met with residents who are interested in
purchasing this propeity. Two of these individuals expressed concerns about the
acquisition and offered an alternative. They suggested adding 3.4 acres that the Town
would purchase and reducing the amount of property that the Sportsman’s Club would be
asked to purchase. They felt this would allow the full enjoyment of the property.

He met with the President of the Sportsman’s Club and presented this option. The
Sportsman’s Club would then purchase the reduced land for $85,000. The Town would
then pay $340,000 and if a grant was received in the amount of $170,000 the net out of
pocket for the Town would be $170,000. This would have to be discussed at a Regular
Meeting of the Board of Selectmen for their consideration and forwarded to the Board of
Finance. It would then be brought to a Town Meeting.

Mr. Formica feels this can be paid for from the CNR Fund, which has $1 millions
in it. $700,000 is earmarked for special items. If it is approved on October 3 the grant
~ writer would begin working on a grant which is due on October 28.

Mike Schultz, of Lovers Lane, East Lyme, supports this going forward under the
conditions Mr. Formica stated. He asked what is the loss of tax revenue from this
property. Mr. Formica replied at the present time $4,000. The Sportsman’s Club portion
would be taxable.

Barbara Johnston, of 35 Seacrest Avenue, supports this purchase. She felt the
taxes are very low. This property is defined as pasture land.

Matthew Fleisher, President of the Sportsman’s Club, who lives at 38 White Birch
Circle, stated we are looking for open space. He felt open space is not readily available,
and we need to take advantage of it.

Frank Wilson, of 211 North Bridebrook Road, feels this is wonderful use of this
property, The group working to bring this forward should be commended. He thanked
the Friends of the Samuel Smith House for their hard work and the Sportsman’s Club for
its agreement with them.

Marvin Schutt, 29 Edgewood Road North, thanked the Sportsman’s Club for their
kind help with this purchase.
Karen Rak, 27 Black Point Road asked as far as maintenance of this property

Dick Waterman of Niantic, stated the Samuel Smith House is a few of the
remaining houses that represent the early history of the Town. Considering that we are at
the beginning of the development of the U.S. We are aware that the Lee House is the
oldest wooden structure in Connecticut. It was a chicken coop before it was preserved.
A group of ladies were able to save it. A small group of citizens believe the Samuel
Smith House is also worth saving. Since the Colonial period our Town has grown and is
continuing to grow and as residents we should support the heritage of our Town. I urge
citizens to support the purchase of the Samuel Smith House. History is important.

Ms. Lange, Chairlady of the Friends of the Samuel Smith House, informed those
present we have worked through the State Department on the grant. She will discuss the
process with the First Selectman. She is not concerned about receiving the grant. There
is every indication that it’s a worthwhile project. There are not many houses of this age.

The Historical Society, after checking with its attorney, approved setting up a
separate checking account for the Samuel Smith House. Donations have been received.

She feels monies are available. The Samuel Smith House was built in 1685. We
have Bylaws in place.

Karen Rak, of 27 Black Point Road, asked how much is in that checking account?

She read an internal memo stating some of the repairs that are needed. What if
the Friends of the Samuel Smith House do not have the money to take care of these

repairs.

Bob Ramsay of 3 Stoneywood Drive reported he is a carpenter and has gone
through the report from the structural engineer. Some of these items happened over 300
years ago. Two issues he sees are bulging of the foundation. It appears it has been stable
for thirty years and evidence it probably has not moved in 60-70 years. As far as what it
would take for the foundation, we propose to get equipment to dig out the outside of that
foundation to examine what is out there and stabilize it. He felt it could be stabilized at a

cost of $100.

The only other issue was the deteriorating flooring. During the non visitor season
the floor boards could be removed and a new subfloor put in. Then the existing flooring
would be installed. He felt the flooring would be all volunteer labor and the plywood
needed would cost approximately $200.

The floor beams for the upstairs are a concern, because this house was built with
individual ceiling beams that don’t go across and as the roof load increases the beams
pull out. In some areas these cross beams are out of the slots. He would like to carefully
remove the trim and jack the wall into position. This would be a six month project with
all volunteer labor.

There are trees that need to be taken down to protect the property. We would like
to drop the power lines underground. Volunteers could dig a ditch.

Down the road siding and window sills and trim and gutters need to be replaced.
Materials are not expensive.

John Drabik asked if Mr. Ramsay died who would finish the work. How will we
handle insurance for these volunteers. If they can maintain a bank account maybe the
Town should require them to maintain a minimum. It might be better if the Town owned
the property and leased it to them.

Marvin Schutt reported we have approximately eight people ready to work on
these repairs. We need to look into insurance.

Mr. Formica stated the Lee House has a similar arrangement. We don’t require a
minimum in their checking account. When the 5013c is in place we will be able to
address this.

Matthew Fleisher of 38 White Birch Circle stated the Sportsman’s Club
