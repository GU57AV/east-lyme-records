2b. Discussion / Action — MOU Waterford / East Lyme Joint Harbor Mgt. Vessel

Mr. Formica noted that this item concerned an agreement between Waterford and East
Lyme for use of Waterford's police boat by our Harbormaster and joint Harbor
Management Commissions. He said that the boat will be used by Harbor Master's and
Deputy Harbormasters to manage the mooring program both in East Lyme in Waterford.
He said the boat will be docked or moored in the upper Niantic River on alternating
weeks in East Lyme and Waterford. The boat has a unique official dual town emblem and
the words" Harbor Master" on each side. In addition to routine patrols by both police
departments, it will enhance safety by providing an added official presence on the river. It
will be used by both Harbor Masters and their Deputies to conduct marine safety outreach
and education with the local boating community. He added that expenses for fuel will be
shared by both towns Harbor Management Commissions from revenues generated by the
mooring permit programs.

SAMUEL SMITH PROPERTY
HISTORIC PRESERVATION

"As state archaeologists, we often get the chance to conduct field
reviews of historic properties around Connecticut, but it is a rare
opportunity to see a late 1600's house that has maintained such
wonderful historic characteristics and adjoining property that has
great integrity for archaeological resources. The property is a gem for
both its natural and cultural resources and to preserve it for the
community is a positive step to the future quality of life in your town."

Nicolas Bellantoni
State Archaeologist
November 17, 2010

Why preserve the Samuel Smith Property?

The Samuel Smith House, a rare surviving example of a late 1600s house in its original rural setting, is the only
privately owned unrestored historic dwelling still in existence in East Lyme. On the National Register of Historic
Houses since June 1979, it provides many opportunities to enhance understanding of cultural changes and differences
in construction techniques from 1685 to 1812. This use of the house would complement the existing historic house
museums in East Lyme, and support the town's commitment to historic preservation.

The 17 acres of adjoining farmland includes potential archaeological sites and would offer expanded educational and
community uses. Preservation of this land would help protect 800 feet of Bride Brook and a part of East Lyme's
watershed.

What does the Samuel Smith Property have to offer?

Because the Samuel Smith House has not been restored, it can be used immediately as an historical destination. The
barn next to the house, built on the original foundation, can be used for meetings and educational or cultural
programs. In addition, the 17 acres of adjoining farmland can be used to enhance understanding of agricultural history,
for community gardens and for hiking and walking trails.

How will the Samuel Smith Property be funded?

The Town of East Lyme is currently in discussions with the property owners concerning the purchase of the Samuel
Smith House and adjoining property. Once approved by Town boards, the proposal would be sent to a Town Meeting
and Referendum for approval by the town’s taxpayers. Upon passage, the Town will move to complete a purchase
agreement with the current owners. An application for an acquisition grant for up to $200,000 toward the purchase
price will be submitted to the Connecticut Department of Culture and Tourism, Historic Preservation Division. The
potential for accepting additional donations toward the purchase price is still being investigated.

Even if no grant money is received for the purchase, the estimated cost to bond the purchase, at 3%
per year over 20 years, would be less than $4 per individual taxpayer per year.

Who will manage and maintain the Samuel Smith Property?
After the property has been purchased, a volunteer-based, not-for-profit group will be responsible for managing and

maintaining the house, developing educational and cultural programs in coordination with other community groups,
and raising the funds necessary to support the Samuel Smith Property.

LUANE LANGE, Historic Properties Commission, 160 Pennsylvania Ave., Niantic

During the July 18 Board of Selectman's meeting, there was a question about a revenue
"flow" to the town from the Samuel Smith House. It seems necessary to clarify my
comments that were viewed by some on Channel 22 but not reflected in the Public
Discussion part of the Minutes.

It is not anticipated that there would be a direct revenue from the Samuel Smith House to
the General Fund. The not-for-profit group, waiting to meet with town officials for
discussion, are looking at a management model that would have the town lease the
property to the not-for-profit. This is a model used in other towns. The not-for-profit
would be primarily responsible for planning and implementing programs and raising
funds for the property's primary operation and maintenance.

To pursue another path, I was asked by the Samuel Smith Property Support Group to
investigate tourism dollars that might result from adding this property to the other two
historic properties to create an historic triangle.

Communication from the Connecticut Commission on Economic and Community
Development and the Department of Culture and Tourism, and the Historic Preservation
Division all indicated that this type of information does not exist for Connecticut at the
present time. There is hope that, with the (Gov.) Malloy structure, placing all three
agencies together, funds will be available to aggregate this type of information into a
database similar to that of several other states.

Curiosity led me to websites for "Revenues from tourism." I found those of Florida,
California, Ohio, Maine and Canada to have interesting information. While this
information cannot be applied to East Lyme, the following thoughts were consistent:
¢ Jobs were created to service the tourist visitor, and those wages are spent;
¢ Skills were taught to high school students and the under-employed through
summer/tourist related employment;
© Overnight visitors, and several days visit produce more destination-site
opportunities and sales than a 2-3 hour visitor;
Income from State sales taxes and income taxes are generated for the State;
Such State income saves the State taxpayers money; and
In one state, tourism generated $1. of each $5. in sales.

So, depending on the terms of a Management Agreement between the Town and the
planned not-for-profit corporation, revenues from educational programs, camps, events,
grants, etc. would go to the operation and maintenance of the property.

Tourism revenues cannot be estimated. The existence of the property as a third historic
site would increase the town's recognition as an historically interesting community. Last
year, the over 400 visitors to the Lee House came from twenty-three states, at least
twenty Connecticut communities, and six other countries. There is no way of knowing
where else they spent their dollars.
