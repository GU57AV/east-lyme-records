1.c. Delegations
Mr. Formica called for Delegations. He noted that the Samuel Smith House discussion was listed under
Item 3. and that at that time Art Carlson would update them. He asked that others, who wished to speak
regarding that item, please speak at this time.

Maggie Prokop, 52 Lovers Lane said that she is an Alternate on the Historic Properties Commission
and also serves on the Smith Harris House Commission. She said that she came this evening to speak
against the Samuel Smith House on Plants Dam Road as the Town does not need another museum –
we already have two with the Smith Harris House and the Thomas Lee House. She said that
approximately 450 people visited those combined homes last year. The owners of the Samuel Smith
House are paying $4300 in tax revenue to the Town and it should not be taken away. As far as the
aquifer and water situation – there will be a pipeline to Lake Konomoc soon and the acquisition of the
Darrow Pond property will also help with the water situation for current and future needs. With regard to
walking trails – the public has access to plenty of walking trails already – such as the new Darrow Pond
property, Oswegatchie Hills, Nehantic State Forest, Rocky Neck State Park and the Smith Harris
Property just to mention a few. She said that she thinks that the property would be more of a liability
than an asset. (Letter submitted; attached)

Ms. Hardy asked Ms. Prokop if she was speaking as a member of the Commissions that she serves on.
Ms. Prokop said no, she was speaking as a citizen.
Elizabeth Kuchta, East Lyme Town Historian said that she was here to speak in support of the Town
acquiring the Samuel Smith House and property on Plants Dam Road. She said that she believes that
they need to preserve this historic property as it is a rare occasion that such unused land and a house
becomes available. She told of the history that brought the Smith Harris House and Thomas Lee House
to the Town as museums. She said that the Lee House open house last year drew 200 visitors and that
the Smith Harris House also draws visitors. This house presents another great learning tool for the
children and saving the aquifer. (Letter submitted; attached)

Normal Peck, President, East Lyme Historical Society said that he was speaking on behalf of the East
Lyme Historical Society and that they wished to support the Town’s purchase of the Samuel Smith
House provided that there be formed a non-profit organization of volunteers responsible for preserving
the building and maintaining the land as historically correct. This organization should be as self
sufficient as possible. They also recommend that the support organization with the assistance of the
Historic Properties Commission diligently pursue grants and donations to assist in a purchase. (Letter
submitted; attached)

3. Unfinished Business
a. Discussion – Samuel Smith House
Mr. Formica noted that they have Art Carlson from the Natural Resources Commission and Luane
Lange from the Historic Properties Commission present to participate in this discussion.

Ms. Hardy asked for an update on any new information that they had for them since their last meeting.
Mr. Carlson, Chairman of the Natural Resources Commission said that they have added information
that with the Samuel Smith House that they would have a historical triangle in the Town – with the
Smith Harris House, Thomas Lee House and Samuel Smith House. With regard to if it is historically true
or not – he said that a year and a half ago that Nick Bellantoni the CT State Archaeologist had written
them that he was aware of the property and had visited it in the summer of 2010. It is a late 1600’s
house and compares with the Thomas Lee House. They were pleased to support the preservation
efforts and to assist in help with any Federal or State funding that might be available for that effort.
Mr. Carlson continued that they are talking about 17.4 acres of deeded space and that he thinks that
they would look very smart years from now for having purchased this property. He said their position is
to secure it so as to not have any roads built on it so that they can protect the aquifer.

Mr. Formica noted that part of the Natural Resources Commission charge is to bring items such as
these before the Board of Selectmen for consideration and that the Historic Properties Commission of

the houses and private organizations in some areas also take charge of them. With respect to the
