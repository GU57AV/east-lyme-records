2b. Presentation – Ledge Light Health District
Mr. Nickerson introduced Baker Salisbury, Director of the Ledge Light Health District.

Mr. Salisbury said that he likes to come before them at least two times a year to update them and
provide information on what they have been doing. He noted that the last time that they met that they
had decided to keep the District intact and subsequently Ledyard has also decided to do the same and
remain in the District. He added that they have been in several meetings with Stonington and that they
may also join the District. If they do this would bring a reduction in the per capita fee. At this time he
said that he could not give them that fee as it is based upon the numbers and the addition of Stonington
would add roughly another 18,000 to those numbers.

He said that their aim is to prevent disease, illness and injury. He said that this evening he would give a
brief outline on a food borne salmonella outbreak. He said that they had such an event this past year on
the shoreline. It was the first time in Connecticut history that a local public health department found and
identified the food service outbreak. It was also something that the public did not hear about. He passed
out a chart of the timeline from the initial exposure to confirmed exposure to the time that the food
service workers were cleared and released back to work (Day1 to Day 51). He noted the various stages
where people were ill, and some were hospitalized and interviews taken to narrow down the origin.
Samples were sent to the State laboratory for testing and analysis and confirmation. He said that it was
important and unique in that it was handled by a local public health department.

Ms. Hardy asked how many people were affected by this outbreak.
Mr. Salisbury said that they will never know.
While they do know of 14 people and the food handlers – the number of people impacted over 42 days
will never be known as some had very mild cases and would not have reported it.

Ms. Hardy asked if it was the food that was contaminated or the food workers who contaminated the
food that caused it.
Mr. Salisbury said that it was probably a combination of both although that is also something that they
will probably never know. It appears more likely that it was poor hygiene and a person who was ill who
handled the food as it was all in fresh food and not the cooked food.

Ms. Hardy asked where the lab is that the samples are sent to for testing.
Mr. Salisbury said that it is the State laboratory in Hartford.

Ms. Hardy asked if the delay in getting a response from the lab was due to the time it takes for the
cultures to age.
Mr. Salisbury said yes and added that there is also travel time and the time that it takes to check and re-
check the findings.

Ms. Cheeseman asked how many food establishments are in the Ledge Light Health District area.
Mr. Salisbury said that there are 1500 which includes the convenience stores. There are 568 that had to
be inspected prior to re-opening after the storms that took the power out for many days.

Mr. Seery asked what to do about the fresh vegetables as you would usually just wash and eat them
whereas with food you would cook it and it would normally kill any bacteria.
Mr. Salisbury said that you would soak them in cold water and then rinse. He added that most of the
problems come with chicken and that it needs to be thoroughly cooked.

Mr. Nickerson thanked Mr. Salisbury for the informative presentation.

2d. Discussion – Energy Assistance Program
Mr. Nickerson said that Ms. Wilson would present this.

Ms. Wilson explained that the energy assistance monies have been cut coming from the federal level
and that the households last year received around $800 of assistance. This year the number of people
applying has increased and the basic benefit has decreased to around $225 per household which is
probably only one delivery or payment (electric heat). They have been somewhat fortunate in that the
weather so far has been mild so they have dodged a bullet although they cannot count on that to
continue. She said that she is looking for some discussion on how to handle this.

Mr. Nickerson asked where the source of the money was.
Ms. Wilson said that it was the federal money funding that was cut.

Mr. Seery asked if there was any discussion at the State level of adding to help with some of the federal
cut.
Ms. Wilson said that there has been talk but with the mild winter, they have not done anything yet.

Ms. Hardy asked if Ms. Wilson had any idea of how much money has been expended so far this year so
that they would have an idea of how the program is doing.
Ms. Wilson said that it is difficult now as the program is in process and the way in which it is handled
does not make it easy to determine where they are in the program. People have to call for a delivery to
TVCCA and it is at that time that they find out if they have exhausted their individual allotment. She said
that she would try to get some information on where they stand.

Ms. Cheeseman said that she understands that the electric companies cannot shut people off during
the winter protection period.
Ms. Wilson said that was true and added that they have to apply for that winter protection program.

Ms. Cheeseman asked if there is any history to refer to so that they could have an idea of how this
would have been taken care of.

Ms. Wilson said that she is not aware of any and that generally private organizations have helped and
kicked in when benefits have been exhausted in certain instances. She said that this year she is
concerned as the funding has been cut so drastically that as there are more people seeking assistance
that there will be more whose benefit will be exhausted during the crucial winter months.

The Board asked that Ms. Wilson please bring them further information and thanked her for being
proactive in bringing this forward to them.
