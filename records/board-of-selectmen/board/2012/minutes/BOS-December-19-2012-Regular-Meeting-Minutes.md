2. NEW BUSINESS
A. LEDGE LIGHT UPDATE / BRIEFING

Mr. Salsbury of the Ledge Light Health District provided updates to the Board of what Ledge
Light has been involved in since his last update to them. He discussed bacterial well
contamination in Ledyard. Tick-borne diseases within the Health District. They have also been
involved with Lawrence and Memorial Hospital’s health needs assessment in an effort to
determine where resources should go. They have been working on a new website and logo.
They also have been conducting drive thru flu shot clinics. This helps them to plan for possible
mass immunizations.

B. DISCUSSION: DOCKS/STORM DAMAGE -

Mr. Formica stated the lease does require the tenant to perform maintenance on the building
and the dock, but they are town owned. The original intent of this lease was to honor the
environment of fishing in our community. Money was not the object of this lease. Since that
time we have added handicapped access, a sport fishing dock, sidewalks, picnic tables. The
expiration of the lease coincides somewhat with the Amtrak work being finished. The lease
ends on December 31, 2012. FEMA has inspected the dock as to any storm damage. The
insurance adjuster will be visiting the location. They have applied for a grant to remove the
building and put a new building with a bathroom and storage for a new beachcomber. They
asked Amtrak to keep pieces of the old bridge and a Town Resident will be making a statue with
those pieces.

A letter from Captain Greg Dubrule of the Black Hawk along with pictures is attached as Exhibit
B.

There was discussion of the building and dock being in rough shape and clearly needing to be
repaired, and the tenants being responsible for the maintenance. The area has been blocked
off to the public.

C. DISCUSSION: POTENTIAL NOISE ORDINANCE
This item was moved up on the Agenda.

Mr. Formica stated this is a problem in Mr. Scheel’s neighborhood. If they decide to implement
this they would have to work on the language, and have a Public Hearing.

Mr. Seery has dealt with enforcement of this type of issue. He understands what Mr. Mingo
stated, but this would give the police the ability to take action.

Mr. Formica learned that Waterford does have this type of ordinance. They have one officer
trained in using the meter. That costs approximately $1500.00 and has a calibration fee of
$300. The Waterford Police Chief is of the opinion that it does help to resolve large noise
issues.

Mr. Nickerson stated there is a compelling argument for enacting this ordinance. He was not in
favor of the noise ordinance prior to hearing this. He is very frustrated by the lack of common
courtesy. It is sad that it has to get to this level. This could be a tool for law enforcement when
people can’t be neighborly.

Ms. Cheeseman shared the dislike for adding an ordinance, but it does seem that some of these
activities were illegal. She would like to see the final language. Her concern is that it would be
used as a way to report a disliked neighbor. She would like to hear more from Waterford.

Ms. Hardy stated previous boards have considered this type of ordinance. Last time the decibel
meter was more costly for an accurate one. She would like to hear about the practicality of
using the meter.

There was discussion of variances, and who would administer those variances. There was also
discussion of the penalty, and if it is enough for people to respond to the ordinance.

Mr. Formica stated they will keep moving this forward and it will be on the Agenda for January
3%,

Ms. Hardy stated they did a good job putting this together. She asked that they contact CCM
and see how many towns have a noise ordinance.

Attorney O’Connell presented the Board with a list from DEEP of towns that do have Noise
Regulations (Attached as Exhibit C).

Mr. Scheel presented them with a short video clip from the internet that he found of his
neighbors.

Statement of Robert G. Scheel, 31 Upper Walnut Hill Road, East Lyme to
the First Lyme Board of Selectmen, Wednesday, December 17, 2012, Re:
Draft EL Noise Ordinance

The purpose of this statement is to support enactment of an East Lyme Noise Ordinance. Bob and Anne
Scheel have lived at 31 Upper Walnut Hill Road for almost 16 years. Our property consists of
approximately 18 acres with a home and a barn. Road frontage is approximately 250 feet and the
property extends approximately 1100 feet deep as it widens at the back. In 2008, our neighbor
immediately to the North, Stephen Slavtcheff, passed away and his land was sold by his heirs. This
property consists of two parcels. One parcel, a two acre home site, consists of a small A-frame home
and a Quonset Hut that Mr. Slavtcheff used for storing his automobile and farming equipment. The A-
frame home is located approximately 230 feet from our house. The remaining parcel consists of
approximately 73 acres and includes a utility easement (High Tension Wires), the head-lands of
Cranberry Creek, extensive wetlands and uplands.

The buyer has allowed his daughter and boyfriend (now husband) to live in the A-frame house and
utilize the Quonset Hut. After the change of ownership, extensive changes in use of the property took
place as follows:

a. Mr. Slavtcheff’s large vegetable gardens were dismantled to facilitate construction of a Moto-
Cross Track. These gardens were located approximately 20 feet from my property line and 100
feet from my home.

b. The Quonset Hut was used to store ATVs, motorcycles, Off-Road “Mules” and other recreational
vehicles. It is also used to repair and test race cars used at local race tracks.

c. The property was used to host friends who brought ATVs and other recreational vehicles to the
property. The group called themselves “The Pure Bred Rednecks” and according to Facebook
posts consists of over 80 members.

d. Automatic weapons, Assault Rifles and semi-automatic pistols have been fired near my home.

e. Wetlands adjacent to my property and feeding Cranberry Creek were damaged. A road was cut
onto my property and across Cranberry Creek to facilitate trespassing

#. The Quonset Hut was defaced with Graffiti regarding the “Pure Bred Rednecks”

g. The property was used to stage ATV riding on nearby State Forest Lands.

h. The property was used to facilitate the sale of narcotics.

These activities have been documented by members of “The Pure Bred Rednecks” and posted on line.
The can be viewed by going to You-Tube and searching for “SuperDom666”, “MegaDfox” or “dred819”
or by going to Google and searching for the same identifier. In September 2010 the A-Frame home was
raided by the ELPD, the State Police Swat Team and ATF. Arrests were made for weapons charges (the

boyfriend is a previous felon and had loaded handguns in his possession), operation of a drug factory,
sale and possession of narcotics. In November or 2010, the buyer of Mr. Slavtcheff’s property
purchased another house in Oakdale and moved his daughter and her boyfriend to that location. They
still utilize the Quonset Hut for storage and appear to engage in the sale of firewood harvested from the
larger of the two parcels. The A-frame is currently rented. Guns are still fired next door (High Velocity
Semi -Automatic Weapons with high capacity clips — 50 or more rounds fired in less than 30 seconds and
semi-automatic pistols). In the spring of 2012, one resident suffered an accidental gunshot wound.

Despite all the issues summarized above, the irresponsible use of ATVs, motorcycles and 4WD trucks
has been the most disruptive to my family’s lifestyle. These machines apparently are modified to
increase power by minimizing the exhaust system , mufflers and spark arresters. They are also used for
jumping, racing, “mudding” , etc. so the sound levels are not constant, but emit “impact” type noises
that cannot be disregarded. Prior to the arrest, these machines were used at all hours of the day. After
the arrest, they are used primarily on weekends. Using a decibel meter purchased at an electronics
supply store, | have at times measured sound amplitudes of over 100db. This is equivalent to sitting in
the stands at the Waterford Speed Bowl during a racing event or adjacent to an airport runway. Sound
levels of this type can be damaging to health and wellbeing.

Starting in 2009, | have taken the following action regarding noise:

a. | notified the Town of East Lyme Zoning Official (on numerous occasions) of extensive ATV
usage. Mr. Mulholland sent a letter notifying the new owner that RU-80 property could be
utilized to operate ATVs, but the property could not be used to operate a “Club”. Guest
riders continued to use the property and no additional enforcement action has been taken.

b. | notified the First Selectman of the situation.

c. | notified the ELPD of the situation.
| contacted the State DEP (DEEP) regarding noise. They indicated that State regulations only
apply to licensed vehicles on roadways. Off road vehicles are not covered by any state
statute. DEEP patrols of the Nehantic State Forest were increased.

e. contacted other towns about Noise Ordinances. | found that many towns had successfully
enacted noise ordinances and that enforcement problems were minimal. The only Town
that reported a problem with enactment was Waterford. Speed Bowl supporters opposed
the ordinance as written. The Waterford Ordinance currently excludes the racetrack.

f. | called the ELPD regarding ATV usage and noise. Occupants were told that they could not
ride ATV on town roads. However. ATV use and noise is not regulated in East Lyme on
Private Property, so no citation was issuedtnor could preventative action could be taken.

g. | talked to other ATV and motorcycle users. While sympathetic, they indicated that
ordinances restricting the use of ATV are rarely enacted because ATV and motorcycle
associations actively oppose any legislation intended to restrict use or sale of these vehicles.

Members suggested a noise ordinance to help control irresponsible use of these machines.
Some residents of my road that also race motorcycles indicated that the sounds coming
from the property adjacent to my home were far in excess of what would be permitted
during motorcycle racing.

h. While no other neighbors are “abutting” the old Slavtcheff property, | requested their
support to help convince the town that stop excessive noise should be stopped. All were
reluctant to become involved.

In the Fall of 2011, it became clear that the “boyfriend” would be sentenced to prison. At that time |
again approached the Town regarding a noise ordinance. | hoped to have a “protective measure” in
place before a prison sentence was completed. With the help of Mr. Formica, Mr. Mulholland, the Town
Attorney and the DEEP, an ordinance has been drafted, reviewed and approved by the town attorney
and reviewed and approved by the DEEP. Itis now (hopefully) ready for enactment.

The “boyfriend” was released by the State in August 2012 after the death of his father. He will be on
parole for 3 additional years. While ATV usage is no longer occurring on a daily basis, it still occurs on
weekends. Even with house windows closed, the sound of ATV’s are quite clear and can make talking on
the phone or other normal tasks more difficult. Also, my wife is currently going through Chemotherapy,
so the noise makes it difficult to rest or sleep.

Many who have heard this story find it hard to believe that something like this could happen in East
Lyme. | find the reverse is true.......... people can’t really appreciate what has happened next door until
they have lived it. | urge you to watch the You Tube Videos to obtain a better appreciation of how my
life has been affected by noise. | have also attached some still pictures to this statement to show some
of the activities that occur next door or can generate high intensity noise. | urge the Town to adopt the
Noise Ordinance at the earliest opportunity.

Re: Storm Damage to Black Hawk Dock

Dear First Selectman Formica,

As owner and captain of the Black Hawk sportfishing boat, I am writing to you and the boards to
express my concerns over the condition of the town owned dock and adjacent buildings where
the Black Hawk is moored and conducts our business.

As you are aware from your recent visit to the dock a few months ago, many of the planks are
rotted, loose and unsafe. We’ve made dozens of Band-Aid repairs over the years to both the
dock and buildings. I’ve spent thousands of dollars of my own, trying desperately to keep ahead
of problems. The shack there was so badly depilated that we jacked it up and sister framed the
entire framework with 2 x 12s. I paid a local contractor to help. We also sheet-rocked the
inside, put in new windows, and painted. If the building was privately owned, the town would
condemn it and make you tear it down. On the dock, we’ve replaced planks and tried to
maintain what we can. Unfortunately, even though we tried our best to remedy the situation,
we’ve still had employees, and even a handful of customers, fall through the rotten planks of the
dock and suffer minor cuts, bruises, and splinters.

In the aftermath of Hurricane Sandy, the dock condition has worsened to the point that it is now
unusable. Please see the enclosed photos, taken right after the storm. The extreme tidal surge

has broken everything in its path. Many of the dock pilings, which are the backbone of the entire

dock, have shifted and are now at an angle. The landing is just hanging there after dropping

more than a foot. This is an urgent safety and liability issue for the town of East Lyme. Due

to the unsafe conditions, we have had to cancel the last two weekends of our fishing season as

we cannot possibly have customers on the dock. This past weekend the weather was beautiful,

and our phone was ringing off the hook with people wanting to fish. We sent them all to the

Sunbeam, costing us valuable end of the season revenue. Se 4e prey is
