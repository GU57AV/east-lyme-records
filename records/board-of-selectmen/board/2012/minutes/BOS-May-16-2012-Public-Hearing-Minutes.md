Mr. Formica called the Public Hearing regarding two proposed leases for portions of
Town Property by Care and Share and the Children’s Museum respectively to order at
7:03 p.m. and led the Pledge.

Attorney O’Connell stated that a new state statute had been enacted recently which
required a public hearing be held on any sale or lease of municipal property. He added
that he had worked closely with both proposed tenants on the lease terms. He said that
Care and Share wanted to lease part of the old garage for a term of 5 years with an option
for an additional 5 years with one year notification by either party for cancelling the
lease. He said that the town makes no representation of any improvements and the lease
is “as is, where is”. He added that there is designated parking and that Care and Share
plans to make renovations all at their cost. He said that the utilities attributable to the
leased portion will be prorated and they will be their responsibility. He added that the
insurance requirements are set forth in the lease.

Attorney O’Connell said that the Children’s Museum wanted to lease the upper part of
the garage area of the Park and Recreation facility for one year, with either party
terminating the lease with 4 weeks advance notice. He said that they have fully inspected
the premises and the Town is not responsible for any repairs and that they will accept the
premises “as is, where is, in its present condition”.

Mr. Nickerson entered the meeting at 7:17 p.m.

Attorney O’Connell said that there is parking in front of the old carpentry shop and that
all utilities necessary will be borne by the Children’s Museum.

Mr. Formica said that Care and Share has had a nomadic lifestyle ~ constantly moving
with no permanent home. He said that they had reviewed a formula for heating oil for
their section and-had come up with $150 per month and noted that the Children’s

‘Museum would also be responsible for electricity. He said that it was his hope that once
the lease was finalized and made public, people would come forward to help out with the
building.

My name is Angie McLean, 180 Chesterfield Road, East Lyme. As President of Care &
Share, and on behalf of the organization, I would like to make a few comments for the
record at this public hearing relative to our proposed use of space at the 'Old Town Garage’
on Roxbury Road. ,

Care & Share was started by four local women in 1989 who at that time distributed eight
Thanksgiving baskets to local families in need. Now we are a 501 (c) (3) charitable
corporation, with approximately 100 volunteers, serving some 500 clients in East
Lyme/Niantic and Salem, with our organization supported through the generosity of
residents, businesses, churches and our school-age children.

Our services include:

*a year-round food pantry;

*daily bread deliveries;

*spring, summer and Thanksgiving food baskets;

*Christmas/Chanukah food baskets as well as a holiday store filled with new
items, where clients 'shop' for free to supplement their holiday gifts;

*emergency financial, housing and energy assistance;

*college scholarships and camperships;

*emergency snacks and clothing te local school children;

*support to the community with a winter coat program.

While it would be too time consuming to provide specifics on all our programs, we would
like to highlight the extent of outreach relevant to some of them. So far this year there have
been 183 food deliveries, feeding 581 people; 25 clients receive monthly either $50, $100 or
$150 toward electric, oil or rent, and year to date, payment to cover these same types of
expenses have intermittently been disbursed to an additional 17 clients. Last year 58 kids
were able to attend a variety of day or evening summer camps, and 6 college scholarships
totaling $15,000 were awarded to students.

The proposed single location for Care & Share would particularly facilitate our service to
the East Lyme/Niantic and Salem communities by centralizing our pantry and our holiday
store operations under one roof, thereby eliminating the annual need to call upon the
generosity of the area's businesses for donations of holiday space. Additionally, and
importantly, it will eliminate the need of seasonally operating and maintaining two separate
locations, a situation that requires transporting various dropped-off items from one store to
another. Assuming we become established in a fixed location, our visibility can be expected
to increase, thereby eliminating confusion over our whereabouts for donors, clients and
volunteers alike.

Your consideration and time is much appreciated.
