2f. Discussion – Historic Properties – Samuel Smith House
Mr. Formica said that this property has become available and that Art Carlson, Chairman of the Natural
Resources Commission and Luane Lange, Chairman of the Historic Properties Commission have been
actively researching how to secure this 1685 house and property.



East Lyme Board of Selectmen Regular Meeting Minutes of September 7, 2011                                 3
Art Carlson offered some background and explained that this came up during the development of the
open space plan for the Town. They had sent some 65 letters out for different properties and this was
the first response that they received back. They would like to secure it as it is in the Bride brook
watershed and because it has a circa 1685 house on the property. He said that he spoke with the
Historical Properties Commission and has been working with them on it. He said that he thinks that this
is a very good property for the Town to have and they are at the point where they need the go ahead
from the Selectmen to proceed with further essential information such as an appraisal.

Luane Lange said that the Historic Properties Commission is interested in this property as the house on
it is the fourth oldest house in this community and has 17 acres of original land that goes with it. She
said that she had provided them with a ‘Proposed plan for the purchase and preservation of the Samuel
Smith House and land’. The land has the potential for archaeological digs and there may also be an
Indian burial ground there. The Historical Society has been apprised of this potential. She said that they
looked at the potential uses for the property and thought that they could have community gardens or a
Town landscape nursery.

Mr. Carlson said that he thinks that they should look at this as a 25 year project – a place to add other
historical buildings to such as a blacksmith shop and other buildings of that period. This is a top of the
line property and it is also important in protecting the drinking water. He said that they are at the point
where they need the consent of the Board of Selectmen to move this process forward. He has looked at
the quality of the house and it is in remarkable condition for 300 years old. He said that he would
recommend that they establish a 501c3 ‘friends of’ for this house as they have for the Thomas Lee and
Smith Harris House.

Ms. Lange said that there are a variety of historic entities that have funds however they have to have
the property first.

Mr. Formica asked to be clear on the request here – authorization to engage an appraiser to determine
the value and engage a person to evaluate the house.
Mr. Carlson and Ms. Lange said that was correct and added that they would also want to be able to
discuss with the owners a cost.

Mr. Formica called for discussion from the Board members.

Mr. Birmingham noted that the back page of the information that had been provided to them has a list of
organizations that can help – he asked how many of them they have enlisted.
Mr. Carlson said none at this time.
Mr. Birmingham asked if they are just solid possibilities.
Ms. Lange said that as they have discussed potential uses, these things have come up and they have
kept track of them.

Ms. Hardy asked if part of the request is that the Town pays for the appraisal.
Mr. Carlson said yes, as they do not have any money.
Ms. Hardy asked Mr. Formica if he had any idea on the cost.
Mr. Formica said that he heard that it was low for a place of this size – not more than $1200.
Ms. Lange said that Mr. Peck of the Historic Properties Commission said that it could be done for
$1000.

Ms. Hardy said that if the make a motion to go forward that they would have to put the appraisal value
in it. She added that she is also somewhat biased and could envision an early American village re-
created there.

Mr. Nickerson said that he thinks that Mr. Carlson should take his energy and enthusiasm and moved
ahead with gathering this information.

5. b. First Selectman’s Report
Mr. Formica said that Town staff was phenomenal during the hurricane and that the Connecticut
National Guard was instrumental in the clearing of Route 161. He said that one of the challenges going
forward is how to best communicate with people when the power is out. There is also the crisis of CL&P
and how their upper management handled this event. It was not well organized from the top. The
linemen and workers however were great. The Town had just over 150 roads impacted by downed
trees and wires.
He said that they have been using Vet’s Field for the brush and will get a chipper in to chip it up. For
those who have put brush at the side of the road he asked that they please make sure that it is off the
road. They will get to picking it up as they take another sweep throughout the Town. He said that as of
Sunday only eight people were without power. On Tuesday they were back to a couple hundred
however now everyone is back up. He thanked everyone for their support and the EOC who did a great
job.
