2b. Proclamation - Loving Parting Day

Town Historian Elizabeth Kuchta reviewed the history of the parting of Lyme and Old Saybrook
which took place 350 years ago. She was present this evening to have the proclamation signed
and added that it would also be signed by the other First Selectmen of Old Lyme, Lyme, and
Salem.

MOTION (9)
Mr. Seery moved the following:
Whereas...

e in 1631, the English Earl of Warwick granted an extensive tract of land in the
Connecticut River Valley to Lord Saye and Sele, Lord Brooke and several
others; and

e this land, extending north on both the east and west sides of the Connecticut
River, became the Saybrook Colony, one of the earliest settlements in New
England; and

e in 1644, the Saybrook Colony was incorporated into the Connecticut Colony by
order of the Connecticut General Court in Hartford; and

e in 1663, the Saybrook Colony requested the Connecticut General Court “to set up
a plantation on the east side of the great River”, known as East Saybrook; and

e on February 13, 1665, representatives of the settlers from both sides of the river
signed an agreement, known as “The Loving Parting”, making East Saybrook, a
self- supporting community, separate from the Saybrook Colony; and

e in 1667, the Connecticut General Court assigned the name “Lyme” to this new
community.

Therefore...
in recognition of the 350" Anniversary

of the signing of the “Loving Parting” and
of the shared history of the Saybrook Colony with
the present towns of Old Saybrook, Lyme, Old Lyme, East Lyme and Salem,
February 13, 2015 is hereby proclaimed “Loving Parting Day”.
East Lyme Board of Selectmen
Seconded by Ms. Cheeseman. Motion passed 5-0.
