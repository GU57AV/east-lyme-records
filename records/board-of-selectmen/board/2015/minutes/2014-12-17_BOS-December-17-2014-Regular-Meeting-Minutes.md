2a1. Action — Proposed New England National Settlement
MOTION (5)

Mr. Wilson moved to approve a settlement with New England National, LLC and
all of its affiliates for $650,000 as reported to the United States District Court
following mediation and to be reflected in a written agreement to be signed by the
parties and approved by the United States District Court. An outline of the general
terms of the settlement being as follows:

1. Town of East Lyme to pay New England National the sum of $650,000

2. New England National to convey the approximate 8 acres of Darrow’s Pond
to the Town of East Lyme;

3. New England National and its affiliates will release all of the easements
property rights its controls on the Darrow’s Pond Property;

4, New England National and its affiliates will be permitted to retain certain
properties. These properties may be sold, but New England National and its

affiliates will not be permitted to develop any of the retained properties or
apply for any land use permits, nor may New England National or its
affiliates have any financial interest in any person or business which may
purchase the properties. In other words, New England National may sell the
properties but may not develop the properties;

5. New England National and all related parties will execute a covenant not to
develop any further real estate in the Town of East Lyme for a period of 20
years;

6. Anne Torrance will be permitted to subdivide the 3 acre parcel on which her
home sits into no more than 3 lots, provided the subdivision of the property
meets existing zoning and land use regulations and no variances or waivers
of the regulations are required; and

7. The parties will exchange full and final releases.

Payment of the $650,000 is to come from the following sources of funds:
1. $325,000 from Open Space Fund 59;

2. $50,000 from General Fund Contingency Account;
3. $146,000 from Undesignated Fund Balance; and
4. $129,000 from Legal Settlement Fund

Seconded by Ms. Cheeseman. Motion passed 6-0.

2b. Special Appropriation $4546 Grant — National Fish and Wildlife Foundation —
Support field trip to Stormwater Classroom

Town Engineer Victor Benni noted that the $4,546 Grant was awarded to the Town
of East Lyme to purchase signs, a three dimensional stormwater model, and two
educational programs that will be tailored for the event. Long Island Sound is
considered CT’s largest and most important natural resource and untreated
stormwater runoff creates water quality problems for Long Island Sound. The
Outdoor Stormwater & Environmental Classroom at the Hole-in-the-Wall
showcases many of the latest technologies & techniques utilized to treat & reduce
stormwater and improve water quality before being discharged to Long Island
Sound. All ten of the Town’s 3™ grade classes will rotate through five stations at
the Hole-in-the- Wall parking lot, which include presentations on the Stormwater
Classroom, Plants & Soils, Shoreline Species & Habitat, a three dimensional
Coastal Stormwater Model, and Recycling. The information presented at each of
the stations complements the East Lyme Public School science curriculum. Last
spring the Event partnered with the following organizations: Save the River-Save
the Hills, CT DEEP Marine Fisheries Division and Bureau of Watershed
Protection & Land Reuse, East Lyme High School AP Environmental Science,
Southeastern CT Regional Resources Recovery Authority (SCRRRA), Niantic
River Watershed Committee, and the East Lyme Public Works Department. The
remaining project cost of $ 10,063 is based on an estimated “matching”
contribution from Town staff and volunteers. The matching contribution is based
on Town employees’ and other local individuals & organizations time to prepare
and run the event; there will be NO in-kind funds that the Town of East Lyme
allocates for this program.

Hello, my name is Will Christensen and I live at 66
Grassy Hill, East Lyme. I am in Mrs. Cunningham’s
Civics class at ELHS. I will soon be getting my
drivers license, and I wish to bring to your attention
dangerous conditions on Route 161, also known as
Chesterfield Rd.

I’ve noticed that there are many passing zones on
Route 161, and these zones create dangerous
conditions. For instance, Mostoway Road was
recently moved several hundred feet to the south,
however, the passing zone remained the same. There
is now a dangerous passing zone directly in front of
Mostoway Rd. A situation could arise where a car
could be heading north, passing in a legal passing
zone and create a head-on collision with a car turning
out of Mostoway Rd., heading south. Also, there are
several busstops along Route 161. A bus could be
stopped, with several trucks or cars behind the bus
blocking the view of the bus from approaching
drivers. There is a bus stop ina legal passing zone,
and this may encourage drivers to pass the slowing
cars behind the bus, and it is dangerous.

Finally, driving south on Route 161, the
speed limit drops from 45mph to 35mph by the golf
course to 25mph in the school zone. There is actually

a legal passing zone beginning at the 35mph sign,
heading towards the school zone! One the one hand,
the CT. Department of Transportation is saying
slow down, and on the other hand, they are saying
it’s ok to pass! This is foolish, because cars rarely
will do 35mph in that zone, so the result will be
drivers passing dangerously at high speeds, heading
towards a school zone!

There are clearly too many intersections and too
many private driveways to allow ANY passing zones
on Route 161. There are also many bike riders on this
road. Route 1 in East Lyme is a similar state road,
and it has no passing zones in East Lyme.

As East Lyme’s governing body, it is up to you,
the Selectman, to ask the Ct. Department of
Transportation to take away these dangerous passing
zones. I urge you to do this immediately, before
someone gets seriously hurt due to unsafe passing
zones on Route 161.1 thank you for your attention on
this matter.

Sincerely,

Will Christensen

Student, Mrs. Cunningham’s civics class, ELHS
Soon to be a driver!
