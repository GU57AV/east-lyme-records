2c. Authorization First Selectman to Sign Temporary Easement and Waiver of Compensation between
State and Town — Route 1

Mr. Nickerson noted that the State of Connecticut Department of Transportation (DOT) is
replacing the Route 1 bridge over the Pattagansett River adjacent to Upper Pattagansett Road
during the construction season from April to November 2016. To facilitate construction, the
overhead utility lines need to be temporarily relocated to the south over private property
including property owned by the Town of East Lyme. The utility wires will be moved back to
their original location away from Town property at the completion of the project. The DOT is
requesting that the Town of East Lyme sign:

1. A “Waiver of Compensation and Appraisal” which is a document where Town waives its right
to seek monetary compensation for the easement. The value of this easement would most
likely be between $100 and $500. The effort by the State to do the appraisal and associated
paper work would far exceed that value. The engineering department recommends waiving the
compensation and appraisal to save the DOT money and to foster our continued cooperation
with this state agency.

2. Sign a temporary construction easement (aerial only) to the State of Connecticut for the
temporary relocation of overhead utility wires over a property owned by the Town of East Lyme
near the route 1 bridge over the Pattagansett River. The final easement language is still being
prepared.

Sec. 12-71d. Schedule of motor vehicle values. On or before the first day of October each
year, the Secretary of the Office of Policy and Management shall recommend a schedule
of motor vehicle values which shall be used by assessors in each municipality in
determining the assessed value of motor vehicles for purposes of property taxation. For
every vehicle not listed in the schedule the determination of the assessed value of any
motor vehicle for purposes of the property tax assessment list in any municipality shall
continue to be the responsibility of the assessor in such municipality, provided the

legislative body of the municipality may, by resolution, approve any change in the
assessor’s method of valuing motor vehicles. Any appeal from the findings of assessors

concerning motor vehicle values shall be made in accordance with provisions related to
such appeals under this chapter. Such schedule of values shall include, to the extent that
information for such purpose is available, the value for assessment purposes of any motor
vehicle currently in use. The value for each motor vehicle as listed shall represent one
hundred per cent of the average retail price applicable to such motor vehicle in this state
as of the first day of October in such year as determined by said secretary in cooperation
with the Connecticut Association of Assessing Officers.

Re: Position Paper on Motor Vehicle Assessments
By: Mr. David Godbout

Dear Mr. Collins:

On behalf of Representative Ed Jutila, you requested a review of the above-captioned position paper with
feedback regarding if any of the ideas or proposals contained within had any merit.

First and foremost, CAAO does not take a position on what proposal(s) may or may not have merit. However,
any action will cause a reaction and as technicians, after reviewing the document, we have pointed out what
outcome or ramifications would result in having implemented each proposal. Additionally, as technicians, we
felt obligated to correct any information that was presented by the author as factual when we disagreed with his
representations of the facts, interpretation of statutes, or where the only a portion of the statute was quoted
(taken out of context) therefore changing its meaning/intent . We have pointed these out so that Representative
Jutila can conduct further research on those issues/statements.

We begin with the facts concerning how motor vehicles are assessed currently in Connecticut.

1. Beginning with Pg. 3 (but throughout the entire paper), the author states that in CT the tax on a Motor
Vehicle is required to be based on “market value”.

We disagree. In CT we place assessments on three separate classes of property and they are Real
Estate, Personal Property (mainly business equipment) and Registered Motor Vehicles. While
CGS Sec. 12-63(a) Rule of valuation (attached) states that “The present true and actual value of
all other property shall be deemed by all assessors to be the fair market value thereof and not its
value at a forced or auction sale”

This “fair market value” is applicable only to real estate.

As you continue reading 12-63, section (b)(2) (attached) states “the present true and actual value
of tangible personal property, other than motor vehicles, shall be determined in accordance with
the provisions of this subsection”. The subsection that follows has depreciation tables for
business equipment that is similar to that of the tables used by IRS for depreciating business
equipment as an expense.
The controlling statute for the assessment and taxation of motor vehicles is CGS Sec. 12-71d
Schedule of Motor Vehicle Values (attached) and states that the value for each vehicle “shall
represent one hundred per cent of the average retail price applicable to such motor vehicle”

CGS Sec. 12-71d contains “one hundred percent” intentionally. This is so that assessors are
NOT required to consider the mileage and condition of each vehicle and attempt to find market
value. The fact that CGS Sec. 12-71d is entitled “schedule of motor vehicle values” confirms
that motor vehicles are valued based on a “schedule” and not market value. This statute requires
assessors to value motor vehicles at “100% of the retail price” with no consideration for mileage,
condition, or any of the other factors that would determine “market value”.

It would be impossible for assessors to personally appraise every vehicle every year in their
jurisdiction. Consider the Waterbury assessor’s office attempting to personally view 70,000 cars
every October to appraise them based on their actual market value. One problem is obviously
the manpower and used car expertise that does not currently exist and would have to be
contracted but additionally, most people take their car to work during the same timeframe city
employees, or contracted employees, would need to view them. This would not only be an
expensive burdensome project to be undertaken by the municipalities, but would also be equally
inconvenient for the tax payer whom would be required to have their vehicle inspected by the
city and likely take time off from their employment. The expense incurred by jurisdictions being
required to value MVS at Market Value would be astronomical.

because “average retail” used to be a column in most motor vehicle pricing guides. The biggest truth that
this position paper uncovered is that NADA no longer has an “average retail” column and renamed that
same column as “clean retail” causing some confusion and complaints.
