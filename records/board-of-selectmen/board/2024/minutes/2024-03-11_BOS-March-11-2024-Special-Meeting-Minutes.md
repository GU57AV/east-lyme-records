    RE:           Board of Selectmen Authority to Modify Proposed Capital Expenditures
                  in Board of Education Budget

    Dear Mr. Cunningham:

           You have inquired whether the Board of Selectmen (the'BOS') may reduce or
    modify line items in the capital expenditures budget proposed by the Board of Education
    (the "BOE"). You have reported to us that the BOE prepares two separate budgets - an
    operating budget and a capital expenditures budget. As explained herein, the answer
    is, under oertain circumstances, yes,

           Whether the BOS may reduce or modify a line item in a budget proposed by the
    BOE depends on the type of expenditure at issue. Generally, the BOE proposes two
    types of budgets - an operating budget and a capital expenditures budget. The BOE's
    authority to propose each of these budgets derives from separate statutory authorities
    as explained below.

            Authority for the operating budget is derived from General $tatutes $ 10-222.
    This statute charges the BOE with an obligation to provide for the "maintenance of the
    public schools." "Maintenance of the public schools" is understood to mean "general
    maintenance, such as the daily upkeep of school facilities and equipment" and
    educational programing and staff. See Board of Education v. New Haven,237 Conn.
    169, :178-80 (1996). The State has delegated these responsibilities to local BOEs in
    order to satisfy the State's obligation to provide a free public education as set forth in
    Article 8, $ of the Constitution of the State of Connecticut. ln carrying out these
    statutory duties, the BOE acts as an agent of the State. Connecticut Courts have held
    that where an expenditure is related to this delegation of State authority, the Town does
    not have the power to modify the BOE budget, except in very limited circumstances not
    addressed here. See Board of Education v. Ellington, 151 Conn" 1 (1963).

        Authority lor the capital expenditures budget is derived from General Statutes $$
10-240 and 1A-241. Through these statutes, the State has delegated to towns the
obligation to provide for the capital expenses necessary to support the BOE's operation
of the public schools. Section 10-241 provides that these capital expenditures include
expenses such as "purchase . . . real and personal property for school purposes; to
build, equip, purchase and rent schoolhouses and make major repairs thereto and to
supply them with fuel, furniture, and other appendages and accommodations . . . ."
Because responsibility for these capital expenditures ultimately rests within the authority
and discretion of the Town, Connecticut Courts have held that the Town retains its
authority to, at its discretion, make changes to the capital expenditure budget because
"a local board of education must comply with the charter, ordinances and established
procedures of its respective municipality when expending capital funds that have been
appropriated to it for the accomplishment of the municipality's statutory education
duties." Board af Education v. New Haven, 237 Conn. 1 69, 1 83 (1996). East Lyme, like
many towns, has delegated the responsibility for proposing a capital budget to the BOE
by ordinance and customary practice. See e.9., East Lyme Code of Ordinances $
31.04. When the BOE exercises these statutory authorities, it is an agent of the Town.
The Town's established procedures, consistent with the above-cited Court decisions,
has been for the BOE to distinguish between these various types of expenses by
proposing an operating budget and a separate capital expenditures budget. When the
Town is reviewing proposed expenditures in the capital budget, it retains its statutory
authority to make changes in that budget,

       To summarize, the BOS may,reduce or modifo line items in the BOE's capital
expenditure budget, but not in the BOE's operating budget. The reason for this is that
the BOE's budget making authority derives from multiple statutory sources, and the
Town retains authority over only the capital expenditure budget and generally has no
authority to modify the operating budget, except in very limited circumstances beyond
the scope of this opinion.
