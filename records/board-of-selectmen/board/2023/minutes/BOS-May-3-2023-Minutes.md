Good Evening, Mr. Seery, Members of the Board, and residents,

My name is Mary Cutillo, my address is 74 Fairhaven Road, Niantic.
Am here tonight as president of the Historic East Lyme Cemetery Association.l have with me
our town Historian Liz Kuchta, a member of our group, and Steve Alligood, vice president and
I last came before you in February of this year requesting that the Town apply for a state grant
titled Neglected Cemetery Account Program which was available for up to $5000. We asked
that the Town apply for the funds to repair the stone wall at Macks Mill Cemetery on Scott Road.
We had an estimate of $7000 to do the work and were fortunate that the Town was granted the
full $5000 towards this project. We want to thank you so much for your efforts on our behalf.
There are 16 cemeteries in East Lyme which come under our care. Annually we have the 3
largest and most visible mowed 3 to 5 times... always for Memorial Day, Fourth of July and
Labor Day. Volunteers rake, pick up trash, cut brush and saplings at no cost to the
Town throughout the year.

We have a small endowment with which we are very frugal, so that it will enable us to fund
projects for a long time to come. The Town gives us $2,500 annually. While we are very grateful
for this assistance, that money does not even cover the mowings, much less wall and stone
repair. To date this year we have already spent $2000 for necessary tree and branch removal at
Macks Mill Cemetery that we could not safely remove ourselves.WJ Tree Service has done our
cemetery work since 1989. Some of you may remember that Wes Jezierski was the Town Tree
Warden for many years and presently his son runs the business. The have the equiptment and
expertise to safely work around the old and fragile stones. Their prices are very reasonable and
we can trust that the work will be done in a timely manner.Supporting local businesses is very
important to us.

We understand that there may be some unused funds left from the ARPA fund and would like to
be considered as recipients of a portion of those funds.

We have a situation at Old Fox Farm Cemetery on Upper Walnut Hill Road which needs to be
addressed immediately. There are a large number of dead and dying trees which need to
removed professionally. WJ Tree Service has identified and marked them and given us an
estimate of $7400. Earlier this year a large tree fell on the wall there. One of our members has
been able to cut it up, luckily it did not damage any of the ancient stones.The trees which have
been identified need to be removed for the safety of anyone walking or working in the cemetery
itself.

Old Fox Farm Cemetery is about 90'x 100'and contains 102 gravestones.45 are marked with
names and 57 are fieldstones. The earliest marked stone is that of Ezra Moore in 1786. There
are2 veterans from the War of 1812 who receive flags on Memorial Day each year.

I have submitted photos which will show you the proximity of the trees to the stones and the
very difficult working conditions.
lf you have any questions I would be happy to try to answer them for you or consult with other
members present. Thank you for your time and attention
