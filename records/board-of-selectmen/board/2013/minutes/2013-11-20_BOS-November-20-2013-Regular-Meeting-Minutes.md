Preventing Tick-borne Disease |

Simple Steps that Can Make a Difference. Learn more at www.IIhd.org

\ Prevent Exposure to Ticks
> Be aware that infected ticks are endemic in our area. Ticks become active at 40 degrees

Fahrenheit. .
> Tick ‘danger zones’ include shady borders where recreation areas meet wooded areas.

Create a protective border several feet wide of wood chips or gravel along ‘danger
zones’.
> Ticks require ground-level moisture to thrive; cut trees back and locate play areas in full

sun.

y) Prevent Tick Bites

> Use DEET or wear treated clothing when working or playing outside.

> Bathe or shower shortly after working or playing outdoors. Ticks do not latch on and
begin feeding for several hours; scrubbing and lathering can get rid of loose ticks.

> Check people and pets for ticks. Treating pets with flea/tick prevention medicine
protects pets from getting ill but does not stop them from bringing ticks into the home.

> Check clothing for ticks. Put clothing in the dryer on high heat to kill unseen ticks.

4 Prevent Illness After a Bite

> Proper removal of ticks is important! Squeezing a ticks’ body or only partially removing a
tick can increase the likelihood of illness. Use tweezers to grasp the tick where it enters
the skin and pull upward in a slow, steady motion.

> Be vigilant for disease symptoms. You may have been bitten by a tick and not know. You
may or may not have the “bulls eye” rash at the beginning of an illness. Be aware that
tick-borne disease is very common in our area and ask your doctor to be tested if flu-like
symptoms, headaches, joint pain or other symptoms are not resolved.
