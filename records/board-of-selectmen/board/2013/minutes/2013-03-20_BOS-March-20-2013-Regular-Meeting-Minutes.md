Statement of Robert Scheel, Upper Walnut Hill Road, East Lyme

This summer, for the first time in about 30 years, | drove through a well known beach/resort area in
Rhode Island. | was struck by the features of this town as compared to East Lyme. We have tree lined
roads, conservative signs, reasonable traffic flow and no “mega bars” or bars catering to adults. In
comparison, this town had little vegetation, blinking neon signs and other unhealthy characteristics.
Why? It is because your predecessors developed a vision for East Lyme......a quiet beach front
community that is family friendly with good schools and good neighbors. To implement this vision, the
town has developed a library of ordinances that help guide development, taxation and behavior. These
ordinances have evolved over time........some date back to the 1800s while many were developed in the
1950s through the 1980s.

Imagine........You live in a beautiful home on one of the lakes that grace our town. After a prolonged
cold snap, ATVs and snowmobiles start racing around the lake front destroying the community's peace
and quiet as well as creating potential injury to the riders and our emergency response team. Well it
can’t happen. The town has ordinances against use of ATVs and snowmobiles on frozen surfaces.

Imagine......... Your neighbor falls on hard times, loses their job and becomes dependent on alcohol or
drugs. They supplement their income by putting a trailer on their property and having other people live
there. Junk and abandoned vehicles litter the property. People are defecating outside the home
because the septic system has been overloaded. Well it can’t happen. The town has ordinances against
living in trailers, sleeping outside and littering property with unregistered or nonworking vehicles.

Imagine........A mall close to our Middle School in is financial trouble. A developer wants to open a “Juice
Bar” for those 18 years or older.....they are profitable in some adjacent towns. It can’t happe: The
town has ordinances against adult establishments that are so restrictive they practically assure adult
establishments can’t be opened in East Lyme.

Imagine......... A mall close to our Middle School is in financial trouble. A store opens within 200 feet of
our school premises that specializes in the sale of drug paraphernali: crack pipes, bongs, wrapping
paper. It has happened.......no ordinances prohibit this type of establishment although it is clearly the
town’s vision to have drug free schools. No one could have for seen that such establishments would
even be possible 10 or 20 years ago. Ordinances must evolve as times and our society change.

Imagine.....You live in your home peacefully for many years. A chronic noise generator then moves in
next door. It could be loud music, race cars, ATVs. It is like having a running lawn mower inside your
house, even when the windows are closed. It can happen. You have heard examples from me and other
member of the community. The impact is almost impossible to describe

My message......please don’t be swayed by the argument that we don’t need more ordinances. They
implement your vision and our vision for the town. If you believe that it is wrong to allow a “chronic

noise generator” to invade the privacy of another's home and property, please endorse the noise
ordinance.

Lastly....On Saturday, ATVs and motorcycles were present next to my home for most of the day. As my
wife left for the grocery store a Yellow Dirt bike raced out of the property and quickly approached within
100 feet of her car. It stopped in front of my driveway, turned around and pulled a wheelie all the way
down the street making the maximum amount of noise possible. Neither Anne nor | could positively
identify the rider.......hence ticketing or arrest is difficult. While | appreciate the thought that existing
laws and ordinances can help improve my current situation, enforcing these laws is often difficult. | also
understand that enforcing a noise ordinance might be difficult. However, one of the major advantages
of this initiative is that a standard for noise has been set by the town........55dB may not mean much to
most people, but if officers are armed with a readily understood perspective statement it can certainly
help...most people can understand that the noise they generate at their property line should not exceed
a (lawn mower, bagpipe, truck at idle, industrial air conditioner, etc). When they understand the limit, |
believe most will try to conform without the police ever having to use a dB meter. This may not be true
in my case.......but | can only hope.

Please endorse and support the enactment of an East Lyme noise ordinance.

Noise Control Ordinance

I. PURPOSE: Pursuant to the general authority of Connecticut General Statutes § 7-148 and the
specific authority of Connecticut General Statute § 22a-73(c) it is hereby resolved that to
preserve and promote the health, safety and general welfare of the citizens of the Town of East
Lyme (the “Town”), it is in the common interest to enact a reasonable regulation pertaining to
the reduction, control and/or prevention of noise as to promote a general environment free from
excessive noise and vibration.

This ordinance is enacted to preserve, protect and promote the public health, safety, convenience,
general welfare, peace, quality of life, and property values for the citizens of East Lyme, and to
facilitate the enjoyment of natural resources and tranquility of the town, through the reduction,
control and prevention of excessive noise and vibration.

enforcement of this chapter:

AMBIENT NOISE OR BACKGROUND NOISE - Noise of a measurable intensity
which exists at a point as a result of a combination of many distant sources individually
indistinguishable.

VIL. ENFORCEMENT AND PENALTIES FOR OFFENSES

A. The East Lyme Police Department shall be responsible for investigating and documenting,
through acoustic measurements, violations of this ordinance. Violators may be served with an
ordinance citation, which shall be known as a “Noise Ticket”. Payment of the fine prescribed by
such Noise Ticket within the time specified thereon shall constitute a plea of nolo contendere and
shall save the violator harmless from prosecution for the offense cited. Fines shall be in the
amount of $99.00 for each day that the violation continues until the noise is abated. Nothing in
this ordinance shall prevent the Police Department, because of the perceived nature of the
offense, from proceeding not under the terms of this ordinance, but under the provisions of
Connecticut General Statutes §53a-181 and §53a-182, as they may be amended from time to
time, which sections relate, generally, to breach of peace, etc.

B. An appeal process relative to noise tickets is established below. The First Selectman shall
appoint a hearing officer who shall serve at his/her discretion to hear appeals from the issuance
of noise tickets. A Person shall have thirty (30) days from the date of the Noise Ticket to deliver
a written application for a hearing. The Hearing Officer shall hold a hearing within five days of
the receipt of the application, and his/her decision shall be final.

C. In those individual cases where the background noise levels caused by sources not subject to
this ordinance exceed the standards contained herein, a source shall be considered to cause
excessive noise if the noise emitted by such source exceeds the background noise level by 5
dBA, provided that no source subject to the provisions of Section A, Noise Levels, shall emit
noise in excess of 80 dBA at any time, and provided that this section does not decrease the
permissible levels of the other sections of this ordinance.

IX. VARIANCES

A. Any person living or doing business in East Lyme may apply to the Town’s Board of
Selectman for a variance from one (1) or more of the provisions of the ordinance which
are more stringent than the Connecticut Department of Environmental Protection
regulations for the control of noise, provided that the applicant supplied all of the
following information to the Board of Selectmen at least twenty (20) days prior to the
start of said activity. :

1. The location and nature of the activity.

2. The time period and hours of operation of said activity.

3. The nature and intensity of the noise that will be generated.
4. Any other information required by the Board of Selectmen.

B, No variance from these regulations shall be allowed unless it has been demonstrated that:
1. The proposed activity will not violate any provisions of the Connecticut
Department of Environmental Protection regulations.
2. The noise levels generated by the proposed activity will not constitute a danger to

the public health.
3. Compliance with the regulations constitutes an unreasonable hardship on the
applicant.
C. The application for variance shall be reviewed and either approved or rejected at least

five (5) days prior to the proposed start of said activity. The approval or rejection shall be
in writing and shall state the condition(s) of approval, if any, or the reason(s) for
rejection.

Dz. Failure to rule on the application in the designated time shall constitute approval of the
variance.
