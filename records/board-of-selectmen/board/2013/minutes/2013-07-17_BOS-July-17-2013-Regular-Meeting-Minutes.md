2c. Discussion — Samuel Smith House

Attorney Collins said that the Board has three options regarding the duties associated
with the Samuel Smith property and that these duties arise from the Historic Preservation
Easement and Connecticut law. The first option would be that the Town would monitor
the property as it does all town property and perform the duties outlined in the Easement
such as opening it for public viewing at least 12 days per year and publishing a notice in
the newspaper. She said that the second option would be to designate the property as a
historic property by following the procedures set forth in the Statute Section 7-147p and
Town Ordinance and give oversight to the Historic Properties Commission. This would
require reporting to the DECD and several hearings and publications and amending the
Town’s current ordinance. She noted that the third option would be to assign the duties to
any charitable organization or trust whose purpose is to maintain and preserve historic
properties. She added that the Selectmen could not assign responsibility to the Friends of
Samuel Smith since it was not yet a charitable organization.

MOTION (7)

Ms. Hardy moved the following:

RESOLVED, to request the Historic Properties Commission to conduct a study and submit a
report to the Department of Economic and Community Development, the Planning
Commission, the Zoning Commission and the First Selectman regarding the proposed
designation of the Samuel Smith House as a Historic Property and to hold a public hearing
thereon, all pursuant to Connecticut General Statutes Section 7-147p, et seq.

Seconded by Mr. Seery.

Atty. Collins noted that the Board of Selectmen are the owners of the property and could
reject the report when offered.

John O’Neill, Vice President of the East Lyme Historical Society said that the process for
forming a commission and adding a new property are the same and they are familiar with the
process.

Motion passed 6-0.

2d. Resolution — 18 Hill Road — Septic

Mr. Formica noted that this item had been added to the agenda for action so that the property
owners could address the problem. He said that sewage system on 18 Hill Road had collapsed
and they were proposing to utilize the unpaved and untraveled portion of the right-of-way in
front of the property to install a new leaching system. This same problem occurred in 1986 to
a neighboring property and Attorney Marrion’s letter along with Board of Selectmen meeting
minutes were included for the Board’s perusal to see how it was addressed in the past. He
said that the Director of Public Works had sent a memo noting he had reviewed the material
and that he supported the plan.
