2b.       Discussion and Possible Action - Parks & Rec / Damows Pond
DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson noted that Parks and Recreation Director David Putnam, as well as
Commission Chairman Tom Beebe and Member Todd Donovan were in attendance tonight. He stated that
he has met with the Town Attorney, and they found that there are some deed restrictions set in place as part
of a conservation easement to benefit the Friends of Oswegatchie Hills, who are the conservator, so they
will need to take a step back and review this matter a little further and bring this up again at a future
meeting. He suggested possibly giving 100 acres to Parks and Recreation versus the 300 acres that was
initially discussed. Mr. Putnam stated that there are no active fields planned, and that there are many types
of passive recreation activities that would be appropriate for that piece of land. The Commission is aware
of the conservation easement and understands the restrictions, and what they have planned would be
allowable within those restrictions. Mr. Todd Donovan heads up a newly created subcommittee of the
Parks and Recreation Commission, and he stated that they met for the first-time last Wednesday and that
they had good resident turnout. He reported that they spent some time suggesting possible uses for that land
and created a short survey that that plan to send out to residents to get more feedback on what types of
passive activities the residents would like to see and would be more likely to utilize. He stated that the
commission will continue to seek out land north of route 95 as the commission has few parks in that part of
town.

4a.      Ex-Officio Reports
         Mr. Salerno reported that the Planning Commission discussed creating a subcommittee to work on
the affordable housing needs of the town and provide a two-year plan to the State as directed. He noted that
the Commission currently does have an Affordable Housing Plan, so this committee will review that and
investigate future needs and solutions. Mr. Salerno reported thatLarry Fitzgeruld has recently been
appointed to the Planning Commission to fill the term of a member who resigned.
         Ms. Hardy reported that the Zoning Commission is meeting tomorrow night and they will have a
public hearing regarding proposed work at the building on Boston Post Road next to East Lyme Pizza. This
building was the former rectory for the church across the street, and most recently has served as a hair and
nail salon. They are proposing the demolition of the current structure and building a new building with
commercial zoned spaced on the first floor and five one-bedroom apartments on the second floor.
         Mr. Dagle reported that the Harbor Management Commission is working on the five-year shellfish
lease renewal to T.J. Londergan for his scallop farm in the bay. They would like to make changes to such
lease and would like to send that along to the Town Attorney for review, and Mr. Nickerson stated that yes
it would be appropriate for the Town Attorney to review said lease, and the Town Attorney should
determine if it is the Harbor Management Commission's authority to issue such leases or is it the Board of
Selectmen's authority. Mr. Dagle reported that they continue to wait for the outcome of the First
Selectman's discussions with Waterford on the town boundary line dispute in the river. He stated that they
are pursuing habitat restoration opportunities in the Pattaganseff Estuary area and will seek funding for that
project. Mr. Dagle reported that they will begin reviewing the Shellfish Management Plan, which is due to
be reviewed and updated; this will be a lengthy process and will involve first the Board of Selectmen
approval and then State approval.

4b.      First Selectman's Report
         Mr. Nickerson reported that the Senior Center is in receipt of their new bus; it is now being
registered with the DMV and will be on the road and in use shortly. He reported that the Starlight Motel
and the Mobil station have both been purchased by the State of Connecticut via eminent domain and that
the project is moving along, with both facilities closing in a month. Mr. Nickerson stated that there are
some permanent residents that may be displaced and in need of assistance in a month from now, and the
Town will be obligated to assist them should that need arise; he will pursue assistance from the State for
such expenses should this happen.


5.       Public Comment
         Ms. Anneliese Lapides, 39 Romagna Road, Niantic, introduced herself as the Director of
Community Engagement for the Southeastern Connecticut Organization for Racial Equity (SCORE). Ms.
Lapides stated that she is a medical student at the University of Vermont and that one of the most important
concepts that she has learned so far in her education is not related directly to biology or how the body
works, but rather how social determent affects one's health. Ms. Lapides stated that they have been
gathering signatures from members of the community and currently have over 600, as well as letters of
support including a letter from the Ledge Light Health District calling for this discussion to be a priority in
the community. Ms. Lapides requested that the Board of Selectmen put this matter on a future agenda so
that they may present their information and they have a resolution that they would ask that the Board
support declaring racism a public health crisis.
