PRESENT: Mark Nickerson, Kevin Seery, Marc Salerno, Rose Ann Hardy, Paul Dagle and Dan Cunningham
ALSO PRESENT: John Holtz, President; Bruce Cohen, Treasurer; and Lindsay Rush, Secretary of the East
Lyme Giving Garden,

Mr. Nickerson called the meeting to order at7:01p,m. and welcomed the members of the East Lyme Giving
Garden.

Discussion on possible use of American Rescue Plan funds - East Lyme Giving Garden
         Mr. John Holtz stated that he is President of the East Lyme Giving Garden, and introduced Bruce
Cohen, Treasurer, and Lindsay Rush, Secretary, who were also in attendance. Mr, Holtz presented a
PowerPoint presentation, which is attached hereto as Exhibit l. He stated that the Mission of the East Lyme
Giving Garden is to provide sustainably grown produce to those in need in our community, to make a
meaningful impact on local food insecurity. The Garden also provides education on healthy eating and food
preparation, sound growing practices, and fosters community engagement. The Garden promotes preservation
of our region's open spaces while using sustainable growing methods focusing on regenerative soil practices.
We value the diversity within our community and want to provide a common and welcoming space for all. He
stated that they plan to provide locally grown, chemical free food to members of our community that are in
need; and he noted that there has been an increased urgency at our local food pantries, so they accelerated their
plans and were able to help out this past summer by providing 700 lbs. of produce to the Shoreline Soup
Kitchen and Pantry. Going forward they will be partnering with St. John's Church, the Shoreline Soup Kitchen
and Pantry, and the Gemma E. Moran United Way/Labor Food Bank to provide locally grown fruits and
vegetables to help feed our community, and Mr. Nickerson noted that they should also reach out to Care and
Share. The East Lyme Giving Garden was established in 2020 with a plot of land donated by and located
behind Samuel Smith Homestead. In 2021 they received 4.5 acres on Church Lane and in partnership with
Flanders Baptist & Community Church are preparing the land for planting. Mr. Holtz stated that in the past
month they have been designated at 501(c)(3) and that they have partnered with the Eastern CT Community
Garden Association. They have enjoyed a productive partnership with numerous community agencies, raising
more than $50,000 to date. They hope to partner with the schools and the library and stated that they have
already developed strong community relationships and strong community support and is an organization that
the Town can trust and depend on. Mr. Holtz stated that their needs in order of priority are tools, equipment
sheds, a high tunnel and a pavilion. The operating budget for East Lyme Giving Garden is between $5,000-
$7,000 annually, 100% of the food that is grown will be donated.

Mr. Nickerson and the entire Board thanked Mr. Holtz and the whole group of volunteers for a very organized
and suooessful launch of the East Lyme Giving Garden and pledged his full support to help this group continue
to get offto a strong start.
