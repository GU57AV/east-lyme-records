Dear Selectmen


On behalf of our membership, the Board of Directors of the Pattagansett Lake Association (PLA) are
expressing our support for the efforts of The East Lyme Land Trust (ELLT) in acquiring the tract of land
referred to as the Hathaway property.

This parcel of approximately 120 acres borders the northwestern portion of Pattagansett Lake, including
approximately 3,000 feet of shoreline. This property has been rated as the highest priority for
acquisition of any undeveloped property in the Town of East Lyme in the town's Plan of Conservation
and Development. As already noted by many people in our town, preserving this property is seen as
essential for protecting our town's drinking water supply. As you know, Pattagansett Lake lies within one
of the aquifers providing public drinking water to town residents and businesses. The Hathaway
property also contains wetlands and a prized mature forested area, which is part of a much larger forest
block having many positive ecological attributes.

We wish to emphasize that protecting this land also serves to protect Pattagansett Lake itself. lf this
property were to be developed for housing, which reportedly would be as many as 70 homes, this would
result in significant additional inputs of nutrients into the lake through groundwater septic discharges
and lawn fertilizer usage. lncreased nutrient input to water bodies results in increased growth of aquatic
plants and algae and furthers the degradation of a lake's trophic level. For many years the PLA and its
members, many of whom are lakeside residents, have been faced with the issue of controlling aquatic
weed overgrowth, which limits lake usage and recreational activities.

Pattagansett Lake is a significantly important natural feature within East Lyme and its publicly accessible
boat ramp provides many town residents and visitors the opportunity to engage in many types of water-
related recreational activities. The lake itself provides an environment for many types of wildlife, both
fully aquatic and terrestrial. The Fisheries Division of the Connecticut Department of Environmental
Protection manages Pattagansett Lake as a Trophy Bass Lake, one of the few in the state receiving that
distinction. Degradation of the lake's water quality would most likely result in fewer human recreational
opportunities, loss of some natural wildlife habitat, and reduction in the value of lakeside homes.

Given the importance of the lake itself and its placement within one of our drinking water aquifers the
PLA hereby urges the Board of Selectmen to assist the ELLT financially and by any other means as
necessary so that the Hathaway property can be acquired to become a significantly important open
space for the Town of East Lyme.
