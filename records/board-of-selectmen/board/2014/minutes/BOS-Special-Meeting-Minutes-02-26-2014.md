3. Presentation — Boardwalk

Mr. Formica introduced Rachel Burckhardt of Parsons Brindkerhoff and John Ramsey of
Applied Coastal who are the consultants hired to review and design a boardwalk that
would sustain a 100 year storm. He noted that the funding for this project was
accumulated from two insurance settlements from hurricanes; $1.5 million from Irene
settlement and $1.151 from Sandy settlement, a $500,000 STEAP Grant and a settlement
from the original builder of the boardwalk in the amount of $1.6 million dollars because
when originally built, it was supposed to sustain a 25 year storm and it did not, and
$203,000 from FEMA. He noted that this totaled $4.9 million dollars which was set
aside and that $500,000 had already been appropriated. He said that approximately
$20,000 had been spent of the $500,000 on the design, leaving approximately $4.4
million to appropriate. He said that we had received 12 bids ranging from $4.388 million
to $8,353,000. He noted that an East Lyme contractor had won the bid at $4.388, 499 and
therefore, we could build within our budget. He noted that approximately $200,000
would be needed for Amtrak traffic and safety personnel. Please note Attachment A.

Mr. Ramsey reviewed his 100 year design with the Board noting that the walking surface
would be of concrete, and that sheet pilings would be utilized. He showed pictures of the
original Boardwalk pre Irene, post Irene and post Sandy. He said that his design is similar
to Amtrak’s,

Deputy Director of Public Works Bill Scheer said that since Hole in the Wall was
complete, they plan on letting the contractor utilize the upper end of McCook’s parking
lot and the pavilion will be blocked off. He said that we have all the permits necessary
and that the town’s portion of the boardwalk will be of 10’ concrete sidewalk fenced on
both sides. He said that Amtrak’s revetment along our part of the Boardwalk was
East Lyme Board of Selectmen Special Meeting of February 26, 2014

compromised during the storms and that rocks have washed down. He said that we are
trying to get Amtrak to pay for their revetment and for flagmen which would total
approximately $400,000.

There was some discussion about Amtrak’s revetment and the necessity to fix it. Mr.
Formica noted that he was in negotiations with Amtrak at this time and that Congressman
Courtney had viewed the area and was talking to Amtrak also. He said that negotiations
will continue.

Ms. Cheeseman inquired what would happen if Amtrak refused to fix their revetment.

Mr. Ramsey replied that there is no issue for small storms, but there would be an issue
when we experience a big storm. He stressed that their revetment would be destabilized,
not ours. He said the revetment could be fixed now or later.

Ms. Cheeseman asked if there were other examples of steel sheeting being used in this
manner to withstand 100 year storms.

Mr. Ramsey replied that it is utilized like this, all over the world.

Town Building Chairman Ray O’Connor noted that since there were no utilities in the
area, change orders should be at a minimum. He added that if Amtrak comes through
with the cost of revetment repair and the cost of the flagmen we would be golden. He said
that we would need to make a motion to assign the Town Building Committee to oversee
the construction. He also added that the Committee was short one member.

8. Budget Reviews:

Police:

Sgt. Resident Trooper Blanchette was present and reviewed his budget, noting that
presently, he was down three officers. He commented that a good deal of his budget line
increases were contractual.

Mr. Formica noted that there were a number of bills for a public hearing at the State
regarding the percentage of what the resident trooper towns pay. He said in the past we
paid 70% of the fringe benefits and now, we are being assessed for 100% of the fringe.
He said part of the additional money is being utilized for the unfunded pensions and they
believe that this is an unfair burden to the towns that have resident trooper programs.

Mr. Wilson commented that he was not sure that there was enough money in the overtime
line.

Ms. Johnson replied it was in the Public Works budget.

Parks and Recreation:

Director Dave Putnam reviewed his budget. He noted that line 412 Part time Seasonal
Labor was to staff Cini Park on a daily basis where this year, it was only staffed on
weekends. He said it would cost approximately $35,000 of additional funding to staff the
full week with the minimum wage increase. He noted that private beach associations are
charged for the equipment and operator when they groom the beach.

what had changed.
