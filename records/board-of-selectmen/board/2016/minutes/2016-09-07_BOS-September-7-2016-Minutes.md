1c. Delegations EAST LYME TOWN CLERK
Ron Rando of 194 Boston Post Road suggested that the SCRRRA item be postponed to enable
the public to review the contract. He added that there is a very bad drug problem in town and a
good friend of his had been threatened for calling the police. He suggested more police action
was needed and possibly use of cameras would help. He inquired about a lawsuit the town had
about a woman who bought a piece of land in town and then was told she couldn’t build on it.
She won in court and now he believes there is an appeal and he would like to know the money
this has cost. He said that he was on the Board of Selectmen when the first SCRRRA contract
was signed and after 25 years of taking trash there, the town still does not own anything and has
no recourse if they shut down. He added that his daughter had a fire in her house and because of
town personnel; it is taking forever to rebuild.

Mike Schulz of Lovers Lane reviewed a May 6, 2016 news clipping regarding Senate Bill 202
which would allow the municipal allowance for military service to receive up to $15,000 in tax
exemption. He asked that this be checked to see if it passed and to see if the Assessor would
apply it. He added that the drug activity in town is significant and if anyone had been held
accountable for the death of Holly Lombardi.

John Drabik of 18 Drabik Road noted that Mr. Rando had inquired about the boat 3 times and

Deputy Director of Public Works Bill Scheer noted that the State required this resolution from

2d. Special Appropriation — Brownfields

Mr. Scheer noted that the Town of East Lyme purchased the property at 224 Main Street after
completing Phase I and Phase II environmental studies. The area under the existing building and
a magnetic anomaly were not completely investigated. The $200,000 Brownfield assessment
grant will fund the removal of the building and an environmental investigation of the areas in
question to determine if any remediation is required. The grant will also fund preparation of a
plan to perform remediation if necessary. The State of Connecticut has approved Weston and
Sampson Inc. Engineers to assist the Town during this process.

MOTION (5)

Mr. Seery moved to appropriate a $200,000 Brownfield Assessment grant to complete an
environmental assessment of 224 Main Street, remove the building as part of this assessment,
and to develop plans and cost estimates associated with any remediation necessary and the
ultimate reuse of the property.

5a. Ex-Officio

Mr. Cunningham noted that there would be an upcoming bicycle race to benefit the Samuel
Smith House, the Smith Harris House and the Historical Society on October 1, 2016. He added
that he attended the Waterford East Lyme Shellfish meeting and there were some questions about
the potential dredging of the river in the future and where they would dump the dredged material.

Ms. Cheeseman noted that she attended the Zoning meeting and there were two items that went
to public hearing: proposed text amendment to no longer make it necessary for repeat applicants
to erect a sign noticing an outdoor dining permit was being sought. This was approved. The
second public hearing was for a text amendment for bees to be added to the section regarding
animals being raised for agricultural purposes. This was withdrawn and new verbiage would be
created for a better way to allow the keeping of bees in East Lyme.

Mr. Salerno noted that there was no Harbor Management meeting due to a lack of quorum. He
said he attended the special meeting of the Inland Wetlands where they discussed East Society
Road and the millings that the Town had placed on an unpaved area. He added that these
millings had washed out and the town had fixed the area. He also attended two elementary
design meetings for the elementary schools and added that they do plan to meet next June’s
deadline.

Mr. Seery attended the Planning meeting where there were no action items. There was an
alternate appointment for Mr. Arias’s vacated position. He added that Mr. Goeschel offered an
update on JLUS. He said that the Parks and Recreation department has been extremely busy this
summer and parking capacity was reached on July 4"" and on Celebrate East Lyme Day. He
noted that the band shell would be relocated adjacent to the pavilion.

5. First Selectman’s Report

Mr. Nickerson noted that the Town has received and successfully defended many FOI
complaints at a huge financial burden to the taxpayer. He added that the Town prevailed on the
Election complaint. He said that he took a boat ride with the new Port Authority Commissioner
Scott Bates. He added that Friday night had been the last night for Music feeding Music at
Protek. He said that over a ten year period, they had raised approximately $22,000 in donations
which they had given to area school music programs. He said that the Parks and Recreation
department is hosting Movie Nights on Friday nights at McCooks. He noted that there had been a
meeting with him, Montville and Waterford First Selectmen, and Corrigan personnel regarding a
dog pound. He commented that the old police boat had been sold. It had a hole in the bottom of it
and had been posted in the area marinas for sale. One of the marinas had offered $5000 and a
gentleman from Stonington offered $8500 and it was sold to him. He agreed that there is a big
drug problem and that he was working with the schools, police and emergency services to make
a difference. He noted that a parcel of land was sold at tax auction which had been identified as
open space. The buyer has been trying to build on this parcel, even though when she purchased
it, she was informed it was not a building lot. He said that the Town has lost the first court case
and the town is appealing. He said that this is very important to the town because it would be
precedent setting.

Mr. Salerno said that Romagna Way looks good and Ms. Cheeseman added that the Children’s
Museum is still looking for accommodations.

Southeastern Connecticut Regional Resources Recovery
Authority (SCRRRA)
Solid Waste Disposal Plan for Southeastern Connecticut

Extending its membership with SCRRRA provides the Town of East Lyme with
these Benefits:

1. Price Stability:

SCRRRA has provided its member communities with a stable tipping fee at or below $60 for 16
consecutive years and currently enjoys the most favorable rate in the state. By extending the MSA East
Lyme will continue to receive the benefit of SCRRRA’ subsidized rate. Additionally, East Lyme will
continue to have a local place to deliver its garbage utilizing its existing delivery resources.

2. Value of services (at no additional cost):
In the last year SCRRRA has provided services to the Town of East Lyme with the following direct
economic benefit:
Transfer Station Support - $49,293 Hazardous Household Waste Collection - $18,349
Wood Grinding Service - $35,765 Municipal Hazardous Waste Collection-$ 2,130

3. Regionalized Benefits:

Removed from the waste stream and included in the tip fee:

Grinding of green waste, producing mulch for public works departments and citizenry.

Annual hazardous material collection for residents.

Semi-Annual hazardous material collection for municipal government operations and schools.

Removal and processing of tires, recycling of fluorescent bulbs, Freon, oil, oil filters, oily debris,

antifreeze, propane tanks, mattresses, paint and electronics.

In addition: Programs for schools and youth organizations that focus on recycling, marine debris

and environmental stewardship.

Recycling program coordination, negotiation, at cost sale of composting equipment.

Clear Stream Recycling Containers for events.

Representation of the Southeast region at the State Legislature, Department of Energy &

Environment.

>» Assisting towns in meeting requirement of the State Comprehensive Material Management
Strategy requirements.

>» SCRRRA fulfills town’s statutory responsibility for municipal solid waste planning (22a-227).
