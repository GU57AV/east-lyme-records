2a. Presentation/Discussion — Youth Services — Results of 2015 Youth Survey ~ Drugs and Alcohol
Dave Putnam, Parks & Recreation and Youth Services Director introduced Michelle Devine, Director SERAC
and Christine Miskell, Epidemiologist at SERAC to present the PowerPoint presentation — East Lyme Youth
Survey 2015 Results. (Attached)

Ms. Devine said that they are working with the East Lyme community as there are different parts of the
community that do different things.

Mr. Putnam said that one of the things tht came from this is that 1 in 9 students had contemplated suicide —
that jumped out at them and they will be working on this with the Daigle Foundation.

Mr. Nickerson noted that Superintendent Newton and Jaime Barr Shelbume were also present from the
school.

Ms. Hardy asked if there was a block on this so that a student could only answer once.
Ms. Miskell said that it would be difficult for them to do it twice as it is very lengthy.

Mr. Nickerson asked what else jumped out at them from this survey for East Lyme.
Ms. Devine said that they have a strong concern around marijuana use. They have also heard that the rates
of use of prescription drugs are getting higher in both adults and children.

Mr. Cunningham said that he gets the sense that prescription drug use has a connection to heroin use.
Ms. Devine said that they could say that — among other things.

Mr. Seery said, from his former role as a police officer, that it is a chemical dependency and that the cost of
heroin is less than that of prescription drugs. He added that parental involvement is key and that they must
talk with their kids.

Ms. Devine said that there is a difference within Towns as to why they use drugs and that it varies. She
added that they work with the whole community such as the dentists who prescribe drugs and the doctors
who also do for sports injuries.

Superintendent Newton said that they have a part-time Alcohol and Drug counselor at the High School this
year and this is a great connection. There is also a mental health side to this and a call center for students in
crises and it gets used regularly.

Mr. Salerno thanked them for the presentation and asked about the low percentage of people who talk to a
teacher and if that includes a guidance counselor.

Ms. Devine said that it relates to how they perceive a teacher and a guidance counselor can be in that
category. She added that this perception is consistent for the region and the Towns.

Mr. Cunningham asked about the connection with energy drinks.
Ms. Devine said that is due to the amount of caffeine in the drinks and the effect on a younger person. They
also mix the drinks with alcohol.

Mr. Nickerson thanked them for the presentation.

a. Discussion - Blight Ordinance

Mr. Salerno said that they have a draft that has been updated to all of the latest Statutes. This would affect
public and private property and most of the language came from the CT General Statutes. Iffwhen approved
they would have to assign a Blight Enforcement Officer, a Blight Hearing Citation Officer (who cannot be a
policeman or anyone in conflict with the property) and a Lien Officer (in the vent that liens are levied). He

noted that the other thing not in the ordinance at present is the option for the Town to clean up and then lien
the property. He said that they could put a provision in for that. He also noted that the Statute could change
again as it was going to be changed in the last session but was not however that does not mean that it won't
be changed in this session.

MEMORANDUM OF UNDERSTANDING
‘BETWEEN THE TOWNS OF WATERFORD, EAST LYME
AND MONTVILLE, CONNECTICUT
FOR REGIONAL ANIMAL CONTROL FACILITY

WHEREAS, Connecticut General Statute §7-148cc authorizes two or more
municipalities to jointly perform any function that each municipality may perform
separately under any provisions of the general statutes or any special act, charter, or
home rule ordinance upon entering into an agreement to such effect with other
municipalities;

WHEREAS, C.G.S §7-148cc requires that any such agreement be approved by each
participating municipality i in the same manner as ordinances are approved or, if no
ordinances are approved, in the same manner as the budget is approved;

WHEREAS, the Towns of Waterford, East Lyme and Montville require a new Animal
Control Facility;

WHEREAS, the Chief Executives of all three Towns have discussed regionalizing these
services;

WHEREAS, a regional animal facility that will serve the three communities has been
proposed to be built on state property on Route 32 in Montville with the assistance,
labor and cooperation of the Department of Corrections of the State of Connecticut;

WHEREAS, the municipalities recognize that regionalizing services may result in
equally divided cost savings, along with the future cost savings of utilizing labor funded
through the Department of Corrections and the use of existing state land;

WHEREAS, the municipalities named seek to enhance existing regional animal control
services and facilities with the assistance of State correctional inmates working with

Town personnel;

NOW THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual promises and covenants herein
contained, the parties agree as follows:

PURPOSE OF THIS AGREEMENT

To establish a Working Committee with staff representatives from all three
communities to present findings, recommendations and comments to each Chief
Executive for such a regional animal shelter by September 1, 2016;

Each Chief Executive will appoint a committee staff member to represent each

respective municipality;

Working Committee Members will be responsible to convene as a committee,
initiate the proposed project, and report their initial findings and
recommendations to each Chief Executive;

The Committee will initially focus on:

The design, budget and logistics of designing a new regional animal
shelter;

Labor, including donated labor by the State Department of
Corrections;

Budget of future capital and operating expenses;

Breakout of cost share formula for each municipality;
Leasehold/license with the State of Connecticut for the location of
the improvements.

The Working Committee’s recommendations will be advisory, and will not be binding on
any municipality. Within 60 days of receipt of the Committee recommendations, each
town will consider whether it desires to implement them. If all three towns determine
that the recommendations (or any amendments or modifications thereto) are
acceptable, each town shall initiate its applicable appropriation process, and also enter
into a mutually agreeable interlocal agreement with the other towns in accordance with

State and local law.
