4c. Non-Affiliated Group

DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that two members of the Board of Selectmen will be directed to meet

4d. Fishing Dock Leases

DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that the Town owns four docks at Cini Park which are leased out, and
the leases are up for renewal on December 31, 2018. He stated that the State has indicated that they do not
like the idea of having one of the docks directly under the bridge, so we may end up with only three docks
depending on how the conversation with the DOT goes. He also noted that parking will be greatly reduced
during the State’s project to re-paint the bridge, at which time they will be securing off a good deal of the
lower parking area down by the docks. One of the leases, the Blackhawk, has written into his individual
lease the right to renew, so potentially there will only be two docks available and up for lease at the end of
the year. He noted that in attendance at tonight’s meeting are two of the lessee’s, Greg Dubrule from the
Blackhawk, and Michael Stepski of the Tartan II. Mr. Nickerson stated that there is some interest in the

5b. First Selectman’s Report

Mr. Nickerson reported that Election Day went well yesterday, with the polling locations running

As a kid growing up on the Niantic river | knew at a very young age | wanted to fish for a living. At the
age of 14 | bought an old lobster boat and spent two years rebuilding it into fine combination
lobster\dragger. That same year | joined the NBCFA. The NBCFA was formed with the main purpose of
pursuing a town dock on the old swing bridge span as a place for the areas many fishermen to tie up
their boats, At that time, we had over 20 members both full time and part time fishermen that were
having a hard time finding permanent dockage to grow their business. Many of the area’s marinas don’t
allow commercial boats. Similar town fishing docks had been put in Stonington and Old Saybrook and
there was a pretty desperate need for them in Niantic.

At about the same time the Cini park area became available for sale which is also when the boardwalk
was in its initial phases. | think its important to note that our association persuaded the town to buy the
land to coincide with the boardwalk planning, and lease us the dockage where a few of our members
were already tied. | was fishing full time by then and needing a dock the town let me build my own dock
where | am today.

Some years later a state grant was awarded to rebuild the docks that were there. Unfortunately, much
of the money was used up in the planning so instead of one long dock connecting mine with the
Blackhawk they just replaced what was there which was 4 separate docks. Our original intent was to
build finger piers off the old bridge abutment were the kayak dock is today all the way to the end. We
had blue prints for a fixed dock at the end and even floating docks inside the lagoon. | have the
blueprints with me.

Due to the lobster die off in the late 90s early 00s our association membership declined and the need
for more docks wasn’t as strong, so we didn’t keep pushing for docks on the old bridge span. | got out of
lobstering and more into fishing. | bought another boat a larger boat and permit which | turned into an
offshore monkfish gillnetter for fishing in the winter months. | did much of the work on the boat in
Niantic and was encouraged to unload there, however for trucking and shipping reasons we did much of
the offloading at Capt. Scotts in New London. | always kept Niantic as home base and did much of the
gear work and net work here to keep the docks as active as possible. Over the years I’ve also run other
monkfish boats which we would occasionally bring into Niantic to do gear work as well, Mike Beebe was
nice enough to let me pull the last one into his dock for a short time off season a couple years ago to do
some hydraulic work before setting up at New London seafood for the fishing season. I’ve always kept
my smaller green boat the Phyllis Anne going working off a mooring in the river as well as off the dock.

Now fishing for a living has always been extremely difficult in many different ways, over regulation hit
me pretty hard on the amount of days we could fish and amount we could catch to the extent | got out
of offshore monk fishing and my wife and | decided to purchase an old wooden charter boat which we
named the Tartanll after my fathers charter boat that sailed out of Niantic in the 70s. We almost named
it the Niantic. Traditionally chartering and commercial fishing have always gone hand in hand. | started
working on the local charter boats when | was 12 which is how | was able to buy my first lobster boat so
young. The real value of this boat to me was while it was still fairly small as head boats go it has a CG
certificate to carry more than 6 passengers. While the odds were against us, we pulled it off after 5
years | can say we have built a successful charter/party business! However, we could not have done it
without having this dock. The harbor of Niantic has become a fishing destination. Myself, Greg, JB, the
mijoy and sunbeam all spend a lot of money advertising to bring people into town. Many of our
customers after finding out what a family friendly town this is come back with their families to spend the
day in town spending money at all the other businesses. We sailed almost every day from Memorial Day
to veteran’s day. That’s a lot of people we bring into town.

We've always tried to keep the docks as community oriented as possible we sell fish there, we teach
people about fishing, constantly answering questions from people walking by. All of us have let other
fishermen in the area use the docks to load or unload their traps, bait etc. A couple of local guys, the
Meek brothers were trying to get going fishing, both Greg and | let them tie up for a while when they
were getting their boat going. That’s exactly what a town fishing dock is for to support small businesses.
If you’ve ever stopped at any other fishing docks from Stonington to Pt. Judith, there’s constantly boats
tied 2-3 deep doing all sorts of repairs. We just have a smaller version of that.

This dock has been a huge asset to my business and my family. My wife Beth and my 3 young children
are all very much involved in our business. From painting to bookkeeping and everything else involved in
running a small business. We also employed 2 high school boys and another Niantic man just this year.
We all work together very well. The main point is | am using the docks for their original intent. However
after the lobster collapse some of the guys were forced to find new work and a couple of the boats
weren't being used much. One in particular, however that boat has just been bought by Matt Dobratz
who is trying to make a living fishing. | know he would love to stay there, and | know there are other
people that would like to come in. So its not the action but rather the inaction at the docks which have a
few people questioning things. Starting another association has been discussed as there is some new
blood around. Timmy the oyster farmer has had some very good ideas. Originally a cooperative was part
of the vision. There once was a fish market and boat rental place there. Don’t forget the original grant
was awarded because low income businesses were present there. We could apply for another one to
finish the docks as originally planned and make room for more boats. There is still an open area were
Craig Engelman tied up, so there is room. Stonington applies every year and they are regularly upgrading
the docks.

Now | know my boat has to move over for the bridge work planned ahead. Ideally | hope we can all
move over one spot, but my business does depend on this dock. | didn’t even buy the boat until | had
talked to Paul Formica then the first selectman about operating it out of there. | can’t stress enough how
important docking there is to my family’s business. Without it | would literally be out of business and all
the work we started so many years ago will have been wasted. I’m the last original member of the
NBCFA that still fishes for a living out of here. | do all the weedwacking and litter pickup near my area.
When the wall collapsed | fixed it. | never complain about anything and don’t forget we don’t have water
or power down there. A working waterfront is the true character of Niantic. The very first house on
main st was built by a fisherman. There are many people who appreciate that.
