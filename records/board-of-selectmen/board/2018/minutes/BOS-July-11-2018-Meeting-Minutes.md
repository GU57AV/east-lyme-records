2a. Review and possible action — proposed ordinances

DISCUSSION: Attorney O’Connell reported that the previously discussed changes were made to this
ordinance. Summary of updates are as follows: section 2b Penalties was amended to $50.00; section 4
Appeals, the condensed version is being inserted; section 6 Towing, the condensed version Option 2 will be
used; added section 5 regarding snow emergencies; and per Chief Finkelstein, we will utilize the CT
General Statute for handicap parking consistent with surrounding towns. There was discussion about
raising the time limit to 28 days, but it was decided that 14 days was the best choice. All Selectmen were in
agreement with this revised document. Mr. Cunningham noted that trash pickup day on Main Street is
hectic. Mr. Nickerson noted that Main Street is the only road that the guys get out of the truck to move the
cans into place. Although it requires more manpower and time, the Public Works guys do a great job of
taking care of it, There was no discussion on the Ordinance to Repeal the Ordinance Establishing Fines for
Illegal Parking.

4, Communications

Mr. Nickerson acknowledged receipt of resignations from Al Littlefield, Board of Education, and Joanie
DiMartino, Smith-Harris House; and thanked both for all of their hard work in their respective roles. He
stated that given to each member of the Board in their respective folder is a draft copy of the proposed
Purchasing Plan for review; received from the subcommittee through Selectman Salerno.

5. Information and Reports
There were none.

6a. Mrs. Hardy reported on the Commission on Aging, and stated that the focus of their discussion was
contemplating the current transportation issues that they face, which is primarily that they have more need
for the driving services than they have resources to get everyone where they need to be. Primary need is for
additional drivers, but an additional van is also needed; the town may be eligible for another grant in a
couple of years. Mr. Nickerson stated that the employees are doing a great job with the limited resources
that they currently have to work with.

Mr. Cunningham reported on Historic Properties, and stated that they are doing a lot of advertising
and public relations for this year’s Bike & BBQ. The current exhibit at the Thomas Lee House is of “The
Turtle”, considered to be the first attack submarine.

Mr. Dagle reported that the Twin Valley Lakes matter was postponed at one meeting, and that he
was not able to attend the second meeting.

6b. Mr. Nickerson reported that Celebrate East Lyme is coming up on Saturday, July 21“ beginning at
2pm and ending with fireworks at 9 p.m. At 10:00 a.m. that morning, there will be a ribbon cutting for the
Main Street Park, which will then be open and available to the public for Celebrate East Lyme and beyond;
it is amazing to see this project finally come to fruition after decades of discussion and planning. Regarding
the recent vandalism of some of the benches on the Boardwalk, he reported that there is now a $2,000.00
reward for any information given that leads to the people who caused this damage. You can contact the
police department or the First Selectman’s office with information. Also available if a person wishes to
remain anonymous is the Tip411 application, but remember if you remain anonymous you will not be able
to collect the reward. He reported that a brand new, state of the art camera system is being installed which
will allow our dispatch center to monitor from Cini Park, down the boardwalk and as far over as McCook’s
24 hours a day, 7 days a week. This new system is being generously funded by the East Lyme Public Trust.
He stated that if anyone is interested in purchasing a plaque or a memorial bench that they should contact
the EL Public Trust directly.

7. Public Comment

Ms. Holly Cheeseman, State Representative stated that she arrived late because she attended a very exciting
baseball game between East Lyme and Mystic; Mystic won tonight’s game, which was game one of a 3
game series. She reported that Operation Fuel is available to assist residents with their electric bill all year,
not just winter time. If you are struggling to pay your electric bill, please contact 2-1-1 for more
information and to see if you are eligible for up to $500 in assistance. Regarding the proposed parking
ordinance, she is concerned about the two hour parking limit, specifically in the area of the Children’s
Museum; two hours is not a lot of time if a parent is inside the museum with children. She will plan to
attend the public hearing for further discussion and with possible options.

8. Selectman’s Response

Mr. Nickerson stated that the Parking Ordinance only addresses the fees associated with illegal parking. He
feels that the concerns that Ms. Cheeseman has for parking in that area can be addressed and resolved with
proper signage in that area allowing for more than two hours, Signage issues would be addressed by the
Town’s traffic authority, which is the Police Commission.
