NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN, of a public hearing to be held by the East Lyme Board of Selectmen on

DEMOLITION DELAY ORDINANCE

1, Pursuant to Connecticut General Statutes Sec, 29-406, there shall be a 90-day waiting period before
a permit to demolish any structure located in the Town of East Lyme may be issued. The 90-day
waiting period shall commence upon the filing of a demolition permit application with the East
Lyme Building Department.

2. The applicant for a demolition permit must install, within 7 business days of submitting the
‘ application, a sign provided by the building department, upon submission of a $200.00 deposit, on
the front wall of the structure, or such other location as directed by the Building Official. The sign
must remain in place until such time that the waiting period has expired.

3. Within 7 days of the filing of the application, the Building Official shall determine if the structure
proposed for demolition is included in the East Lyme Historic Property Inventory, as it may be
amended from time to time. If the structure is included in the Inventory, the Building Official shall
notify the Historic Properties Commission in writing within 7 business days of such determination.
If the structure is not included in the Inventory, the Building Official may issue the permit
forthwith.

4. Upon notification as set forth above, the Commission shall review the application at its next
scheduled meeting. The Commission may waive the balance of the waiting period or may

e Thank you for this opportunity to offer this brief presentation tonight. My
name is Joanie DiMartino, and I’m the director of the Smith-Harris House.

« What we are asking for today requires NO money on the part of the Town.

¢ The Smith-Harris House Commission and Friends BoD has, with the
Town’s prior approval back in 2015, completed a two-year intensive
professional development program called STEPS-CT, or Standards and
Excellence in History Programs, managed by the CLHO & CTHumanities.

¢ Through STEPS we were able to obtain a grant to create our first Strategic
Plan, and hired museum consultant Kathy Craughwell-Varda to oversee and
lead us through the process.

e We met jointly over a period of 5 months, which included visits to and
participation by, the ELHS, the HPC, and Sam Smith House, for intensive
discussions on potential new directions for the Smith-Harris House and site.

© One challenge that came to the forefront repeatedly is the site name. Our
current name, Smith-Hartis House, does not fully embrace who we are, the
history of the site, or who we want to be to our community in the future.

© The name is ill-fitting: it recognizes the final residents of the House, yet the
house is decorated to the time period of the original residents. There is
considerable confusion between our site, the Sam Smith House, and even
Smith Acres, and we have noticed this with visitors, users of social media,
and even our professional peers.

e Names are important, I spent today just outside Hartford at the SHPO
Annual Conference, where one of the featured speakers stated that, “A name
is a story. Places have names for a reason, and without those stories
something is lost.” And } found that sentiment today speaking to exactly
why we are here tonight.

After intensive internal discussion with the Commission and Friends Board,
as well as input from two peer consultants, our colleagues at CT Humanities
and the First Selectman, we are formally, respectfully, requesting to
officially change our name to: “Brookside Farm Museum” with the tag le,
“Cultivating the Story: Avery, Smith and Harris, 1845-1955.”

This new re-branding, which will later include a new, refreshed logo and
changing exhibits in the museum, allows us to better “tell our story,” to
share the full history of the site, from the building of the House in 1845
through wars, depressions, changes in industry, agriculture, and social
movements to 1955.

The site itself was once known as “Brookside Farm” or “Brookside Dairies,”
during the Smith and Harris ownership, and we have been able to trace the
name to when the Avery family owned parcels of land alongside Robbin’s
Brook, now known as the Pattaganset River.

[t is felt that returning to this name best reflects the true and complete
history of the site, and we are still honoring the families that resided in the
house by encapsulating all three in our tag line, as well as acknowledging
our agricultural history with the strong verb to “cultivate.” as we continue to
encourage new stories and new histories through research, exhibits.
preservation, and programs that are relevant to the public and residents of
East Lyme.

We have already been informed by CTHumanities that there is further grant
money to fund hinng a local firm to create a new logo, new signage, and
redecorating the inside of the House to offer changing exhibits that will draw
new and returning visitors, and the Friends Board is committed to seeking
out funds to continue this project.

In our research, we, to our pleasant surprise, have found that our history as a
museum has come full circle: [ recently found in the papers of Smith-Flarris
House Founder and long-time Commission member Barbara Odgers, a paper
dated October 19, 1978, where she states, “The Smith-Harris Commission
will complete their outline of a 5-year plan to return the 1840 farmhouse to a
working farm.”

¢ While we don’t have the staff or resources to return us completely to a
“working farm,” it is nice to know that in reconnecting to our agricultural
history we are embracing the work, direction, and intent of the original
Commission.

e As I learned today at the SHPO conference, places have names that are
important to the history and heritage of a community. We want to use the
name that best allows us to tell our story in the long and fascinating history
of East Lyme,

¢ We believe the new name “Brookside Farm Museum,” with the tagline,

“Cultivating the Story: Avery, Smith and Harris, 1845-1955” is the most
suited to our historic site, and we ask for your support as we embark on
these new endeavors.
