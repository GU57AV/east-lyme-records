2a. Discussion and possible action — Transfer Station Fees

DISCUSSION: Joe Bragaw, Director of Public Works, stated that the Town’s transfer station fees have not
changed in over ten years and it is in the Town’s best interest to review this policy. He stated that he
researched other town’s fees and believes that he has come up with a fair revised fee structure. Mr. Seery
noted that these increases will not affect all taxpayers, this is just affect people who choose to dispose of
items at the transfer station. Mr. Dagle inquired as to whether or not the Town would offer free disposal of
trees and brush after the recent storms that we had. Mr. Bragaw stated that the town does offer this type of
free pickup usually at a designated area such as Vet’s field, on a storm by storm basis. During the recent
storms the Town did offer to waive fees on brush drop off for about a week following those storms. The
public should please check the Town webpage for information on a storm by storm basis to see if this is
being offered.

MOTION (5)

Mr. Seery MOVED to approve the revised fees for the Transfer Station and Commercial Haulers as
submitted and to be effective May 1, 2018.

Seconded by Mr. Dagle. Motion passed 4-0.

2b. Discussion and possible action — Installation of a street light on Dodge Court

DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that we heard some great feedback from a couple of residents on this
matter and asked Mr. Bragaw for his thoughts. Mr. Bragaw stated that adding such a light is looked at on a
case by case basis and the safety need. He stated that he needs the Board of Selectmen to review this
instance as he does not believe there is a safety need for the light to be installed however a resident as
requested it. Mr. Nickerson stated that he appreciates the neighbors coming to express their opposing
views, and he directed the members of the Board to go and take a look at the area at their leisure and bring
back their thoughts on the matter to the next meeting.

2c. Discussion — Tax abatements for volunteer fire fighters

DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that there has been a huge drop in volunteer fire fighters and it is time
to consider offering an incentive for our volunteers. Many other towns do offer their volunteers incentives;
and as a discussion starting point we are considering up to $1,000.00 tax abatement on real estate/personal
property to a volunteer. Mr. Nickerson will direct the Town Attorney to work with the two Chiefs to devise
a proposed ordinance and work out all of the details. This matter will be re-addressed at a future meeting.

3a. Discussion and possible action regarding the alcohol policy for the Smith-Harris House.
DISCUSSION: Mr. Nickerson stated that he is amenable to giving this a trial run. These nonprofit
organizations do a lot of fundraising to keep the properties up and running, and this may be a very lucrative
way to bring in funds. Mr. Seery did a lot of research on this matter and found that each event is done
differently as far as what types of alcoholic beverages are to be served, but that they have come up with a
list of specifications that will need to be followed.
MOTION (9)
Mr. Seery MOVED that the Board of Selectmen authorizes the issuance of up to three (3) special permits to
serve alcoholic beverages at the Smith-Harris House, located at 33 Society Road, Niantic, Connecticut, for a
period of one (1) year commencing May 1, 2018, with the following specifications:
— No more than three (3) events per calendar year.
— Only during a period of time when there are no students on campus at either the East Lyme Middle
School or Lillie B. Haynes Elementary School.
- Certificate of Liability Insurance in an amount not less than $1,000,000.00 for the event. Such
certificate shall specify liquor liability.
— A signed Indemnity and Hold Harmless Agreement.

6a. Ex-Officio

Mr. Dagle reported that a new application was submitted to the Harbor Management Shellfish
Commission and that WELSCO is in the process of reviewing the existing bylaws. The Commission has
instructed the applicant to place buoys in the proposed areas as a visual aid to the proposed project. The
entire process continues to be under review. The Inland Wetlands Agency reports issuing two cease and
desist orders and there is one new application in the north end of town.

Mr. Seery reported that the Youth Services Commission welcomed new member Julie Horner. The
Parks & Recreation department is working hard despite and against the weather to start preparing the fields
for spring sports and the summer season. A moment of silence was observed in honor and remembrance of
Warren “Whitey” Whitehouse, who contributed greatly to the youth in East Lyme. Also remembered was
Sal Carpenteri, long time resident and local business owner, who also recently passed.

6b. First Selectman’s Report

Mr. Nickerson reported that as we all know it has been a very gloomy spring to start, but that Earth
Day will go on as planned this Saturday at McCook’s Point Park from 10am until 2pm with lots of activities
for kids and adults alike. The annual Niantic Boardwalk 5K will be held this Sunday, April 21st; and this
year will benefit the Miracle League Field project. On Tuesday night the 17th there was a jazz performance
from Stan Kenton at the East Lyme High School, which included participation of our high school band. We
have a music program in East Lyme that we should all be very proud of. This year’s Rotary Gala will be
held on May Sth and will also be benefiting the Miracle League Field project. Mr. Nickerson reported that
he has been attending many SCCOG and CCM meetings to keep on top of what is transpiring in Hartford.
We are wrapping up the budget process with the Board of Finance Public Hearing scheduled for April 30th
at the Middle School. Town Meeting will be held on May 14th at the East Lyme High School, and the
Budget Referendum is scheduled for May 24th from 8am until 8pm at the East Lyme Community Center.
