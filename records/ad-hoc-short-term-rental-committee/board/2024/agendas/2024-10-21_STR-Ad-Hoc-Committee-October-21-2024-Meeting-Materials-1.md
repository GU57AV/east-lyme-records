   1. Discussion with Counsel - Wihbey v. Pine Orchard Association ZBA decision and
      consideration of actions in response

      [11:10] Casey began by describing the recent decision of the CT Supreme Courts. The
      trial court judge made the determination it was appropriate to rent out a single-family
      home as a short-term rental (STR) with the idea that there is no time limit in the
      regulations that would alert anyone a STR was not allowed. This case’s judge
      determined the concept of property rights as the owner has the right to rent property
      regardless of time.

      This case then went to appellate court where the decision was upheld. It was again
      upheld in the CT Supreme Court. Casey continued, if the regulations don’t have some
      temporal limitations, then the right to rent your property is the right to rent your property.
      To control STRs in your zoning district, a time limitation is needed to put someone on
      notice. Casey noted the judge understood the defense of neighborhood character, and
      appreciated the use is in a residential zone, but a review of relevant definitions of
      dwelling, unit, house, home, etc., the absence of a temporal limitation in the regulations
      gave no notice for property owners, making it an allowed use.

      Casey stated Noank’s interpretation of the regulations, based on the character of zoning
      districts, is for residential zones to allow long-term renting. Additionally, another course
      for this determination is STRs are lodging and therefore not a residential use. Casey
      noted dwellings must be rented to a single-family, which is another thing to delve in to.
      Noank interpreted short-term rentals using the State’s definition to include any rental
      under 30 consecutive days.

      Casey added the Cease & Desist Orders from ZEO Mulholland have been very
      successful, and his enforcement had been done very well compared to a number of other
      jurisdictions. However, in light of the recent court decision, Casey suggested if the
      commission wants to codify that portion of the regulations, they needed to add language
      that rentals be for 30 consecutive days or more.

      Steele asked what would happen if other STRs are grandfathered in. Casey replied
      particular individual situations have been remanded to the Zoning Board of Appeals,
      since the questions is whether they actually established a pre-existing non-conforming
      use. The owner would have to prove they had been renting to the specific definition of a
      family in that zone.

The Noank Zoning Regulations define a family as:

“Section 18.2.28: Family – A single person keeping house separately or any number of
individuals related by blood, marriage or adoption, living and cooking together as a
single housekeeping unit, provided that a group of not more than two persons keeping
house together, but not necessarily related by blood or marriage, is considered a family
for the purpose of these regulations.”

Steele asked, regardless of temporal aspect, what is the prerequisite for being
grandfathered in? Casey replied there is no easy answer, and added doing something for
one day would not qualify, would have to be repeated. Renters would have to submit
evidence to ZEO Mulholland as proof.

Steele asked, with respect to regulating it now, are we permitted to issue a moratorium?
Casey replied, if you want to codify the prior decision that STRs are not allowed, it
would be a simple text amendment. Smith had had asked Casey to draft language.

[23:50] Oviatt asked what about Pine Orchards regulations allowed for this use? Casey
replied the Court took a very broad view of use for single-family residences. They threw
a wide enough net - the use is the occupancy by a family, and it does not matter how long
that use is, if not specified in your regulations. The Court allowed for a more expansive
granting of property rights.

Drakos asked about a single renter as a family, Casey replied definition was two people
or less living as a house keeping unit. Casey added no more than two people, preventing
a group of six people living in a house together. Casey furthered it was very difficult to
police the definition of a family, so the focus is on the time of the rental and not who is in
there.

Proposed Amendments to the Zoning Regulations were distributed [Attachment A].
[32:50] Casey described the zoning districts affected and new definitions, reiterating the
focus is on the time limited. Casey then addressed a moratorium on STRs - he suggested
if there was a moratorium on restaurants and you ban that use, there would be no
applications. Since single-family rentals are allowed as of right in almost all zones, this
use can continue.

Hewitt noted if three family units stay for short period under the same name reservation,
there is nothing to regulate this. Casey replied Zoning Regulations are like traffic laws,
they set a standard knowing not everyone will follow them. [47:57] Casey recommended
notifying rental agencies. Mulholland noted this procedure had been used previously.
