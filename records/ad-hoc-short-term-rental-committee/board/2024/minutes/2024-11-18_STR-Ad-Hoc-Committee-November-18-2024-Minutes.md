5. . Discussion of Answers to Questions Posed in Memo from Co-Chairs to Committee Members

  Note see attachment.
      Short-Term_Rental_Committee--Questions_to_Answer_for_11_18_24_Meeting.docx

  Note The Committee discussed the desirability of short-term rentals in East Lyme. They reviewed evidence supporting both
 allowing and banning short-term rentals, considering community preferences and historical practices.

  Note Ms. Santoro read Ms. Thurlow's response to the questions into the record.
      STR.docx

  Note The Committee discussed the challenges and considerations of managing short-term rentals in beach communities.


  Note Mr. McIntire noted they've discussed the long history of short-term rentals in East Lyme multiple times, and for him, it's
 the most compelling evidence for allowing STRs. This led to a discussion on the evolution of short-term rentals, particularly
 with platforms like Airbnb, and their impact on beach communities. Concerns were raised about the loss of community
 character and the financial implications for local businesses and property owners.

  Note Ms. Santoro discussed some of the differences between both the beach communities and the North end of town, noting
 that one size doesn't fit all. She added that the financial benefits of STRs are persuasive but not compelling; they're unable to
 quantify the effect on local businesses. Ms. Santoro added that given property evaluation and the mass appraisal process,
 anything the town gets directly is a very small amount.

  Note see attachment.
      1085_001.pdf

  Note Mr. Farrugia discussed his response to the questions (see attachment.)
      1088_001.pdf

  Note Mr. Farrugia addressed the lack of explicit zoning regulations for short-term rentals in East Lyme, highlighting the town's
 vulnerability to enforcement issues. The need for a regulatory framework was discussed to manage the growth of short-term
 rentals. Mr. Formica contended that Attorney Zamarka addressed their allowance under the town's sign regulation, drawing
 parallels to the sign regulation issue in the Wihbey case.

  Note Mr. Farrugia discussed coming up with regulations fair to residents and STR owners, having an ordinance and permit
 process, with corresponding zoning regulations.

  Note Mr. Cicchiello asked if there should be any constraints and Mr. Farrugia observed that they don't have a good handle on
 how many STRs are actually out there, and how this type of framework will help to provide this data.

 Note Concerns about the impact of short-term rentals on neighborhood character and community cohesion were discussed.
 The need for proactive policymaking to balance property rights and community interests was emphasized.

  Note Ms. Santoro emphasized the prudence of not delaying action until a crisis occurs, reflecting on it from a policy
 standpoint. The essence of their Committee's existence is to address such issues. They're on the brink of change, aware that
 things are no longer as they were, and are striving to understand and manage this. The goal is to develop a policy that, while
 not perfect, meets as many objectives as possible without frequent delays. Her reservation is that, although she appreciates
 the concept of a cautious approach, they must act decisively on behalf of all citizens. They cannot ignore the few, yet
 significant, incidents that highlight the gravity of the situation.

6. Greg Mclntire's Summary of Vermont Fire Safety Regulations for STRs

  Note Mr. McIntire mentioned that his friend owns a condo where a fire occurred, and the owners received a set of rules. He
 found the occupancy limits imposed due to the fire codes particularly interesting.

  Note Fire Marshal Bundy discussed the CT Fire Code, NFPA, and IFC. He noted that he and Mr. Quinn did some research
 with the code and found some verbiage relative to would be a short-term rental, which would be the rental of a single family,
 and when rented to 7 or more people, an inspection is required.

  Note Assistant Fire Marshal Quinn explained that during a property inspection, adding an extra bed to a room with only one
 entrance and exit is prohibited. Inspectors would provide a code violation notice and require compliance with entry and exit
 regulations. Specific systems are applicable to each case. Inspections of single-family homes often reveal that smoke
 detectors are certified, up-to-date, and functional. In our town, some homes are subject to safety inspections upon request,
 complete with prepared documentation and certification forms.

  Note Fire Marshal Bundy emphasized the specificity of the situation. When a family of eight visits the shoreline to stay in a
 property, there is an inherent expectation of safety. This includes functioning equipment, unblocked exits, and the assurance
 that, whether it's a short-term rental or longer, safety regulations are met. While he understands the concerns about over-
 regulation, he reiterated from the first meeting that any time people are housed in a property, there should be safeguards in
 place to ensure their safety and protect their lives.

  Note Mr. Quinn said when they do inspections, it's to make sure that the building is safe for everybody, but their primary goal
 is preventing other emergencies.

  Note Mr. Cellino asked if there is a fee associated with the inspection and Mr. Bundy confirmed that there is, and it's based on
 occupancy and square footage.

  Note The conversation included the need for fire code compliance and the potential for annual inspections.


  Note The Committee asked about a definition of what they consider short-term, and Mr. Bundy replied that
 the only difference the code tells them is between transient and non-transient. It's not up to them to make that definition.
 Whatever the town decides is the definition of a short-term rental, and they'll apply the code accordingly. But the code of
 current FIRE code defines the word transient as up to 30 days.

  Note Mr. Bundy discussed how the current fees are anywhere from $60 to $120 and may need to be revisited. He noted that a
 database as a means of contacting people would be extremely helpful.

  Note The Committee thanked Mr. Bundy and Mr. Quinn for attending the meeting.
