                          ANNE SANTORO DRAFT RECOMMENDATIONS

                         STR REGULAR MEETING OF SEPTEMBER 23,2024




   Drafting final recommendations to the Board of Selectmen to address short-term rentals in
East Lyme is a challenging task. There is much to consider. ln addition to all of the information
and issues presented in our Status Report, Committee members must now also consider the
recent state Supreme Court decision in the Wihbev case as well as newly enacted Public Act 24-
1,43.


    Our Committee's objective is to suggest what is best for the Town as concerns short-term
rentals. This requires a determination of the desirability of short-term rentals in Town and a
consideration of all options-banning short-term rental operations, regulating them through
zoning and/or ordinance, or taking no action. To this end, I first revisited the goals the
Committee had asserted (Status Report, p.13). I then focused on (1) weighing competing
interests and concerns; (2) addressing the relevant legal framework; and (3) proposing
attainable and affordable enforcement. A summary of my analysis, conclusions, and draft
recom mendations fol lows.

    (1) Competing Interests and Concerns

    Our Status Report, in particular; Sections 2 and 5, details and documentsthe competing
    interests and concerns that short-term rentals present generally and here in Town. To
    summarize, owners gain financially; renters/travelers enjoy non-traditional lodging; our
    state receives revenues from its occupancy tax; local businesses can benefit from renters'
    patronage; and the Town may be more likely to receive expected property tax revenue.
    Unfortunately, neighbors and residents stand to be adversely impacted, as there are
    significant negative consequences of short-term rental operations that cities and towns
    commonly experience, including to some extent, East Lyme. The most serious of these are
    the change in the nature of residential neighborhoods and the effect on local housing stock.
    Aflow of transient guests not connected to a neighborhood, along with excessive traffic and
    noise as well as parking and trash issues undermine the permanence and quality of life
    expected in residential communities. Further, an increase in the number of short-term
    rentals results in a decrease in the number of housing units available for permanent
    residents, and can contribute to higher home prices.

    These developments, among others, have resulted in the efforts of municipalities in our
    state, country, and beyond to limitthe groMh of short-term rentals through regulation or to
    ban them. lndeed, as is evident in Section L0 of our Status Report, these are the main
    concerns of a number of Connecticut towns about short-term rentals and are the focus of

Connecticut, are neighboring or nearby communities to East Lyme, and some share our
features of beaches and tourists.

Although our Committee has received more public comment from owners of short-term
rentalsthan neighbors of them, our residents have spoken or written to us about their
experiences and concerns. The two developments discussed above-erosion of residential
neighborhoods and adverse impact on housing availability and affordability-have been
repeatedly mentioned. This is troublesome as these outcomes are not desirable in any
community and they may become more apparent here. As pointed out in Section 2 of our
Status Report, the short-term rental market in East Lyme increased 1-5% from 2023 to 2024
This growth can be expected to continue if East Lyme follows the market trend of other
smaller communities.

(2) Relevant Legal Framework

The legal framework that existed at the time of writing our Status Report has changed in
two significant ways as a result of the Wihbev decision and the passage of Public Acl24-L43.

Arguments aside differentiating the zoning regulations at issue in Wihbev from our zoning
regulations, the state Supreme Court's decision challenges our previous and generally
accepted legal understanding that short-term rentals are not an allowed use under our
Town's zoning law because the use is not expressly stated. Wihbev tells us that to avoid
inferred uses, zoning regulations need to be specific. Further, and very importantly, the
decision sets a path forward, stating that"zoning authorities are free to odopt regulotions
thot permit only long-term rentals...to promote stability ond a sense of community within a
si ngl e -fo m i ly re si de nti ol zone --."



Public Act 24-143 provides express authority for "any municipality" in our state to "adopt an
ordinonce requiring the licensure of short-term rental properties in such municipality and
reguloting the operotion ond use of such properties,..." f he statute is effective October 1,
2024 and also allows a municipality to contract with consultant(s) to draft a short-term
rental ordinance. The statute does not address, however, howthe authority itconfers
relates to zoning authority to regulate short-term rentals. The authority of municipalities to
regulate land use through zoning is conferred by state statute and is well established under
our law. lt should be pointed out thatthe new law is really untested and may be open to
legal challenge, as Attorney Mahaney for Mr. Wihbey stated in a recent interview. To date
and prior to the enactment of Public Act24-t43,just a few Connecticut municipalities have
regulated or attempted to regulate short-term rentals by ordinance alone. Regulation in the
state of these operations has primarily been through zoning and some have regulated or are
trying to regulate through a combination of zoning regulation and ordinance. These
examples are discussed in Section L0 of our Status Report.




                                                                                              2
What has not changed since the writing of our Status Report is the challenge of legally
addressing differing preferences asto short-term rentals in our beach communities and
throughout the rest of Town. Section 6 of our Status Report outlines the preferences of our
beach communities. Three of them-Black Point Beach Club Association, Crescent Beach
Association, and Giants Neck Beach Association--have their own zoning authority and can
adopt short-term rental regulations as they each prefer. lndeed, this is what Black Point has
done, allowing such rentals for 7 days or more. The remaining beach communities do not
have their own zoning authority. lt is unclear; however, if these communities, incorporated
as Special Act communities, have other authority to regulate short-term rentals-perhaps
even under Public Act24-L43. (This is a question I have previously raised thatTown Counsel
can best answer.) lf not, they will be subject to whatever the Town decides, regardless of
preferences expressed in bylaws or elsewhere (Oak Grove Beach Community Association;
Pine Grove).

Finally, we should keep in mind our Noise Control Ordinance and Parking Ordinance,
discussed at length in Section 38 of our Status Report. Despite our recommendations
concerning these ordinances, nothing has been done to advance their use. We can reiterate
our suggestions when making our final recommendations.

(3) Enforcement

As stated in Section 8 of our Status Report, enforcement of any short-term rental regulations
the Town adopts will be an ongoing challenge and expense. Administration of regulations
will require staff and other resources. lf regulation is done through zoning, the Town's
Zoning Department can administer the program and may still require additional office help.
lf regulation is done through ordinance, additional staff would likely need to be hired.

Further; we know from data provided directly to the Committee by Granicus that where a
short-term rental registration program exists, less than 10% of owners voluntarily register
and pay all of their taxes. This is not a promising statistic for any regulatory program,
particularly one that establishes several requirements for operation. As compliance would
likely be a continuous issue, penalties should be effective. Zoning enforcement affords the
power of an immediate cease-and-desist order; coupled with accumulated fines.
Enforcement of a short-term rental ordinance, in contrast, consists of the revocation of a
permit by commencing legal action and/or issuing a citation (with a fine).

(4) A Suggested Approach

Based on the foregoing I am suggesting that East Lyme consider amending its zoning
regulations to allow rentals of greater than 30 days only. Short-term rentals would not be
permitted. Short-term rentals would be defined as provided in C.G.S. Sec. L2-408h(aXl) as
stays of 30 days or less. They would not include bed and breakfast establishments, inns,
hotels, and motels as defined and regulated in our zoning code.




                                                                                                3
Owners of existing short-term rentals would be encouraged by our Zoning Enforcement
Officer to convert to longer-term rentals permitted under the new regulations. Short-term
rentals that do not convert may remain in operation, depending upon the applicability of
Wihbev or an analysis of their pre-existing non-conforming use. Enforcement would be the
responsibility of the Zoning Officer and existing staff within the Zoning Department.
Enforcement would consist primarily of monitoring short-term rental platforms, followed by
letter writing or orders as necessary.

An additional feature of the program could be voluntary registration and voluntarysafety
inspections for remaining short-term rentals. This would be administered through the
Zoning Department and no ordinance would be needed. A modest fee for registration could
be charged. Fees for fire and building inspections are already set by the Town. An incentive
for registration and inspections would be the abilityfor an owner to advertise certified
compliance on short-term rental platforms used.

This suggested approach aims to preserve the nature of the Town's residential
