2. Public Delegations
   2-1. Tristan Winkler of 10 Vermont Drive, Oakdale

        Note Mr. Winkler offered some of the following:
        He is a trial attorney and is here in steadfast defense of short-term rentals in East Lyme.
        He wants to make one thing clear, any attempt to ban or restrict short-term rentals will likely be bad and
        convey an apparent prejudice.
        East Lyme's charm and appeal have remained intact, and the presence of short-term
        rentals have done nothing to erode the fabric of this community.
        The focus and comparisons in towns like Noank, Groton, and Ledyard seem to be highly arbitrary. Why are
        you not comparing East Lyme to very similar towns like Mystic, New London, or
        Waterford, where short-term rentals have been embraced and proven successful?
        The positives of short-term rentals, including increased tourism, economic benefits to local
        businesses, and suppling income for homeowners, who are also taxpayers are
        conveniently omitted from this Committee’s discussion.
        Any party attempting to ban short-term rentals will likely set themselves up for inevitable legal battles.
        He believes that ultimately, this Commission will act in accordance with the law and recognize that the
        continued operation of short-term rentals is both lawful and beneficial to the town of East Lyme.

 on housing availability, and the potential need for state assistance.

  Note The discussion also revolved around the proposal for a town ordinance to regulate rental properties
 through a permitting process. This ordinance would apply to long-term, short-term, and academic rentals,
 aiming to create a comprehensive database for research and ensure compliance with zoning regulations. The
 ordinance would provide a uniform framework for landlords and allow the town to address complaints and
 revoke permits if necessary. The conversation also touched on the need to balance community character with
 the benefits of short-term rentals.

6. Determination of Outstanding issues and Process to reach Final Recommendations
  6-1. Need for each member to consider threshold questions and evidence gathered, and to
  make a comparative analysis

      Note Mr. Cicchiello emphasized the urgency of reaching a decision on whether East Lyme should continue
     to permit short-term rentals. He stressed the importance of a structured discussion process, rather than
     hastily forming opinions on the matter. It's crucial to assess the merits and drawbacks of short-term rentals.
     As Committee Members, they must all address this question, and Mr. Cicchiello feels he has yet to hear
     everyone's stance; it’s easy to jump right into regulations, that’s the easy stuff, but we first need to answer
     that question.

      Note Ms. Santoro mentioned that undertaking the task was quite challenging and laborious. She had
     envisioned certain outcomes, but she revisited the initial stages and re-examined the report, weighing the
     pros and cons, and considering the direction she wished to take. This led to a slightly different and perhaps
     more negative conclusion than others, but it was the result of her analysis. The conclusions in her draft
     recommendation may not be her final stance; they were part of an exploratory process. She agrees with
     Mr. Cicchiello's point that such analysis is essential. This doesn't preclude reaching a definitive conclusion.
     As Mr. Cicchiello's chart suggests, there are numerous potential outcomes, some better, some worse, and
     each can be argued for.

      Note Ms. Santoro and Mr. Cicchiello will collaborate to compile the areas of agreement and disagreement
     within the Committee and will discuss finalizing their approach at the next meeting.
