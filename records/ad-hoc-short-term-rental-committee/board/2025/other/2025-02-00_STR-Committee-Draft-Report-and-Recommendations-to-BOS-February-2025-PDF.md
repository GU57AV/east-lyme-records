SECTION 1—PURPOSE AND SCOPE OF THIS REPORT

This Draft Final Report and Recommendations to the Board of Selectmen is an update of the
Committee’s earlier Status Report to the Board of Selectmen of April 2024. The Status Report
highlights information gathered, issues identified, as well as conclusions formulated by the
Committee prior to the Connecticut Supreme Court’s recent decision concerning the regulation
of short-term rentals in Wihbey v. Zoning Board of Appeals of the Pine Orchard Association.

This Report includes a consideration of the Wihbey decision and related recent developments,
as well as key content from the Status Report, utilized by the Committee in making its
recommendations about short-term rentals in East Lyme. It is a draft, as the Committee may
make edits to it following the public’s review and commentary made at the upcoming Public
Hearing of February 25, 2025. See Exhibit 1. Once finalized, the Report and Recommendations
will be presented to the Board of Selectmen and the Committee will have then completed its
charge to advise the Board on the matter of short-term rentals in Town.

The Committee’s Status Report and entire body of work to date, including all meeting minutes
and videos, may be easily viewed at the Committee’s webpage:

SECTION 2—THE WIHBEY DECISION AND ITS APPLICATION

In a 5-2 decision, the Connecticut Supreme Court in Wihbey upheld the Appellate Court’s ruling
that short-term rentals constituted a permissible use of the plaintiff’s property under the 1994
zoning regulations of the Pine Orchard Association, a beach community in Branford. The 1994
regulations provide for 8 permitted uses, including use as a single-family dwelling, but do not
specifically address short-term rentals. In response to complaints about short-term rentals in
the community of Pine Orchard, the regulations were amended in 2018 to expressly prohibit
short-term rentals, defined as a rental of a residential dwelling unit for less than 30 days.

The majority opinion concluded that the 1994 regulations permit the rental of Mr. Wihbey’s
property without any time restrictions. The Court relied on expansive definitions of terms such
as “single-family dwelling”, as well as the fact that the regulations permit the use of rental signs.
See Exhibit 2. The minority opinion disagreed, explaining that the definition of “single-family
dwelling” in the 1994 regulations requires a degree of permanency not present with transient,
short-term occupancy. See Exhibit 3.



                                                                                                   1
The Court’s ruling tells us that as concerns zoning regulations, temporal restrictions on rentals
cannot be inferred, and accordingly, if time limitations on rentals are not expressly stated,
rentals of all duration, including short-term, are permitted. In fact, the Court sets at least one
path forward, stating in its conclusion that “zoning authorities are free to adopt regulations that
permit only long-term rentals…to promote stability and a sense of community within a single-
family residential zone—”

It should be noted that the Committee discussed the decision and its implications with Town
Attorney Zamarka at our August 26, 2024 meeting. The Committee also had the benefit of
listening to the audio tape of Attorney John Casey’s discussion of Wihbey with Noank’s Zoning
Commission at its meeting of September 17, 2024.

As discussed in Section 3A of the Committee’s Status Report, East Lyme’s zoning regulations do
not specifically permit short-term rentals in any zone, nor do the regulations define the term.
The regulations also do not expressly permit or define long-term rentals. They do, however,
expressly define and permit types of traditional lodging, namely, bed and breakfast
establishments, inns, hotels, and motels. Additionally, Section 18.1.11.1.3 of our zoning
regulations allows temporary real estate signs to advertise “the sale, rental or lease” of
premises. For the Committee, the Wihbey decision highlighted the issue already well under
discussion of amending our zoning code to define short-term rentals (and other types of rental
operations) and to clearly state related regulation, if any.

It should be pointed out that although the state Supreme Court ruled in favor of Mr. Wihbey as
concerns the legality of short-term rental operations under the 1994 regulations, the Zoning
Board of Appeals of Pine Orchard Association recently determined, on remand from the state
Appellate Court, that Mr. Wihbey failed to establish a pre-existing non-conforming use of his
property as he claimed. See Exhibit 4. He could not produce any evidence that he rented to
“single families” as required and defined under the 1994 regulations. The Zoning Board of
Appeals decision here provides an example of grandfathering and the analysis of establishing a
pre-existing nonconforming use. These are issues that accompany the adoption of any new
regulation and are further discussed below.


SECTION 3—CONNECTICUT PUBLIC ACT 24-143

Effective October 1, 2024, Public Act 24-143 provides express authority for “any municipality” in
our state to “adopt an ordinance requiring the licensure of short-term rental properties in such
municipality and regulating the operation and use of such properties…” See Exhibit 5. This new
statute also allows a municipality to contract with consultant(s) to draft a short-term rental
ordinance. The statute’s provisions are permissive, not mandatory.

Under the statute, “short-term-rental” is defined as it is under Section 12-408h(a)(1) of the
general statutes: “the transfer for a consideration of the occupancy in a furnished residence or
similar accommodation for a period of thirty consecutive calendar days or less”, and does not


                                                                                                   2
include specified traditional lodging such as a hotel, motel, or bed and breakfast establishment.
This definition of short-term rentals is consistent with the generally accepted definition of these
operations. It is also the definition the Committee has most often used in its discussions,
although the Committee is aware that certain of our beach communities and other
municipalities have expressed a differing view concerning the length of stay for a short-term
rental.

It is important to point out that Public Act 24-143 does not address how the authority it confers
relates to zoning authority to regulate short-term rentals. The authority of municipalities to
regulate land use through zoning is conferred by state statute and is well established under our
law. To date and prior to the enactment of the new law, just a few Connecticut municipalities
have regulated short-term rentals by ordinance alone. Regulation in the state of these
operations has primarily been through zoning and some municipalities have regulated or are
trying to regulate through a combination of zoning regulation and ordinance. These various
examples are discussed in Section 10 of the Committee’s Status Report.

In East Lyme, reliance solely on an ordinance to regulate short-term rentals is complicated by
the fact that 3 of our beach communities—Black Point Beach Club Association, Crescent Beach
Association, and Giants Neck Beach Association—are Special Act communities that have their
own zoning authority.

It is nevertheless encouraging that the state legislature is beginning to address short-term rental
issues beyond the state’s collection of room occupancy taxes. Two other bills were introduced
during the 2024 session but did not become law. H.B. 5186 sought to have the state share the
room occupancy taxes it collects on short-term rentals with the municipalities in which they are
located. S.B.267 proposed that the state conduct a study of the effects of short-term rentals on
the housing market.


SECTION 4—METHODOLOGY TO REACH DRAFT RECOMMENDATIONS

Committee members have worked through an extensive process to formulate draft
recommendations to the Board of Selectmen to address short-term rentals in East Lyme. This is
so because addressing short-term rentals anywhere is a challenging task, requiring
municipalities to weigh competing interests, differing viewpoints, legal complications, and a
variety of possible approaches. Through much research and considerable discussion, the
Committee was able to begin shaping policy suggestions, evident in its Status Report.

Post-Wihbey, the Committee has conducted a deeper analysis requiring detailed discussion and
debate. A goal was to determine areas of agreement and difference among members. The
process began with members working individually to outline draft recommendations and then
sharing and discussing these suggestions at our meetings. The process continued with the
Committee Co-Chairs asking all members to answer 2 threshold questions as to the desirability
of short-term rentals in East Lyme, and to identify the most compelling evidence the Committee


                                                                                                  3
had gathered in support of the differing viewpoints. Again, members individually responded to
the questions and shared their answers at our meetings, engaging in debate and revealing some
significant differences. Most recently, Committee member Gary Farrugia created a draft survey
of specific questions for members to complete. Responses to the survey questions provided a
helpful tool to facilitate further discussion at our meetings and has resulted in the Committee
being able to make the draft recommendations as detailed in the following Sections.

It is important to point out that at various times during this process, Committee members
discussed the need to adhere to the policy goals the group established, set forth in Section 7 of
its Status Report, and to ensure that any policy recommendations address a need and have a
purpose. See Exhibit 6. These goals and standards have in fact been relied upon by the
Committee in formulating its draft recommendations.


SECTION 5—DRAFT RECOMMENDATION 1—GENERAL POLICY:

                  ALLOW SHORT-TERM RENTALS AND LIMIT THEIR GROWTH

This is the recommendation of the majority of Committee members, although there are
variations expressed among members as to the methods of limiting growth. One member in
the minority favors either a total ban or a partial ban. A partial ban would allow only rentals of
30 days or more and would grandfather existing short-term rentals, that is, stays of 30 days or
less. Another member in the minority also supports a partial ban, or at a minimum, limiting the
location of short-term rentals by zoning district as a member of the majority prefers.

It should be pointed out here that no member favors inaction by the Town on the issue of short-
term rentals. The Committee has discussed this approach from time to time but it never gained
acceptance. Reasons stated have been the existence and growth of short-term rentals in Town,
despite the fact that our zoning regulations do not specifically define or allow them, and more
recently, the ruling in Wihbey that in zoning regulations, time restriction on rentals cannot be
inferred.

The majority and minority approaches to a general policy on short-term rentals reflect to some
extent differing views on the evidence and information the Committee gathered as well as on
the desirability of having short-term rentals in our Town. The majority found compelling the
evidence of financial benefits for short-term rental owners and businesses, as well as the history
of summer rentals in our beach communities. In contrast, the minority found persuasive those
concerns expressed about the erosion of residential neighborhoods and adverse impacts on
housing availability and affordability, as well as concerns about the suitability of imposing one
approach for all areas in Town.

As discussed in Section 2 of our Status Report, the Granicus assessment of the East Lyme short-
term rental market tells us that there are short-term rentals throughout the Town, with a
concentration in the beach communities. The short-term rental market increased by 15% in


                                                                                                    4
Town from 2023 to 2024, and according to Granicus, it is expected to continue to grow. This can
be compared to the traditional lodging market in Town which to date has seen little to no
growth. According to our Zoning Officer, Bill Mulholland, there are 8 such operations in East
Lyme but it is his view that while there have been recent improvements, we are lacking quality
motel/hotel units. Should short-term rentals be limited here, Mr. Mulholland anticipates
additional interest in developing traditional lodging. If built, these businesses would result in an
increase in the Town’s tax base.

Importantly, all Committee members favor limiting the growth of short-term rentals in our
Town. That there is unanimity on this point is understandable. As highlighted in Sections 5 and
6 of the Status Report, our residents have spoken or written to us about their negative
experiences and concerns, although to date we received more public comment from owners of
short-term rentals than neighbors of them. The Committee’s view is consistent with that of
municipalities locally, nationally, and globally and with their efforts to limit the growth of short-
term rentals through regulation or bans. A review of Section 10 of our Status Report clearly
shows the many ways neighboring and other Connecticut towns have exercised their authority
to curb the growth of short-term rentals.

The Committee has discussed a number of methods of limiting the growth of short-term rentals.
These include: (1) Setting a cap on the number of short-term rentals allowed; (2) Limiting the
location of short-term rentals to certain zoning districts or areas; (3) Allowing only 1 permit per
rental owner, regardless of the number of properties held; (4) Requiring hosted rentals/rental
property to be the primary residence of the rental owner. Our draft survey included questions
about methods (1), (2), and (3).

A majority of members favor setting a cap, that is, limiting the number of short-term rentals to a
percentage of existing residential properties. A minority preferred to wait on caps and
cautioned that up-to-date data would be necessary to establish them. The Committee
discussed the pros and cons of setting caps by neighborhood versus establishing a Town-wide
cap. A Town-wide cap might be easier to administer than caps by neighborhood, but it may not
sufficiently limit short-term rentals concentrated in the beach communities.

Limiting the location of short-term rentals by zoning districts or areas is a common tool used to
control their growth. It is also an approach taken to preserve residential neighborhoods and the
quality of life expected there, reflecting the viewpoint that short-term rentals are businesses,
operating as mini-hotels. Some Committee members have suggested banning short-term
rentals in residential areas in Town while allowing them in mixed use and commercial zones.
This approach does not interfere with the zoning authority of our 3 beach communities noted
above to regulate their short-term rentals as they wish. It also meets the preference and
practice of 2 of our other beach communities—Pine Grove and Old Black Point Association—
that do not allow short-term rentals (30 days or less) but instead permit longer-term rentals.

Locally, the zoning regulations of the towns of Groton and Preston provide very current
examples of using location to limit short-term rentals. Section 10 of our Status Report outlines


                                                                                                    5
the circumstances leading up to these developments. Of note, as of May 15, 2024, the Town of
Groton prohibits short-term rentals in single-unit residential zones as well as in heavy industrial,
working waterfront, open space, and green zones. Short-term rentals are allowed in residential
zones with 1-2unit dwellings as well as in rural residential, multi-unit residential, commercial,
and mixed-use zones. See Exhibit 7. Interestingly, as of September 24, 2024, Preston ended its
moratorium and amended its zoning regulations to include guidance for its Planning and Zoning
Commission in permitting short-term rentals in residential zones, so as not to “impact the
residential integrity of the surrounding (neighborhood)”. See Exhibit 8.

In our draft survey, 3 members favored allowing only one permit per short-term rental owner as
another method of limiting the number and growth of short-term rentals. An example of this
approach locally may be found in the short-term ordinance of the Town of Bozrah. See Exhibit 9.
Section 3A of the ordinance states that “(n)o more than one permit for a Short-Term Rental may
be held by the same individual, partnership, or entity at one time, regardless of the number of
properties held by that individual, partnership, or entity in the Town of Bozrah”.

Bozrah’s ordinance also requires in Section 2 that the rental owner or the owner’s designated
representative be present when the property is rented. This requirement and/or having the
rental be the owner’s primary residence is another tool commonly used by municipalities to
limit the growing short-term rental market and some of its adverse impacts. Like Bozrah, Lyme
allows only hosted short-term rentals, although through its zoning regulations and not an
ordinance. See Exhibit 10. Similarly, in the residential districts where short-term rentals are
allowed, the Town of Groton zoning regulations require that “(t)he owner or lessee of the lot
must have their primary residence in…the dwelling unit, or in a legally permitted accessory
dwelling unit.”

It should be emphasized that despite the unique features of each of these towns, there is a
commonality of concerns about short-term rentals and methods of addressing them that is
useful in formulating a policy for East Lyme.



SECTION 6—DRAFT RECCOMMENDATION 2—ZONING REGULATIONS:

   AMEND OUR CURRENT ZONING REGULATIONS TO DEFINE AND REGULATE SHORT-TERM
            RENTALS, INCLUDING ESTABLISHING A PERMITTING PROCESS

A majority of Committee members favor addressing short-term rentals in our Town through a
combination of zoning and ordinance. Committee discussion, however, has focused on our
zoning code as the primary method of regulation. This is understandable, as it respects the
authority of zoning to regulate land use issues and allows our 3 beach communities with their
own zoning authority to address their short-term rentals as they wish. Further, it allows the
Town to limit the location of short-term rentals by zoning districts and areas, an important
regulatory tool. An ordinance, in contrast, would apply uniformly Town-wide and cannot


                                                                                                   6
legislate permissible uses in zoning districts. A recommended role for an ordinance is described
in Section 7 below.

                                           A. Definition

Importantly, as discussed in Section 2 above, the Wihbey decision urges the Town to correct
course as concerns our zoning regulations to include a definition of short-term rentals and to
expressly state any related regulation. Indeed, this is the rationale given by the Noank Zoning
Commission in recently codifying its community’s position and practice that short-term rentals
are not permitted. See Exhibit 11. Noank defines such rentals as those of 30 days or less.

Our Committee suggests that “short-term rental” be defined as it is under our state statutes,
that is, the rental of a furnished residence or like accommodation of 30 calendar days or less
and that does not include traditional lodging. As described in Section 3 above, this definition is
set forth in Section 12-408h(a)(1) of our general statutes and is used in our state regulations to
collect room occupancy taxes as well as in the new Public Act 24-143 allowing municipal short-
term rental ordinances. This is also the generally accepted definition of short-term rentals and
the definition the Committee has used in its many discussions. Should the Town adopt a
companion ordinance to zoning regulations in order to address short-term rentals, that
ordinance would be enacted under Public Act 24-143, requiring the above definition.

It should be pointed out, as some Committee members have, that amending our zoning
regulations to include this definition does not preclude the Town from mandating minimum
nights of stay at short-term rentals in specific zoning districts, i.e., 3-day minimum in mixed-use
and commercial zones or greater than 30 days in residential zones.

Of considerable concern to Committee members has been the status of long-term rentals,
although they are beyond the Committee’s charge. Accordingly, we have not researched the
topic or taken testimony about them from the public. Nevertheless, the Committee would like
to suggest that long-term rentals, including academic and year-long rentals, likewise be defined
and addressed in our zoning regulations.

                                          B. Regulations

There are a number of zoning regulations the Town may wish to adopt that are intended to
lessen adverse impacts associated with short-term rentals, in addition to those regulations
outlined in Section 1 above aimed at limiting the growth of these operations, particularly in
residential neighborhoods. Adverse (and positive) impacts generally and also evident in East
Lyme were fully discussed in Sections 2C and 5 of our Status Report. Troubling impacts include
excessive noise and traffic, parking and trash problems, party houses/overoccupancy, septic
problems, as well as the risk of fire or building failure.




                                                                                                      7
In answering our draft survey, Committee members all agree that a short-term rental owner
must provide 24/7 contact information. Members view this requirement as central to any
approach to address short-term rental concerns.

Committee members are also in agreement that occupancy for a short-term rental should be
based on the number of bedrooms indicated on the Assessor’s field card for that property and
that density rates be considered. Two members favor additionally setting a maximum
occupancy, an approach used in the zoning regulations of Lyme and Preston, as well as in
Simsbury’s comparable ordinance. All members agree that minors should be prohibited from
renting or hosting a short-term rental. This is a common restriction found in short-term rental
regulations.

A majority of the Committee supports using the Town’s Noise Control Ordinance and existing
police authority to handle noise issues and sees no need to further regulate the topic in our
zoning regulations or in another ordinance. The Noise Control Ordinance applies directly Town-
wide and is detailed in Section 3B (1) of our Status Report. As was recommended to the Board
of Selectmen last Spring, a compliant sound level meter and appropriate training are needed in
order to implement and enforce the ordinance.

Members in the minority on the topic of noise point out that noise is a main concern of
residents living by short-term rentals, as it was in the Wihbey case, and that the Committee
reviewed short-term regulations from other Connecticut towns that prohibit amplified music,
outdoor events, parties, etc. One member made the distinction between tolerating an
occasional party by one’s neighbor versus ongoing parties at a short-term rental.

A majority of members favor requiring on-site parking for short-term rentals and prohibiting on-
street parking for them. This is in fact a common requirement in short-term rental regulations,
particularly for residential neighborhoods. A minority of members favor instead reliance on the
Town’s existing Parking Ordinance, detailed in Section 3B (2) of our Status Report. It should be
noted, however, that our Police cannot enforce parking signage unless it is on a “highway” as
defined under the ordinance and it is approved by our Police Commission.

All Committee members agree that fire and building inspections be required in order to operate
a short-term rental. These safety inspections are required for other traditional lodging
structures in our Town and are part of the many municipal short-term regulations the
Committee has reviewed. At a Committee meeting of November 18, 2024, our Fire Marshal
Bundy and Assistant Fire Marshal Quinn discussed the need for inspections of short-term
rentals, emphasizing that regardless of the length of stay, visitors have an expectation of safety
that should be met. Our fire officials also pointed out that under the Connecticut Fire Code, an
inspection is required of a single-family home when it is rented to 7 or more people.

                                      C. Permitting Process




                                                                                                  8
A majority of our Committee favors the establishment of a permitting process in order to
operate a short-term rental. Permitting is a tool to apply and enforce regulations. Indeed, the
majority stresses the importance of staff review of plans as well as the power to revoke a permit
for violations.

For the minority, reasons for opposing a permitting process are varied. One member, who
favors short-term rentals in all areas of Town, instead supports a more streamlined system
requiring contact/emergency information and safety inspections. Another member, who
advocates short-term rentals of 7 days or longer in residential areas, favors permitting with
safety inspections of rentals under 7 days in commercial and other zones, as for traditional
lodging operations. Yet another member who supports a total or partial ban does favor
permitting to insure at least contact/emergency information and safety inspections.

The requirements for any permitting process depend upon the specific regulations adopted. For
example, based on the preferred regulations discussed above in Section 6B, an applicant for a
short-term rental in Town would have to provide 24/7 contact information as well as evidence
of on-site parking and fire and building inspections. The applicant would also have to agree to
occupancy limits and adhering to the Noise Control Ordinance.

All Committee members favoring a permitting process also agree that fees be charged to offset
the costs of administration and enforcement. Indeed, the permitting processes the Committee
has reviewed all require various fees. There is an application fee, possible inspection fees, and a
renewal fee. Our Fire and Building Departments already have established fees for their safety
inspections of properties in Town. The Committee does not make any recommendation as to
the amounts of permitting fees the Town would charge as they would be set using calculations
made at the time of establishing permitting, and would necessarily include current fees for
safety inspections.


SECTION 7—DRAFT RECOMMENDATION 3—ORDINANCE:

             ADOPT AN ORDINANCE TO COMPLEMENT NEW SHORT-TERM RENTAL
                               ZONING REGULATIONS

The Committee has not expressed support for the adoption of an ordinance alone as the
method of addressing short-term rentals in Town, preferring instead zoning regulations and an
ordinance. Any ordinance adopted should be consistent with the Committee’s recommendation
of zoning regulations and permitting outlined above in Section 6. Very importantly, unless the
Town wishes to establish a sweeping short-term rental policy that dictates regulation and
permitting in the 3 beach communities having their own zoning authority, any ordinance must
be limited in scope.

A particularly useful role for a short-term rental ordinance in Town would be establishing a
registration system and a data base of short-term rentals operating here. Such an ordinance


                                                                                                 9
would be authorized under the new Public Act 24-143. The information gathered may help the
Town more accurately track the number of short-term rentals available and to change policy as
necessary. The Committee has also discussed the need for having additional information
through such a data base with officials from our Police and Fire Departments. They have stated
that this tool would be especially helpful in efficiently responding to emergency and other calls.
Our Tax Assessor, Diane Vitagliano, has also told the Committee that the information from a
registration process would allow her office to properly add short-term rentals to the tax rolls.
As explained in Section 9 of our Status Report, this would assist in the assessment of only
personal property taxes on short-term rentals; they are assessed for real estate property taxes
as single-family residences, not as commercial properties.

The registration information would include the short-term rental owner’s name, the address of
the property, 24/7 contact information (of the owner and a designated individual in the owner’s
absence), and a brief description of the property. A copy of any current short-term rental
permit could also be included, making the data base useful to the Town Zoning Department and
to those beach communities with zoning that may require permits. Registration would be
mandatory, with fines imposed for failure to comply. Annual updates should be required. The
system could be administered through the Town Clerk’s Office or through the Assessor’s Office.


SECTION 8—DRAFT RECOMMENDATION 4—BEACH COMMUNITIY PRACTICES
          AND PREFERENCES:

DETERMINE WHETHER INCORPORATED BEACH COMMUNITIES WITHOUT ZONING AUTHORITY
                      CAN REGULATE SHORT-TERM RENTALS

As previously noted, we know from our Granicus assessment that there is a concentration of
short-term rentals in our beach communities. The Committee researched the short-term rental
practices and preferences of these communities and detailed its findings in Section 6 of our
Status Report. It is clear that there are differing rules and issues unique to each community.
This circumstance makes addressing short-term rentals in Town especially challenging.

In summary, Black Point Beach Club Association permits through its zoning regulations, and Oak
Grove Beach Community Association recommends through its by-laws, rentals of 7 days or
more. Old Black Point Association and Pine Grove do not allow short-term rentals (30 days or
less) and instead permit longer-term rentals (more than 30 days). Giants Neck Heights Beach
Association does not specify the length of a short-term rental stay in its guidelines that
acknowledge these operations. Through historic practice, both short-term rentals and long-
term rentals operate in Oswegatchie Hills Club. To date, the zoning regulations of Crescent
Beach Association do not mention short-term rentals at all. Finally, at the time of this writing,
the zoning regulations of Giants Neck Beach Association were amended, effective as of March
15, 2025, to expressly define short-term rentals as “vacation rentals” with no time limitations
and to allow them as a permitted use.



                                                                                                10
It is clear that the 3 beach communities with zoning authority can adopt regulations for short-
term rentals within their borders, as Black Point Beach Association and Giants Neck Beach
Association have in fact done. As previously noted, any short-term rental zoning regulations the
Town adopts would not apply to these 3 communities. What is left to determine is whether any
of the 5 remaining beach communities without zoning authority, through their articles of
incorporation or otherwise, have the power to regulate their short-term rentals. Town Counsel
can fully research and best answer this question. The legal opinion is necessary in order to
clarify the reach of Town zoning regulations adopted. If Town regulation controls as to the 5
communities, it may be helpful then to consider and weigh their short-term rental practices in
formulating the regulations—3 of these beach communities allow short-term rentals, while 2 do
not.

On a related issue, as discussed in Section 3C of our Status Report, it is also important to keep in
mind in formulating policy the role of private deed restrictions, (restrictive covenants) as well as
homeowner’s association bylaws (adopted pursuant to Connecticut’s Common Interest
Ownership Act) that may limit or even ban short-term rentals. These are private agreements
that have their own force and effect, separate from municipal zoning and ordinances.
Accordingly, even if the Town adopts regulations allowing and restricting short-term rentals, a
deed restriction or homeowner’s association bylaw can nevertheless prevent short-term rental
operations within its purview.


SECTION 9—PRIOR USES

The discussion in Section 8 above raises the issue of how to address short-term rental
operations in existence before the adoption of regulations by zoning or through an ordinance.
As the Committee has discussed with Town Attorney Zamarka, imposing any restrictions by
either method creates the issue. It is important to point out that the ordinance suggested in
Section 7 above, establishing a registration system, really does not impose restrictions. Having a
registration period (i.e., 90 days) for existing rentals following the enactment of this ordinance
would likely suffice.

In Section 1.54 of our zoning regulations, a prior use is a “non-conforming use”, defined as “one
which existed lawfully on the date of (the) regulations or any amendment (thereto) became
effective, which fails…to conform to the present regulations.” Allowing a pre-existing non-
conforming use, commonly known as “grandfathering”, requires an applicant to provide
evidence of repeated use and intention. As noted in Section 2 above, a very recent and local
example of this analysis for a short-term rental may be found in the decision of the Pine Orchard
Association Zoning Board of Appeals in Branford, finding that Mr. Wihbey could not provide
evidence to support his grandfathering claim.

If the Town adopts short-term zoning regulations such as those suggested in Sections 5 and 6
above, grandfathering claims are to be expected. Some conversions to longer or long-term
rentals will result. Indeed, this is a common outcome and one that we have observed locally, for


                                                                                                 11
example, in Lyme. Nevertheless, the grandfathering claims will require staff time and resources
to resolve.


SECTION 10—DRAFT RECOMMENDATION 5—ENFORCEMENT:

         ESTABLISH AN ENFORCEMENT PROCESS THAT IS EFFECTIVE AND AFFORDABLE

In our discussions, Committee members have all concluded that the enforcement of any short-
term rental regulations that the Town adopts will present an ongoing challenge and expense.
Members are in agreement that enforcement must be strong, workable, and financially feasible.

As highlighted in our Status Report, we know from data provided directly to us by Granicus that
where a short-term rental registration program exists, less than 10% of owners voluntarily
register and pay all of their taxes. This is not a promising statistic for any regulatory program,
particularly one that establishes several requirements for operation. Committee members
spoke to a few local Zoning Officers about their short-term rental enforcement experiences who
indicated mostly positive results. Going forward, it may be helpful if the Town stays informed as
to the enforcement results in the Town of Groton and in Preston, as their recent regulations
have features that we may wish to adopt.

Enforcement, along with administering a permitting process, will require staff and other
resources. Our Zoning Officer Mulholland has stated that the Zoning Department would be
prepared to take on the responsibility, perhaps with some additional office help. It is important
to note that staff would have the use of the digital data bases of platforms such as Airbnb or
VRBO to determine listings and possible non-compliance. The Committee suggests that a
financial analysis be made of the cost to the Town of implementing proposed regulations. Fees
collected and fines imposed should at least offset expenses related to staff and equipment.

Most importantly, penalties for non-compliance must be significant. Fortunately, zoning
enforcement affords the power of an immediate cease-and-desist order, coupled with
accumulated fines. Further, a short-term rental permit can be paused, revoked or not renewed
if applicable regulations are not followed.


SECTION 11—DRAFT RECOMMENDATION 6—POLICY PURPOSE:

              INCLUDE A STATEMENT OF PURPOSE IN THE ZONING REGULATIONS
                              AND ORDINANCE ADOPTED

As outlined in Section 4 above, the Committee collected information, established goals, and
drafted its recommendations to meet needs and have purpose. This process has been
especially helpful in formulating policy suggestions to address the complexities of the issue of
short-term rentals in our Town. With so much to weigh and so many possible approaches, the


                                                                                               12
Committee recommends that any zoning regulations or ordinance adopted clearly include a
statement of purpose at the outset.

To consider as examples are the short-term rental zoning regulations of Lyme and the short-
term rental ordinance of Bozrah that include statements of purpose aligning with the
restrictions each establishes. It is evident from these statements what objectives are
prioritized.

From the draft recommendations and discussion provided in this Report, amended zoning
regulations to allow and limit short-term rentals, if adopted in East Lyme, might include a
statement of purpose that expresses the following priorities:

       (1) To recognize short-term rentals as an alternative to traditional lodging options
       (2) To ensure public safety in the operation of short-term rentals
       (3) To maintain stability and a sense of community in residential neighborhoods
       (4) To deter potential adverse impacts of short-term rental operations
       (5) To encourage the most appropriate use of land throughout the Town, consistent with
           the remaining provisions of our Zoning Regulations

Similarly, the ordinance recommended in this Report to complement the new zoning regulations
may also include a statement of purpose that sets forth priorities such as:

       (1) To enhance the efficiency of our Police and Fire Department response to emergency
           and other calls
       (2) To monitor the number of short-term rentals operating in Town and alter policy as
           necessary
       (3) To improve compliance with our short-term rental zoning regulations


SECTION 12—CONCLUSION

Through much research, public opinion, discussion, and deliberation, the Committee has
provided considerable information and specific recommendations to the Town to meet the
policy challenge of addressing short-term rentals. Using zoning regulations and an ordinance,
the policy proposed in this Report will require a joint effort of the Zoning Commission and the
Board of Selectmen.

Indeed, the Committee has tried to respect zoning as the primary way to regulate land use,
including regulating short-term rentals. Further, our recommended approach does not interfere
with the zoning authority held by 3 of our beach communities. It also adheres to the ruling in
the Wihbey case and follows from our own conclusion about the need to specifically address
short-term (and other) rentals in our zoning regulations. Nevertheless, there is an important
role for an ordinance to complement new zoning regulations, as well as for our existing noise
and parking ordinances.


                                                                                                  13
It is our hope that the policy East Lyme finally adopts benefits the Town as a whole and
positively impacts the greatest number of residents for the foreseeable future. The draft
recommendations the Committee has made are designed with this policy principle in mind. The
key recommendations are:

       (1) Allow short-term rentals and limit their growth.
       (2) Amend our current zoning regulations to define and regulate short-term rentals,
           including establishing a permitting process.
       (3) Adopt an ordinance to complement new short-term rental zoning regulations.
       (4) Determine whether incorporated beach communities without zoning authority can
           regulate short-term rentals.
       (5) Establish an enforcement process that is effective and affordable.
       (6) Include a statement of purpose in the zoning regulations and ordinance adopted.

Further, there are a number of related recommendations with details offered throughout the
Report intended to help structure an entire policy on short-term rentals.

The Committee looks forward to the upcoming Public Hearing. Our members are open to
additional public opinion and information that we will consider in finalizing our
recommendations.

                            ‘‘roomer[s], boarder[s] or lodger[s]’’ from the definition
                            of ‘‘family’’ and, therefore, from being appropriate users
                            of the single-family homes in the district. Pine Orchard
                            Assn. Zoning Regs., § XIII (1994). The parties agree that,

                               in a [single] room of another’s property or with a family
                               in that property and who may receive regular meals

                               regular meals and lodging’); id., p. 731 (‘lodger’ is
                               defined as ‘roomer’); [Black’s Law Dictionary (11th Ed.

                               clusion that the 1994 regulations did not intend to allow
                               for short-term, transient uses of the properties subject
                               to the regulations. There would be no purpose in exclud-
                               ing the short-term use of one room in a home but
                               allowing for the short-term use of the home itself. A
                               more sensible reading of the 1994 regulations, then, is
                               that they prohibit both types of transient uses in favor
                               of uses that have a degree of permanence and stability
                               that is in keeping with the whole community or district.

                               in the context of the POA’s entire zoning regulatory
                               scheme, it is clear that, as used in the 1994 regulations,
                               both terms unambiguously mandate that the property
                               be used with some degree of permanence. Such a con-

         *AN ORDINANCE CREATING A LICENSING PROCEDURE
        FOR SHORT-TERM RENTAL PROPERTIES IN THE TOWN OF
                     BOZRAH, CONNECTICUT

        Section 1 – Purpose.
        It is the intent of this Ordinance to accommodate the desire of certain property owners in the
        Town of Bozrah, Connecticut to rent all or a portion of a residential dwelling or detached
        structure on a short-term basis and to establish appropriate requirements to mitigate the
        disruption that short-term rental of these spaces may have on a neighborhood.
        This ordinance is not intended to regulate residential property rentals that occur on a longer
        basis, for the purpose of providing a primary residence.


        Section 2 – Definitions.
        For the purpose of this Ordinance, the following words and phrases shall have the meaning
        ascribed herein.
        “Dwelling Unit” – Any single domicile providing complete, independent living facilities for one
        or more persons, including bathing and sanitary facilities, and provisions for living, cooking, and
        sleeping.
        “Hosted Rental” –A Short-term Rental that is the primary residence of its owner, said owner of
        the property being evidenced by the records of the Tax Assessor. The owner of the Hosted
        Rental will be present at a time when it is rented. A property that is owned by a company or

                                                        1

      corporation will have a designated representative (owner) specified in the permit when issued,
      which designated representative will be present at a time when it is rented.
       “Off-street Parking Area” – An area located on the property of a Short-term Rental designed and
      used for the temporary parking of motor vehicles, including all-weather surfaced off-street
      parking areas, garages, or private driveways.
      “Short-term Rental” – A dwelling unit or portion thereof, or all or a portion of an accessory
      structure on a residential lot that contains sleeping quarters but does not meet the definition of
      dwelling unit, that is rented for compensation, direct or indirect, in currency or in kind, to
      occupants for a term of twenty-one (21) or fewer days at a time, with no consecutive rental
      agreements allowed with the same tenants. For the purposes of this Ordinance, Short-term
      Rentals do not include Bed & Breakfasts, Hotels, or Motels as defined by, and in compliance
      with, the Zoning Regulations of the Town of Bozrah.
      “Short-term Rental Enforcement Official” – The First Selectman and/or an employee appointed
      by the Board of Selectmen for an indefinite term, authorized to administer and enforce this
      Ordinance. The Short-term Rental Enforcement Official may issue or rescind licenses, undertake
      enforcement proceedings, issue fines, and undertake any other task necessary to carry out this
      Ordinance.
      Section 3 – Permit Requirements for Short-term Rentals.
      A. Permit Required. Any individual, partnership, or business entity owning residential property
      in the Town of Bozrah that is a Hosted Rental and wishing to use all or a portion of the Hosted
      Rental property as a Short-term Rental must first obtain a permit from the Short-term Rental
      Enforcement Official. All current Short-term Rentals must apply for a permit within fifteen (15)
      days of the effective date of this Ordinance or otherwise cease all rentals at their property until a
      permit is obtained. No more than one permit for a Short-term Rental may be held by the same
      individual, partnership, or entity at one time, regardless of the number of properties held by that
      individual, partnership, or entity in the Town of Bozrah. A permit shall apply to the owner of a
      property and not to the property itself. A permit is valid for one (1) year and is not transferrable.
      B. Permit Application Requirements. An application for a Short-term Rental permit shall contain
      sufficient information to allow the Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer to judge its
      consistency with requirements of this section. Minimum permit application requirements are as
      follows:
             1. Completed and signed Short-term Rental permit application form. Applicants are
             required to respond to all inquiries in a form created to carry out the application process.
             2. Paid permit fee. The fee for a Short-term Rental permit application shall be $200.
             3. Proof of insurance. The property owner shall maintain an up-to-date certificate of
             insurance documenting that insurance policy for the property covers its use as a Short-
             term Rental. A copy of the certificate of insurance shall be provided.

     B. Information Packet. An Information Packet shall be prepared and submitted as part of an
     application package. The Information Packet shall be available to renters of a Short-term Rental.
     The Information Packet shall consist of:
            1. Maximum allowed occupancy (see Section 4.C).
            2. Noise and use restrictions.
            3. Location(s) of off-street parking area(s).
            4. Location(s) of trash receptacles.
            5. Primary Point of Contact and Alternative Point of Contact information.

            7. Explanation of the renters’ responsibility not to create a disturbance perceptible off-
            property.
            8. Notification of the renters’ responsibility to comply with this ordinance.
     C. Occupancy Limit. The maximum occupancy shall be prescribed at the time of application by
     the Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer. The maximum occupancy of the Short-term Rental
     shall be either all members of the immediate family (children and parents only) of the person
     renting the Short-term Rental or 5 unrelated persons, including the owner or Primary Point of

     D. Restrictions on Use. Short-term Rentals are meant to provide temporary lodging in a
     residential setting. Short-term Rentals may not be used for any of the following purposes:
            1. Receptions for weddings or other gatherings and/or receptions.
            2. Banquets, fundraisers, or events sponsored by a business or other agency, such as
            corporate retreats.
            3. Concerts.
            4. Parties, including but not limited to bachelor/bachelorette parties and reunions, which
            create a perceptible off-site impact to other properties. Such off-site impact includes loud
            noise, trespass, or lewd behavior.
     E. Parking. Guests of a Short-term Rental shall not park on the public street and shall only park
     within any off-street parking area servicing the Short-term Rental property.
     F. Nuisances. Disruptive noise is not allowed. It is the intent of this ordinance to allow Short-
     term Rentals to occur in a manner consistent with the quiet nature of the Town of Bozrah’s
     residential areas.
     G. Compliance with Other Ordinances and Regulations. Properties used as a Short-term Rental
     shall be in good standing with all other town ordinances and regulations, including the Zoning
     Regulations.
     H. Inspection. A Short-term Rental is subject to inspection by the Short-term Rental
     Enforcement Officer, with a minimum twenty-four (24) hour notice to the owner of the Short-
     term Rental. Such inspections may be made to determine compliance with this Ordinance.


     Section 5 – Administration.
     A. Permit Application Process. An application for a Short-term Rental permit must be made by
     the owner(s) of the property as evidenced by the records of the Tax Assessor.
            1. Review by the Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer. The Short-term Rental
            Enforcement Officer shall review all permit applications received, including all
            supporting documentation required by this Ordinance, and render a decision on the
            application within fifteen (15) days of date the application is filed at the Bozrah Town
            Hall.
            2. Application Approval. A permit application may only be approved if it meets the
            requirements of this Ordinance. The Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer may request
            additional information from the applicant if such information is needed to determine the
            permit application’s consistency with this Ordinance. After a permit is issued by the

            Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer, a Short-term Rental shall remain in compliance
            with the terms of this ordinance.
            3. Application Denial. A permit application may be denied on the following grounds:
                    a. Insufficient information is received within the fifteen- (15) day decision period.
                    Such denial shall be without prejudice and an applicant may re-apply for a permit
                    with additional information requested by the Short-term Rental Enforcement
                    Officer.
                    b. The proposed Short-term Rental does not meet one or more requirements of
                    this Ordinance, including the requirement that each rental be a Hosted rental.
                    c. The applicant is delinquent on property taxes for the subject property.
                    d. The subject property has outstanding zoning, wetlands, health code, building
                    code, or fire code violations.
     B. Violations. The Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer is charged with enforcing this
     ordinance. The Short-term Rental Enforcement Officer may revoke a permit for a Short-term
     Rental found to be in violation of the provisions of this Ordinance. The Short-term Rental
     Enforcement Officer may commence legal action and/or issue fines in order to remediate a
     violation of this Ordinance. Additionally, any person who violates any provision of this
     ordinance may receive a citation and be fined Two Hundred Fifty ($250.00) Dollars. Each day
     on which a violation occurs or continues after the time for correction of violation given in any
     order has elapsed shall be considered a separate violation of this ordinance.
     C. Operating without a Permit. A Short-term Rental in operation without a permit is unlawful
     and found to be in violation of this Ordinance.


     D. Severability. If any provision of this Ordinance shall be held invalid by a court having
     competent jurisdiction, such invalidity shall not affect any of the other provisions of this
     Ordinance that can be given effect without the invalid provision and for this purpose the
     provisions of this Ordinance are hereby declared severable.
     E. Conflict. All ordinances or parts of ordinances in conflict with this ordinance are hereby
     repealed.

                    § 315-8.3. Short-term rentals (STRs). [Amended 9-12-2022]
                    A.   Purpose of short-term rental regulations. This section defines the administration and
                         standards for all short-term rental activity for no more than 30 consecutive calendar days.
                         These standards include all such short-term rentals, including those labeled as "bed-and-
                         breakfast." This section supersedes the previous regulations, § 315-8.3, Bed-and-breakfast
                         establishments. These regulations are intended to:
                         (1) Maintain the tranquility of the Town's rural neighborhoods for all residents, particularly
                             with regard to lighting, noise, parking, parties, and other activities that interfere with
                             residents' quiet enjoyment of their neighborhoods.
                         (2) Enable homeowners to derive some extra income from their primary residence, with the
                             goal of keeping properties intact.
                         (3) Regulate all short-term rental activity as defined by these regulations in order to
                             minimize the off-site impact of short-term rentals, and to support public health standards
                             in neighborhoods.
                         (4) Reinforce public safety standards by proscribing parking along streets and other public
                             areas.
                         (5) Better ensure that the owners and hosts of short-term rental properties have clear
                             understandings of their legal responsibility to oversee and control the occupants of
                             short-term rentals.
                         (6) Be consistent with the vision, policies and recommendations of Lyme's most recent Plan
                             of Conservation and Development, and to encourage the most appropriate use of land.
                    B.   Permitted short-term rental activities.
                         (1) No property in the Town of Lyme shall be used as a short-term rental establishment as
                             defined by these regulations until the appropriate authority of the Town of Lyme issues
                             a zoning permit or a special permit, as the case might be, to authorize such use.
                                (a) A zoning permit shall be required for a premises on a conforming lot and having
                                    three or fewer guest rooms, at which there may be no more than six overnight
                                    guests during any rental period.
                                (b) A special permit shall be required for any short-term rental:
                                     [1] To have more than six overnight guests.
                                     [2] For a short-term rental on a nonconforming lot, regardless of the number of
                                         guest rooms and/or overnight guests.
                                     [3] A short-term rental containing more than three but not more than six guest
                                         rooms. Such a short-term rental may serve no more than 12 guests at a time.
                                (c) Any application for a zoning permit or a special permit shall comply with all
                                    application requirements set out in these regulations.
