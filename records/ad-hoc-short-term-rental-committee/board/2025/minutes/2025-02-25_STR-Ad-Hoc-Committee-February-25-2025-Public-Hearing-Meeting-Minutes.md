      He's here tonight advocating for short-term rentals and defending property rights, fairness, and the rule of
      law.
      The Committee's proposal seems very restrictive-legally dangerous, prejudiced, and inconsistent with
      established legal precedent.
      ln the Wihbey case, the court ruled clearly and decisively that short-term rentals are permissible unless
      explicitly prohibited by zoning regulations.
      The court affirmed that time restrictions on rentals cannot be inferred;they must stated outright. Despite
      this, the Committee's draft report proposes measures seem designed to bypass and undermine this rule.
      The Committee has proposed limiting the growth of short-term rentals, caps, zoning, and minimum stay,
      which seems like an indirect attempt to prohibit short-term rentals without directly stating it.
      These measures create barriers so high that short-term rentals will become unworkable in East Lyme.
      The Committee's preference for long-term and suggestion to restrict short-term rentals in specific zones
      appears to defy the Wihbey decision.
      The court ruled that short-term rentals are a legitimate residential land use and that no temporal distinction
      is required unless specified in the zoning code.
      The Committee seems determined to reframe short-term as commercial operations.pushing them out of
      residential areas.
      This seems inconsistent with the law, unfair, and discriminatory against short-term rentals.
      The licensing burdens being proposed, such as24-7 contact information, application processes, andthe
      required permits seem overly restrictive and barring to short-term rental operation.
      He hopes that they instead choose to work towards a more reasonable regulatory framework that aligns
      with both the state law and respects the rights of all citizens.
