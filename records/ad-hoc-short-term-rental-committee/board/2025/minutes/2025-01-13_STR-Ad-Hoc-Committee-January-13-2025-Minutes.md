 5-1. Preferred Course of Action

    Note Ms. Santoro discussed Pine Grove noting its unique situation given that they don't have their own
   zoning, and how further legal input is still needed in regard to this community. She added that she can
   appreciate the point that the legal outlook of STR(s) is an ever changing and evolving issue. Mr. Formica
   observed that legislation is a moment in time.

    Note Mr. Farrugia's summary detailed how four votes were cast in favor of a partial prohibition, which
   would grandfather all currently running STRs in the town and prohibit the operation of any new ones. Five
   votes were cast in favor of approving STRs through ordinance and zoning regulations. Equal treatment of
   STRs and LTRs garnered three votes. There was only one vote in favor of approving STRs with zoning
   regulations. Both a partial prohibition and an ordinance with zoning regulations were discussed as feasible
   option.

    Note There was a debate on implementing a partial ban on rentals, with some favoring it as a way to
   control community impact.
   When discussing the potential for a complete ban or a partial one, a 30-day definition was utilized with the
   consideration of growth limits like 5% or 2%. Ms. Santoro advocated for allowing beach communities to
   self-govern, and noted she was not opposed to Ms. Thurlow's idea of permitting new STRs in mixed use or
   commercial zones.

    Note Mr. Farrugia noted how these options are not mutually exclusive, and Mr. McIntire wondered how any
   of these options can be entertained without having a registration system in place.

    Note The Committee discussed how an ordinance can simply be a reference back to zoning.


    Note Ms. Thurlow said she thinks the ordinance component should cover the one size fits all items- noise,
   parking, and registration.

   Note Ms. Santoro noted that given the Wihbey decision, they have to act, and do something, since it really
   weakened the current position of the town.

    Note Mr. Formica replied that he thinks the Wihbey decision is secondary, zoning has to recognize long-
   term, academic, and short-term rentals in their definitions at a minimum.

    Note Ms. Santoro said between signage for rentals, inferring these things in the regulations, or not inferring
   them, they have to clearly state what they want in our zoning regulations.

    Note Ms. Thurlow advocated for allowing beach communities to self-govern, especially in zoning matters, if
   they have their own zoning.

    Note Ms. Thurlow highlighted the difference between short-term and long-term rentals, noting that long-
   term renters become part of the community. She alluded to the public commentary they heard at the
   previous meeting, and the example given from the Giant’s Neck Heights community.

    Note Mr. Formica replied that he's always had an issue with this idea given that he has many individuals
   who repeatedly use his short-term rental, and at least 50% are here for a reason, and are invested in the
   community in some way, which he briefly detailed and described.

    Note The conversation touched on the overlap between short-term and long-term rentals and how this
   affects regulation.

5-2. Limits on Number of STRs Permitted

    Note Mr. Beachy noted that 5 members are in favor of some kind of cap while 2 members are not. Mr.
   Formica said given Mr. Cellino's response, they're evenly split, more or less.

    Note Ms. Santoro discussed how the town would need updated data in order to choose a cap number and
   methodology. She added resources such as Granicus would be helpful. Mr. Formica discussed how annual
   reports from various town departments can facilitate in collecting data and aid in determining if there are
   any issues rental wise.

    Note Ms. Thurlow said she doesn't like the idea of a town wide cap since all the STRs could end up being
   located on the shoreline. Mr. Farrugia said this could lead to an administrative quagmire for neighborhoods.

    Note Ms. Santoro noted that Attorney Zamarka didn't recommend caps due to the potential legal
   challenges.

    Note Mr. Farrugia said only allowing a party one rental property is another way of limiting the number.

5-3. Minimum Nights Stay at an STR

    Note The Committee discussed the difference between LTRs and STRs and Ms. Santoro said they're
   continuing to use the state definition of 30 days or under for STRs.

    Note Mr. Farrugia's summary illustrated that 5 votes were cast for establishing no minimum number of
   rental days, a 30-day minimum for STRs received 4 votes, a 3-day minimum stay, 2 votes, and a 7-day
   minimum received 1 vote.

    Note Mr. Farrugia discussed the overlap between short-term and long-term rentals and how they are
   regulated. Mr. McIntire emphasized that there is nothing wrong with recommending that LTRs be looked
   into as well, despite it not being part of their charge.

5-4. Permitting Process

    Note Mr. Beachy observed that almost everyone seems to be in favor of a permit system with a safety
   inspection. Mr. Formica discussed how he's in favor of a registration system and not a permit system.
   Permits lead to more fees and overregulation.

    Note Ms. Santoro and Mr. McIntire discussed the importance of a permit system to understand the scale
   of rental issues.

    Note Mr. McIntire emphasized that this system could be used as a tool; if there is a repeat offense by a
   landlord, the permit can be revoked. Mr. Farrugia and Ms. Santoro agreed that it could serve as a beneficial
   tool.

    Note Ms. Santoro said in terms of annual rental permit information, they're not looking to be too intrusive.


    Note Ms. Santoro referred to specific pages in their status report outlining permit requirements, including
   registration, contact information, and fire inspections. The need for formal agreements in the permitting
   process was discussed as a means of ensuring compliance.

5-5. Occupancy

    Note Mr. Farrugia's summary reported how there was unanimous agreement that occupancy for rentals
   should be based on the number of bedrooms on file with the Tax Assessor’s office. The Committee also
   was unified in favor of prohibiting rentals to minors.
