5. Committee Review & Editing of Draft Final Report/Recommendations to Board of
Selectmen

  Note The Committee reviewed and edited a draft report on short-term rentals, discussing its structure, content,
 and recommendations.
 Some of the following was highlighted and discussed:
 The need to consider competing interests- the rights of residents and the rights of landlords and renters.
 The complexity of the situation.
 The current regulations in place for single-family dwellings with more than 6 people and the Fire Marshal
 involvement required in such cases.
 Regulations associated with multi-family dwellings.
 The survey and methodology utilized for the report were reviewed again.
 It included questions about various methods for regulating short-term rentals.
 Methods 1, 2, and 3 were specifically addressed.
 Questions about limiting short-term rental locations in certain districts/areas were also included.
 The report serves as an addendum to the previous report submitted to the Board of Selectmen in April of 2024
 https://eltownhall.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/STR-Committee-Status-Report-4_24-final.pdf.

  Note The Committee reviewed the structure of the Draft Report and some of the following was noted:
 The purpose and scope are clearly defined.
 The methodology section explains the process- informal discussions and outlines followed by evidence
 questions and the survey.
 The Committee's initial goals are regularly revisited throughout the report.
 The report aims to rationalize the different viewpoints- the majority opinions, the minority opinions, and
 reasons for each perspective.
 The main recommendations highlighted in red.
 12 additional sub-recommendations included.
 Considering a visual summary of recommendations- bullet points will be especially helpful when doing a
 presentation for the BOS.

 Note The Committee Members made some of the following comments and edits:
Mr. Beachy believes the draft captures what they've been discussing.
Mr. Farrugia drew attention to the work done by Old Lyme and how they wrote a kind of mission statement
which he is partial to since it's inclusive, broadly written, and balances competing interests.
Mr. Farrugia said in terms of page 10 and Oswegatchie Hills, there's no jurisdiction to allow it or not allow it;
there is merely a long-established history of STRs.
Simply put, they're a social club with no jurisdiction.
Mr. Farrugia and Mr. Formica drew attention to page 6, they can codify Noank's position bust it should also be
clear that Noank isn't a town.
Mr. Formica said he still maintains that permits are more reactive than proactive, while Ms. Thurlow argued
that at least a permit can be revoked if there is a problem.
Mr. Formica stressed the importance of treating people the same regardless of the type of rental, while Mr.
Beachy maintained that their charge was only concerned with short-term rentals.
Mr. McIntire said utilizing the existing digital databases will aid greatly with enforcement and makes it easier
than it would have been in years past.
He thinks passing an ordinance when the regulations don't permit any rentals at all makes the town look
ridiculous.
Mr. Formica thinks it should be clarified what the "red" means anytime it's used.
Mr. Formica called attention to page 6, the 3rd paragraph, which discusses Bozrah and Lyme. He thinks there
are vast differences between those communities and ours, such as the lack of beaches they possess; the
differences in our demographics should be noted in the draft.
Ms. Santoro said In East Lyme there are short-term rentals found throughout town, not just in beach areas.
The concentration varies by neighborhood type but despite differences between towns, consistency is found in
nature of problems faced.
A suggestion was made to include introductory statement acknowledging differences but highlighting common
challenges.
Mr. Formica also expressed concern about the 2 people per bedroom language used at the bottom of page 7
and gave the example of a couple with a crib.
Mr. McIntire said occupancy on number of bedrooms is indicated on Assessor’s field card.
Norm Peck, a member of the East Lyme Zoning Commission who was in attendance, suggested calling it the
"density rate."
Mr. Formica found this problematic, and Ms. Thurlow suggested taking out the "2 people" per bedroom and
adopting an appropriate density rate.
Mr. Formica said he doesn't think they need to quantify it at all.
Mr. Formica asked about the permitting section on page 9 and if they're recommending that fees be
established?
Ms. Santoro replied that she can clarify this section.
Mr. Formica drew their attention to page 10, the last line of the 2nd paragraph noting that Giants Neck Beach
changed their zoning regulations in January to permit STRs.
Mr. Formica said he's in favor of a simple registration system by ordinance. He also thinks the conclusion
paragraph on page 12 should say "rentals" and not limit itself to "short-term rentals."
Mr. Peck added that zoning deals with all land use- residential, retail, and home rentals are the same use as
residential. He thinks there's a limit to the regulations being permissive, that if it's not mentioned, it’s not
permitted. He said that is kind of blurred, and cited examples of a lemonade stand, ice cream stand, or using a
bow and arrow in the back yard.
There is potential to handle registration through assessor’s or zoning office, to assist in enforcement.

Note Ms. Thurlow said she loves the idea of a mission statement that Mr. Farrugia referred to.

  Note The Committee further discussed use of an Ordinance and Zoning and some of the following key points
 were highlighted by Members:
 An ordinance should be intentionally narrow, not containing bulk of restrictions and regulations.
 It should be complementary to, not contradictory to, existing zoning.
 The main focus should be on registration and contact information for short-term rentals.
 Three communities already have their own zoning.
 Suggestion to include a definition of short-term rentals in zoning regulations.
 Ms. Santoro noted that Attorney Zamarka warned about potential non-conformity issues and an ordinance may
 create a class of owners with short-term rentals falling outside regulations.
 Enforcement will allow a span of time for compliance.

 been long term rentals, although they are beyond the committee’s charge.”
 Suggestion to define and regulate long-term rentals in town code.
 10 attachments/exhibits to be included including the public hearing notice.
