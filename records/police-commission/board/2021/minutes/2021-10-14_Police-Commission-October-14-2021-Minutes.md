6. Lieutenants' Reports
     Lt. Mike Macek reported that MHQ is still working on one vehicle which is close to being complete; he noted
that currently they are the only outfitters that are getting vehicles in-house. He reported that he has reached out to
both Columbia Ford and Crest Ford with no response; however, he noted that the new owners of Crest Ford have
expressed a desire to work with the police department so he will reach out to them to begin the conversation. He
stated that we must stick to the current plan of getting the two vehicles outfitted by MHQ, but that they need to
pursue other options for future vehicles. Mr. Nickerson stated that in this circumstance the purchasing policy
prohibits us from initiating the purchase of another vehicle until it is approved in the next budget cycle, but
currently we are already paying for the vehicles approved in the last budget cycle that we still do not have our hands
on. Chief Finkelstein stated that we were already behind because of MHQ's overloaded schedule, and now the parts
shortage caused by the pandemic has amplified the situatiorr. Lt. Macek stated that he has begun looking at
switching over to the Chevy Tahoe, which is a vehicle that is much easier to get and outfit, He reported that the
price on the law enforcement ready model of the Tahoe is a little higher than the Ford, approximately $2,000 to
$3,000, but we will see some savings in that the newly purcliased in-car cameras and computers can be outfitted into
any new vehicle. Lt. Macek reported that he has confirmed with a dealership in the Torington area that we would
be able to secure two 2021Tahoes if we decide to pursue that; he noted that MHQ is not able to offer 2021 vehicles
at this point, just model year 2020.
