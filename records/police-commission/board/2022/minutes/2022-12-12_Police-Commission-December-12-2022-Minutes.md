4a. Pine Grove - Speed Hump Request
DISCUSSION: Chief Finkelstein stated that the Police Department would discourage the use of
speed humps, but that the most impact is to the fire department and their equipment and
access to that tight neighborhood. He reported that historically, speed humps encourage higher
rates of speed in between the humps as drivers speed up and then slow down to go over the
hump. Mr. Powers asked if adding stop signs would reduce speed, and the Chief responded that
increased number of stop signs, as in the Black Point neighborhood, also encourage higher
speeds from one sign to the next. As recommended by the Town Engineer, stop signs should
not be used to try to control speed. Chief Finkelstein noted the Town Engineer's statement that
he believes the traffic to be inter-neighborhood as there is no thru traffic in that area, and he
agrees with that assessment and believes that the neighborhood association should encourage a

Commission would be open to receiving more information regarding this request, such as type of
speed humps that are being proposed and the anticipated cost of installing and upkeeping such,

MOTTON (2)
Mr. Perkins MOVED that as per the advice of the Town Engineer and the Chief of Police, and
because of the reasons communicated above, the Police Commission does not recommend speed
humps be installed in the area of Broadway in the Pine Grove neighborhood,
Seconded by Ms. Hitchery. Motion passed 5-0.

5a. Speed Limits in Attawan Beach
DISCUSSION: Mr. Donald Bridge, Jr,, Attawan Beach Association President, 13 Goodwin Street,
was invited to participate in this discussion by Chairman Price. Mr. Bridge referenced a letter he
sent to the Board of Police Commissioners dated September 15,2022, attached hereto as
Exhibit #1, Ms. Hitchery inquired if the Association had queried the entire membership; Mr.
Bridge stated that they did not send out a notice to everyone, but that he has heard no
objection to this request from anyone in the association. The reasoning behind the request by
the applicant is twofold; the roads are narrow and relatively short in distance, and in the
summertime are filled with pedestrians walking down to the beach and the other beach
communitibs have already had their speed limits reduced to 20 mph, so this would put Attawan
Beach in line with the other beach communities, Chief Finkelstein confirmed that there have
been no extraordinary issues because of speed or accidents in this neighborhood, but it would
not hurt to send the application for the reduction to OSTA (Office of State Traffic
Ad m inistration),

All seven roads are paved and maintained by the Town of East Lyme. There is no on-street parking
allowed. The association has roughly ninety residential homes within its boundaries. Residents'
 main destinations are the three beaches referred to by our residents as the main beach, boat beach
 and the south beach as well as the jetty that includes a boat dock. All of these areas are on the east
 side of Attawan Ave. The residents (adults and children of all ages) must walk to the beaches and
jetty as there are no parking areas in the association. Residents must park their vehicles on their
 own property.

Why lower the speed limit?

    - Roadways are narrow
    - Roadways are short
    - Pedestrian traffic from residentso homes to the beach and back
    - Pedestrian traffic from residents' homes to the jetty and back
    - Pedestrian traffic includes young people focused on beach/boat fun
    - Increased pass-through traffic; cars, motorcycles, bicycles, and pedestrians
        (walking/running), who travel along Attawan Ave past the beaches enjoying the beautiful
        views of Niantic Bay

The Attawan Beach Association Board of Governors believes that the reduction of the speed limit
to twenty (20) miles per hour is warranted and consistent with similar neighboring beaches.
Furthermore, the safety and well-being of all East Lyme residents using the narrow and relatively
