Discussion about using town's Hope St. parking lot (behind the Black Sheep Pub) and running a
hay wagon between OFD sites. Concerns about traffic on Main St. (rte.156) prompted plan to use
Hope, Grand, & Smith Streets as route.
Beekeeping: Mark Christianson said he'd taken the 11-17-2016 draft of the proposed East Lyme
beekeeping ordinance and it's incorporated CT Beekeeper's Association “Best Practices” to the
E.L. Conservation Commission. The E.L.C.C. suggested that it include the scientific name of the
bees (Apis Mellifera) or European Honeybee, and also, that under conditions under which
limitations will not be imposed #2 be clarified as to what happens if undeveloped land converts to
developed land. Consensus is that in such cases the beekeeper will comply with any restriction
the new condition brings into effect. Steve Dinsmore said he will make those amendments to the
ordinance.

Mark Christianson said he expects town planner Gary Geshall to get back to him about how to
best proceed with zoning applications and especially waiver of application fees since we are a town
committee.

Harbor Management Report: Steve Dinsmore reports that there is a mediation meeting scheduled
for Feb.1" 2018 for Niantic Bay Shellfish Farms, several Waterford, CT agencies, and others.
Mark Christianson reads a letter from the E.L. Conservation Commission in of support of shellfish
aquaculture in the Niantic River.

New Business:

.

Organic Gardening Course: Tom Kalal said that on the recommendation of Lisa Picarazzi he has
been developing a course in organic gardening that he, with the support of other Agribusiness.
Committee members could offer through the Parks & Recreation Commission. He proposed an 8
hour course to be divided into four 2 hour classes, and scheduled for March 6" 6-8PM , March 13"
6-8PM , May 19" 6-8PM , and July 14" 6-8PM. Enthusiastic discussion followed; Thomas Hall
offered his help as did others, Tom K. outlined course as being two classroom sessions and two
field sessions covering topics like soils, siting a garden, composting, seed selection, planting,
watering, pests, harvesting, and storage. A $95.00 fee for the course was agreed on with $10
going to Parks & Rec. for administering the course. It was noted that East Lyme has lost farmland
to development and that since many homes are located on “prime soils” they could be viewed as
smallholdings and “backyard agriculture” on such sites would help mitigate the loss of agricultural
land.

A motion was made by Mark Christianson, seconded by Sally Uden to offer a course on organic
gardening and to set the fee for it at $95.00. The Committee voted unanimously in favor of the
motion.

Backyard Chickens: The Committee discussed the merits of promoting backyard poultry
husbandry and decided to put it on hold for now so it could pursue organic gardening education
with greater focus.

Agriculture Viability Grants: Mark Christianson suggested that the Agribusiness Committee
undertake an agriculture viability study and compile a report based on it's findings as other
Connecticut towns have done. Discussion followed wherein it was noted that such studies typically
find that agricultural land contributes more to the town in direct taxes than it costs in services
whereas residential land often costs as much as 25% more in services than it pays in taxes. And
that farms have direct economic benefits to other aspects of the town such as secondary
businesses which benefit from agri-tourisim and the pastoral beauty and open space maintained
by the town’s farms in fact works to support residential property values. Thomas Hall Volunteered
to try and get copies of successful grant applications made by other towns to study in preparation
of writing our own.

Committee member Nancy Kalal was interviewed by high school student Nick Trakario who was
seeking information about how regulations affect farms East Lyme. She said she thought his

questions were astute and intelligent.
Motion to Adjourn: A motion to adjourn was made by Mark Christianson at 7:42PM and seconded
by Nancy Kalal. The committee voted unanimously in favor of adjournment.
