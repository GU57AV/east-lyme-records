     VI.NEW BUSINESS
        a. MS Teams Presentation by Lisa Stark of Newtown, CT on Use of Birth Control to Reduce
           Rodent Populations. Ms. Stark and her colleague Ms. Kocet discussed how Newtown dealt
           with rodenticides. Newtown was the first town in CT to ban the use of SGAR anti-coagulant

L
    rodenticides. Three other CT towns (Southbury, Bethel, and Farmington) followed suit.
    Winchester is in the process of doing so and Ms. Meekhoff has a copy of the town's
    proposed document. These chemicals cause serious environmental problems, with raptor
    birds and other bird, mammal, and reptile consumers of rodents subsequently ingesting
    SGARs into their systems. This results in secondary poisoning, with most of them dying. Ms.
    Stark showed a video presentation produced by "A Place Called Hope", a wildlife
    rehabilitation center in Killingworth, which provided details on this issue. Necropsies done
    at UConn with further work at Michigan State University found that in examining over 200
    expired specimens, more than 80% were positive for rodenticides. Poisons do not
    necessarily solve rodent issues as many unconstrained human activities provide food, water,
    nesting materials, and shelter. Proper sanitation and exclusion practices work better to
    control rodents than poisons. Unfortunately, many pest control company personnel are
    stuck in old habits whereby it is easier for them to place SGAR bait boxes than to address
    issues that could be corrected. Mass poisoning also may lead to a population rebound effect
    where rodent numbers increase rapidly in response. One solution is baits having rodent
    birth control chemicals, which have no secondary effects on animals consuming rodents.
    Following the video, Ms. Stark spoke. She cited a study which showed that 29 of 30 raptors
    died from rodenticides by ingesting affected specimens. Another issue is that there is no
    requirement for rodent bait boxes to be labeled. There are many records of children and
    pets being exposed to and affected by rodenticides. The used boxes also must be disposed
    of as hazardous waste and not be placed into normal waste disposal streams.

    The meeting was opened for questions for Ms. Stark and Ms. Kocet. Ms. Meekhoff asked
    what Newtown is currently doing, including rodent controls other than rodenticides. Ms.
    Stark replied that Southbury is deploying rodent contraceptive called Evolve. Newtown is
    using a new generation smart technology trap, which can humanely kill and collect up to 50
    rodents before needing disposal and reset. Mr. Deeble asked what constitutes a rodent
    infestation, which could trigger the use of SGARs. Ms. Stark said that there is no definition
    for this term, but it is an informed judgement by a pest control expert. A town health
    director might also designate an infestation. ln the town of Bethel, it is the First Selectman
    who does this. Mr. Christensen asked if the state is banning a chemical used to control
    moles. Ms. Stark did not know, but Ms. Kocet did not think moles should be poisoned as
    they also could be subsequently consumed by raptors or other animals. Ms. Heller asked
    where the new bait boxes are being used in Newtown. Ms. Stark replied they are used at the
    town's municipal complex and the senior center, which also had to improve its sanitation
    practices to limit rodents. Ms. Meekhoff thanked Ms. Stark and Ms. Kocet for their
    participation in this meeting. Ms. Stark mentioned that there will be an event called "Think
    Outside the Bait Box" about the rodenticide issue to be held in Old Saybrook on November 5
    at the Acton Library. This meeting will feature A Place Called Hope and others.

    The Commission also thanked Mr. Alex Klose for facilitating the remote meeting. Mr. Klose

The mandate of the Commission for the Conservation of Natural Resources (CCNR) is protection
of the town's natural resources, many of which are especially rich in designated Open Space.
Recently the East Lyme Land Trust has taken steps to convert several pieces of Open Space
placed in under their protection into building lots (for example, land off Cedarbrook Lane, and
land off Goldfinch Terrace). We are requesting that the Planning Commission take all necessary
actions immediately to prevent these conversions and cease any further business with the East
Lyme Land Trust since they have proved themselves to be poor land stewards.
