B, Agribusiness Subcommittee
Commissioner Christensen reported that the Kalal’s have volunteered their farm to be
utilized as a test plot for the manure management course. They have attended a
number of seminars and learned that an average of 49% of nitrogen is attributable to
human sewage (city and septic) along coastal areas in the State. In East Lyme, it is
estimated that 12% of nitrogen derives from animal manure (farm and wild).

While they are still deciding upon the exact theme, the Subcommittee will be hosting a
float for this month’s Memorial Day Parade that will highlight a honeybee hive, goats,
gardening, and tractors. A sign noting that the Subcommittee is under the CNRC
Commission was suggested.

Three booths will be reserved for Celebrate East Lyme for the Subcommittee and
Conservation for Natural Resources Commission.

Three farms located on Grassy Hill have been confirmed for Open Farm Day:
Morgan’s Backyard Chickens, Alicia Gear’s Queensway Farm (horse facility), and
Kalal’s Farm (beef, hay, honey, and, possibly, manure management and a hay ride).
The CT Farm Bureau will be partnering with the Subcommittee and help advertise the
event. Creating and posting a map indicating all of the farms in Town and stars (or
similar) indicating those farms that will be participating in Open Farm Day was
suggested.

A Beekeeping Sub-Subcommittee under the Agribusiness Subcommittee was formed.
Upon discovering that beekeeping is technically illegal in the Town of East Lyme and,
while the Zoning Commission is agreeable to allow beekeeping on two-acre zones, the
Sub-Subcommittee is in the process of creating an Apiary proposal that will allow
beekeeping on smaller parcels of land.

Motion (2): Motion made by Commissioner Nichols, seconded by
Commissioner Heller, to review and sign a letter in support of an
Apiary Ordinance. Voice vote, 5-0, all in favor. Motion carried.
