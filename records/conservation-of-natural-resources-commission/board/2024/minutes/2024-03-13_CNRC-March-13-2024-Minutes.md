     b. East Lyme NDDB Mapping. Mr. Beeman provided a brief overview of his review of East
        Lyme regulations concerning NDDB reviews. He indicated that this is a preliminary review
        since he will be meeting with Gary Goeschel to review his findings to determine if any
        Regulation requirements have been missed. He stated that that NDDB reviews stem from
        the State Endangered Species Act. Under this Act, state agencies are required to ensure that
        any activity authorized, funded, or performed by the state does not threaten the continued
        existence of endangered or threated species. ln addition, applicants for certain state and
        local permits may be required to consult with the DEEP-NDDB as part of the permit process.
        His review found that the current IWA Regulations do not specify the need for an NDDB
        review. The East Lyme Subdivision Regulation, however, does require a Biological Survey if
        conducted as a requirement of a Natural Diversity Database Review and/or any
        recommendations from DEP for avoiding or minimizing impacts to species of special concern
        and significant natural communities. The need for a Biological Review is also reflected in the
        Subdivision Application Checklist used in reviewing Planning Commission Applications.
        Further, the Zoning Regulations Section 23 (Conservation Design Development (CDD))
        requires that applicants for CDD shall follow the subdivision regulation application process.
        A question was raised regarding the definition of a sub-division. Mr. Christianson indicated
        that a subdivision is the division of a tract or parcel of land into three (3) or more parts. The
        question remains whether a NDDB review should be required on individual home lots prior
        to construction. Mr. Beeman indicated that he will provide an update to the commission
        following his discussion with Gary Goeschel.
