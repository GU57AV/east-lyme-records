    B. Methods of Measuring Chloride. Mr. Danila reported sodium cannot be measured with
       a test kit. Any meter capable of measuring sodium would cost several thousands of
       dollars.

       Chloride has many ways of being measured. One can titrate using silver nitrate, but that
       requires instrumentation and results in toxic waste. Water samples can be shipped to a
       laboratory at a cost of about S30 per test. Chloride test strips can be used but are crude
       and not very precise. He has an instrument that can measure chloride. He found many
       colorimetric/photometric instruments available for purchase, ranging from about SgS to
       54,300. He suggests determining what the problem is and creating a plan for sampling.
       Mr. Carlson suggested working with the Water & Sewer Commission. Mr. Danila stated
       he believes they perform water testing once a year per well. Mr. Carlson felt we need to
       determine where the salt is coming from. Mr. Danila added it might be better to take
       water samples where we feel there is a problem. Ms. Hardy reported this is very timely
       because this problem could become worse with more development. Mr. Danila stated
       salt does not evaporate, so ends up in streams or groundwater. Mr. Carlson added First
       Selectman Seery would like this problem taken care of. Mr. Danila stated the health
       issue is sodium. Chloride is cheaper to measure but needs to be done very carefully. Ms.
       Hardy suggested a subcommittee of this Commission to work with Water & Sewer and
       lnland Wetland.
