tv.    REPORTS AND COMMUNICATIONS
       a. Communications. Mr. Danila attended a meeting of the Fishery Advisory Council. A graduate student
       of Dr. Eric Schultz of UConn is studying alewife in Bride Lake. East Lyme has had one of the largest
       alewife populations in New England. Because of the drought this past summer, the water outflow from
       the lake was very low. The flow gradually sunk into sediment. The student showed photos of many
       dead alewife on the dewatered streambed. Mr. Danila plans to meet in July with Dr. Schultz, his
       students, another professor at UCONN, and Ben North of town staff to discuss how the town might
       better utilize its water resources.

       Some time ago, Mr. Danila participated in a remote Zoning Commission Public Hearing concerning the
       North Bride Brook Road multi-family housing development. He commented about protecting the
       alewife in Bride Brook. The development is presently in the construction phase. Early on there was a
       large plume of sediment coming off the site into Bride Brook. Mr. Goeschel, the IWA Agent, moved to
       rectify this issue. There is always an issue when you have storm water if it is not treated properly. lf
       there is no drought this summer it will help the alewife.

       b. Agribusiness Subcommittee. Mr. Christensen reported on June 10 the subcommittee had its second
       Annual Farmstead Vintage Tractor and Truck Show at the Samuel Smith Farmstead. There were L20
       visitors.

       Some commission members participated in EarthFest at McCook Point Park in May, which went well. A
       new CCNR banner was displayed at the booth which increased the commission's visibility with the
       public and many engaged in discussions about composting, chicken and bee keeping, and ways
       everyone can lessen their impact on the natural environment.

       Terry Yuhas is currently working with Middle School and High School students to get youngsters
       interested in agriculture.

       The East Lyme Giving Garden is doing well and an Open House is planned for Saturday of Labor Day
       Weekend.
