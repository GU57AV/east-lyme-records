Pollinator Pathway East Lyme Inc. report:
Networking with the Lyme Land Trust, I found Tanner Steeves, DEEP Wildlife
Biologist whose expertise is managing habitat for the New England Cottontail, a
federally classified endangered species and a sight overseer for controlled field
burns. Denise Dinsmore, Bruce Dasinger and I have a meeting with Tanner on
May 5 to discuss best management practices at Darrow for the NEC and for a
controlled burn at Darrow spring of 2026. Our Fire Marshall, William Bundy
agreed to this plan as a potential training session. The Nature Conservancy may be
brought in for this event.
PPEL and Brookside attended the UCONN Plant Science and Landscape Design
department sophomore and junior charrette presentations implementing landscape
changes for Brookside property and the adjoining hillside near the Community
Center. Following the presentation, UCONN students visited Brookside and the
Community Center to take photos and measurements. They discussed a fruit tree
orchard, foraging trails and hands on events for teenagers such as basket weaving
and wool spinning.

The E.L. Library and the Senior Center has posted a document written by PPEL
concerning the current conditions of the hillside or berm behind the Senior Center.
Many folks are asking why plants and trees are not being installed. On the advice
of Peter Picone, DEEP Wildlife Biologist, we must solarize and smother for at
least one year before any installations can occur. Lisa Timothy, Library Director,
Kristen Caramanica, Senior Center Director, Ron Bence, Building and Grounds
Supervisor and PPEL are meeting to discuss the potential of this area for a seasonal
patio for outdoor reading, bird watching, gathering space, and for an
educationaVnattxe trail beginning at the berm, continuing up the stairs to
Brookside and advancing into the woods.

I gave a 45 minute PowerPoint presentation on The Importance of Native Plants
and Insects for the Shoreline Sierra Club at the Acton Library. 45 people attended.

I attended rhe 2025 Connecticut Land Conservation Council at Wesleyan Univ.
Some key points learned were Dark Sky Laws, Nature- based solutions for Climate
Change, Connecticut's Wildlife Action Plano Impacts of Neonicotinoid
Insecticides, and a five year assessment of knotweed removal by GOSA.
