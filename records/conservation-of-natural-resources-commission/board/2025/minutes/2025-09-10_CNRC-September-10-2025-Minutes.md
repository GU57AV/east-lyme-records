Tree Condition Overview

   . Species: European beech
   r Diameter:74"
   o Current health: Overall fair condition, though the top shows decline.
   . Diseases/Issuesobserved:
         o Beech Leaf Disease (BLD): Present, though European beeches tend to tolerate it
              relatively well. Dark leaf coloration also helps mask symptoms.
           o Decay: Present within the tree; the tree has been worked on previously.
           o Root Rot: Minor presence noted.
           o Possible Beech Bark Disease: Alligator-like bark texture observed.
           o Canker: Some evidence present.
   .   Additional concerns: Soil compaction around the root flare and minor structural risks
       from deadwood in the crown.




Treatment Options Discussed

   1. Fungicide Injection (Recommended)
          o Product: Arbotect 20-S (thiabendazole hypophosphite).
          o Method: Systemic injection into the root flare (currently well exposed).
          o Purpose: Historically used for Dutch elm disease and sycamore anthracnose; now
              approved for use against BLD.
          o Effectiveness: Suppresses BLD symptoms and reduces nematode populations for
              up to two growing seasons when properly applied.
          o Timing: Best applied from full leaf expansion through late July/early August,
              before nematodes migrate into buds.
          o Estimated cost: -$600 per treatment, applied every other year.
   2. Nutrient Support
          o Potassium polyphosphate fertilizer (both injection and soil application)
             recommended to strengthen the tree's natural defenses against BLD.
   3. Soil and Root Health
          o  Root Invigoration: Use compressed air to reduce soil compaction.
          o  Mulching: Apply mulch around the root flare to protect from foot traffic, regulate
             soil temperature, and add organic matter.
   4. Structural Support & Maintenance
          o Update and replace existing cabling between the two stems.
          o Remove deadwood in the upper canopy (safety measure).
