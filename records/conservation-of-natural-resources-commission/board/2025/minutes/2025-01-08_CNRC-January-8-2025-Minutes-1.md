b. Results of eDNA sampling at selected Niantic River stormwater outfalls as performed
     by the Niantic River Watershed Committee.

    Mr. Danila reported the Niantic River Watershed Committee (NRWC) has done
    sampling in the Niantic River in order to look for eDNA, which might help determine
    what kinds of animals are introducing bacteria into stormwater entering the Niantic
    River. Sampling was done at Kiddie Beach, downstream of Stony Brook in Waterford,
    and Banning Cove, into which Latimer Brook discharges. E. coliwas found at both sites
    and Canada Geese eDNA was 50% higher than from dogs. One surprising finding was
    finding high swine eDNA at Kiddie beach. They are looking atthe garden center on
    Route 1to see if they are using swine manure that may be washed into the brook. The
    NRWC plans to contact Maureen Fitzgerald, Waterford's Environmental Planner about
    these findings.

c. Update on tree clearing in the Oswegatchie Hills. Mr. Danila reported since our last

However, I woutd tike to make it known that I did not seek authorization from any of these bodies to

great interest to atl these groups. I note that upon completion or my remarks, I witt provide a
comptete written record of my comments to this Agency's recording secretary.

The matter about which I speak has been previousty brought before this agency at the October 1,
2024 meeling by Kristin Lambert, President of Friends of the Oswegatchie Hitts Nature Preserve.
Under Pubtic Detegations, Ms. Lambert noted concerns about the excessive tree clearing occurring
on Landmark Devetopment Group's Oswegatchie Hitts property. I recenttyviewed a brief video
overf tight of this parcel that was recorded on January 1 . I provided a tink to this video via emaits to
Gary Goeschet and your chair Ms. Chantrett. I am hoping that this video might be shown at this time
to att Agency members and others in attendance. This video, which shoutd speak for itsetf, shows
further excessive tree clearing occurring on this property as we[[ as other environmentaI damages.

As the members of this agency have changed over the years, let me briefly review the circumstances of
how this egregious tree clearing has come to be. At the August L4,2OL7 Agency meeting under the
heading of "Landmark Development for a tree Farm", Attorney Doug Dubitsky (whom I note was
variously named as Dubinsky or Dubickiin the Agency's2OL7 monthly minuies)submitted an application
to this Agency for a determination of jurisdiction for a tree farm on a parcel of fully wooded land. As
stated in the minutes the applicant would "selectively pick certain areas to cut down trees and establish
new growth by planting of saplings. At this time he doesn't have plans to stump it. lf needed in the
future we may decide to remove some stumps. He is looking for a ruling under CT General Statute
22.a.4O - establishing tree farm on the property is exempt for wetland jurisdiction." Further noted in the
minutes is "not sure how much will use of the acreage for a tree farm [sic]... ln the beginning, we may
only use five acres." There were some questions by Agency members, including if the wetlands had been
delineated. Mr. Dubitsky stated that performing a survey on the 250-acre property would cost tens of
thousands of dollars and they would cut down trees based on the acreage they wanted to use. This
matter was continued to the Agency meeting in the following month.

As noted in the September 11, 20L7 Agency meeting minutes, Mr. Dubitsky sought an application for a
Determination of Non-regulated Activity for a Tree Farm at Oswegatchie Hills. I will not burden you with
the considerable back and forth found in these minutes, but only to mention a few facts. Mr. Dubitsky
was asked about the tree planting, and he responded that Landmark was looking at 5 acres, but this
could be expanded. White pine trees were to be planted. He once again noted there was no intent to
perform a wetlands survey. There was much discussion about maps provided to the Agency and where
roads would be constructed with respect to wetlands on this property. Considerable discussion ensued
over the town and state wetlands regulations with respect to wetlands road crossings, fill, replacing old
existing culverts, and permittable agricultural uses. ln essence, according to Connecticut wetlands
statutes there are agricultural exemptions with respect to wetlands activities. The conclusion of this

                                                     1
Agency was that this application was indeed a farming activity. Given the facts in hand at the time, the
Agency approved the application with several conditions. One such condition was "should our lnland
Wetlands Agent feels any of our regulations have been violated he will be able to take appropriate
action."

As part of this record, I reviewed a subsequent January 4,2019 email from East Lyme town attorney
Mark Zamarka to Bill Mulholland, East Lyme's Zoning Official. Mr. Zamarka notes that the applicant refers
to "tree clearing limits", which raised the question of whether the applicant intended to establish and
manage a forest or engage in clear cutting or logging. Mr. Zamarka then says of the latter that it "may fall
outside the definition of forestry." He recommended that the applicant clarify his intended uses and that
the town should request a site plan showing areas of clearing, re-planting, management, etc. There is a
subsequent paragraph regarding Affordable Housing District zoning and what that might mean for tree
clearing and the placement of barns or other structures proposed by the applicant, but I believe much of
this may be beyond the purview of this Agency. Mr. Zamarka concludes by stating "the permitted use of
forestry implies a level of expertise beyond landscaping or farming." He goes on to mention a
Connecticut statute defining "Commercial forest practices". He concludes that is specifically what the
applicant has proposed and further presumes that Landmark Development has retained a certified
forester for this purpose. He finishes by recommending that the name of such a forester be provided to
the town so that he or she can help clarify the scope of the proposed use for this property.

Now we have come to our present situation whereby for several years unconstrained clearcutting and
road building has been undertaken on this parcel. I will concede that although a S-acre clearing was
bandied about by the applicant in the 2017 Agency meetings, the applicant's attorney did cover himself
by stating this area could be expanded, as it apparently has been. As seen in the drone overflights of this
cleared area, although some slash remains, nearly all stumps have been removed. There are
considerable roads used by heavy equipment found on the site. Several standing pools of water are
present in this cleared area, which may be evidence for wetlands and wetlands soils. There is no
evidence that any "saplings" or "white pines" have been planted. Has a certified forester ever provided
the town with a plan for this property? I believe that words are important in any regulatory context as
they form the basis for a decision. I do not think one can seek approval for a farming operation (namely,
a stated tree farm) without any attempts to make it so. Based on the evidence on hand, I believe that the
current condition of this portion of the Oswegatchie Hills represents an attempt to circumvent East
Lyme's wetlands and perhaps zoning regulations regarding property development. I believe this and
perhaps other agricultural exemptions granted in this state with respect to wetlands to be unfortunate.
Such exemptions are certainly appropriate for working farmers and sincere agricultural uses. However,
this loophole is larger than a bulldozer, skiddel and logging truck combined. Thus, it can be used to
circumvent inland wetlands and other local regulations. I therefore urge this Agency to consider the
available evidence and take the appropriate actions to stop any further environmental degradation of
this property in Oswegatchie Hills.

Thank you for allowing me to speak this evening.
