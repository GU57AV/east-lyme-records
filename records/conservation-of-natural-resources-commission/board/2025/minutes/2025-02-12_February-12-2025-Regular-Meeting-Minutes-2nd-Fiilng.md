    VI.NEW BUSINESS
     a. Recommendations for Protecting East Lyme's Watershed. Mr. Christensen
        reported he developed a plan to rezone the north end of town after it was
        discovered salt in our wells is increasing significantly. Much pavement is being
        added as a result of development. East Lyme residents are being told to check with
        their doctor about high sodium levels in town drinking water. He added that Old
        Lyme uses sand rather than salt on their roads during winter. However, this sand is
        also getting into brooks and surface water. There is concern with run-off into the
        Niantic River. He shared photos with commission members about the East Lyme
        solar field erosion that occurred a few years ago. Erosion controls there were
        insignificant. Septic fields are also discharging nitrogen into the groundwater that
        enters streams and the Niantic River. He emailed Commission members a copy of
        Connecticut House Bill 5749, which has provisions to have nitrogen removed from
        septic fields. He recommends that the East Lyme POCD consider larger lot zoning in
        the northern part of town.

        Ms. Ashburn added that development needs to be limited in sensitive areas to
        protect our watersheds. A UCONN document clearly refers to the negative effects
        of impervious surfaces. Mr. Christensen and Ms. Heller went to a Zoning
        Commission meeting and were told the state mandates a small Aquifer Protection
        Zone when in fact it sets a minimum protected area which the town is free to
        enlarge as it sees necessary to ensure safe drinking water quality . Mr. Christensen
        referred to the Commission's Water Report that explains those laws. Ms. Ashburn
