DEEP Discussion

Chairman Carlson and Commissioner Danila met with two DEEP (Department of Energy
and Environmental Protection) representatives to discuss the aquifer protection zone. It was
confirmed that the area is a wellhead protection zone. The Commission’s understanding
regarding the direction of the aquifer is correct in that it flows from the high point in the
north to the low point in the south. Those businesses, e.g., dry cleaners, gas stations, steel
mills, etc., which utilize heavy chemicals and solvents that cannot be naturally broken down
are not permitted in the zone. Discussion ensued regarding the inclusion of the possible
growth in population and the continuation of the drought. Commissioner Heller noted that
both DEEP and USGS (United States Geological Survey) are encouraging State Drought
Committees and local communities to be aware of their groundwater levels due to the
ongoing drought. The Commission will request bi-annual or annual updates regarding the
Town’s water level.

Also discussed was the importance of having a maximum amount of clean water flushing
into the aquifers as part of the attempt to dilute any pollution, the critical and non-critical
areas of storm water runoff, and the possible need for water gardens and/or artificial
wetlands.

Chairman Carlson proposed presenting a fully documented report, with specific citations
and an appendix containing supporting documents, based on their studies and discussions.

B. Agribusiness Subcommittee
A Beekeeping Educational Class will be held on March 17, 2017 in the Olive T. Chendali
Room, East Lyme Community Center. Based on preliminary discussions, the Zoning
Commission appears to be in favor of establishing a regulation regarding beekeeping. The
town of Wallingford is also currently in the process of formulating a similar regulation,
which the Town plans to utilize in formulating theirs.

Open Farm Day will be held the day after Celebrate East Lyme. The Samuel Smith House,
Scott’s Yankee Farmer, and Foster’s Old Orchard Farm have been contacted regarding their
possible interest in participating in the event.

Foster’s Old Orchard Farm is seeking an amendment to Section 25 of the Town’s Zoning
Regulations to allow farms of 5+ acres to obtain a special permit for farm and agritourism
events, including hayrides, com mazes. The issue was met with, primarily, positive
feedback at a recent public hearing. The original proposal is in the process of being revised.

Approximately 45 people, including a member of Old Lyme’s Flood and Erosion Control
Board and a former member of Old Lyme’s Conservation Commission, attended their
Fourth Annual Potluck Party where both Chairman Carlson and Commissioner Danila as
well as the Garden Club and Betty Murphy (historic cemeteries) provided presentations.

A proposal for the establishment of solar panels on brownfields or on the roof of an existing
building, rather than on farmland, has been created by the State.
