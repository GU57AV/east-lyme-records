“SustainableCT” Program Summary

An online framework for logistical support to CT town officials looking to implement a
range of economic, environmental and cultural improvements and attractions.

Program provides grant opportunities to fund infrastructure and transportation
projects, protect the environment, and create efficiencies in housing and public services.
Program is coordinated by Eastern Connecticut State University Institute for Sustainable
energy, and funded by three CT nonprofits: the Emily Hall Tremaine Foundation,
Common Sense fund, and Hampshire Foundation (Hampshire plans to provide $250,000
for small grants to towns for climate and energy-related projects).

Each participating town becomes certified when at least one of many listed actions is
completed in each of nine different program aspects (see list below). Higher certification
levels are earned when more actions are completed.

To date 34 towns, including Waterford and Groton, have signed on to the program;
none have completed their certification process.

A goal of the program is to save towns money by helping to improve energy efficiency,
promote partnerships within and among towns, and assist with planning tools that help
avoid expensive infrastructure damage during storms and power outages.

A recent article in The New London Day discussed more details about the program

(NL, Groton eye recognition in program on sustainability , By Benjamin Kail, Day Staff Writer,
Jan 25, 2018).
