       MEMO TO:         East Lyme Zoning Commission

       RE:              Parkers Place, LLC
                        8-30g Multi-Family Affordable Residential Housing Development
                        Park Place, Niantic, CT Assessor’s Map #11.1, Lot #11

       DATE:           January 22, 2026


               This application is being submitted as an 8-30g Affordable Housing application along with a site
       plan, a zone change request and a Municipal Coastal Site Plan review. The applicant is submitting for a
       sixty (60) unit multifamily project on 6.98 acres on property identified in the application as being on the
       westerly wide of Park Place, further identified as Assessor’s Map #11.1, Lot #11.
              I note here that the applicant has granted the Town an extension of time within which to consider
       the application resulting in the hearing being conducted on this date.
               The application’s Affordability Plan identifies the application as a thirty percent (30%)
       affordable development, resulting in eighteen (18) workforce units. Schedule B in the plan identifies
       which affordable units are located in specific buildings. All units will be two (2) bedrooms. All
       affordable units are restricted as such for a forty (40) year period. The tenant eligibility section of the
       plan states that “not less than fifteen percent (15%) (nine (9) units) of the dwelling for rent in the
       community shall be rented to persons or families whose income is less than or equal to sixty percent
       (60%) of the area or statewide median income, whichever is less. The remainder of the affordable units
       for rent (nine (9) units) shall be rented to persons or families whose income is less than or equal to
       eighty percent (80%) of the area or statewide median income, whichever is less.”
              The site itself as noted is approximately seven (7) acres in size, and slopes uphill to the west
       from Park Place. The sixty units are contained within ten (10) multifamily apartment style buildings
       designated on the plan as A through J. I note that Village Crossing, a 66-unit affordable housing
       development is under construction adjacent to the northerly property line. This project is on
       approximately sixteen (16) acres. The applicant’s engineer will review the site plan, drainage, traffic,
       landscaping and other pertinent matters.

       Subject: Opposition to Proposed Zoning Change for Parkers Place affordable Apartments

       To: Town of East Lyme Zoning Commission, Mr. William Mulholland, Zoning Enforcement Officer

       Regarding: Parcel Lot 11 (11.1 on the East Lyme, CT Assesors map)

       Dear Mr. Mullholland:

       We are writing as concerned residents of Village Xing, # 16, in Niantic, CT. to respectfully express our
       opposition to the zoning changes required to accomodate the proposed Parkers Place, LLC affordable
       housing development that would allow for the development of sixty (60) two (2) bedroom affordable
       housing units to be built by Pazz construction, Jason Pazzaglia owner.

       While we understand and support the need for thoughtful housing solutions, we also understand the
       benefits afforded to builders who agree to take on such projects. For instance, Mr. Pazzaglia's recently
       built affordable housing project, Brookside Apartments at 90 N. Bridebrook Rd. in East Lyme, which are
       available for rent at $1800-$3400 per month depending on the unit's size, offers 18 - 24 of 80 total units
       (22 - 30%) to lower income familes based on gross and/or area median income. Considering the recently
       passed robust pro affordable housing CT legislation, we understand builders offering even a small
       percentage of affordable housing will receive a 3.5% sales tax reduction on the materials needed to
       build the project, and potentially a myriad other significant financial incentives such as Federal low
       income housing credits administered by CHFA, Build for CT funding, CHFA Multifamily rental dollars, tax
       abatements and others. So while the incentives are potentially quite lucrative for builders like Mr.
       Pazzaglia, (and I don't profess to be a construction expert - I invite you all to do your own research on
       line, as I did) seemingly all that is offered to the existing homeowners in the area is construction noise
       and nuisance, hundreds of additional residents and cars, accident risks, general inconvenience the
       following:

       Infastructure Capacity – Our local roads, water supply, and sewer systems are already operating near
       capacity. In fact, as we understand it, on 7/1/25 the town of East Lyme placed a moratorium on the
       building of new, large scale building projects (20 units plus) due to the strain on both sewer and water
       systems. After this moratorium was placed, Mr. Pazzaglia and his attorneys sought a solution through
       the State of CT and ultimately prevailed when the Superior Court for the Judicial District of Hartford
       (Land Use Division) stipulated a judgement which will allow the sewers in the proposed project to
       interconnect to the existing municipal utilities in Park Place. The sewer shed line was in fact modified by
       action of the Town of East Lyme in the regularly scheduled meeting of the East Lyme water and sewer
       commission on September, 23, 2025. If Mr. Pazzaglia's proposed project is approved, it remains to be
       seen if this modification will be sufficient to support the 60 (2) bedroom units, along with the 66 Village
       Xing units and the the other homes and businesses in the Park Place vicinity that utilize the public
       utilities. Beside potential strain to the water and sewer infastructure, adding a significant number of
       new units will likely lead to could lead to the congestion, service interruptions, and safety concerns
       noted below. This ambitious proposed project is just too dense - 60 units in less than 7 acres whereas
       the Village Xing development has 66 units on twice the land allowing for a spacious wooded feel, less
       pavement and a more suitable asphalt to green space ratio. Brookside apartments have 80 units on
over 20 acres!

Traffic and Safety – The proposed development would substantially increase traffic on Main St. In the
summer, it is already nearly impossible to exit Park Place onto Main St. At times, the traffic is stopped
all the way down to Rocky Neck State Park. This raises concerns for pedestrian and driver safety. As
the affordable units will likely draw families with children, the concern is also for school bus safety as
children are picked up and dropped off as existing residents are trying to get to or return from work.
Pedestrians walking and parking on Park Place or at the adjoining shopping plaza to visit Book Barn are
already a huge problem; many seemingly feel that Park Place is a parking area, with children and
preoccupied adults often crossing in front of moving cars trying to get in and out of their homes.

Environmental Impact – The site in question would include the destruction of a heavily wooded area
where wildlife abounds much to the enjoyment of all who reside nearby. Development will disrupt local
ecosystems, displace wildlife and potentially increase stormwater runoff, whic would contribute to
flooding risks.

Community Character – The proposed affordable housing project's density is inconsistent with the
surrounding neighborhood’s zoning and character. A sudden change in scale could alter the look, feel,
and property values of the area, changing it from a charming small town, wooded setting to an urban
feeling setting with too much paved space, parked cars, motorcycles, ATVs and bicycles.

We respectfully urge the Board to consider alternative locations or at the very least less dense project
approaches that will address affordable housing needs while preserving the safety, infrastructure
stability, environmental integrity and natural beauty of our community.

Thank you for your time and for considering the perspectives of residents who will be directly affected
by this decision. We look forward to your careful review and respectfully request that you will vote
against the proposed zoning change as it currently stands.

Sincerely,



Michele      and Raymond Bissell

16 Village Xing, Niantic, CT 06357
