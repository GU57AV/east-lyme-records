    Note Ms. Thurlow reopened the Public Hearing for the application of Coastal Crust LLC for a Special Permit
   under Section 20.25 of the East Lyme Zoning Regulations for restaurant outdoor dining at 228 Flanders Road,
   continued from April 23, 2026.

   Note Mr. Mulholland confirmed that the legal ad had been run.


    Note Mr. Mulholland stated:
   *This is not a renewal, but an initial application for outdoor dining.
   *He reviewed the proposal and found it compliant with all regulations.
   *In his opinion, the plan is an improvement over existing conditions and should be beneficial to the site.
   *The applicant is present to answer questions.
   *He has no issues with the application and recommends the Commission move forward.

    Note The Applicant, Lauren O'Rourke of 25 Billow Road, explained some of the following:
   *She proposes an outdoor patio in the front area of the property.
   *The space currently includes four parking spaces located approximately 26 feet from the road, and vehicles
   pulling in can feel uncomfortably close to traffic.
   *Converting this area to a patio would improve safety and aesthetics, especially given its visibility as a main
   entry point into town.

    Note To create the patio enclosure, she proposed using smaller mafia blocks measuring 2' × 2' × 3' and adding
   wood cladding on top to give the structure a planter-style appearance. The goal is to improve the overall
   aesthetics while still maintaining the necessary safety protection. She emphasized that the intent is to make the
   area attractive and welcoming, rather than simply functional.

    Note Commission Members acknowledged the enclosure’s dual purpose, noting that while safety barriers are
   required, the Applicant is making a clear effort to ensure they were also visually appealing. Ms. O'Rourke added
   that the building had already come a long way in terms of appearance, and she hoped to further enhance the
   space with plants and twinkle lights once the weather permitted.

    Note After some discussion Ms. O'Rourke clarified that the brick surface shown in the conceptual image was
   only for visualization purposes and that the patio would remain on the existing pavement. She explained that the
   area features a gradual slope rather than a sharp drop, and that tables would sit flat and stable, even if some
   appeared slightly higher or angled because of the grade.
   She noted that tables had previously been placed in the area during the Certificate of Occupancy period without
   issue. Commissioners commented that the proposed planters would be far more attractive than the jersey
   barriers previously used. Ms. O'Rourke confirmed that jersey-style blocks would still be utilized, but with
   aesthetic enhancements such as wood cladding, planters, and decorative features to better blend them into the
   site.

    Note She explained that some blocks would be positioned along the Flanders Road side, but all would stay
   within the property boundaries. Mr. Mulholland added that once installed, vehicles would no longer be able to
   drive into the area. He stated that the blocks would be placed as close to the road as safely possible without
   compromising stability, and that final placement would be determined on site during installation, with minor
   adjustments made as needed. He noted that the completed layout would resemble the COVID-era patio
   previously used at the site, but with larger blocks and picnic tables.

   Note There were no comments from the Public.

7. Subcommittee Reports
  7-1. Short-Term Rental Subcommittee

     Note Ms. Jett Harris reported that the Subcommittee had completed its review of data gathered from multiple
     sources and public hearings and presented a proposed text amendment to regulate short-term rentals.
     The amendment would add Section 20.29 to the Zoning Regulations, permitting short-term rentals in designated
     commercial and mixed-use districts, while also establishing operational requirements such as registration with
     the Zoning Department, on-site parking provisions, 24/7 contact information from the property owner, annual
     permitting, and enforcement provisions that could include permit revocation after repeated verified complaints.
     The draft also included a $500 permit fee and outlined that the regulation would not apply uniformly in all areas
     due to existing jurisdictional differences, including exemptions for beach communities with independent Zoning
     authority and recognition that some properties may be governed by restrictive deeds or HOA rules that
     supersede town regulations.

      Note Commission Members discussed several elements of the proposal, including the definition of “family,”
     which was ultimately struck from the draft in favor of allowing occupancy by up to five unrelated individuals
     consistent with existing zoning definitions.
     There was also clarification that 24/7 contact information must be provided by the property owner. The
     Commission debated the proposed $500 permit fee, with some Members suggesting higher amounts due to
     administrative workload and the scale of short-term rental activity, while others preferred keeping it lower;
     consensus remained near the $500 level as a reasonable starting point given annual permitting.

      Note Additional discussion addressed enforcement and coordination with other Departments, including Fire and
     Building officials, with Mr. Mulholland noting that while zoning cannot mandate Fire Department inspections,
     referrals and interdepartmental review could be built into the process. Commission Members emphasized that
     short-term rentals function as businesses and should be regulated accordingly, while also noting that the
     proposal would still require future public hearings before adoption and could be further adjusted based on public
     feedback.

      Decision MOTION (14)
     Ms. Jett Harris moved to approve the STR text amendment and to move the text amendment to Public Hearing,
     as read into the record.
     Ms. Kalal seconded the motion.
     Motion carried, 6-0-0.

      Note Ms. Thurlow stated that a Public Hearing would be scheduled so the Commission could hear feedback
     from the public on the proposed short-term rental regulations.
