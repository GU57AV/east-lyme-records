    e. Application of the East Lyme Zoning Commission for a proposed Text
       Amendment to the East Lyme Zoning Regulations by adding Section 20.29 to
       permit and regulate Short Term Rentals.

       -Ms. Jett-Harris read a memo from the Zoning Official available on the website

       -Ms. Thurlow reminded the commission that, legally, anyone who is running an STR
       in a residential area currently, would be grandfathered and could continue running
       them and have guests stay for whatever duration. A text amendment would only apply
       to a new operation. Current owners would have no change. Additionally, the beach
       communities that have their own zoning regulations would not be affected as well as
       different subdivisions in town. And the goal of this text amendment is to limit the
       growth of the STRs in residential areas. The proposal would allow STRs in
       commercial CA, CB, and mixed-use buildings only, not residential housing.

       -Ms. Jett-Harris wondered if the current owners would need to pay the proposed fee
       and it was confirmed they would.

       -Mr. Peck was concerned with the l4-day minimum.

       -Ms. Yuhas read the purpose of the zoning regulations, noting their permissive nature.
       She reminded the commission of the ad-hoc committee's work, the complaints
       received, the public hearings held, the public comments made. The courts did not say
       the towns must allow STRs but if they were to be distinguished, they need their own
       regulations. She defined a STR as a shofi term, income producing, transient lodging
       businesses and the activities they bring. Zoning determines which uses are

                                             4
        appropriate in town and noted that not all uses are allowed in all zones. Federal tax
        rules may reward some investors who maintain average stays of 7 days or less,
        encouraging frequent turnover, meaning it creates a federal loophole allowing that not
        to be claimed as income. There are at least 4 hotels, a l6-room B&B and 2
        campgrounds in town, indicating plenty of options for people to stay. There may be
        some money spent here but because East Lyme is part of a regionaltourist area,
        meaning many surrounding towns benefit from guests spending money. The
        economic impact does not outweigh the impact of East Lyme residential
        neighborhoods. She supports the text amendment noting residential neighborhoods
        should remain residential. Her intention is to support families and neighborhoods.

        -Ms. Thurlow stated that although at the previous meeting, many owners spoke in
        favor of STRs, that was not indicative of all the people she had spoken to over several
        years and during the ad-hoc committee who were not excited to live next door to a
        hotel. There are already several hundred Airbnb's in town and those already running
        will not be affected and with 8,000 people proposed to be hired by EB next year, the
        need for housing is greater than the need for more Airbnbs. She stated that though
        owners have said guests bring money to the town, mostly it brings money to the
        owners. The guests she's spoken to in her neighborhood have mostly been staying in
        East Lyme but visiting other attractions and spending money in different towns.

        -Ms. Kalal agreed with Ms. Yuhas

        -Ms. Markovitz is in favor of a modification of the amendment, that 14 days is too
        much. She also stated that the noise ordinance in town would take care of any noise
        complaints. She believes that the commission cannot tell people they cannot rent their
        houses on a short-term basis.

        -Ms. Jett-Harris commented that there isn't a housing shortage in town, there's a
        short-term rental problem in town. The stock would be more in town if there weren't
        so many. She is concerned about the turnover in neighborhoods.
