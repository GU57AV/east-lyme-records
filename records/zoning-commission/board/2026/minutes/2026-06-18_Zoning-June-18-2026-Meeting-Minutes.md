     *It's below the CJL (Coastal Jurisdiction Line) so the State (DEEP) controls the dock and above the CJL, the

     review docks and today, DEEP handles the dock itself, and the Commission only reviews upland impacts.

      Note *The Commission is not reviewing the float, ramp, or in‑water parts.
     *The Commission is reviewing minor grading, any site disturbance, neighborhood/area impacts and whether the
     upland work meets special permit criteria.
     *The project is exempt from CAM review because CAM rules specifically exempt special permits for docks.

      Note *The motion will say “approve the dock” because that’s how the regulation is written.
     But in reality, the Commission is only approving the upland portion and making sure the record shows any
     grading or site work.

      Note Jerry Lokken, Director of Parks & Rec, gave a brief history of the town’s dog‑park efforts. He noted that
     East Lyme has an estimated 3,000 dogs, with about 1,450 licenses issued. The town has been exploring a dog
     park for more than 10 years.
     In 2023, the Parks and Recreation Commission created a Dog Park Subcommittee, which reviewed
     town‑owned properties, researched best practices, drafted proposed rules, and surveyed residents. Their report
     was endorsed by the Parks and Recreation Commission and then by the Board of Selectmen, who supported
     the concept and allowed fundraising to begin.

      Note He explained that the subcommittee previously applied for a special permit at Darrow Pond, but the
     Zoning Commission denied that application due to concerns about noise, traffic, and water quality. The group
     then revisited its list of possible sites and identified the Roxbury Road property as the next location to bring
     forward.

      Note He noted that the resident surveys showed strong support for a dog park, but also noted concerns such
     as maintenance, aggressive dogs, irresponsible owners, and potential illness.

      Note Mr. Lokken gave an overview of the Roxbury Road Site:
     *The property is the open field on the right side when driving from the transfer station toward Bridebrook Park.
     *The land was acquired in 2009 with a requirement that it be used for open space and recreation, which a dog
     park satisfies.
     *The Parks & Rec Department has maintained the meadow since then.
     *Roxbury Road was originally the subcommittee’s preferred site, but they pursued Darrow Pond first because
     the Parks & Rec Commission asked them to.

      Note *Dog park is on a two‑lane road with a 25‑mph speed limit and no sidewalks.
     *Roxbury Road can easily handle the small increase in traffic and the impact is negligible.
     *Vegetative buffers are included in the plan.
     *Fenced area was moved farther away to reduce noise impact.

15. Zoning Official

  Note Mr. Mulholland reported that the regional Council of Governments (COG) recently presented information on
  new state‑mandated housing growth statutes. He said only about 15 people attended. He distributed the handouts
  from that meeting for informational purposes. He explained that all towns must update their zoning regulations by
  July 1st, but most towns, including East Lyme, will not meet that deadline because the requirements are extensive
  and the COGs statewide are behind.

   Note He said the work is estimated at about 600 hours, and hiring a consultant would cost around $100,000, which
  is not feasible with current budgets. The alternative is to let the COG draft the regulations, but they are also far
  behind. Therefore, he and the Town Planner will begin drafting materials themselves, despite limited staff. He has
  asked the Town Attorney for assistance to ensure the regulations will hold up legally.

   Note Mr. Mulholland noted that the new statutes include concepts like transit‑oriented districts and may affect
  parking requirements, though East Lyme’s land‑use makeup might allow some flexibility. He said staff will continue
  working on the mandate and hopes to have something assembled by early fall, though the timeline depends on
  available resources.

   Note He emphasized that the town should write its own regulations rather than rely entirely on outside agencies,
  because these rules will shape the community’s future. He also expects significant litigation statewide once towns
  begin adopting the required changes.

  Note Commission members thanked Mr. Mulholland and Mr. Goeschel for their work. Mr. Mulholland said the
  Commission will continue receiving materials and updates as staff works through the mandate.
