    Note The Commission reviewed the CAM application submitted by Jerry Lokken for replacement of a
   damaged seawall at McCook's Point Beach, located at 8-10 Atlantic Street. The seawall had been severely
   damaged by storms, requiring temporary repairs. The permanent solution involves removing about 120 feet
   of the old concrete seawall and replacing it with approximately 105 feet of steel sheet piling capped with
   poured concrete, along with replacement of an existing beach stairway.

    Note Ms. Markovitz read Mr. Mulholland's memorandum dated March 4th, 2026, into the record.
           Adobe_Scan_Mar_12__2026_(1).pdf

    Note Mr. Mulholland's letter explained that because the wall is above the Coastal Jurisdiction Line, the
   Commission must ensure it meets state environmental goals, if the project minimizes damage to coastal
   resources, and if the town took enough steps to mitigate environmental impact.

    Note Ms. Jett-Harris read the memorandum from Alex Klose, P.E., the Deputy Public Works Director,
   dated March 4th, 2026, into the record.
        Town_Engineer_letter.pdf

    Note Mr. Klose's letter warned that the current emergency repairs are at risk of undermining and collapse
   because the concrete blocks are sitting on soft sand rather than a solid foundation. The proposed plan is
   the most cost-effective and durable way to protect the beach while keeping a consistent look with the rest
   of the wall.

    Note Ms. Kalal read the Planning Commission referral letter dated January 20th, 2026, into the record.
           Planning_referral_letter.pdf

    Note The Planning Commission correspondence confirmed that they unanimously backed the project on
   January 13th, 2026, and that the seawall repair matches the town’s POCD.

    Note Mr. Lokken discussed the CAM application with the Commission and some of the following was
   highlighted:
   *McCook Point Park is a major town asset, providing public access to the beach.
   *Although work will occur above the Coastal Jurisdiction Line and falls under local zoning authority, the
   town notified CT DEEP as a courtesy.
   *This plan was developed with input from Town Engineering, Public Works, Parks & Recreation,
   as well as Professional coastal engineers
   *The chosen solution best protects the parking lot and preserves the beach,
   *The proposed steel sheet‑pile design is comparable to the construction of the boardwalk, which is built on
   sheet piles driven deep below sand.
   *The estimated cost is approximately $800,000, with funding subject to the town’s budget process and
   capital improvement plan.
   *Construction is likely to begin in fall 2027.
   *Temporary repairs are holding well but could fail if undermined by storm action.
   *The permanent fix is designed to last many years and prevent future undermining.
   *This is the first major failure of the seawall area since late 2023 after several storms.
   *The project will preserve the beach in front of the wall and maintain public access.

    Note Ms. Thurlow said she views this as essentially a "housekeeping" item, it's a necessity to protect town
   infrastructure and she sees no reason to deny the application.

12. Comments from Chairman

  Note Ms. Thurlow issued an urgent alert regarding a sophisticated scam targeting individuals with active
 applications before the town.
 An applicant received a fraudulent email that appeared to be from the Land Use Department. The message
 claimed her application was approved but demanded an immediate $4,000 permit fee via credit card.
 The scam was caught by Town Hall staff before any payment was made. Because the scam is brand new
 (appearing within the last 24–48 hours), it may not be widely known yet.
 Ms. Thurlow emphasized two key facts to help residents identify fraud:
 *The town will never request payment via email.
 *The Department does not accept credit cards; payments must be made in person via cash or check.

  Note Ms. Jett-Harris suggested that the First Selectman should publish a formal warning on the town website
 or official Facebook page to ensure maximum visibility. Ms. Thurlow said she also plans to share the details of
 the scam on her personal Facebook page to alert other local applicants.
