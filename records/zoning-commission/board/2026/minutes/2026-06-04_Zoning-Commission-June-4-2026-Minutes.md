    Note Darren Owen of EIO Associates came forward representing the Applicant.


    Note Mr. Owen submitted a letter of authorization from applicant James Quill (Exhibit 1).


    Note He explained that their proposal is for a zoning text amendment to allow storage facilities as a
   Special Permitted Use in the CA Zone. The proposal would add a new subsection (8.2.18) and update the
   definition section (1.80) to clarify that a storage facility refers to self‑storage units used only for personal
   property, and not vehicles, trailers, vessels, or residential use.

    Note He also reviewed the proposed additional controls in Section 25.5, including:
   *Minimum lot size of 40,000 sq. ft.
   *Conservation easement on 20% of the lot.
   *Direct access from an arterial road connected to a major street.
   *Wetlands plantings included in the site plan if wetlands are present.
   *Architectural design review consistent with Section 34 to ensure compatibility with neighborhood character
   and community aesthetics.

    Note He explained that the CA Zone is intended for neighborhood‑ and community‑oriented commercial
   uses. He listed the types of uses currently allowed by site plan or special permit and noted that storage
   facilities are not presently permitted in the CA Zone. They are allowed only in the LI Zone, where outdoor
   storage of vehicles and trailers is permitted if screened. The proposed amendment would prohibit any
   outdoor storage and limit the use strictly to indoor self‑storage units.

    Note He emphasized the difference between traditional warehouse‑style storage buildings and the type of
   architecturally regulated design required under Section 34. He stated that large metal warehouse buildings
   with minimal windows would not be allowed. Instead, any proposed facility would need to meet the New
   England architectural standards outlined in Section 34, including requirements for building massing, scale,
   materials, roof types, landscaping, windows, lighting, and overall character.

    Note Mr. Owen provided visual examples, including a comparison of a standard Stop & Shop building and
   a more context‑sensitive version on Nantucket, noting that Nantucket’s approach to preserving community
   character inspired the strict design requirements included in the amendment. He stated that while East
   Lyme is not Nantucket, preserving local character is equally important, which is why the proposal includes
   strong adherence to Section 34.

    Note He stated that traffic impacts from storage facilities are typically low because renters visit infrequently
   and represent a wide range of users. He noted that this use would generate far less traffic than other
   permitted or special‑permit uses in the CA Zone, such as restaurants with drive‑throughs, coffee shops,
   gas stations, or other high‑turnover businesses.

    Note He added that storage facilities generally require minimal staffing, often only one employee, and only
   one bathroom under current building codes. As a result, the use would have little to no impact on the town’s
   sewer capacity and may represent one of the lowest‑demand uses allowed in the district.

    Note He summarized the administrative reviews, noting that SECOG found no likely intermunicipal impacts
   and that DEEP determined the proposal to be consistent with the Connecticut Coastal Management Act.

   Note Ms. Yuhas asked why the LI Zone, where self‑storage is already permitted, was not sufficient, and
   what problem the amendment was intended to solve for the town rather than for a single applicant.

    Note Mr. Owen replied that the proposal would meet a community need while creating a less intensive
   version of storage use. He explained that unlike the LI Zone, which allows outdoor storage of motor
   vehicles and trailers, the proposed CA‑Zone use would prohibit vehicle and trailer storage and limit units to
   personal‑property storage only. Because the CA Zone includes both residential and commercial areas, this
   is a more restricted form of storage that would have minimal neighborhood impact.

    Note Ms. Thurlow asked about the minimum lot size of 40,000 sq. ft. and how large a building could be on
   such a lot. Mr. Owen responded that a building footprint could be approximately 10,000–15,000 sq. ft.

    Note She then asked what percentage of the lot would be paved. Mr. Owen said that on a 40,000‑sq‑ft lot,
   a good portion would be pavement but emphasized that he could not reference any specific property. He
   added that a larger lot would be more appropriate for this type of use.

    Note He further stated that impervious and building coverage should be around 30%, though this was not
   written into the amendment because the Commission has authority to modify or make the regulation more
   restrictive during review. Mr. Mulholland confirmed that current CA‑zone regulations allow 35% lot
   coverage for building footprint.

    Note Ms. Thurlow asked Mr. Mulholland why the Planning Commission found the proposal inconsistent,
   noting that Gary Goeschel typically provides written reasoning and none was included.
   Mr. Mulholland said he did not know the specific reason but speculated it may be because storage facilities
   are already permitted in the LI Zone and because the Zoning Commission previously removed them from
   the CA Zone.

    Note Ms. Thurlow asked whether this type of storage facility would require significant outdoor lighting at
   night. Mr. Owen responded that lighting requirements fall under Section 34 and that the facility would not
   operate 24 hours. He noted that hours of operation would limit nighttime lighting impacts, which is
   important because some CA‑zoned areas are near residential neighborhoods.

    Note Ms. Thurlow asked whether any member of the public wished to speak in support of the amendment.
   No one came forward.

    Note Ms. Thurlow reminded the public that a text amendment applies to the entire zoning district, not to
   any specific property. For legal reasons, individual addresses could not be discussed. She then invited
   anyone wishing to speak against the amendment to do so.

    Note Teresa Kegley of Long Hill Road, Middletown (formerly of Demick Road, Waterford and Rathbun
   Road, Niantic) asked the Applicant how many storage units would be included, noting that the discussion
   had only covered unit size, not total quantity.

    Note Mr. Mulholland responded that the question was site‑specific and therefore could not be addressed
   during a text‑amendment hearing. He noted that only general comments, not project‑specific details, could
   be taken at this stage.

    Note Ms. Kegley tried to restate her concerns in general, non‑site‑specific terms as a resident of the
   surrounding area. She expressed worry about the potential number of units, hours of operation, lighting,
   and noise, noting that in her experience in Middletown, people often attempt to access storage units
   outside posted hours, creating disturbances.
   She also raised concerns about environmental impacts, including the presence of wetlands and ledge, the
   possibility of blasting, and potential effects on nearby private wells on rural roads served by well water
   rather than public water.
