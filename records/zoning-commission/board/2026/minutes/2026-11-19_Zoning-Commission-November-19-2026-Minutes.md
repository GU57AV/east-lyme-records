-He described St. John's Church and noted some of the services they provide such as:

-Cost of electricity is $30,000 annually because of the refrigerators and coolers which

-The currentZoningRegs do not allow for modest solar energy systems on site
-The text amendment would benefit other property owners in similar circumstances
rather than seeking a variance
-This amendment is not an application to put a solar system in, but to change the
regulations
-He described the proposal: Up to 100kw, accessory to a principal use on the same lot
as primary use and the power generated shall be primarily for onsite use, minimum
lot area of 40,000 sq ft, shall not be located within 50 feet of property lines, shall be
suitably screened.
-100kw:1/10 of megawatt, not a large-scale solar facility, this would mean a project
would be less than 4,000 sq ft in area.
-Could not operate a solar farm on a vacant lot.
-Addressing the Planning Commission comments: St. John's Church is an
environmentally sensitive property by virtue of proximity to Long Island Sound
(LIS), but it does not contain inland wetlands, tidal wetlands, flood hazard areas,
beaches dunes or coastal bluffs. According to CT DEEP letter, there is no mention of
adverse impacts to resources. The POCD wants to protect areas, and the proposal is
not an intensive use. All regulations protecting sensitive areas would stay intact after
the amendment. Clean reliable sustainable energy section of the POCD only speaks to
limiting large (or larger) scale solar energy projects to appropriate lots. 4,000 sq ft is
less than 1/10 ofan acre.
-Reading the entire POCD as a whole finds that there is much support for this
proposal because its small size.
-Protecting East Lyme's character, and qualities and natural resources, and the POCD
promotes renewable energy, encouraging sustainability, reducing reliance on
traditional fossil fuels. It is also important to preserve the viability of existing
community institutions that provide valuable services to residents.
