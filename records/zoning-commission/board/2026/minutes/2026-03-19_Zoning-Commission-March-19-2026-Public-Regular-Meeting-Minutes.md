   Note Mr. Bodnar introduced himself as owner of Fox Glove Cider LLC, a new business producing cider and
  apple wine and explained some of the following:
  *His goal is to open a manufacturing facility with a small tasting area.
  *The current definition of “brew pub” is beer‑specific, which excludes cider and other beverages he intends to
  produce.
  *He requested that the definition be updated to reflect modern craft‑beverage manufacturing, which often
  includes multiple beverage types under a single permit.
  *His proposed language mirrors the categories authorized under Connecticut’s manufacturing permit statute.
  *Cider and apple wine are legally defined under CGS §30‑16, separate from beer.
  *He has identified potential space at 11 Freedom Way, located in the Light Industrial zone, where brew pubs
  are already permitted by special permit.

  Note *Tonight’s request is only for a town‑wide text amendment, not site approval.
  *The current definition of “brew pub” is beer‑specific, which excludes cider, wine, mead, and spirits even
  though they are manufactured in similar ways.
  *His proposed amendment would update the definition to include all alcoholic beverages authorized under
  CGS and align zoning language with modern craft‑beverage manufacturing practices.

  Note Ms. Thurlow reminded the Commission that a text amendment applies town‑wide, not to a specific site.


   Note It was asked why the amendment includes spirits, given the Applicant’s focus on cider. Mr. Bodnar
  explained that including spirits avoids future applicants needing additional text amendments, and ensures the
  definition is comprehensive and future‑proof.

   Note He personally does not plan to manufacture spirits but wants the regulation to be consistent with all
  categories allowed under CGS. His federal and state manufacturing permits (TTB and CT Liquor Control) will
  be for cider and apple wine only. His intent was to create a comprehensive, future‑proof definition, not to
  introduce bars into town.

  Note Ms. Jett-Harris expressed concern that including spirits could effectively allow a bar, which is not
  permitted in any East Lyme zoning district.

   Note Commission Members noted that beer, cider, and mead are “beer‑adjacent,” but spirits introduce a
  different level of service. Mr. Bodnar stated he is not opposed to removing spirits from the amendment if that is
  the Commission’s preference.

   Note Mr. Bodnar explained he does not plan to serve prepared meals. He may offer packaged snacks,
  depending on state regulations. He believes breweries cannot operate full kitchens under certain permit types.

   Note Ms. Thurlow asked for Public Comment for, against, or neutral on the proposed text amendment and
  no members of the public came forward.
