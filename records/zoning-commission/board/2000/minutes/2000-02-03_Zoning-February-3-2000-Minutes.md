J              Zonins. Commission proposal to amend Section 20.25 of the zoninq code to extend
               the water moratorium to December 1. 2000.

Mrs. Cone opened discussion by stating that she would like to have discussion regarding extending
        the moratorium to July 1, 2000.

Mr. Nickerson stated that he agreed that the extension of the water moratorium should be aligned
         with the Planning Commission date of July 1, 2000.

Mr. Peck stated that he is concerned about the DEP delay in approval of the permits. In addition,
        he stated his concem regarding the restriction only on multifamily housing in the zoning
        code comes under the moratorium. He added that if there is a real water problem, the
        moratorium perhaps should be more comprehensive. He noted that there is little activity
        with regard to multifamily development, but a number of elderly housing and assisted
        living housing has been actively growing.

Mr. Weber agreed that consideration and thought should be given to perhaps a more
       comprehensive moratorium beyond multifamily housing. He stated that the moratorium
       could be ended when the permits are approved. If the DEP does not approve the permits
       by July 1, 2000, the Commission will be revisiting this issue to again extend the
       moratorium. If the Zoning moratorium is staggered with the Planning Commission, i.e.
       to December 1,2000, those individuals who make the presentation additional time to
       direct the request to the Zoning Commission. He therefore stated that he is not opposed
       to extending the moratorium to December 1, 2000.

Mr. Peck indicated that only multifamily housing is effected by the moratorium and when there is
        no multifamily applications or activity in the Zoning office, extending the moratorium
         does not make total sense. If there is a water problem, why wasn'tthe Zoning
         Commission requested to include elderly housing.

Mr. Weber noted that what is before the Commission is the extension of the existing moratorium.
       Another public hearing would be required before additional restrictions can be included
       in the moratorium. He agreed with aligning with the Planning Commission date and that
       perhaps the moratorium should be more comprehensive.

Mr. Mclaughlin stated that the Commission is being asked to extend the existing moratorium date
        only. He suggested the Zoning Commission send a letter to the Water & Sewer
        Commission inquiring if a rnoratorium with more restriction, including elderly housing
         and perhaps other development, is reasonable.

Mr. Nickerson stated that the moratorium appears to be inconsistent by restricting only multifamily
         housing, but not elderly housing such as Chapman Woods and assisted living facilities.
         If there is a water problem, restriction on the increase in growth, and therefore, water use
         should be more consistent and comprehensive. Mr. Nickerson stated that he was in favor
         of extending the moratorium until July 1, 2000. He added that the Commission should

         permiued by July 1,2000.

Mr. Gada agreed that the moratorium should be continued until July 1, 2000 and should be more
        inclusive.

Mr. Mulholland will draft a letter to the Chairman of the Water & Sewer Commission requesting
        that Commission's input regarding the limit of the moratorium to only multifamily
        housing and that Commission's opinion as to the need to expand the reach to elderly
        housing, assisted living facilities and/or other growth.
