Mr. Bulmer stated that he has listened to all the testimony of the Public Hearing. He stated that
Aces High appears to be a wonderful place. He noted that Aces High has been awarded a
National Award, probably well deserved. However, the Commission does not award a Special
Permit to a person. A Special Permit is awarded to a piece of property and is irrevocable.
When the property is sold, the Special Permit goes with the property. He stated that his
concern is that by allowing 12 month occupancy per year, the Commission is allowing a de
facto trailer camp and not a recreational vehicle park. He noted that although the applicants
operate a wonderful facility, his concern must be with compromising the Town and the future
of the property if the applicants sell the property. He also read into the record Zoning
Regulation Section 20.17. He noted the recreational purposes limitation. He state that the
Commission had no choice in its decision, as it cannot disregard the existing Zoning
Regulations and would vote to deny the application.

Mr. Mclaughlin concuned with Mr. Bulmer. Section 20.17 restricts use to recreational
purposes. Occupancy for 12 months per year for transient workers is contrary to the Zoning
Regulations and he would vote to deny the application.

Mr. Chamberlain inquired of Mr. Mulholland if the existing restrictions, i.e. time limitation and
recreational use, was antiquated as stated by the applicant and Mr. Beavers at the Public
Hearing.

Mr. Mulholland stated that that is a subjective judgement. The issue is, is this recreational
camping? The Commission is obligated to abide by the Regulations. The Commission has the
power to change the Regulations through the Public Hearing process if they chose to do that.
The applicant also has the opportunity to apply to change the Regulations. He stated that every
community adopts regulations for what they think is appropriate within their community. At
this point, the Commission needs to deal with this application. Whether the ZoningRegulation
is antiquated on this issue, is not the subject of the hearing or application.

      (applicant) and the Regulations and, therefore, the Commission must enforce the Regulations.

      Mr. Weber stated that the winter residents would be primarily working at a job site and would
      use the trailer for habitation primarily. This does not preclude recreation, however. He stated
      that if one admits to violation of one regulation (60-day limitation), one can assume a higher
      likelihood of violating other Regulations. Additionally, if the property is sold, the Special
      Permit goes with the property, not to the applicant. He stated he is inclined to deny the
      application based on the factthat Section 20.17 restricts campers to 60 days per year for
      recreational purposes.
