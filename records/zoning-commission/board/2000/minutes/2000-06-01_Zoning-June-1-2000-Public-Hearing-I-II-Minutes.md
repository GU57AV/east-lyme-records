Mr. Granville addressed the concerns expressed by the Commission regarding off-street parking.
He noted the one of the requirements of the proposed amendment is that there is one off-street
parking place for each bedroom in the guesthouse. This is in addition to the requirement for the

Mr. Granville also addressed the Commission's concern regarding enforcement. Any time a
Special Permit is issued, the Zoning Official must enforce the conditions of the Permit. This
Special Permit is not different from any other Special Permit issued.

proposed amendment.

comments or concerns they wished to express.

Mr. Mulholland commented that this application is not specific to a particular property. The
Commission is to focus on and consider the evidence presented.

the Public Hearing.

Donald W. Gerwick, place of business 17 Avery Lane, Waterford, CT represented the applicant
and prepared the plans. He indicated that this application is a request for renewal of a previous
application. The plans presented tonight are identical to what was submitted approximately two
years ago. The 127 acres mentioned in the narrative is located in the rear half of the property. An
additional 100 acres, approximately, is located between this acreage and Route 161. The activity
area is approximately 1200 feet from Route 161

There will be about 46,000 cubic yards that would be excavated if all the work were completed
within the time frame. In the past two years, the applicant has performed minimal work on the site
due to other business demands and therefore the site is nearly identical to what it was two years ago
as reflected on the plans. Over the past 20-30 years, the previous owner under several permits
substantially excavated approximately 75-80Yo of the front 100 acres. Most of this area is flat and
has been re-vegetated. There is an existing one-lane steel and wood structure bridge at the entrance
to the property.

Mr. Gerwick noted that the applicant had previously posted a $5,000 cash bond and a $25,000
performance bond and requests that the bonds be continued.

The applicant is also in the process of applying to the Planning Commission with respect to the
property for subdivision approval. The Planning Cornmission had recommended that the applicant,
and the applicant agreed, cease and close out the gravel operation before the issuance ofany
building permits.

The applicant anticipates a minimal amount of truck traffic, i.e. a couple of trucks would be
entering, or leaving the site per day between 9:00 AM and 4:30 PM recommended by the Planning
Commission. Mr. Gerwick stated that the original hours of operation requested were 7:30 AM-
4:30 PM, Monday through Friday and 7:30 AM - l:00 PM on Saturday. He added that if the
Commission rolled back beginning hour to 9:00 AM, that it only apply to those days on which
school is in session. The closest school is 2.4 miles from the site.

Mr. Mulholland noted that the applicant would be using a stone crusher. Section 17.4.5 only

Mrs. Cone inquired as to the location of houses in the area around the site. Mr. Gerwick pointed
out on the plan the location of houses. The closest house is approximately 1500 feet from the

Mr. Mulholland inquired as to the timing for any proposed subdivision construction on the site.

Mr. Gerwick stated that he anticipates and application to the Conservation Commission in the
summer of 2000, followed by an application to the Planning Commission in the late summer 2000.
Optimistically, Mr. Gerwick stated that the first foundation dug might be in the summer of 2001.
He noted that the applicant has a Conservation Permit since there is a small wetlands area (seepage
breakout). He also noted that the Director of Planning of Waterford was notified on March 13,
2000.

Mr. Weber inquired if there was any other access to the site.

Mr. Gerwick stated that there is an access off Quail Crest Rd. through private property, but the
applicant does not have right of access at this time.

Mr. Weber opened the floor to those wishing to speak in favor of the application.

Mr. R. Rogers, who resides on Route 161 directly across from the site, stated that he is not opposed
to the application but he does have concerns about the security of the site. He noted the dirt bike
traffic that accesses the property. He added that the police have been to the site about 20 times per
month to remove dirt bikers. He stated his concern regarding the safety of the bridge, which passes
over Latimer Brook, and its insuffrcient turn ratio. He also inquired if any blasting were planned on
the site.

There being no other speakers in favor of the application, Mr. Weber opened the floor to those
wishing to speak in opposition.

Mr. Brian Hardink, 21 Wayne Drive stated that the turn radius onto the bridge is insufficient. He
noted that with an l8-wheel truck, several point turns would be required to negotiate the bridge.
He recommended widening the bridge to accommodate the trucks.
