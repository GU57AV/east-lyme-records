The East Lyme Zoning Commission held a Public Hearing and Regular Meeting on October 19,2000
at the Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania Ave., Niantic, CT. Mrs. Athena Cone chaired the meeting. Mrs.
Cone called the ineeting to order at 7:30 PM.

PRESENT: Secretary Athena Cone, Shawn Mclaughlin, David Chamberlain, Norman Peck,
                   Alternates Mark Nickerson, Donna Orefice, and Robert Bulmer,

Absent:            Chairman William Weber, Ed Gada, William Mulholland,ZoningOfficer,

Also present: Rose Ann Hardy, Ex-officio.

I.     PUBLIC HEARING

Application of Patrick and Ann Quinn for a Special Permit to permit occupancy of the Aces
High Recreational Park, 301 Chesterfield Road, twelve (12) months per year.

Panel: Mrs. Cone, Mrs. Orefice, Mr. Peck, Mr. Mclaughlin, Mr. Chamberlain, and Mr. Nickerson

Mrs. Cone opened the Public Hearing at 7:30 PM.

Mrs. Orefice read the Legal Ad and noted it had been properly published. She also read into the
record:
        1. Letter from M. Ousterhout, Conservation Commission dated October 19,2000.
        2. Letter from William Mulholland,Zoning Official, dated October 19,2000.
The applicant, Patrick Quinn, addressed the Commission. Mr. Quinn stated that he, along with his
wife, Ann, and daughter, Kathy operate Aces High RV Park. He added that Allen Beavers, Executive
Director of the CT Campground Owners Association was also present. Mr. Quinn stated he is
requesting the option to have adult only camping from November I and March 31.

Mr. Quinn read the "Applicants Comments" submitted to the Commission. He indicated that the
request for modification of the Special Permit to allow winter camping is the ooresult of evolution", i.e.,
demand by transient workers servicing Millstone Station, Pfizers and other projects in the area.

Mr. Quinn also reviewed the Winter Camping Rules and Regulations that Aces High will impose upon
winter campers. Among these rules was the prohibition of children. Mr. Quinn stated that the rules

prevent mobile homes in the park, use of tarp or storage sheds, and use of decks. He indicated that the
campers must be movable and usable for roads within 10 minutes of notice.

Mrs. Cone inquired as to the definition of "adult". Mr. Quinn stated that winter camping would be
restricted to persons over the age of 18 years. He added that the Park cannot be used as a permanent
mailing address. He stated that everyone gets a copy of the Rules & Regulations and sign a statement
indicating that they will abide by them. These Rules & Regulations are also posted.

Mrs. Cone inquired what the usual stay would be by these transient workers. Mr. Quinn stated that the
time ',vould vary for hansient workers, but usually would last2-3 months. Sometimes there may be
campers staying overnight or just a few days on their way to some other destination. He added that
these individuals would be using businesses and restaurants in East Lyme.

Mr. Peck read the applicable Section 20.17 regarding mobile homes and recreational trailers of the
Zoning Regulations.
       "Such units occupied as a residence will not be permitted on any lot in any district except for
      recreational purposes and in an approved camping site. Occupancy of trailer shall be limited to
      60 days per year."
He questioned how the town could monitor this.

Mr. Quinn stated that he did not think that there has been any enforcement of that regulations in any
RV park. He stated that seasonal campers (6-8 -usually retired persons) stay the whole summer
season, some leave their trailer and come on weekends and/or for a week(s) vacation. He added that
he charges both a weekly rate and a monthly rate.

Mr. Peck inquired if Mr. Quinn had considered requesting an amendment to the regulations to loosen
the 60-day time restriction. Mr. Quinn stated that the regulations were antiquated, as most
campgrounds in the State of CT do not place a 60-day limit on camping. He added that the CT
campground owners have volunteered to assist towns in revising their regulations pertaining to
campgrounds.

Mr. Peck inquired if a 90-day limitation could be workable. Mr. Quinn stated that possibly 9O-days
would work in the winter, but it places severe restrictions on the RV Park in the summer months. The
only campgrounds in Connecticut which have short time restrictions are the State Campgrounds.

Mrs. Cone stated, in response to Mrs. Hardy inquiry, that the application had not gone to Planning
Commission for comment because it had been preapproved. She added that she had spoken with Mr.
Mulholland in the afternoon regarding this.

Mrs. Cone opened the floor to anyone wishing to speak in favor of the application.

Allen Beaver, Executive Director, CT Campground Owners Association stated that there are 54
private and 12 State campgrounds in the state and the regulations governing those are in general the
same except for the length of stay. The State of CT campgrounds have a 2-week limit; private
campgrounds do not have that limitation. Mr. Beaver also stated that the Aces High RV Park has
received an award from the National Association as the 'State of the Art' campground.

Mr. Beaver added that the CCOA Adult Only Policy is that no children from a year round campground
shall be enrolled in the public schools. He stated that this policy has been tested and stood.
Additionally, by state law, a campground cannot be a permanent address.

Mr. Beaver noted that the East Lyme Regulations limit a camper's stay to 60 days per year. He stated
that this was extremely restrictive. Where 60-day stays exist, the camper can physically move out of
the campground for 24 hours, retum and reregister. This regulation has been removed from the
regulations of many towns.

Mr. Chamberlain inquired as to the definition of 'tourist'. Mr. Beaver stated that a 'tourist' is 60 miles
from home.

Mr. Beaver stated that currently there are in Connecticut 8,000 private camp sites and 14,000 State
camp sites. Of these, 60Yo are Connecticut residents.

Marvin Schutt,29 Edgewood Rd. North stated that he is familiar with the efforts of the applicants in
their making Aces High a high quality RV park. He added that the applicants have, since the initial
planning for the Park, attempted to comply with all East Lyme Regulations. He added that the 60-day
requirement be amended to 6-months, as it is customary for campgrounds to allow seasonal camping
and the 60-day limitation is too restrictive. Mr. Schutt also indicated that, as mentioned by Mr. Quinn
previously, there is a time factor involved with this application.
