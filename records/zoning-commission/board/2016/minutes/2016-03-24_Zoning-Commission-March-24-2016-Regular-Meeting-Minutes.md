1. CONTINUATION OF APPLICATION OF EDWARD M. CASSELLA, ESQ., FOR WHITE GATE FARM,
FOR AN AMENDMENT TO THE EAST LYME ZONING REGULATIONS SECTION 1, SECTION 5.2.9,
AND SECTION 25 FOR FARM EVENTS.

Chairman Walker stated they have 65 days to make a decision.
Mr. Donovan stated he would like to look at other town’s Regulations.
Mr. Mulholland stated he can review that in order to clarify for the Commission.

Mr. Manning stated we can see what the State recommends to make farming viable, and what other
Commissions are doing.

Chairman Walker stated we want to know how to strike a balance.

Mr. Donovan stated we have to look out for the town, most of the testimony was in support of White
Gate Farm, but this affects the whole town. He wants to see what other towns do.

Mr. Peck stated he is fine with holding off. He strongly supports locally home grown food; it is a little
price to pay for the benefit. It is extremely important to encourage farming. He is confident the
Commission can filter out the nice words said about White Gate Farm because this is not a site specific

application.
Mr. Manning stated it seems that there is one issue, noise from amplified music at a wedding reception.
Mr. Donovan stated there is dancing and drinking at weddings, we have to look out for the town.
Chairman Walker stated the focus will be on amplification of music.

Mr. Donovan stated they have the amplified music stopping at 10:00 p.m.

Mr. McPherson stated it sounds like we should continue this.

Mr. Dwyer stated this could be every weekend of the summer.

Mr. Mulholland stated he can research this for the next meeting.

This item was continued to April 7.

2. APPLICATION OF THEODORE A. HARRIS FOR A TEXT AMENDMENT TO THE EAST LYME ZONING
REGULATIONS TO ADD A DEFINITION FOR AN ANCHOR STORE IN THE GATEWAY DISTRICT.

Motion (1) Mr. McPherson moved to approve the application of Theodore A. Harris for a text
amendment to the East Lyme Zoning Regulations to add a definition for an anchor store in the
Gateway District.

Seconded by Mr. Manning.
