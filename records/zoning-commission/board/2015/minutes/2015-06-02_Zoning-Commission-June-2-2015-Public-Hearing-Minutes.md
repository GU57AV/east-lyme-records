1. APPLICATION OF TODD SAKOWSKI, TO AMEND THE EAST LYME ZONING REGULATIONS
SECTION 1.79, 9.2.10, 20.20.8 AND 25.5 TO PERMIT THE OPERATION OF A TAPROOM
BREWERY

Chairman Walker confirmed the legal advertisement ran on this item.
Mr. McPherson read a letter from the Planning Commission.
Mr. McPherson read a letter from James Rabbitt of SCCOG Regional Planning Commission.

1
Mr. McPherson read a memo from Mr. Mulholland.

Todd Sakowski of 968 Shewville Road, Ledyard stated he has been at some of the past meeting, and this
Commission does not have an easy job. In 2012 Connecticut started allowing breweries to sell directly
to consumers. Niantic is prime location for this type of business. They will brew three barrels per batch.
50% of what they sell will be sold at retail. A large percentage of sales will be growler sales. Connecticut
prohibits the sale of growlers beyond when package stores close. They would close between 7:00 p.m.
and 9:00 p.m. Their business plan calls for them to close at 9:00 p.m. There will be a "bring your own
food" policy. There is no shortage of restaurants in Niantic. There are 2 other restaurants in their
building, and one is willing to delivery to the brewery.

Mr. Mulholland stated they would have to apply for a Special Permit if they find this favorable. This is
just background information.

Mr. Sakowski stated he and his wife are TIPS trained, and they are trained to recognize and mitigate
intoxication. The price of a craft beer pint is $5.50 to $8.00, the higher price reduces overconsumption.
There are thirteen craft breweries in Connecticut right now and none of them stay open past 9:00 p.m.

Cranberry Meadow Farm will take their spent grain every day free of charge to use on their farm. The
spent grain does have an odor.

Mr. Donovan asked if there is an odor during manufacturing.
Mr. Sakowski stated it is a sweet smell, and doesn’t travel far.
Mr. Mulholland asked if it will be vented to outside.

Mr. Sakowski stated that depends on the brewery, their brewery would not be vented to the outside.
He spoke to Mr. Mulholland about the wording of Section 20.20.8, and that should read “malt
beverage”.

Mr. Mulholland stated he is asking in the definition for the manufacture and sale of malt beverages.
Connecticut Statutes allow different things. We want this business to be successful. The definition
seems to limit what can be sold, and the definition is what the Town would be charged with enforcing.

Mr. Sakowski stated they would produce and sell what they are producing, along with other beer and
wine.

Mr. Mulholland stated the definition may need to be broadened.
PUBLIC COMMENT
Mr. Nickerson entered a letter (Exhibit 1) in support of this application.

Mr. McPherson asked about the recommendation from the Planning Commission to include the CA
zone.
Mr. Mulholland stated that would be a subject for another night. The legal advertisement was run on
this application. It is food for thought for the future though.

Mr. Peck suggested the following sentence be added to the definition: “Taproom Brewery may also
sell other non-manufactured fermented beverages.”

Mr. Sakowski stated it would be beer and wine only.

Mr. Peck also suggested that the language “no more than 50% of the above products may be
manufactured elsewhere.”

Mr. Sakowski stated fermented beverages include beer, wine, sake, mead, and cider.
Chairman Walker asked if they would sell cider in the fall.

Mr. Sakowski stated they have no plans to, but they may do that.

Mr. Donovan asked if they would stay open past 9:00 p.m.

Mr. Sakowski stated there is a chance, they may look to expand.

Mr. Dwyer stated he feels they are broadening it too much.

Chairman Walker stated this is a unique application. The downtown has come to life. The goal is to
keep it that way. He asked Mr. Sakowski if they will have any food at all.

Mr. Sakowski stated they will have a popcorn machine, and may have some snacks.
Mr. Mulholland asked about the setting of other breweries within the State.

Mr. Sakowski stated some are in downtown settings. A lot are in warehouses, those with big budgets
also have a restaurant.

Chairman Walker stated we don’t want a host of bars downtown. Mr. Dwyer has concerns about this
evolving into a bar. The cost will reduce over consumption. When would trucks be leaving to deliver the
product elsewhere?

Mr. Sakowski stated it will not be an invasive delivery system.

Mr. Peck asked if the applicant could live with the stipulation that other sales of non-manufactured
products are the minority percentage of their retail sales, at 50%.

Mr. Sakowski stated those don’t envision that being the majority of sales. They will only have a few
guest taps.

Motion (1) Mr. Donovan moved to close the Public Hearing.

Seconded by Mr. McPherson.
Motion Passed 5-0.

2. APPLICATION OF JULIE SMART, FOR SMARTY’S, FOR A SPECIAL PERMIT FOR OUTDOOR DINING
AT PROPERTY IDENTIFIED IN THE APPLICATION AS 215 MAIN STREET, NIANTIC

Chairman Walker confirmed the legal advertisement ran on this item.
Mr. Mulholland stated this is an application for a patio, and they would like to have music.
Gilbert Smart stated they would like to have outdoor music. There would be no entertainment beyond
10:00 p.m. That is stated in their lease with their landlord. They do not have a young crowd. They are a
restaurant. There is nothing across the street from them. It wouldn’t intrude on anyone. They would
like to have a single guitar player for entertainment. They are looking for ways to generate funds. They
would use a small amplifier. He will control the volume. It is really more of background music.
Mr. Mulholland asked what the hours of operation for the music would be.
Mr. Smart stated 6:30 p.m. to 9:30 p.m.
Mr. Donovan asked if that would be the same on weekends.
Mr. Smart stated it would only be done on weekends.
There was no public comment.
Motion (2) Mr. McPherson moved to close the Public Hearing.
Seconded by Mr. Donovan.
Motion Passed 5-0.
