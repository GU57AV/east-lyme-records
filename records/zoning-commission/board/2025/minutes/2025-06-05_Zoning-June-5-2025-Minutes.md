             -Anne Santoro made the following remarks:
             -The Ad-Hoc was established by the Board of Selectmen, a result of 14 months of research
             analysis and discussion.
             -The recommendations establish a key role inZoning in addressing STRs in town.
             -The recommendation is to allow STR's but establish reasonable restrictions on them,
             accomplished through Zoning, Permitting, Ordinance, and effective enforcement, this would
             re spect Zoning authority.
             -This report being described does not take the place of the status report of 412024, rather it is a
             compliment to it.
             -Section 1: purpose and scope.
             -Section 2: Whibhey Decision. The Supreme Court decision regarding regulating STRs. It is
             very impactful to the recommendations. A beach community in Branford, Pine Orchards, where
             Mr. Whibhey owned a home, he did not resid there, but he rented it out as STR. The seemed to
             be used as party house The 1994 Regulations in place when Mr. Whibhey was renting the
house changed in2018 and expressly prohibited STRs. He received a cease and desist and then
litigation ensued, ultimately making it to the Supreme Court.
-The Court's ruling was: under the 1994 Zoning Regulations, time limitations that are not
expressly stated for the rental of a single family dwelling will not be inferred. The minority
opinion was that there is a difference between single family use and shoft term transient use.
-The decision highlights the need to amend the Town's regulations as concerns STRS, such as
defining them and adding any related regulations as necessary, just as they do for B&B's Inns,
hotels/motels and other traditional forms of lodging.
-Whibhey sets a path forward through Zoningto promote stability in residential zones.
-Section 3: P.A. 24-143 and pending legislation before the CT legislature. Any municipality in
CT is authorized to adopt, by ordinance, to license STRs and to regulate their operation and
use, in a land use situation, does not speak to zoning. It is not mandatory. 30 day or less rental,
and does not include traditional lodging, is the universal definition of STR. (Can change the
number for East Lyme but must be under 30.)
-If the Town went down a path of using an ordinance to deal with STRs, they would apply to
all of the Town, including the 3 beach communities which have their own Zoning Regulations.
-Another piece of legislation to pass is raised bill72-38 and would establish a statewide STR
registry and allow municipalities the option of imposing a supplemental tax. Instead of the state
sharing apart of the l5Yo they get, towns would have the ability to charge up to 2.75Yo of the
proceeds from that.
-Section 4: methodology. The governing principals of the committee, when considering
recommending changes, should meet a need(s), have a pu{pose, and should benefit the town as
a whole.
-The committee held 3 public hearings and took public delegations at virtually all meetings;
they received many emails.
-Section 11:policy priorities. Recognize that STRs are an alternative to traditional lodging, to
ensure public safety in the operation of STRs, to maintain stability and a sense of community,
to deter potential adverse impacts, and to encourage the most appropriate use of land
throughout the town, consistent with the remaining provisions of the Zoning Regulations.
-recommend reviewing other towns such as Groton or Preston and their regulations, and how
they are doing with enforcement.

-Sections 5: general policy. Doing nothing is not an option.

-Other towns in CT which address STRs include Groton, Preston, Noank, and those regulations

-Gary Farrugia, 31 Saunders Dr, made the following remarks:
-Section 10: enforcement. Consulted with the Granicus Firm regarding STRs. Granicus
guestimates that only l}Yo of STR landlords comply with mandates to obtain a permit. The
level of compliance EL achieves will be directly related to the level of enforcement the town
applies to regulating STRs.
-There was a lot of support from homeowners and absentee landlords, who only use their
homes for STRs.
-About a dozen residents made complaints about problems arising from STRs in their
neighborhoods. Many more residents raised fears about the future of their neighborhoods.
-The committee was unanimous about having some form of registration as an effective tool for
enforcement. It was also unanimous that enforcement should be strong, workable and
financially feasible. They were unable to come up with a majority to specify what level of
enforcement was appropriate.
-The options included 3 levels: Minimum, such as in the Town of Lyme, safety inspections
recommended but not required, no monitoring or enforcement procedures in place and a
nominal permit fee ($25lannually). Moderate, such as in Preston & Simsbury, mandatory safety
inspections, part-time clerical help in zoningto administer the permitting process, the Zoning
Commission adjudicates the complaints over STRs. Fees range $100-$200. Robust, such as in
Bozrah & Ledyard, mandatory safety inspections by Fire Dept, FT zoning officer in place here
and this person reviews/approves applications, monitors STR social media apps for permitting
compliance, investigates noncompliance and has the authority to reject or void permits for
noncompliance. Fees typically $250 and up.
-The EL for marshal charges $65 for a safety inspection.
-A process that mandates safety inspections, annual renewal and administrative oversight will
help preserve the character ofthe neighborhoods.
-A robust enforcement procedure gives the Zoning Dept a powerful tool to revoke permits for
those who disregard the regulations authorized. Zoning regulations will give landlords clear
compliance accountability.
