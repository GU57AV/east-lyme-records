PUBLIC HEARING

1. Application of Keith B. Neilson, PE of Docko, Inc,, for John & Lisa
Lazine, owners for a Special] Permit to construct a dock in a Tidal Marsh
Zone at property identified in the application as 90 Old Black Point
Road, Niantic, Assessor’s Map 08.3, Lot 5.

Mr. Mulholland stated the legal ad was run on this item.
Mr. Carpenteri read a memo from Mr. Mulholland.

Keith Neilson of Docko Inc. stated the dock will be approximately 30 feet in length, and
then there will be a 30 foot hinged ramp onto a float for securing boats, The DEP and the
Army Corps of Engineers have approved the project. The float will be 18 inches above
the bottom. The pier has to have 5 feet of clearance over the marsh crossing. They
would be using pressure treated southern yellow pine, the New York DEC found that to
be acceptable and would not be harmful to the marsh or the estuary. This dock will be in
harmony with the adjacent development, and will not diminish the value of the adjacent
properties. It will be centered on the site so there are no setback issues.

Chairman Salerno asked about the history of the tidal marsh.

Mr. Mulholland stated the applicants want to put in this dock, and it has already been
permitted by the DEP, This is their final step. This commission’s jurisdiction is at the
mean high water mark, below that is the DEP’s jurisdiction. We are to be looking at
where the dock touches the land. He suggested that they ask the applicant to clarify as far
as parking goes, since sometimes residents let their friends keep their boats on their
docks, and sometimes parking issues do arise.

Mr. Neilson stated they can have up to a 24 foot boat, and it will be for their personal use,
there will be one, or maybe 2 boats.

Ms. Carabelas stated we can be specific in the motion.

Mr. Mulholland stated they can specify that it be for personal use only. Any parking on
the road is the jurisdiction of the Board of Selectmen. They can find out if parking is to

be done on the site.

Mr. Neilson stated the applicants live across the street, and they have parking areas in
their driveway.

Mr. Mulholland asked if they have no intent to park there.
Mr. Neilson stated that is correct.

Chairman Salerno stated this is for one lot, and the members have the DEP permit in their
packet.

Mr. Bulmer asked about the pressure treated southern pine’s toxicity into the water.

Mr. Neilson stated the chromium stays in the wood, about 10 to 20% of the copper
leaches out in approximately 6 months, and approximately 60 to 80% of the arsenic
leaches out in approximately 6 months. . That is only the parts that are in contact with
the water, and most of this structure is out of the water. The amount of actual chemical in

six months would probably be a gallon of arsenic and a quarter cup of copper. The
amount of dilution is significant; the tidal flow past this area is millions of gallons. The

impact is negligible.

Mr. Bulmer asked about the impact on the flora and fish population,

Mr. Neilson stated the fish population is transient, and the impact is considered
negligible.

Mr. Bulmer asked if there are any local wells in the area,

Mr. Neilson stated it would have to be in the immediate area to be affected by the
leaching of the chemicals and there are none within 100 feet.

Mr. Bulmer asked what the mean low water level is.

Mr. Neilson stated 1 foot.

Mr. Bulmer asked if that precludes the use of big boats.
Mr. Neilson stated yes it does preclude the use of big boats.

Ms. Carabelas asked how they would protect this from being used by people without
permission.

Mr. Neilson stated there will be a gate at the first pile bend.
Ms. Carabelas asked if they will have a private property sign.

Mr. Neilson stated they can do that if this Commission wants them to.
Public Comment

George Perinis of 112 Old Black Point Road asked why they are coming to this
Commission now after construction has already begun. Normally in hearings the permit
is obtained first.

Mr. Mulholland stated the dock is already permitted by DEP, but at this point they do not
have the right to attach it to land. Most people do not know about these regulations and
they very seldom even see these applications.

Mr. Perinis stated as far as this being in harmony with the land, these pilings are much
larger than anything else along Old Black Point Road, there seems to be 8 pilings there.
This dock seems much bigger. He asked if hoists are involved in this construction. He
stated the fish may be transient, but the shellfish are not.

Ruth Reluga of 10 Bronson Street is a member of the Commission for the Conservation
of Natural Resources, but is here tonight speaking for herself. She agrees with Mr.
Perinis, The appropriateness of this dock is questionable. She did contact the DEP when
she saw the construction and she was told the permit is for 92 Oid Black Point Road. The
amount of water in that area is negligible on normal low tide, and absent on the winter
tides and full moon tides. This dock extends out where people used to bring boats
through, such as canoes or rowboats. The town has a lot more say in this, and should be
involved from the beginning.

Heather Perinis of 112 Old Black Point Road stated she read the proposal, and the
consent was for two new pilings. Who provides the oversight once this is constructed?
She questioned the scale of the dock. She believes it will affect their property values, A
gallon of arsenic leaching would be high potency not only to the shellfish, but to the
vegetation. It appears the hoist will stay there through the winter months.

Mr. Neilson stated the DEP does not regulate boat usage in regard to what is berthed at
that facility. There are many boats in the area left in the marsh at various times. The
Shellfish Commission and the Harbor Commission did address this application. The
permit is for 90 Old Black Point Road. The float and ramp will be removed in November
and will be relaunched in April. The DEP requires as built drawings and those will be
filed at the completion of the work. He can submit a copy of the New York DEC study
on the arsenic issue. There are a lot of docks along this stretch of road. The float
suspension devices are designed to keep the float off of the bottom.

Chairman Salerno asked about the number of pilings.
Mr. Neilson stated there are six holding the pier and 4 holding the float in place.
Ms. Carabelas stated the permit states a certified copy or Notice of Permit must be

recorded on the land records within 30 days of the issuance, and proof of that filing must
be submitted to DEP within 60 days.
Mr. Neilson stated he will have to check on that.

Mr. Walker asked about the concern that it protrudes out too far to impede small boat
traffic.

Mr. Neilson stated that standard criteria are that the dock cannot protrude out more than
25% of the water way width.

Motion (1) Ms. Carabelas moved to close the Public Hearing,
Seconded by Mr. Carpenteri.

Motion Passed 6-0.
