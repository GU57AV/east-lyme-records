MarkZamarka, Town Attorney, came forward to detail accessory units and shared some of
the following:
                                                                                            3



o The Act allows accessory dwelling units or ad use as of right on the same lot as a
    single family dwelling.
o A town can opt out of this requirement, but must do so by January of 2023.
o lf not, after that any. any existing regulations that do not comport with the Act will
    be considered invalid.
r Essentially the choice A town has is to either adopt zoning regulations consistent
    with the act or decide to opt out.
o lf a town does not opt out, and does decide to allow this use in the town, the town
    can still regulate or prohibit their use as short term or vacation rentals.
r   lf the town decides to allow accessory dwelling units, the regulations must allow
    three types of these units: it can be detached, detached or contained within the
    principal dwelling.
o   lt has to provide for a maximum floor area of not less than 30% of the principal
    dwelling floor area or 1000 square feet whichever is less.
r   Any setbacks and lot size coverage for detached units have to be less than or
    equal to that required for the principal dwelling.
o   They can't enforce stricter lot coverages and setbacks than the ones required for
    the principal dwelling.
o   Any landscaping or architectural designs have to be the same as those apply to the
    principal dwelling.
o   The approval process is a little bit different than most zoning permits because it's
    allowed as of right.
e The approval process cannot require a public hearing or special permit decisions
    must be made within 65 days from the date that the application is received.
o An applicant can grant an extension of no more than 65 days.
r A town cannot require a passageway between the unit and the principal dwelling or
    an exterior door except as required by the fire code.
o A town cannot require more than one parking space for the unit, cannot require a
    familial, marital or employment relationship between occupants of the unit and the
    principal dwelling.
o   A town cannot require a minimum age for occupants of the dwelling, and cannot
    require the correction of the nonconformity be a condition of approval.
o   A town has until January 1st of 2023 to opt out.
o   Opting out is a two step process that begins with this Commission.
o   lf the Commission decides it wants to opt out, it has to hold a public hearing on the
    proposed opt out, subject to the usual guidelines, and it has to do so affirmatively
    by a two thirds vote.
o   The reasons for the decision must be stated on the record.
o   The decision must be published within 15 days as per usual guidelines and if that
    happens it has to go before the Board of Selectmen, the town's legislative body,
    and they also have to confirm it by a two thirds vote.
o   East Lyme currently does not allow accessory dwelling units as they're defined in
    the act.

    a      The definition section of your zoning regulations does allow in-law
           accommodations, which are single efficiency dwelling units that are located within
           the principal building, and are subject to special permit control found in Section 25
           of the regulations.
    o      Because this is an as of right approval and there can be no special permit or public
           hearings attached to an application for an accessory unit.
    o      It bypasses the usual process that the Zoning Commission would have for both the
           adoption of a regulation and the approval of a zoning permit application.
    o      Accessory units cannot be counted as part of the town's affordable housing stock.
