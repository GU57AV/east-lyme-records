     2. Legislative Updates in relation to Public Act21-29, the Town Attorney to present
         information on new legislation regarding Accessory Apartments, Commission
         Training, Affordable Housing Plans and PA21-1 Recreational Marijuana.

Attorney Zamarka reported the following:
     o A couple of recent bills signed into law that will have an impact on applications coming
       before this commission.
     o  PA 21-29 covers a lot of ground, the main point of which is dealing with accessory
       apartments.
     o An accessory apartment is defined as a separate dwelling unit that is located on the
       same block as the principal dwelling that has cooking facilities, and is compliant or
       exempt from any applicable building health, fire or safety rules.
     o  Beginning January 1st,2022, zoning regulation shall provide that at least one accessory
       apartment shall be allowed, as of right, on any lot that contains a single family dwelling,
        unless the Town decides to opt out.
     o "As of right", means no public hearing is held; once the application is filed, the new law
 .     states that a decision has to be made on it within 65 days from receipt of the application.
     o An accessory apartment can be attached or located within an existing principal dwelling,
       or it can be detached and located on the same lot.
     o Currently our regulations do not address these in any way.
     o  lt goes into effect in January of next year and if the Town decides to opt out that
        process starts with the Zoning Commission.

    Mr. Zamarka said the final legislative item he wishes to discuss is responsible and equitable
    regulation of adult use cannabis, and shared the following:
        o   There are several definitions that apply to the Zoning Commission in regards to
            cannabis.
        o   First is a cannabis establishment; there are 10 different types of cannabis
            establishments, including a producer, a dispensary facility cultivator, and micro
            cultivator.
        e   The difference between those is how much growth space is in the particular
            establishment, a retailer, a hybrid retailer, delivery service or transport.
        o   A town through its zoning can prohibit the establishment of any cannabis establishment
            within the town, or it can establish reasonable regulations regarding our signage and
            proximity of a cannabis establishment.
        o   A town cannot allow more than one retailer and one micro cultivator, for every 25,000
            residents.
        o   We're still under 20,000 so until June 30 of 2024 one would be permitted, and after that
            date, the Department of Consumer Protection may change those figures and
            percentages.
        o   lt's an allowed use, but it is allowed as a special permit use which would garner sorne
            controls.
        o   There's no opt out provision, the Zoning Commission either decides to prohibit it by an
            amendment to its regulations, or there's a provision where 1O% of the electors of the
            town can file a petition with the clerk, asking that the matter be put on the ballot for the
            next regular election.
        o   This is very new and we haven't gotten any guidance from the Office of Legislative
            Research yet but he's sure that's forthcoming.
       o    All of this is just an overview.
       r    He's sure we'll potentially be getting applications for accessory apartments, and the
            Commission willwant to schedule this for a public hearing.
