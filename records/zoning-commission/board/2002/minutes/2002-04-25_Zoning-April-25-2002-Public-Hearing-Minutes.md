acquire housing in Connecticut and that housing costs are going up dramatically for people living in

Appeals Act allows the court to examine evidence presented to aZoning Commission and to

Mr. Zizka indicated that the applicant is proposing zoning regulations appropriate for the
development of Affordable Housing on this specific parcel. The proposed regulation stipulates the
height of multi-family structures would be 3-stories, less than 45 feet; the number of units would be
on a sliding scale based on the number of bedrooms; front yard setback of 25 feet; coverage of 50%
of the lot area; off street parking would be 2 for one bedroom units and 3 off street spaces for 2
bedroom or larger. He presented a concept drawing (Exhibit #3 - Development Information -

Ms. Balint stated that the OLISP has reviewed the zone change proposal for consistency with the
policies and standards of the CT Coastal Management Act. The OLISP found that the zone change
and proposed regulation change are inconsistent with the policies and standards of the Act based on
severe development constraints, unacceptable adverse impact to water quality and coastal resources
and inconsistency with the water use criteria of Coastal Management Act, as well as with the goals
to preserve and protect of the Coastal Area Development Plan, Town Plan of Conservation and
Development and Harbor Management Plan. The proposal would allow for inappropriately
intensive development in an area incapable of supporting intensive development without significant
environmental consequences. Such development would cause sedimentation and erosion, nitrogen
loading, impact on SAVs, finfish, shellfish and wildlife on the site and in Niantic River and Latimer
Brook. CT DEP recommended denial of the proposal for zone change and the addition of Section
32 of the zoning regulations.

Mr. Bulmer inquired if the DEP would characterize the intensive development proposed as a public
health and safety hazard. Ms. Balint stated that the proposal and site have been reviewed by many
people in the DEP who have extensive knowledge in the area of coastal resources, environment,
water quality, etc. and this is the opinion stated in the letter submitted as Exhibit 10.

sewers that are within the sewershed, but the sewers have not yet been built in these areas. The
difference between 1.5 MGD and 0.9 MGD would be used to accommodate these areas.
