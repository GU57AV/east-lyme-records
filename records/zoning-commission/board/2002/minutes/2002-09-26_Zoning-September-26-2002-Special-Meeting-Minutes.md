Mr. Fraser indicated that the Town of East Lyme has had a standing sewer facilities plan approved by
the State of CT to provide sewers to identified areas for future development and existing dwelopments
that may require them in the future, He stated that to be forced to provide all of our resources and
reserve capacity to an area never planned for intense development, would be contrary to the State's
mandates and imposes a sever financial and environmental hardship to East Lyme.

Mr, Fraser stated that the Town has signed a Consent Order with DEP. Recently, the Water Dept.
completed well #6 to help in the current demand, and provide a safe yield. On weekends in the ,u*rr,
with increasing State use, the Town routinely pumps over 3 million gallons per day. This now requires
that all of the town wells be onJine and under the mandated Consent Order, the Town is forced to shut
down town wells when stream flow is below certain trigger levels. A forced legal ruling to connect a
limited water supply to a development of this size, would place the town in jeopardy foi prior protection
and present use.

Mr. Fraser indicated that the town has worked to acquire the subject land. He stated he has worked with
DEP officials over the last four years, and the Governor's office, on the open space funding program.
He stated, he recently worked with the developer on obtaining fair appraisals by using a conceptual site
plan of five acre lots that met all the zoning requirements. A 35-39 house development was used as the
basis for discussion using on-site sewers and well, including setbacks and roads to have a minimal
impact on the Town and for appraisal purposes. The town has never said the land cannot be developed.
But, he argued, you cannot do something that imposes a financial and environmental hardship on the
community. He state that he has been working with third party investors with respect to preierving the
site and area. He stated he supports the fair and reasonable transition of land in the open space
preservation process.

Mr. Fraser also stated that with respect to the Golden Spur area where there are about 12 wells that need
to be replaced with municipal water, he is working with New London to obtain water. The problem at
this time is that East Lyme is only allowed 35,000 GPD and would only supply the existing dwellings.
