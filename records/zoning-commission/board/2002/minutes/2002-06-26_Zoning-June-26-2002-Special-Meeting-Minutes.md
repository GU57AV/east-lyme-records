Mr. Chamberlain noted that the First Selectman testified at the Public Hearing that a substantial
offer of over $2 million was made to purchase the piece by the State of CT Dept. of Environmental
Protection. The landowners and developer indicated in testimony that they want to and had a right

Mr. Bulmer commented that the Town of East Lyme has Affordable Housing on Boston Post Rd.
and King Arthur Drive. He stated he supports Affordable Housing but not at the sacrifice of a piece
of ecologically fragile land. The benefits gained are far outweighed by the disaster one would
create by developing the site in the manner indicated in the applicant's conceptual plan. Mr.
Nickerson stated that he is not opposed to Affordable Housing, but does not support it on the subject
property. There is other property in town that is less sensitive ecologically, doesn't have health and
safety issues, and that is better suited to development of multiple housing units.

Mr. Bulmer stated that he does not support approval of the application as it does not protect the
public interest in health and safety issues: availability of water and sewers, potential pollution of the
Niantic River and Latimers Brook, unsafe sight lines at the access road onto US Route 1. He added
that the public interest clearly outweighs the need for Affordable Housing in this particular case.
He added that the public interest couldn't be protected by reasonable changes to the Affordable
Housing development.

C      Such public interest clearly outweighs the need for Affordable Housing.
       Given the substantial public interests cited above; the collective totality of the evidence in
       the hearing record (Exhibits #l through #45) documenting the inconsistency of the

       impact on the public health and safety; and the historical efforts to preserve the Oswegatchie
       Hills area in particular, the Commission finds that the substantial public interests clearly
       outweigh the need for Affordable Housing in the specified location.

D      The public interest cannot be protected by reasonable changes to the Affordable Housing
       development.
       Because there is no specific proposal for a particular housing development project, the Zonin
       g Commission cannot consider whether any "plan specific" problems might be eliminated or
       adjusted by reasonable design modifications. The Commission cannot require modifications
       of plans that do not exist and are not before the Commission. The physical "site specific"
       constraints in the area ofthe proposed zoning district cannot be changed by a decree ofthis
       Commission.

       The Commission further finds that there is a substantial public interest in rejecting the
