Mr. Mark Powers,4 Round Rock Road, Niantic, CT addressed the hearing. Mr. Powers served in
the CT General Assembly during the 1980s as a member of the House and then of the Senate
representing the areas of, among others, East Lyme, Waterford. Mr. Powers and Mr. Hoyt, State
Representative at the time, requested legislation, which was passed unanimously, concerning the
Niantic River Gateway Public Act 87-512. The General Assembly established a specific
ConservationZone from south of the Golden Spur area, south along the shore of the Niantic River
on the west side and ending approximately 5,000 ft to the south as well as a smallet area on the east
side in the town of Waterford. The legislation allowed for the towns of East Lyme and Waterford to
establish the Niantic River Gateway Commission and to develop standards by which any
development that would occur in this designated ConservationZone would adhere to. Mr. Powers
submitted Exhibit 16 - Public Act 87-512 and Exhibit 17 - Public Act 88-364 and pointed out
Section 4 in Public Act 87-512, which required the Niantic River Gateway Commission to "hold
public hearings for the purpose of developing the minimum standards, preserve the area within the
ConservationZone as here and after provided".

Don Landers from the Harbor Management Commission presented the Harbor
Management Commission Report. Exhibit 18. He stated that the Commission finds the
proposal fundamentally o'inconsistent with the East Lyme Harbor Management Plan". Mr.
Landers pointed out that the Commission reviewed the proposal with respect to its
consistency with the goals, objectives, and policies contained in Chapter 4 of the Plan. In
addition, water use and management guidelines contained in Chapter 5 were also reviewed
to determine if the proposal was consistent with those guidelines. Mr. Landers read that
portion of Chapter 5, pg. 4-5, which specifically address Oswegatchie Hills waterfront. "The
town should implement through appropriate zoning and other regulations, the policies of the
East Lyme Coastal Area Development Plan to protect the natural resource values of the
Oswegatchie Hill area. Any future development in the Oswegatchie Hill area should be of a
density and type consistent with the capability of coastal land and water resources in the area
to accommodate this development without significant impacts on natural resource values
occurring. The town should pursue any opportunities that may arise in the future to acquire
land that is not privately owned in the Oswegatchie Hill area to maintain this land as
permanent open space. The town should also encourage the use of private measures
including use of conservation easements and other measures to protect undeveloped land in
Oswegatchie Hill area." The Commission found the scope and density inconsistent with the
policies, goals and objectives of the Plan and recommended disapproval of the application.

Rick Canter, 27 Wells St., Niantic, CT, Vice Chair of the Waterford/El Shellfish
Commission submitted Exhibit 19 - Shelffish Report. Mr. Canter stated that the
Commission has been mandated by the State of Connecticut to "protect and maintain viable
shellfishing in the Niantic River". He pointed out that the Commission's purview includes
the Niantic River and the area directly effected by any development of Oswegatchie Hills.
Historically, the Niantic River has held large populations of shellfish and finfish. The
overall health of the Niantic River and its ability to sustain its shellfish population is
threatened by over-development in the River's watershed, which can cause damage to the
River's natural ability to cleanse itself through the tidal cycle. The Waterford/East Lyme
Shellfish Commission believes that the topography of Oswegatchie Hills is such that any
run-off from development would be highly detrimental to the Niantic River. The
Commission urges the Zoning Commission to protect Oswegatchie Hills and thereby the
ecology of the Niantic River.

Bruce Dasinger, 12 Stone Cliff Dr., Niantic, CT (Exhibit 20-Letterfrom Bruce Dasinger
and attachments) read his letter which addressed bird life in the Oswegatchie Hills area. He
indicated that development of the property as proposed would result in habitat degradation
for a large number of land and marine dwelling animals and plants, as well as bird life. Mr.
Dasinger indicated that 178 bird species use Oswegatchie Hills for food, cover from
predators and nesting. Many species of waterfowl use the River as a stopping place during
migration. Mr. Dasinger stated that he hoped the Commission would not approve the
application of Landmark Development Group, LLC so this habitat can be protected.

William Matthews, 82 West Cedar Street, Boston, MA, owner of part of the parcel known as
Sargents Head Realty Corp. along with Mrs. Cynthia Matthews, Waterford, CT and Jason
Matthews, Bethesda, MD addressed the Commission. He stated that the Matthews family
has a number of concerns regarding the history of their 160 acres and the bulk of the
waterfront property that the applicant is including in his application for change of zone. He
stated that the family has tried to interest the State and Town in purchasing the property over
the years and keep it as open space, but has not been successful. He added that his family,
as owners, have a right to economic use of their property and that it has been on the market
since 1982. If the property is worthy of protection as presented this evening, the town
should have acted more forcefully to acquire it. In 1987 the General Assembly provided a
$1 million grant in aid for the purchase of Oswegatchie Hills, however Waterford rejected
the idea of two-town zoning commission overseeing development in the area and blocked
the land purchases. The State Legislature repealed the grant in 1989 with no action taken on
the property. Mr. Matthews stated that there is a public good in the Town to having
Affordable Housing available. He stated he supports this application.
