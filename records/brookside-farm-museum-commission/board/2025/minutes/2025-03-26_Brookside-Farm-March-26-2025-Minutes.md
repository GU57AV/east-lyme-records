March 2025 Curator's Report

Grants and Assessments

       Completed the RFP process for the Historic Structures Report and selected AHS, Inc. of
       Storrs, CT. The consultant assigned to the project is James Sexton, who has previously
       worked on the Samuel Smith House.
   a   Had a phone meeting with Jennifer from the Community Foundation of Eastern
       Connecticut regarding the two grant applications I submitted. Based on our conversation,
       it seems that the grant for continuing the Homegrown National Park work has the highest
       chance ofsuccess.

Grounds

   .   Researched and ordered replacement cherry trees for the property.
   .   Installed new light fixtures throughout the house to improve lighting in each room.
   .   Collaborated with Ron Bence to revise the alarm codes while addressing other alarm
       system issues. Ron also replaced the locks on the two main doors to the house.

Programs

   .   Worked with Summer Metcalf on developing a new website for the museum.
   .   Collaborated with Emily Fiore on the cataloging of objects at the house.
   .   Successfully concluded the CFECT Homegrown National Park grant by utilizing all
       allocated funds. This allowed for the purchase of native planting roll-out garden kits. We
       are currently seeking groups and volunteers to help with planting. Additionally, I hired
       Swamp Yankee EarthCare for one more week of invasive species clearing.
   .   Plarured the next Book Club event, focusing on The Nature of Oalrs by Douglas Tallamy.
           o Event Description: Join us at Brookside Farm Museum for a discussion of The
               Nature of Oalcs: The Rich Ecologlt of Our Most Essential Native Trees. Oaks play
               a vital role in our ecosystems, supporting biodiversity and enhancing the
               environment in many ways. The event will be held at the museum, which is
               designated a Homegrown National Park, providing the perfect setting as we
               explore how to foster ecological health in our own backyards.
           o Douglas Tallamy, an entomologist and professor, shares insights into the critical
               role oaks play in sustaining wildlife, making this a must-read for conservationists
               and nature enthusiasts alike.
           o Each session will begin with a presentation by a local expert on nurturing
               biodiversity in our region. We're excited to kick off the series with a virtual
               presentation by the author!
           o First Discussion: Wednesday, May 14, 5:30-7:00 PM
           o Additional Meetings: June 10, July 8, August 12
           o Books are avallable for checkout at the library or for purchase at the event. To
               register, email brooksidefarmmuseum@gmail.com.
