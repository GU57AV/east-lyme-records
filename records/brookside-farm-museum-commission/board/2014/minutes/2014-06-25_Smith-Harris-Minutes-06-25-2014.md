Collections

+ I have purchased 12 pairs of curator gloves. I would like to request that from now on anyone who needs to
touch artifacts to move or clean them will kindly use the gloves to protect the artifacts from the dirt and
natural oils on our fingers.

+ Contacted Andy Derr to inform him that we were accepting his proposal for the textile closet. Waiting for
response.

+ [have been reading many documents and materials on the Averys and Smiths, as I update and re-write our
guidebook. I propose two small but significant changes in the interpretation of the house:

+ The room known as the “dressing room” be changed to the “sewing room.” This room is missing two
key features of standard “dressing rooms” in the 19th-century: direct access from the bedroom only,
and no built-in cabinetry. I believe, given the location of the room (above the front door, and a large
window for best light access) that this was a “sewing room.” This also allows for the interpretation of
technological changes happening in CT, and how those changes would impact small town farming
communities. It also mirrors the pattern of the changes represented in the house, regarding the
technological changes in cooking, from hearth to stove. I would like to move artifacts from the attic to
the room, and move some of the artifacts in there presently to the bedroom. The cost should be
minimal, if at all.

The room known as the “servery” I would like to change to the “office.” As it stands now, we

currently have an 8-room museum with 3 rooms specified for food consumption. That’s nearly half

the museum. I propose keeping the wall paper and rug the same; simply moving some furniture to
illustrate more of the male-working space Mr. Avery would have used to manage his farm and lands.
This will allow for us to interpret more of what his world encompassed; where, without an outside
office, he would have conducted his business. The blending of public and domestic spheres. We can
also continue to talk about how the use of rooms change over time, even in our own homes, and that
Mr. W. H. H. Smith may have used it as a “servery.” However, I think, given our interpretive time
period of 1845-1865, we should present it as an “office,” and then talk about the changes from there.
