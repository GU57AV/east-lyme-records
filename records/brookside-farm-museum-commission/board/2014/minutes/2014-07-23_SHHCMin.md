Curator’s Report--July
Submitted to the Smith-Harris Commission

We enjoy July with opened blinds and windows, and cool breezes blowing through the Smith-
Harris House as visitors enjoy tours! This is such a lovely time of year, where locals and out-of-
state visitors alike indulge in leisurely picnics on the grounds, visit the museum, and enjoy
summer’s vacation days--meanwhile, behind-the-scenes, hard work is going into museum
improvements and fall programming!

Tours/Staffing

I’m happy to announce that Cindy is able to return as part-time interpreter for the final month
of summer. She’ll begin on Friday, July 25th, and take the Saturdays throughout August, along
with a few Fridays and Sundays.

¢ Hank Savin is also on-staff as part-time interpreter and has enjoyed his time so far at the
House.

¢ We have had some visitors to view the House on non-tour days, which has given me the
opportunity to try different tour formats.

¢ I’ve worked with the Friends Board to offer items for sale in the foyer at the end of the tour.

Tour Script

¢ Much of my time this month has been dedicated to a lot of reading and research for rewriting
the tour script. I feel like I have a much better understanding of the Thomas Avery family, and
also how to approach our interpretation of the House so that each time period of the House is
addressed during an approx. 30-minute tour for visitors. Here is a basic draft:
¢ Front Hall (or outside front of House): overview of Avery family & Greek Revival-
style of architecture
¢ Parlor: use of Parlor; finding “The Christian Freeman” newspaper during restoration;
why House is important--daily farm life during technological changes/national events
« Master Bedroom: use of Master Bedroom, continued information about Avery family
(size of family, etc), display hooks in closet which leads to:
¢ Sewing Room: along with hooks Sewing Room will allow for discussion of increased
technological changes in CT and America during that time of Avery family residence
¢ Children’s Room: discuss how this room reflects two generations of Avery children
who lived here;
¢ Murals: brief overview while heading back down to first floor
+ Sitting Room: use of room (less formal than parlor); discuss William inheriting house
upon father’s death, begin introducing Smith family line into tour
¢ Office: discuss what little is known of Avery’s business ventures; how other
generations may have used the space; wrap-up Avery portion of tour; share brief
overview of Smith
¢ Dining Room: use of room; briefly mention changes in cooking (hearth-use out of
fashion by 1845); room reflects slightly later elegance; talk about Smith and introduce
Smith & Harris and the Munger Sisters
¢ Kitchen: use of kitchen--oldest portion of house; discuss the property in 20th-century
known as “Brookside Farm,” and dairy farming, acquisition by Town of Groton, Smith-
Harris Commission

¢ Foyer: wrap-up with photos of house and land before restoration, importance of
Commission in preserving and maintaining property, and share upcoming events and
programs, offer membership/volunteering brochures, encourage visitors to visit
outbuildings, linger on the porch awhile, and to return another time

° I’ve located a photograph of Reverend Abraham Selden Avery, Thomas Avery’s brother, and
would like to have it framed and display it in the parlor or office

¢ I’ve also found a photograph of Frank Harris, and would like to have that on display as well

¢ I think it would be ideal to have a tastefully arranged collage of a few selected ‘before’ photos
to hang in the foyer to demonstrate historic preservation and work put into restoring the House

¢ This is a draft, and please know that I am open to discussion/changes/suggestions, etc. Tours
are always a work in progress, and what is shared can (and should) be tailored to the visitor’s
interests and time.

« **I’m also generating a binder with more detailed information, such as entire deed transactions
for the House, information of the time periods the house covers, etc. so that any interpreters so
inclined can discover more information and have a better understanding of the families who
inhabited the house and the times they lived in.

« **T also plan to give copies of the tour script to the Friends Board, as at times they also
volunteer to staff rooms in the House, during special events and such, and should have the
most current information available to them as well.
