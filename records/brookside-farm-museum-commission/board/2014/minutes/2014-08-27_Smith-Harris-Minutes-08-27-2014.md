Curator’s Report--August
Submitted to the Smith-Harris Commission

We find ourselves, in late August, at the cusp of another season--a time of change. Change is
often hard to embrace, but, as I celebrate one full year as curator of the Smith-Harris House, I
find myself continually impressed with the willingness of everyone I work with to have dialogue
and make decisions that have often lead to change, and this month’s report features many of
those positive initiatives.

Tours/Staffing

¢ We’ve had a steady flow of visitors touring the house this summer; I will have the official
number in my September report, as we have a few “open” days left in the season.

¢ I developed a form and inventory for the Friends’ sale items, to more accurately keep track of
sales and donations.

¢ I met with Cindy, one of our interpreters, to update her fully on changes and current direction
of museum.

Tour Script

¢ The tour script is coming along nicely, if a bit more slowly than I had originally planned. I’ve
located many papers of previously-done research, and have been adding new information that
is scholarly accurate to the best of my knowledge, and plan to continue the research while I
finish. The tour script will always be a “work in progress,” as information will be added or
removed as we learn more about the families that lived in the house. I’ve completed the
introduction, the parlor, and almost all of the second floor. Let me know if you'd like to see a
current draft.

Collections
Many changes have occurred with our collections over the past month; in particular:

¢ The small room off the Sitting Room is now the Office.

¢ The small room off the Master Bedroom is now the Sewing Room.

¢ Several items have been moved to new locations, such as the wash basin, the bath tub, the
banjo clock, the “Christian Freeman” newspaper, and other artifacts, using their new home to
enhance the story of the Averys, Smiths, and Harris Families.

¢ Our textiles closet is now complete, and ready for painting, shelving, and historic artifacts to
store.

« A large amount of my time this past month has been dedicated to making these changes a
reality, from prepping the former library for the carpenter to removing items from furniture to
be moved, to adjusting room settings so that the House still looks nice for our visitors while we
make adjustments.

¢ A special thank you to Friends President Donna Massung, who, along with her granddaughter,
assisted me in preparing the library for the carpenter. Donna will also continue to work with
me as we organize the textiles closet, going through clothing and artifacts, selecting and
purchasing proper boxes for storage, and maintaining proper care.

¢ Another special thank you to Commission members Maggi Prokop, Gary Lakowsky, Dick
MacDonald, and our interpreter Cindy DeHaan for their willingness to come in at different
times throughout the process to assist the carpenter with access to the house, and answer
questions as needed.

¢ Also, a special thank you to Ron Bentz and his staff at the ELPR for assisting us with moving
some heavy pieces of furniture.

CTHumanities Council/Grant update

¢ I’ve had two meetings with Commission member Kathleen Sasso, and several phone
conversations with Lauren Miller, grant officer at CTHumanities. We’ve all agreed that our
best choice is to apply to the STEPs (STandards and Excellence Program for history
organizations) assessment program. It’s a 2-year process that covers foundational museum
standards and assists small to mid-sized museums to meet their goals. They believe we’re
perfectly set up to gain much from the program, so we plan to apply as soon as the application
is available. We can have Lauren review our application ahead of time, and make changes
before the deadline. This allows us to enter a program with our peers, and not compete with
larger museums for a Capacity Building Grant; it also enables us to apply for a small grant for
STEPS participants only to acquire funds to hire a consultant to lead us through the long-range
plan process. This is a very exciting development, and I look forward to working with
everyone on this. Please see attached handout for more information.
