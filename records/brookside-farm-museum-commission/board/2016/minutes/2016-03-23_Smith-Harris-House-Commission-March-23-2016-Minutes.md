Subject: SPECIAL USE 7

With the implementation of the Textile Closet the attic seems to be developing into a “Special Use” storage area.

Sections have been assigned for electronic equipment, games & crafts, costumes & reproduction clothing, linens, and
holidays.

For the past few months the items being offered to the museum by local people would be considered “Special Use”.

In order to capitalize on our special use collection we should consider “Traveling Trunks” and other methods of sharing
our resources.

During the 80s and 90s wrap around skirts (made by Gayle Booth) and reproduction clothing were made available to
students, There were also specific household items that were loaned.

In the 80s Sheila Reams’ Fourth Grade Class researched and wrote biographies of famous people. The students
surveyed the house for appropriate settings for them to read their papers to their classmates. Some dressed for the
part and used props from the museum collection.

In the 90s Rose Ann Hardy brought her American History students to the Barn to read their research papers or perform
before going on to State-wide and National competitions.

“Traveling Trunks” loaned to classrooms could spark interest with the students to come for a tour. The contents of the
trunks along with descriptions of the items would provide enhancement to history lessons and also teach the students

how to handle artifacts.

The schools offer a great chance for promoting the museum. Promotional material would need to be developed for
distribution to the schools. Inventory controls are necessary.
