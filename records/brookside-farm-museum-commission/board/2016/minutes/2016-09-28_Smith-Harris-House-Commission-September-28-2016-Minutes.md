III. REPORTS

A. Public Delegations. Ms. Massung reported the Friends are thinking about having name
tags made and wondering if the Commission would like to also have name tags made. The cost of
each is $10. She felt the cost may be reduced when more are purchased.

Ms. Massung informed the Commission the Friends have purchased A Wheel Within a
Wheel by Frances E. Willard which the speaker's performance at the Bike and BBQ on October 1 is
based on.

She stated the Tea was very successful. Twenty-five people attended and everyone enjoyed
it. Most attendees came with a quilt. Mr. MacDonald asked if it was better to have it in the house
rather than the barn. He was informed it was better in the house.
Sarah Griswold, STepCT mentor, stated she was in attendance to see how this Commission
works. She was also present to commend the Commission on its work on STepCT. She felt it is an
important part of what you have to do as an organization. She was also present to answer any
questions the Commission may have about STepCT.

Ms. Griswold felt adding the Avery name to the Smith-Harris House gives the Commission
an exciting opportunity and sets this Commission apart from other museums in the state. With the
Avery-Smith-Harris House you can discuss East Lyme and Connecticut history during different
periods. She wished to encourage the Commission to incorporate this change into its strategic
planning. She sees it as an opportunity to break barriers in historic houses. She feels this
Commission could win an award. She is in attendance as a resource. Ms. Odgers asked if Ms.
Griswold could give examples of breaking the barrier? Ms. Griswold replied you have three
different families and you can talk about social, economic, conflicts and issues that common people
were facing at different times. Mr. MacDonald stated right now the house focuses on the 1860s.
Ms. Griswold replied we are looking at the complete history of the house. Decorations are
representative of the people who lived there. This is a way of rethinking. She is promoting a
unique story that can bring in more people.

Mr. Lakowsky added it is the Town museum. Ms. Griswold felt it should show the factors
that changed the Town from agricultural to the bedroom community it is now. She stated you have
the benefit of telling the story in a very particular way.

Ms. Griswold encouraged everyone to go to the NEMA Conference on November 9, 10 and
11.

restrooms cannot be at that location? Mr. Cunningham replied it may be possible.

and submitted it to the Board of Selectmen and have not received any comment on it. Mr.

B. Museum Development.

1. STepCT Report. Sarah Griswold, Kathleen Sasso and Robert Seifel attended a
STepCT meeting. There are many items to work through to obtain the basic certificate level. We
need to develop policies and do research to maintain the historic structure and landscape. The work
is done based on historic research on what occurred. How was the land used in the past. Town
records, old photos, correspondence and newspapers can be researched. She suggested taking
advantage of professional expertise. To maintain an historic property, there is a higher standard of
care required. The last section is on interpretation.
