Summary
This report highlights successful fundraising efforts, ongoing collaborations with UConn for
landscape design over time, efforts to secure funding for an intern, preservation initiatives for
the Christian Freeman newspaper, securing grants for archival assessment and an interpretive
plan, and organizing a book discussion group as part of the Homegrown National Park lnitiative.
Action items include scheduling summer music events and addressing funding for a security
system.

- Successful. Recommendation to secure more events like this in the future

Christian Freeman Newspaper Preservation
- Sent off for preservation and initiated discussions on treatment options, including reframing
and digitization.
- Resource: Connecticut State Library offers federal-funded preservation services, primarily
through digitization.

Grants and Assessments
- Connecticut Humanities Assessment Grant: Received for assessing the paper archives. The
assessment, conducted by a consultant from the Connecticut State Library, will help decide on
deaccessioning irrelevant pieces. This consultant also has exeerience working with the East
Lyme Public Library and the East Lyme Historical Society so she is a great to advise on the
matter of transferring archives to the library. Have started preliminary discussions, and
assessment may be completed by end of April.
- lnterpretive Plan Grant: Writing a grant application for $10,000 to fund an interpretive plan by
by CT Humanities. Met with CT Humanities to determine if we might be successful and it was
determined we should apply. A requirement for the application is to have secured the
consultant we would work with. lt took a good deal of outreach before Daryn Rayman-Locke,
was recommended. Daryn is very experienced in this subject matter. Daryn would prepare an
interpretive plan for the museum, incorporating pre-planning features that would establish not
only a narrative direction, but also identify artifacts that are of particular importance in
highlighting underrepresented groups under the backdrop of the Avery, Smith, and Harris
family's and also Amos Watrous who lived in the house. Dr. Raymond Locke will do a great deal
of research and deliver the document along with resources for moving fonruard for the fee of
$10,000. Dr. Raymond Locke is an architectural historian, meeting the National Park Service
and Connecticut State Historic Preservation Office requirements for architectural history.

- Book discussion group on "Nature's Best Hope" by Doug Tallamy, starting June 12th. Doug

discussion group with the library. The library will publicize the discussion group and manage
