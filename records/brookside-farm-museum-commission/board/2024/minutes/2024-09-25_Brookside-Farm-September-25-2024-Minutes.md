Grants and Assessments

I submitted an application to SHPO for a historic structures report.


I continued to work with UConn on the USDA Environmental Justice Grant until it was decided to hold
off on submitting the application until it is better prepared next year. Now, a working group of
stakeholders, including UConn and 4-H, will meet monthly to creat: a better plan and communicate
carefully with stakeholders, including the Town.

I met with the Town, including Kevin Gervais, Joe Bragaw, Gary Goeschel, Cheri, and Bill, to continue to
pursue LoCIP funding for the capital projects the site needs. I am planning a meeting with SHPO to
determine the next steps. At this meeting, we discussed the possibility of using some funding that can
be reappropriated for an architectural assessment (vs. waiting for a grant from SHPO to pay for St0,000
we could spend the entire thing to get the document we need to pursue more funding to move forward
with capital improvements).

          Last year, LoCIP changed its policies, and our allotment of funding became a grant program
rather than an entitlement program. Our funding remained at $100,000. An additional 540,000 that we
had in past budgets was cut. 520,000 remains in the capital budget for "replacement of porch." We need
documentation from SHPO to approve all capital work that will be done in order for the project to be
eligible for the LoCIP grant.

Nothing to report.

Programs

On September 9, we hosted a successful event in partnership with ihe library, bringing Doug Tallamy to
us via Zoom for a terrific presentation and thoughtful discussion. A recording of the presentation is
available on our Facebook page.

l'm happy to be working with Cheri and Jan Larson on Cataloging.

On September L9 I met with someone from the program Maturity Works to see if Brookside would
qualify as a program site. Maturity Works is a state-sponsored ready-to-work program for adults 55+ to
retrain them for the workforce. Maturity Works pays a stipend for the person to work at an
organization up to 20 hours per week. ln the meeting, I learned that Brookside is a perfect fit to host a
worker, except the supervisor must always be on-site with the worker. I will apply and see about the
scheduling issue if we succeed.

On September 11, I met with Erin McBride from State Senator Chris Murphy's office for a tour. This was
a great opportunity to network with someone who can help us. Below is the text of her follow-up:
