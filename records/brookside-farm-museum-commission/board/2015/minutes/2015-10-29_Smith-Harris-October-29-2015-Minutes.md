V. NEW BUSINESS

Mr. Avery reported Ms. Prokop invited him to come to this meeting to discuss the possibility of
renaming the Smith-Harris House.

Mr. Avery understood the only issue with changing the name of the house may be with the IRS. He
is willing to look into it. He informed the Commission that he is related to the Averys who lived in the
Smith-Harris House. He asked that the name be changed to the Thomas Avery house. Mr. Lakowsky felt
many changes would have to be made with the name change. The last residents of the house were the Smith
and Harris families. It was known as that. The house is on the historical registry as the Thomas Avery
House.

Mr. Avery reported he attended an Avery Memorial Associations Meeting in July, and it was well
attended. There is a wealth of knowledge about the Avery family. Thomas Avery built the house, and he
felt it should be recognized for what it is. The house was built in 1845. Children were buried in the
cemetery. He agreed to provide the house with a set of the Avery clan's books.

Mr. MacDonald asked when did Thomas Avery live in the house? Mr. Lakowsky replied he owned
the house from 1845 and lived in it until his death in 1869. He felt the house should be shown through the
ages and eyes of the different families who lived in it. The Smith and Harris families were related to the
Averys. We are a Town Museum and we should show the history of the house from when it was built in
1845 until 1955. Ms. Odgers agreed. Mr. Lakowsky felt the name of the house is not as important as much
as the museum.

Mr. Avery suggested having the history of the house placed in a frame under glass and hung at the
entrance of the house. Ms. Odgers asked if he would consider naming the house the Avery and Smith Harris
House? Ms. Massung stated she was not against changing the name of the house. Mr. Avery reported when
they have the Avery Memorial Association Meeting, they have it at the Avery house in Groton.

OCTOBER 2015 COLLECTION COMMITTEE REPORT

The Time-Life Civil War books are still awaiting a decision from the High School Library.

As mentioned in the September report I have switched my attic focus to ensuring that all textiles
currently in the attic are conveniently arranged near the stairs for the Regional Angels Grant program
scheduled in March.

There are five tubs of textiles so far: Infant, Child, Young Girls Clothing - Women’s Clothing,
Special Use - Women’s Vintage Dark - Women’s Clothing Whites — Bedding & Table Cloths - plus a
small box of Sun Bonnets and a mob cap.

Above the grouped chairs in the attic there is a long clothes pole where I have hung some heavy
men’s clothing and long women’s items. The clothes rod seems to work well but the garments need to
be protected. I would like to find some inexpensive clear plastic bags about 30” Wide by 36” long to
cover each piece of clothing individually. In the second floor bathroom there are about a dozen items
hanging on the shower rod that could be brought up to the attic clothes rod also. Most of these articles
of clothing are Special Use and no doubt would be useful for programs at the house. We are building a
supply of hats, straw and felt.

One of the boxes of ephemera in the attic has a copy of the first Smith-Harris Cookbook. The
cookbook was created when the niece of Lula Smith and Florence Harris gave Betty Morrison some of
the ladies’ recipes. To fill up the booklet, recipes from Miss Leslie’s Cookbook (1857), Eliza Acton
(1845), and Louisa May Alcott were added to the booklet and members of the original commission
contributed some of their own family recipes. As we go through old records we will surely find the date
these booklets were put together (c. 1977-80) and sold for $1.00. Perhaps we could consider a revised
edition for our sales table. Both Joanie and I have collections of antique cook books where we could
cull some more vintage recipes.

Also found in a box of miscellaneous papers were 162 East Lyme Town Museum Smith-Harris House
Post Cards. The picture is the same as that on the First Day Stamp issue. These cards could be placed at
the door for sale. The Post Office sells plain post cards for 39¢ and the Dollar store sells generic New
England cards for 2/1.00.

A sturdy desk has been acquired for the attic. If we are able to get it into the attic the desk will be used
for filing photographs.

As we approach the Holidays the decorations scattered in the attic will also be brought forward away
from in front of the attic window for easy access.

The attic window is in need of repair so it is important to clear away the clutter. Joanie has suggested
there might be a grant that would cover restoration/maintenance.
