Programs/Education

June was/is an extremely busy month for educational programs; we offered 4 programs in the
first two weeks alone, and the Honey Tea scheduled for the end of the month.

¢ The school program (6/1 & 6/3)was a success, despite the rainy weather on Monday! We
had, including teachers and chaperones, just under 300 visitors on site (294 to be exact). A
big thank you to everyone involved!

* The “Novel History” program held it’s third session (6/10); 16 people attended the
discussion led by professor Kate Rushin. Ms. Rushin, poet and scholar, led a wonderful
talk about the poetry collection “Native Guard” by former national Poet Laureate Natasha
Trethewey, which then led to a sensitive, respectful discussion on current race issues today.

¢ Museum Open-House Day, (6/13) was also a success. About 76 people visited and toured

the museum. We had a passport and basket giveaway with the Thomas Lee House and the
Samuel Smith House. Maggi Prokop premiered her Mrs. Elizabeth Avery Henderson
character, and we plan to add to her character as we discover more history about Elizabeth.
Also, we had two high school volunteers assist with the children’s games on the lawn. The
only glitch was that we advertised “refreshments,” and none were provided. In the future
we’ ll need better communication so that we’re offering what is advertised. Otherwise, it
was a great day--many thanks to everyone involved, and special thanks to Maggi for her
first-person interpretation!
The Honey Tea is moving forward as planned, and should be a lovely Sunday afternoon.
I’m awaiting information from Steve Dinsmore, President of the CT Bee Association, and
our featured speaker. Mr. Dinsmore will also offer a honey tasting, and we’re just putting
the final touches on the program. Donna, Maggi, & I will have a final meeting this Friday,
(6/26).
Along with being open on national holidays, I’ve contacted Mike McDowell to plan to add
our folklore storytelling to the Trick or Trunk program again this year. Start thinking of
creepy (but not TOO scary) stories to tell. I’ll need 3 storytellers for this event.
We are in the planning stages with the East Lyme Historical Society and Samuel Smith
House for “Spirit Voices,” a dramatic historical (non-scary) cemetery tour, scheduled for
Friday, October 16th, and Saturday, October 17th. Cost will be $15 per person, $40 for
families. Times TBD. The starting point of the tour will be the Smith-Harris House, which
will feature 19th-century funeral traditions.
