Dear Marc Salerno,



Regarding the proposed Charter revision from Attorney Ed O'Connell, it is my impression that
the Charter is in place to provide guidance for future governing. I have the following concerns
regarding the proposed Charter revision.



   1. The numbers are extremely limiting and would likely result in a correction after every
       revaluation.

   2. The proposed calculation does not account for incre asing mill rates, land values or large
       commercial properties. For example
            A combined commercial property selling at S125,000,00 (we have had higher
            sales to date)
            $ 125,ooo,ooo sale Price
               7Oo/o



               S87,5oo,ooo Assessment

       A 2-million-dollar reduction, which would represent only L.6% reduction in value, would
       have to go the Board of Selectman for approval.


   3. Many of the commercial real estate appeals are a result of contingency fee-based
       companies soliciting work. Having a decision and vote on public record, would greatly
       impact the number of appeals and the negotiating process for the appeals.



   4. The negotiations on tax appeals are not taken lightly and involve the expertise of the
       Assessor, Appraiser, Town Attorney and often a judge. Addingthe approval of the
       Board of Selectman will make an already cumbersome procedure more cumbersome
       and take the fate of the negotiation away from the experts in the field'


   5. Pretrial hearings with the Town Attorney and Judge would have to be put on hold in the
       middle of a proceeding for the Board of Selectmen to meet and vote on a dollar figure
       before accepting or proposing a figure to settle.
    6. Most towns operate with a charter like ours and amongst my colleagues, I have not
        heard of it causing any problems. ln other words, if it isn't broken don't fix it.



Sincerely,



    e Vitagliano,
