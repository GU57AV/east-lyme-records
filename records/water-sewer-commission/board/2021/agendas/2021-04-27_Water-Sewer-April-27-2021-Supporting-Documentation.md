                                 MEMORANDUM

  TO:                   Town of East Lyme, Water and Sewer Commission

  FROM:                 Weston and Sampson

  DATE:                 March 16,2021

  SUBJECT:              condition Assessment strategy for the Niantic Pumping station Force Main


Weston & Sampson was retained by the East Lyme Water & Sewer Commission to develop a concept
level design and overall project cost to replace and/or to provide a redundant force main across the
Niantic River, in addition to providing assessment strategies for determining the force main's existing
condition.

The force main conveying wastewater from East Lyme to Waterford beneath the Niantic River is a critical
component of the Town's wastewater infrastructure that currently has no means for bypassing or
redundant capacity in the event of a catastrophic event. Although buried force mains are not typiCally
designed or constructed with redundancy, those that provide critical service across inaccessible terrain
are best provided with a means of bypass or redundant service. This pipeline's lack of redundancy and
access poses a significant risk to the Town of East Lyme, as damage to or failure of the piping will
undoubtedly result in major pollution of the Niantic River and Long lsland Sound, but also the certain
long-term loss of sanitary sewer treatment for the Towns of East Lyme and Old Lyme who rely on this
critical infrastructure to convey their wastewater to the New London wastewater treatment faciliiy,

Existing Force Main System:

The Niantic Pumping Station Force Main consists of approximately 11,200 linear feet of Class 53 1B-
inch diameter ductile iron pipe that was constructed in late 1980's. The force main conveys wastewater
from the Niantic Pumping Station, which has a firm capacity of approximately 2,900 GpM at 192 TDH.
Flow from the force main discharges to a gravity manhole located at the intersection of Gallop Lane and
Route 156 in Waterford, CT. The pumping station and force main were installed in three (3) separate
Contracts:

     1    Contract #1 consisted of approximately 10,800 linear feet of 1B-inch diameter DIP force main
          beginning near #113 Route 156 in Niantic and continuing easterly under the Niantic River to its
          discharge at a gravity sewer manhole at the intersection of Route 156 and Gallup Lane in
          Waterford, This portion of the force main includes a flow meter located at the intersection of 2"d
          Street and River Street in Waterford. The overland portions of the force main were constructed
          with both push-on and restrained joint pipe. "Ball and socket" type joints were utilized for the
          subaqueous portion of the force main across the Niantic River

Assessment Strategies for Existing Force Main Condition:

The following assessment strategies were explored, evaluated, and ranked for feasibility as the basis of
assessment to determine the force main's existing condition:

Assessment Ootions:

Test Pits and SoilTesting
Test pits provide access to visually inspect the pipe's exterior at discreet locations along the land
portions of the force main, Excavation of the pipeline at these locations will also allow for the collection
of soil samples to test for corrosivity and the need for renewal of the pipeline's galvanic protection
system, Samples would be collected and analyzed for pH, resistivity, sulfates, chlorides, and moisture
content. Test pits can also provide access to the pipe for other assessment strategies as described
below. Test pits are relatively inexpensive and can be performed by town staff or a local contractor, Test
pits, however, are not feasible for evaluating the subaqueous portion of the force main.

Underwater Divers
The subaqueous portion of the Niantic River force main has been installed 6,5 feet below the riverbed.
Underwater divers could be engaged to visually inspect the Niantic River bottom to verify condition of
the pipe's protective backfill and cover, but the scope of services could only consist of assessing the
overall condition of the existing sewer trench, if it could be located. Therefore, we do not believe that
retaining the services of divers to assess the condition of the force main would provide beneficial results,

Recommendations:

It is recommended that, due to the critical nature of the force main and the difficulty in undertaking
emergency repairs in the event of a catastrophic event, that a redundant force main be installed, tested,
and made operational as a precedent to attempting to ascertain the condition of the existing Dl pipeline.
The need for resilient and sustainable infrastructure for this critical conveyance pipeline outweighs and
supersedes the need for evaluation of the existing main's condition. The existing force main could be
evaluated after the redundant force main is made operational.

Hence, the decision regarding the recommended alternative will be based on the identification and
allocation of risk offered by each weighed against the overall cost to construct each.

           Option 1 provides a configuration that offers a 2nd force main to provide the desired redundancy,
           and it locates the new pipeline at a distant downriver location that might be affected differently
           during a catastrophic event, lessening the likelihood of damage to both pipe locations, thereby
           increasing the resiliency of the Town's wastewater conveyance system,

            Option 2 provides a configuration that offers a 2nd force main to provide the desired system
            redundancy, However, the positioning of the new pipeline adjacent and parallel to the existing
            force main may not offer a vastly different level of protection against scouring, exposure,
            damage, and potentialfailure of both pipes during a catastrophic event.

            Option 3 provides the most resilient means to provide redundancy to the system, as it would be
            the alignment most protected against a catastrophic event that could affect the existing
            causeway and the majority of the force main's susceptible pipe length,
