EAST LYME WATER & SEWER COMMISSION
INFORMATIONAL MEETING
Tuesday, JANUARY 26th, 2016
MINUTES

The East Lyme Water & Sewer Commission held an Informational Meeting on: Cardinal Road Water
Main Extension on Tuesday, January 26, 2016 at Town Hall, 108 Pennsyivania Avenue, Niantic, CT.
Chairman Nickerson called this informational meeting to order at 6:32 PM.

PRESENT: Mark Nickerson, Chairman, Dave Bond, Steve DiGiovanna, Dave
Murphy, Dave Jacques, Carol Russell, Roger Spencer

ALSO PRESENT: Joe Bragaw, Public Works Director FILED IN sah

ABSENT: Dave Zoller, Joe Mingo

Chairman Nickerson called the informational meeting to order at 6:32 PM and led the assembly in the
Pledge of Allegiance. He said that they would recap the series of events since the last informational
session first and then answer questions. He asked Mr. Kargl to recap the events.

Mr. Kargl outlined the events noting the following:

# Cardinal Road is comprised of 39 properties built during the mid 1980’s and 1990's located in the
north end of Town close to the Montville border.

* Following a house fire on Cardinal Road last July (2015) some property owners from Cardinal Road
attended the Water & Sewer meeting and expressed interest in having municipal water extended to
that neighborhood to better serve fire protection.

« At the July meeting the Commission authorized staff to conduct some preliminary engineering to
evaluate the feasibility of extending water into Cardinal Road. There is a 16-inch water main on
Chesterfield Road that allows East Lyme to receive water from New London during the summer
months to help with demand. There is also an 8-inch main that was extended to Cardinal Road for
potential future use.

» Atthe August 2015 meeting the findings of the preliminary engineering study were reported. The
project was estimated to cost approximately $809,813 and would result in an assessment cost of
$20,764 per property based on 39 benefitted properties. A survey was sent to better evaluate
interest in the neighborhood.

» {twas also noted that it was estimated to cost each homeowner another $3500 to $4500 to get the
water from the curb stops to the home.

# The results were reported at the October 2015 meeting with 26 residents being in favor although
some qualified their response, 7 were not in favor and 5 did not reply. It was at this meeting that it
was determined that a 2 Hummingbird Lane address did not make the mailing. Staff was authorized
to pursue the installation of a fire hydrant at the end of the 8-inch main on Cardinal Road. This
would eliminate the need to extend fire hose across busy Chesterfield road to the nearest hydrant
and alleviate traffic obstruction during a fire event. Staff was also instructed to do a conceptual
design to a 20% completion.

« The hydrant was installed and put into service during the month of December 2015.

» At the December meeting Flanders Fire Chief Levandoski explained the capabilities of the fire
department to fight a fire in the neighborhood especially with access to the new hydrant. Sufficient

response times could be achieved with this hydrant and no longer having to cross Chesterfield
Road.

* The 20% design was reviewed at the December meeting by the Commission. It included 3,100 feet
of 8-inch water main, 4 hydrants in addition to the one already installed, water services for each
property terminating at the property lines. (Copies of plan and info packet attached)

» As aresult of this design work the preliminary cost estimate was adjusted slightly from the original
estimate to $798,000 — approximately $12,000 lower.

# The design has been taken as far as we can go without expending additional money that would be
required for plans to go for bidding and construction.

»® Some of the residents reported the need to install water treatment systems due to water quality
concerns that they had. Inquiry to LLHD indicated that their records showed some treatment
systems installed primarily due to low pH. Copies of the most recent water quality report for East
Lyme were made available. It was noted that when taking water from New London during the
summer months that the chlorine levels while compliant with federal standards are higher than East
Lyme’s levels and some customers find the taste and odor associated with this to be undesirable.

= There was discussion regarding the process for this project. The Town Attorney clarified that
approval for an extension and levying of a benefit assessment lies with the Board of Selectman
following a recommendation from the Water and Sewer Commission.

= It was noted that all of the properties benefiting from the availability of the water main would be
subject to the assessment. The assessment amount would be calculated on a per lot basis and tied
to the construction cost. It would be paid under terms that have yet to be determined.

Attorney O’Connell said that the assessment system is recommended by the Water & Sewer

Commission to the Board of Selectmen who decides whether or not to adopt the recommendation.

Interest on this is the amount of money that the Town has to pay on its’ bonds.

=» Water customers are billed semi-annually in May and November based on meter readings. The
average residential water bill is approximately $180.00 for a six month period.

Mr. Kargl said that they would answer any questions at this time.

Mr. Murphy asked about 2 Hummingbird Lane and if they have them on record.
Mr. Kargl said yes.

Mr. Nickerson noted that Flanders Fire chief Levandoski is present to answer any questions.

Flanders Fire Chief Levandoski said that they have always has fire protection to Cardinal Road and
great water pressure. The issue for safety is the traffic on Chesterfield Road and having to cross the
road — which they no longer have to do. He said that they can reach the houses on and at the end of
Cardinal Road without issue. He added that he had listened to the tape of the 40 Cardinal Road fire and
from the time of the call to them putting water on the house it was 16 minutes. Further there were
pumper trucks putting water on the house before that. He noted that the Morris Lane house had a
hydrant in their front yard and it did not help to save the house.

Rob Liquori, 20 Cardinal Road asked if there were more hydrants if it would help.
Chief Levandoski said that maybe it could.

Govind Menon, 42 Cardinal Road asked about the 16 minutes as he said that he made the call and that
it felt like a lot longer to him.

Chief Levandoski said that 16 minutes was the actual time yet there was also a pumper truck with water
on the fire before that time. Neighboring fire departments sent their pumper trucks to the scene.

David Godbout, 15 Cardinal Road said that this does not appear to be a proper meeting and that it was
not properly noticed and does not involve everyone in the Town and feels that it should as it involves
bonding issues.

Attorney O’Connell said that it is his opinion that this meeting has been properly convened and that any
action that might be taken is on Item 4 of the regular Meeting Agenda. This complies with the FOI Act.

benefitting with their home insurance.

Mark Hill, 7 Cardinal Road asked for a way to keep up to date on this and what the issue is as it started

Mr. Nickerson said that he would be in favor of reaching out again to everyone as they will be footing
the bill 100%.

Christine Horspool, 46 Cardinal Road said that they live in a very good neighborhood and they do have
a representative on the street who sends out emails and Mr. Hill should put himself on that list so that

pressure for everyone to join.

Mr. Nickerson said that the $20,000 assessment would go to each house on the street.

Mike Fekete, 17 Cardinal Road asked if the fire hydrant line is different than the water line and if the
Town would contribute to the cost.

Mr. Nickerson said that while it might be nice to think that the Town would contribute there just isn’t the
money there to do it for everyone. There are many other streets in Town in this same position.

Jim Jones, 18 Cardinal Road asked about paying the assessment with interest.
Mr. Nickerson said that they had mentioned that it would be whatever the bonding rate is that the Town
was able to get.

Catherine Vincent, 2 Hummingbird Lane supported what Ms. Horspool had said about people
supporting the entire road.

Mr. Nickerson reiterated what Chief Levandoski had said — once they roll out the hose — they roll it very
quickly and they have good water pressure. He said that he knows that there was a lot going on with 40
Cardinal Road with it smoldering for quite awhile and there was no one living there. Had there been
someone there it would have been called in a lot sooner. He noted that they live in one of the best
neighborhoods in the Town and that this should not create issues amongst them. Chief Levandoski has
said that with the hydrant on Cardinal Road that they can run hose quickly and get to the entire road.

Chief Levandoski explained that he does two jobs — he is a Police Officer and the Flanders Fire Chief.
Moving the hydrant to Cardinal Road alleviated traffic issues and accidents and also the potential for
pressure being cut off if the hose was driven over. The issue was not the water — there were huge
amounts of water available with more pressure than they would need. As far as having water run up the
road and more hydrants, the real issue depends on the timing and the equipment and people. He said
that they believe that the fire on Cardinal Road had an hour and a half head start on it by the time it was
noticed and someone called the department. It is not where the hydrants are as they have the hose to
lay to get to the fire and it can be put down in minutes, 500 feet, 1000 feet, they have the hose.

Mark Hill, 7 Cardinal Road said that he thinks that the Town should pay for some of this.

Attorney O’Connell said that in the 1970’s that the Town received a substantial grant from a Federal
Agency to jump start the water system. It was not Town money that they had to go out to bond for as it
would be in this case.

Mr. Bond explained to Mr. Hill that Cardinal Road is not on any capital plan. They came here and
approached the Commission for this.

Eva Menon, 42 Cardinal Road said that it was their neighborhood that pushed for this — that is correct.

Mike Fekete, 17 Cardinal Road asked if this would be a project done right away or in five years.
Mr. Kargl said that if it were undertaken that it would probably be a few years before any completion.

it was asked if a new buyer would assume the balance of the assessment.
Attorney O’Connell said that with some assessments they do and with others there is a ‘due on sale’
clause. Those items, if it gets that far would be determined at a later time.

Mr. Nickerson said that if they have any other questions that they direct them to him. Call the office,
email him and he will be happy to discuss it with them.

Mr. Nickerson thanked everyone for coming and closed the information meeting at 7:42 PM.
