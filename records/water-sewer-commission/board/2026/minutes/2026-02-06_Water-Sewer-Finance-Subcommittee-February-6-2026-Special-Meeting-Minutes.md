2. Ben North opened by clariffing the need for a potential change to the existing rate structure because of
   inigation. He noted that not everyone that irrigates is on sewer. However, those residential inigation
   customers on sewer are dropping into higher tiers creating large/unexpected bills for the sewer portion
   of their bill. Without a submeter, they are charged sewer rates for all water consumed including
   irrigation. And, as a result Sewer gains revenue that essentially belongs to Water.

   The first attempt at an irrigation rate was offered this year. Customers were required to cover the cost
   of the meter, radio, and installation. In doing so, they saw no immediate cost savings and no incentive
   to enroll.

   As one potential solution, Ben North proposed creating a separate rate structure for residential and
   non-residential properties. The span between tiers would be changed andlor additional tiers added.
   Having a lower-level tier would help those using minimal water as well as those on a fixed income. To
   eliminate confusion on the bills, "meter maintenance fee" would be changed to "meter fee" and base
   amount (first 3,500 gallons) would be removed.

   Another solution would be to implement a Mandatory Inigation Program. A volume range would be
   set and customers that fell into that category would be required to install a deduct meter. The rates
   would be restructured based on the volume criteria. Regardless of solution, customers interested in
   inigation meters would utilize Sensus Ally meters so that the Water & Sewer Department could
   automatically shut offthe meters during times of draught, etc.

    Michelle Williams felt that using block tiers would be less complicated than adopting the mandatory
    program. Dave Bond suggested making Inigation Program voluntary and ofler submeters to those that
    were interested. Committee liked the block concept more and wanted to see what 0-10,000 and 0-
    20,000 tiers would look like. Committee was also interested in a cost estimate to cover deduct meters
    for customers initially.

    The concept of customers policing their own usage was briefly discussed. Ben North reported he
    recently tested a potential software system that would give customers current readings through the
    online portal.

    As for generating revenue, quarterly billing and implementing a sewer flat fee or minimum charge
    were mentioned and tabled for later discussion.

3. Next committee meeting set for Friday, February 13,2026, at 10:00 am.
4. Chairman Cunningham motioned meeting adjournment at 11:33 am.
