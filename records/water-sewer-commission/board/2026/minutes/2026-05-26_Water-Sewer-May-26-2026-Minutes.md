5-3. 6 East Rd

    Note see attachment.
           6_East_Road.pdf

    Note Mr. North summarized the following:
   *This is an irrigation‑only account.
   *Usage is a staggering 400,000 gallons (normal usage is 60,000 - 80,000).
   *Meter calibrated at 98% accuracy.
   *No leaks found.
   *1‑in‑10 policy does not apply to irrigation.
   *He cannot recommend a settlement and would not begin to know what to request if he could.

    Note The homeowner Mr. Longo highlighted the following:
   *The irrigation system was professionally installed and serviced.
   *No visible leaks; lines are shallow.
   *Low household water use (they're retired, have a small home, and no pool).
   *They believe there may have been a metering glitch.
   *A $4,000 bill is unaffordable.

    Note Commission Members discussed how underground irrigation leaks can dissipate into sandy soil,
   making them difficult or impossible to detect at the surface even when large volumes are lost. They noted
   that irrigation leaks can discharge significant amounts of water very quickly, and that the Commission has
   never negotiated or approved settlement adjustments for irrigation‑related usage. Members further
   emphasized that, regardless of where the water ultimately went, the water did pass through the meter,
   which is the basis for all billing determinations.

      Note The Commission reviewed the results of the Qualifications‑Based Selection (QBS) process
     conducted for engineering services related to the PFAS Removal Project. Mr. North explained that two
     firms advanced to the final round: Tighe & Bond and Haley & Ward. After interviews, scope reviews, and
     pricing evaluations, staff determined that Haley & Ward offered the strongest overall proposal, combining
     competitive pricing with a highly qualified project team familiar with similar treatment systems. Although
     Tighe & Bond resubmitted revised pricing, their proposal remained approximately 30% higher than Haley &
     Ward’s. Based on the QBS scoring and cost considerations, staff recommended moving forward with Haley
     & Ward for design services.

      Note Mr. North provided an overview of the PFAS treatment approach, noting that the system will rely on
     lead‑and‑lag granular activated carbon vessels to remove PFAS compounds from the water supply. The
     carbon media will require replacement every 6 to 12 months, depending on loading and breakthrough
     rates. Because the vessels are large and must be protected from weather and temperature fluctuations, the
     project includes construction of a new treatment building at each well site.
     He added that while the EPA may adjust regulatory timelines, the Town is proceeding proactively to ensure
     compliance and to avoid delays in permitting, design, and construction. The design phase will include Basis
     of Design development, preliminary engineering, and coordination with state regulators before bidding the
     construction work.
