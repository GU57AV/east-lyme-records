 Note He provided background on the purpose of the FOG (Fats, Oils, and Grease) program, noting that its
primary goal is to protect the Town's sewer collection system and regional wastewater treatment facilities by
reducing grease-related discharges. He emphasized that the program is intended to focus first on education
and compliance rather than punishment and noted that similar programs are common throughout the region.

 Note Mr. North reviewed examples of registration forms, inspection reports, compliance checklists, and
educational materials that would be used as part of the program. He explained that the program would help
identify food service establishments that generate significant grease and establish appropriate inspection and
maintenance schedules. He further noted that some establishments may ultimately require little or no ongoing
oversight based on the nature of their operations.

 Note Mr. Blanchard explained that DEEP's General Permit already requires food service establishments to
follow grease management requirements and conduct regular inspections. He noted that the proposed
regulations would allow the Town to enforce those existing State requirements locally rather than create new
requirements.

 Note Mr. Blanchard reviewed the proposed implementation process, including registration of establishments,
educational outreach, initial inspections, recordkeeping requirements, and cleaning schedules. He explained
that the first year would focus on helping establishments understand and meet the requirements, with
inspections used to establish a baseline of each facility's operations. He also noted that software would be
used to help track compliance, inspections, and required notices.

 Note Mr. Zoller inquired about the number of establishments that could be affected, Mr. Blanchard estimated
that approximately 86 food service establishments (give or take 5 or 6), may initially be included in the
program, although some could later be removed if it is determined that their operations do not create significant
grease-related concerns. He also discussed training opportunities and certification programs available to staff
who will administer the program.

Note Ms. Royce Williams commented that the program appeared similar to the Town's backflow inspection
program. The Commission discussed the anticipated workload, inspection frequency, and staff time associated
with the program. Staff indicated that inspections would generally be brief and manageable within existing
staffing levels.

Note Mr. Cunningham asked about the timeline for implementation. Mr. North stated that staff had originally
considered a January 1st start date but now anticipated beginning outreach and registration efforts within
approximately 90 days. He said staff's goal would be to have registrations generally completed within six
months while providing assistance to establishments as needed.

 Note Mr. North noted that some businesses may require help identifying existing grease control devices and
determining maintenance responsibilities between landlords and tenants.

Note Attorney Tim Bleasdale reviewed the legal authority supporting the proposed regulations. He explained
that Connecticut General Statutes authorize the Commission to regulate discharges that may adversely affect
the sewer system. He also noted that DEEP's General Permit for food service establishments places
responsibility for local implementation and enforcement on municipalities.

Note He reported that DEEP had reviewed the proposed regulations and expressed no concerns. The
proposed regulations are consistent with State requirements and that the Commission retains the authority to
adopt more stringent local requirements in the future if needed. He further explained that while DEEP would
pursue enforcement actions under State regulations rather than local regulations, it had no objections to the
Town's proposed enforcement framework, including the schedule of fines.
