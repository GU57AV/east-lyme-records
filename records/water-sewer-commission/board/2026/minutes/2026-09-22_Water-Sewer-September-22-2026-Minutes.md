4. Discussion and Possible Action on Request for Sewer Connection Exemption at Mohawk
Dr, (Map 8, Block 3, Lot 133)

  Note see attachment.
       Kahn_request.pdf

  Note see attachment.
       Adobe_Scan_Sep_30__2026.pdf

  Note The owner, Owen Kahn, explained that he wants to determine what is required to request an exemption
  from mandatory sewer connection for a future home he plans to build on a large, wooded parcel off Mohawk
  Drive in Black Point.

  Note Mr. Khan explained that the proposed home site is around 920 feet from the road, requiring a very long
 sewer run and crossing significant wetlands. He originally believed the property had neither sewer nor water
 access and only later learned that both utilities run along Mohawk Drive. He noted that the site plan currently
 shows a proposed well, so he also has questions regarding water‑connection requirements.

  Note Mr. North noted that the property has already paid its sewer assessment, and the applicant is not seeking
 reimbursement. The town’s ordinance (from 1998) requires connection to sewer when available unless a
 variance is granted.

  Note Mr. Blanchard added that State law requires water connection when a property line is within 200 feet of a
 public water main, unless the state grants a variance.

 Note Town Attorney Bleasdale explained that the Commission must determine whether strict enforcement
 would cause practical difficulty or unnecessary hardship, and whether that hardship is not self‑created.

  Note Mr. Bleasdale reviewed the legal standard for granting a sewer variance and emphasized the need for a
 complete evidentiary record, including engineering testimony.

  Note Commissioners discussed and briefly highlighted:
 *Mandatory sewer‑connection requirements under the 1998 ordinance.
 *State law requiring water connection within 200 feet of a public main
 *Wetlands constraints and whether they justify a variance.
 *The feasibility and cost of installing sewer, water, and power over such a long distance.
 *Whether septic could be supported on the property.
 *Precedent concerns.

  Note *The town’s limited sewer capacity and how exemptions relate to the broader sewer capacity concerns..
 *Past cases, including Pondcliff Condominiums and opt‑in sewer areas on Boston Post Road and Smith Street.
 *The relationship between benefit assessments, mandatory tie‑ins, and historical sewer installation funding.

  Note Because the applicant’s engineer was not present, the Commission agreed that more information was
 needed, including:
 *Engineering analysis of sewer feasibility.
 *Wetlands impact review.
 *Septic feasibility and pollution risk.
 *Clarification on water‑connection requirements and a possible state variance.
 *Updated data from monitoring wells previously installed on the property.

 Decision MOTION (2)
 Mr. Murphy moved to table this item until the Applicant's Engineer is available to make a presentation.
 Mr. Spencer seconded the motion.
 Motion carried, 8-0-0.

5. Discussion on implementation of Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) Reduction Program
Regulations and Set Public Hearing

  Note see attachment.
       Fats_Oils.pdf

  Note Mr. North and the Commission discussed adopting formal FOG regulations to reduce fats, oils, and
 grease from entering the sewer system.



  Page | 2                                                                       Minutes created with
   Note It was noted that several restaurants have caused sewer blockages and overflows due to inadequate
  grease management. The Health Department already performs some inspections, but the Commission agreed
  that a more proactive, enforceable program is overdue.

  Note The following was highlighted:
  *Chain restaurants typically follow best practices, but smaller establishments vary widely.
  *Grease accumulation has required costly wet‑well cleaning, including upcoming work in Niantic.
  *The town’s vacuum truck is increasingly needed to remove grease buildup.
  *Regulations would establish inspection schedules, maintenance requirements, and enforcement procedures.

  Decision The Commission agreed to move forward with a public hearing to adopt the FOG program formally.
