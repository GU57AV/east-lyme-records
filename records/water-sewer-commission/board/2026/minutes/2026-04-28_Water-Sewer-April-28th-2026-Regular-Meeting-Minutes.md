11. Discussion and Possible Action on Proposed FY26 Sewer Budget

  Note see attachment.
       Sewer_Budget.pdf

   Note Mr. North explained that the Sewer Budget reflects the realities of operating a small sewer utility with a
  limited customer base. He reminded the Commission that:
  *A significant portion of the sewer budget is driven by payments to New London and Waterford for treatment.
  *These treatment costs are outside the town’s control and represent roughly 42 cents of every dollar spent.
  *The Department must maintain capital reserves because it cannot request supplemental funding from the
  Town’s general budget.
  *The budget also includes costs for the new customer portal, insurance, pensions, and routine operations.

   Note Ms. Royce Williams noted that while the Commission has sympathy for customers facing higher bills, the
  budget must reflect actual operating costs, and the Commission cannot adjust the structure for individual
  properties.

  Note Ms. Russell asked whether the Commission should consider alternative billing structures in the future,
  such as splitting usage across tiers rather than placing the entire bill in a single tier.

   Note Mr. North replied that staff did look into it but doing it now, at the same time as rolling out the new billing
  system, would be too risky and could cause problems. He recommended waiting until the new system is fully
  stable before reconsidering the idea.

  Note Mr. Cunningham emphasized that the Commission must ensure the sewer system remains financially
  self‑sustaining, as required by law. He noted that the proposed budget accomplishes this while maintaining
  compliance and preparing for future capital needs.

  Decision MOTION (4)
  Ms. Royce Williams moved approve the FY 2026-2027 Sewer Budget as presented.
  Mr. Murphy seconded the motion.
  Motion carried, 8-0-0.

  Note MOTION (5)
  Mr. Murphy moved to approve the Sewer Rates as published, advertised, and presented at the Public Hearing.
  Mr. Zoller seconded the motion.

  Note Mr. Bragaw reiterated several key points for the record:
  *The sewer system has a much smaller customer base than the water system (approximately 3,700 accounts),
  which increases the impact of cost changes on individual ratepayers.
  *East Lyme does not have its own treatment plant; all wastewater is pumped through New London, and 42
  cents of every dollar collected goes directly to New London and Waterford for treatment.
  *These treatment costs are outside the Town’s control and are the primary driver of the rate increase.
  *The proposed sewer usage tiers now mirror the water tiers for clarity and consistency.

  Note It was noted by Commission Members that lower‑volume residential users would see smaller increases,
  while higher‑volume, more discretionary users like large non‑residential accounts or properties irrigating
  without submeters, would pay more because the tiered system is built to keep things fair. They discussed how
  they're legally required to keep the water and sewer system self‑sustaining, and the proposed rates simply
  cover the real costs of running the system, including rising treatment and infrastructure expenses.

  Note Ms. Russell asked whether the Commission could revisit the structure in the future if new information or
  engineering solutions emerge for master‑metered properties. Mr. North confirmed that approving the rates
  does not prevent future policy discussions, but the Commission cannot alter the structure for a single property
  without creating inequities.

  Decision MOTION (5) Vote:
  Motion carried, 8-0-0. (The proposed 2026 Sewer Rates have passed.)

  Note see attachment.

   Note Mr. Bragaw provided an update on the Town’s sewer capacity status and the ongoing operational pause
  on new sewer commitments while capacity numbers are being re‑evaluated.
  Mr. Bragaw explained that the Town continues to work with New London WPC to confirm updated flow data,
  infiltration estimates, and projected usage from approved developments. East Lyme remains under tight
  capacity constraints, and until New London provides final numbers, the Town must continue limiting new sewer
  allocations.
  Mr. Bragaw stated that he has prepared a draft capacity summary and asked Commission Members to review
  it carefully before the next meeting. Once Members have had time to look it over- and once updated figures
  are received from New London, the Commission will revisit the issue and determine whether any formal action
  is needed.
