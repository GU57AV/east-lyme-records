Mr. North explained that after the town completed a major water meter replacement project, the

demand is extremely high. It was originally believed that tourist activity and restaurants caused the increase,
but new data shows that lawn irrigation systems are actually the biggest cause of excessive summer water
use. Seasonal lawn watering creates major stress on the water system during the exact time when water
supply is most limited.

Mr. North said that several town well sites have stream flow restrictions- the town cannot pump unlimited
water and during dry periods, stream levels drop too low. When that happens, DEEP restrictions limit how
much water can be pumped; water is a limited resource and cannot simply be created.
=| He said the Commission identified lawn irrigation as one of the biggest issues affecting the system and
they wanted to reduce unnecessary water demand, encourage conservation, and avoid imposing mandatory
restrictions if possible. Instead of immediately requiring irrigation controls, the Commission decided to create
incentives to encourage reduced usage.

=| He added that the existing rate structure treated many users similarly even when their water usage was
drastically different. Some customers use very little water but still face the same basic charges, many residents
are on fixed incomes, and they wanted to protect lower-use customers from major increases.
The goal was to shield customers using less than 10,000 gallons during a six-month period from some of the
financial impact.

= Mr. North further explained that the town must install PFAS treatment systems to comply with federal
regulations by 2029. PFAS treatment projects are expensive, and these costs are part of the reason rates
must increase.

=) He explained that the proposed irrigation sub-metering program would address the current problem of
residents paying both water and sewer charges even when irrigation water never enters the sewer system.
Under the plan, homeowners could install a separate irrigation meter, paying the upfront installation cost while
the Water & Sewer Department would take over long-term maintenance. Irrigation water would then be billed
separately at a lower rate with no sewer fees applied. Mr. North emphasized that this is designed as an
optional incentive program rather than a mandatory requirement.

Mr. North reported that data from roughly 7,100 service connections shows a clear pattern of heavy
seasonal irrigation use concentrated among a small group of households. He found that 300-500 accounts,
mostly single-family homes, appear to operate irrigation systems, with some using 80,000 to 400,000 gallons
in a six-month period.

By comparison, a typical household uses only 20,000-30,000 gallons over the same timeframe. He
emphasized that a very small percentage of customers drives a disproportionate share of demand- just 5% of
residential users consume 27% of all summer water, totaling approximately 46 million gallons. East Lyme does
not have an unlimited water supply, and the town must carefully manage water resources because supply is
dependent on natural conditions such as rainfall and stream flow.

He explained the purpose of the new rate structure was to create:
*A more equitable billing system.
*Incentives for conservation.
*Better distribution of infrastructure costs.

Mr. North added that roughly 95% of residential customers use less than the Department's high-volume
threshold, while the remaining 5% of customers account for 27% of all summer water consumption, totaling
about 46 million gallons. These high-volume users are generally households using more than 40,000 gallons in
a six-month period-often substantially more. This concentrated demand places strain on infrastructure, drives
higher operating costs, and increases the risk during drought or low-stream-flow conditions.

The proposed rate changes are intended to:
*Protect lower-volume users.
“Encourage conservation.
*Reduce excessive irrigation demand.
*Help fund federally required PFAS treatment systems,
*Make billing more proportional to actual usage.

4. Discussion on Proposed Sewer Rates
!

:) Mr. Bragaw then began the presentation on the proposed sewer rates, noting that although he oversees
multiple areas including highways and buildings, the Water and Sewer Departments operate as separate
enterprise funds, fully independent from the Town’s general budget.

He explained that the sewer system faces different challenges than the water system, including a much smaller
customer base of approximately 3,700 sewer accounts compared to 7,000 water accounts. East Lyme does
not have its own treatment plant; instead, all wastewater is pumped 10 miles through New London, and 42
cents of every dollar collected goes directly to Waterford and New London for treatment costs. The system

also relies on 23 pump stations, making operations more complex and expensive for a relatively small number
of ratepayers.

*-| He reviewed the sewer budget, noting that the current budget is $3.1 million, and the proposed FY 2027
budget is $3.316 million, a 5.6% increase. He explained that although 5.6% sounds like a lot, 96% of the
increase is driven by four major cost categories, with 76% of the increase tied directly to rising treatment costs
charged by Waterford and New London. Because East Lyme’s share is based on proportional usage, even
small changes in flow between towns can shift costs unpredictably. He emphasized that these expenses are
outside the Town’s control.

Additional cost drivers include health insurance and pension increases, as well as outside services, such
as funding a new customer portal that will allow residents to check their usage online rather than waiting for a
six-month bill. Mr. Bragaw said this tool is important because sewer bills, unlike taxes, can be reduced through
conservation, but customers need real-time information to manage their usage.

He also reviewed the sewer system’s capital needs, explaining that much of the infrastructure dates to

the early 1990s and is aging. The Town must begin setting aside capital funds, something it was unable to do
until two years ago due to financial constraints.
Major upcoming needs include upgrades to the Dad’s Restaurant pump station, the Pattagansett pump station
and the Bridebrook pump station, as well as work on the 40-year-old force main under the Niantic River, which
requires a bypass system for emergency reliability. Because East Lyme shares downstream infrastructure with
Waterford and New London, the Town must also contribute to regional capital projects.

o]

Mr. Bragaw then explained how the proposed irrigation submetering changes will affect sewer revenue.
Historically, customers with irrigation systems were billed sewer charges on all water used, even water applied
to lawns. Under the new structure, irrigation water will no longer be billed for sewer, reducing sewer revenue
and requiring adjustments to sewer rates to compensate across the 3,700 customers.

- He emphasized that the Water and Sewer Departments are entirely ratepayer-funded, receive no tax
revenue, and must remain in full compliance with all public health and environmental regulations. Rising
chemical and infrastructure costs do not follow the consumer price index, and capital projects often increase by
10-15% annually.

Mr. Bragaw stated that the proposed sewer usage tiers will now mirror the water tiers for clarity.
Approximately 30-35% of customers who use under 10,000 gallons per six months will see increases around
3.5%, while most other customers will see increases around 5%. High-volume non-residential users and
residential customers who irrigate without submeters will see higher increases due to their discretionary usage.

He the public for attending, noting that Water and Sewer hearings typically draw few participants. He
emphasized the Department's commitment to transparency, ongoing public updates, and maintaining safe,
compliant, and reliable water and sewer services. He noted that most customers will not see the impact of the
new rates until November and reaffirmed the Department’s goal of setting fair and responsible rates while
managing the realities of operating a complex utility system.

Mr. North explained that the Sewer Budget reflects the realities of operating a small sewer utility with a
limited customer base. He reminded the Commission that:
*A significant portion of the sewer budget is driven by payments to New London and Waterford for treatment.
*These treatment costs are outside the town’s control and represent roughly 42 cents of every dollar spent.
*The Department must maintain capital reserves because it cannot request supplemental funding from the
Town’s general budget.

*The budget also includes costs for the new customer portal, insurance, pensions, and routine operations.

Ms. Royce Williams noted that while the Commission has sympathy for customers facing higher bills, the
budget must reflect actual operating costs, and the Commission cannot adjust the structure for individual
properties.

Ms. Russell asked whether the Commission should consider alternative billing structures in the future,
such as splitting usage across tiers rather than placing the entire bill in a single tier.

Mr. North replied that staff did look into it but doing it now, at the same time as rolling out the new billing
system, would be too risky and could cause problems. He recommended waiting until the new system is fully
stable before reconsidering the idea.

Mr. Cunningham emphasized that the Commission must ensure the sewer system remains financially
self-sustaining, as required by law. He noted that the proposed budget accomplishes this while maintaining
compliance and preparing for future capital needs.

Water Leak Billing Policy: 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment

1. General Billing Policy

All East Lyme water customers are responsible for paying for all water that passes through
their water meter, whether used intentionally or lost due to leaks, accidents, or neglect.
This is in accordance with the current water rate schedule. No billing adjustments will be
made, except as described in this policy.

2. Addressing High Water Bills
If you believe your water bill is unusually high, follow these steps:

Step 1: Contact the Department

Call the Water Department at 860-691-4104 or email us at watersewer@eltownhall.com. A
customer service specialist will begin by reviewing your account and comparing your
current usage to past usage. Due to our Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) smart

meters, we can see your usage and help you determine what is occurring.

Step 2: Identify the Cause
A. If you already know the cause (e.g., major leak, broken pipe), skip ahead to Section 3.

B. If you're unsure of the cause, you and the Department may take the following actions:

1. Toilet Leak Check: A leaking toilet flapper is a common cause of high usage. To
check, place food coloring in the toilet tank. If the color appears in the bowl without
flushing, the flapper needs repair or replacement.

2. Site Visit: If no leaks are found inside, a field technician may visit your property. The
Department will follow up once the inspection is complete.

3. Meter Issues: If the meter is found to be faulty, it will be repaired or replaced.

4. Leak Found: If a leak is discovered, the customer is responsible for timely repairs.
You may be eligible to apply for a 1-in-10 Leak Adjustment (see Section 3).

5. Nolssue Found: If no leaks or meter issues are identified, the full amount of the bill
is due.

3. Applying for a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment
Once a leak is confirmed and repaired, the property owner may apply for a 1-in-10 Leak
Adjustment—available once every ten years per property with the same owner.
Eligibility Criteria:
A. No previous leak adjustment granted in the last ten years.

B. Water usage must exceed the average of the two prior billing cycles By at least 50%,
resulting in a potential bill adjustment of:

e $150+ fora single-family residence
e $500+ for a multi-family residence

e« $1,000+ for non-residential properties
Adjustments will not be made for smaller overages.

C. Applicant must be the property owner and agree to the terms of this policy.
D. Applicant must provide written proof that the issue has been resolved:

° {frepaired by a professional: Include the plumber or contractor invoice.
« If self-repaired: Provide a signed letter explaining the repairs made.

E. Properties with irrigation systems are not eligible. Usage patterns will be reviewed to
verify this.
F. Adjustments will not be granted due to:

e Changes in property use
« Changes in occupancy
e Use of unapproved service lines

G. Commercial or Residential accounts with more than three units will only be granted
relief on the water portion of the bill. The sewer portion will remain unadjusted.

H. Ifa leak is detected via smart meter, the Department will notify the customer.
Customers must take action within 30 days of notification. If a leak survey is requested, the
30-day period restarts from the survey date.

|. Properties without smart meters or radio-read systems are not eligible for adjustment.
J. lf the water did not enter the sewer, the sewer portion of the bill may also be adjusted. If
it did enter the sewer, only the water portion is adjusted.

K. Applications must be submitted within 60 days of the date on the high bill via email,
certified mail, or in-person.
4. How to Apply
Applications can be found on the Town of East Lyme website under the Water Department

section, or picked up at the Department office (Lower Floor, Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania
Ave).
There are two types of applications:

e Residential (up to 3 units per meter)
e Residential over 3 units / Mixed Use / Non-Residential

5. Application Review Process
A. Residential (up to 3 units):

e Staff will approve or deny the application.
e Adecision will be made within 30-60 days.

e During this review period, no interest or penalties will accrue on the disputed
amount.

B. Residential (over 3 units), Mixed Use, Non-Residential:
e The application will be reviewed by the Water & Sewer Commission.
e The applicant must attend the Commission meeting to present their case.

e The Commission’s decision is final.

6. How Adjustments Are Calculated

A. Excess water is determined by subtracting the average usage of the prior two billing
cycles from the excessive bill. Half of the difference is considered “excessive use” and
used to calculate the adjustment.

B. If the excess water went into the sewer, no sewer adjustment is made.

C. If it did not enter the sewer and this can be proven, the sewer charge may be adjusted.
D. If a leak was detected and fixed during a partial billing period, a pro-rated adjustment
may be made.

E. lf a customer has owned the property for less than two years, the department may elect
to monitor usage for 30 to 60 days to gain an accurate representation of usage.

F. Once approved, the adjusted bill must be paid within 30 days or it will become
delinquent.
G. If the bill was already paid, the Department may issue a refund or apply a credit to the
account.

G. For non-residential/mixed-use/over 3-unit properties, staff will calculate a suggested
adjustment, but the final amount is at the Commission’s discretion. Accounts under this
criteria will be adjusted for water use only. The sewer portion of the bill shall not be
adjusted.

7. Appeals Process

A. Ineligible Applicants: If denied due to eligibility, customers may appeal to the Water &
Sewer Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. The applicant must attend the
hearing or the appeal will be denied. The Commission’s decision is final.

B. Disagreement with Staff Decision:

if a residential (up to 3 units) applicant is denied or disagrees with the adjustment, they
may appeal to the Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. Non-
residential/mixed-use applications already go to the Commission and therefore have no
further appeal.

8. Applicant Acknowledgement
By submitting a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment application, the applicant agrees to comply
with all terms and provisions in this policy.

Water Leak Billing Policy: 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment

1. General Billing Policy

All East Lyme water customers are responsible for paying for all water that passes through
their water meter, whether used intentionally or lost due to leaks, accidents, or neglect.
This is in accordance with the current water rate schedule. No billing adjustments will be
made, except as described in this policy.

2. Addressing High Water Bills
If you believe your water bill is unusually high, follow these steps:

Step 1: Contact the Department

Call the Water Department at 860-691-4104 or email us at watersewer@eltownhall.com. A
customer service specialist will begin by reviewing your account and comparing your
current usage to past usage. Due to our Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) smart
meters, we can see your usage and help you determine what is occurring.

Step 2: Identify the Cause
A. If you already know the cause (e.g., major leak, broken pipe), skip ahead to Section 3.

B. If you're unsure of the cause, you and the Department may take the following actions:

1. Toilet Leak Check: A leaking toilet flapper is a common cause of high usage. To
check, place food coloring in the toilet tank. If the color appears in the bowl without
flushing, the flapper needs repair or replacement.

2. Site Visit: If no leaks are found inside, a field technician may visit your property. The
Department will follow up once the inspection is complete.

3. Meter Issues: If the meter is found to be faulty, it will be repaired or replaced.

4. Leak Found: If a leak is discovered, the customer is responsible for timely repairs.
You may be eligible to apply for a 1-in-10 Leak Adjustment (see Section 3).

5. No Issue Found: If no leaks or meter issues are identified, the full amount of the bill
is due.

3. Applying for a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment
Once a leak is confirmed and repaired, the property owner may apply for a 1-in-10 Leak
Adjustment—available once every ten years per property with the same owner.
Eligibility Criteria:

A. No previous leak adjustment granted in the last ten years.

B. Water usage must exceed the average of the two prior billing cycles by at least 50%,
resulting in a potential bill adjustment of:

e« $150+ fora single-family residence
e $500+ for a multi-family residence

« $1,000+ for non-residential properties
Adjustments will not be made for smaller overages.

C. Applicant must be the property owner and agree to the terms of this policy.
D. Applicant must provide written proof that the issue has been resolved:

e Ifrepaired by a professional: Include the plumber or contractor invoice.
e If self-repaired: Provide a signed letter explaining the repairs made.

E. Properties with irrigation systems are not eligible. Usage patterns will be reviewed to
verify this.
F. Adjustments will not be granted due to:

e Changes in property use
e Changes in occupancy
e Use of unapproved service lines

G. Commercial or Residential accounts with more than three units will only be granted
relief on the water portion of the bill. The sewer portion will remain unadjusted.

H. If a leak is detected via smart meter, the Department will notify the customer.
Customers must take action withi30 days of notificatior If a leak survey is requested, the
30-day period restarts from the survey date.

1. Properties without smart meters or radio-read systems are not eligible for adjustment.
J. If the water did not enter the sewer, the sewer portion of the bill may also be adjusted. If
it did enter the sewer, only the water portion is adjusted.

K. Applications must be submitted within 60 days of the date on the high bill via email,
certified mail, or in-person.
4. How to Apply

Applications can be found on the Town of East Lyme website under the Water Department
section, or picked up at the Department office (Lower Floor, Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania
Ave).

There are two types of applications:

e Residential (up to 3 units per meter)

e Residential over 3 units / Mixed Use / Non-Residential

5. Application Review Process
A. Residential (up to 3 units):

e Staff will approve or deny the application.
e Adecision will be made within 30-60 days.

e During this review period, no interest or penalties will accrue on the disputed
amount.

B. Residential (over 3 units), Mixed Use, Non-Residential:
e The application will be reviewed by the Water & Sewer Commission.
e The applicant must attend the Commission meeting to present their case.

e The Commission’s decision is final.

6. How Adjustments Are Calculated

A. Excess water is determined by subtracting the average usage of the prior two billing
cycles from the excessive bill. Half of the difference is considered “excessive use” and
used to calculate the adjustment.

B. If the excess water went into the sewer, no sewer adjustment is made.

C. If it did not enter the sewer and this can be proven, the sewer charge may be adjusted.
D. If a leak was detected and fixed during a partial billing period, a pro-rated adjustment
may be made.

E. If a customer has owned the property for less than two years, the department may elect
to monitor usage for 30 to 60 days to gain an accurate representation of usage.

F. Once approved, the adjusted bill must be paid within 30 days or it will become
delinquent.
G. If the bill was already paid, the Department may issue a refund or apply a credit to the
account.

G. For non-residential/mixed-use/over 3-unit properties, staff will calculate a suggested
adjustment, but the final amount is at the Commission’s discretion. Accounts under this
criteria will be adjusted for water use only. The sewer portion of the bill shall not be
adjusted.

7. Appeals Process

A. Ineligible Applicants: If denied due to eligibility, customers may appeal to the Water &
Sewer Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. The applicant must attend the
hearing or the appeal will be denied. The Commission’s decision is final.

B. Disagreement with Staff Decision:

If a residential (up to 3 units) applicant is denied or disagrees with the adjustment, they
may appeal to the Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. Non-
residential/mixed-use applications already go to the Commission and therefore have no
further appeal.

8. Applicant Acknowledgement
By submitting a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment application, the applicant agrees to comply
with all terms and provisions in this policy.

Water Leak Billing Policy: 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment

1. General Billing Policy |

All East Lyme water customers are responsible for paying for all water that passes through
their water meter, whether used intentionally or lost due to leaks, accidents, or neglect.
This is in accordance with the current water rate schedule. No billing adjustments will be
made, except as described in this policy.

2. Addressing High Water Bills
If you believe your water bill is unusually high, follow these steps:

Step 1: Contact the Department

Call the Water Department at 860-691-4104 or email us at watersewer@eltownhall.com. A
customer service specialist will begin by reviewing your account and comparing your
current usage to past usage. Due to our Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) smart
meters, we can see your usage and help you determine what is occurring.

Step 2: Identify the Cause
A. If you already know the cause (e.g., major leak, broken pipe), skip ahead to Section 3.

B. If you're unsure of the cause, you and the Department may take the following actions:

1. Toilet Leak Check: A leaking toilet flapper is a common cause of high usage. To
check, place food coloring in the toilet tank. If the color appears in the bowl without
flushing, the flapper needs repair or replacement.

2. Site Visit: If no leaks are found inside, a field technician may visit your property. The
Department will follow up once the inspection is complete.

3. Meter Issues: If the meter is found to be faulty, it will be repaired or replaced.

4. Leak Found: If a leak is discovered, the customer is responsible for timely repairs.
You may be eligible to apply for a 1-in-10 Leak Adjustment (see Section 3).

5. Nolssue Found: If no leaks or meter issues are identified, the full amount of the bill
is due.

3. Applying for a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment
Once a leak is confirmed and repaired, the property owner may apply for a 1-in-10 Leak
Adjustment—available once every ten years per property with the same owner.
Eligibility Criteria:

A. No previous leak adjustment granted in the last ten years.

B. Water usage must exceed the average of the two prior billing cycles by at least 50%,
resulting in a potential bill adjustment of:

e $150+ for a single-family residence
e $500+ for a multi-family residence

e $1,000+ for non-residential properties
Adjustments will not be made for smaller overages.

C. Applicant must be the property owner and agree to the terms of this policy.
D. Applicant must provide written proof that the issue has been resolved:

° Ifrepaired by a professional: Include the plumber or contractor invoice.
» Ifself-repaired: Provide a signed letter explaining the repairs made.

E. Properties with irrigation systems are not eligible. Usage patterns will be reviewed to

verify this.
F. Adjustments will not be granted due to:

e Changes in property use
e Changes in occupancy
e Use of unapproved service lines

G. Commercial or Residential accounts with more than three units will only be granted
relief on the water portion of the bill. The sewer portion will remain unadjusted.

H. If a leak is detected via smart meter, the Department will notify the customer.
Customers must take action within 30 days of notification. If a leak survey is requested, the
30-day period restarts from the survey date.

|. Properties without smart meters or radio-read systems are not eligible for adjustment.
J. If the water did not enter the sewer, the sewer portion of the bill may also be adjusted. If
it did enter the sewer, only the water portion is adjusted.

K. Applications must be submitted within 60 days of the date on the high bill via email,
certified mail, or in-person.
4. How to Apply

Applications can be found on the Town of East Lyme website under the Water Department
section, or picked up at the Department office (Lower Floor, Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania
Ave).

There are two types of applications:

¢ Residential (up to 3 units per meter)
e Residential over 3 units / Mixed Use / Non-Residential

5. Application Review Process
A. Residential (up to 3 units):

e Staff will approve or deny the application.
e Adecision will be made within 30-60 days.

e During this review period, no interest or penalties will accrue on the disputed
amount.

B. Residential (over 3 units), Mixed Use, Non-Residential:
e The application will be reviewed by the Water & Sewer Commission.
e The applicant must attend the Commission meeting to present their case.

e The Commission’s decision is final.

6. How Adjustments Are Calculated

A. Excess water is determined by subtracting the average usage of the prior two billing
cycles from the excessive bill. Half of the difference is considered “excessive use” and
used to calculate the adjustment.

B. If the excess water went into the sewer, no sewer adjustment is made.

C. If it did not enter the sewer and this can be proven, the sewer charge may be adjusted.
D. If a leak was detected and fixed during a partial billing period, a pro-rated adjustment
may be made.

E. If a customer has owned the property for less than two years, the department may elect
to monitor usage for 30 to 60 days to gain an accurate representation of usage.

F. Once approved, the adjusted bill must be paid within 30 days or it will become
delinquent.
G. If the bill was already paid, the Department may issue a refund or apply a credit to the
account.

G. For non-residential/mixed-use/over 3-unit properties, staff will calculate a suggested
adjustment, but the final amount is at the Commission’s discretion. Accounts under this
criteria will be adjusted for water use only. The sewer portion of the bill shall not be
adjusted.

7. Appeals Process

A. Ineligible Applicants: If denied due to eligibility, customers may appeal to the Water &
Sewer Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. The applicant must attend the
hearing or the appeal will be denied. The Commission’s decision is final.

B. Disagreement with Staff Decision:

If a residential (up to 3 units) applicant is denied or disagrees with the adjustment, they
may appeal to the Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. Non-
residential/mixed-use applications already go to the Commission and therefore have no
further appeal.

8. Applicant Acknowledgement
By submitting a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment application, the applicant agrees to comply
with all terms and provisions in this policy.

Summary of Agenda Item:

Staff has finished its review of the Request For Qualifications and Request For Proposal
(RFQ/RFP) process to award engineering design services to Haley Ward, following the protocol
regulated by the Connecticut Drinking Water State Revolving Fund (DWSRF) Qualitative Bid
Selection (QBS) process. This is the first step towards constructing new PFAS treatment
facilities at Well 1a/6 and Well 4. The treatment process will consist of the current preliminary
treatment of iron and manganese and then routed to new treatment buildings that will house
carbon filters to remove PFAS via adsorption. Once PFAS is removed, return piping between the
buildings will convey the treated water back into the original building for final chemical addition
of chlorine, caustic for pH adjustment, and fluoride.

The Water and Sewer Commission may vote to move this bonding request forward to Board of
Selectman / Board of Finance and then finally a town meeting to secure the bonding required for
these two projects. After the design is completed, another bonding request will be made to cover
the construction and engineering oversight of the two projects, which is anticipated to be ready to
bid by this time next year.
