SEC. 1 PURPOSE

The purpose of the section of the Sewer Regulation is to aid in preventing the accumulation of fats, oils and
grease into the East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission’s sanitary sewer system thus reducing the
potential for sanitary sewer overflows. This Sewer Regulation outlines the wastewater pretreatment
requirements for food service establishments and other commercial facilities that have the potential to
discharge fats, oils and grease in their wastewater flow. All new and existing facilities that discharge fats,
oils, and grease in their wastewater are applicable to this program. This Section of the Sewer Regulation
shall be an integral part of the Commission’s Regulation in the application, administration and
enforcement thereto.

SEC. 2 DEFINITIONS

The following terms shall have the meanings indicated hereafter where used in this Regulation and are in
addition to those defined in Part 1, General Sewer Regulation, Section S1b, Definitions:

2a. “FOG - FATS, OILS AND GREASE”
Any fats, oils and grease generated from the food preparation process as identified per the most current
EPA method as listed in 40 CFR 136.3., as may be amended from time to time.

2b. “FOOD SERVICE ESTABLISHMENT (FSE)”

Food service establishment means a Class Ill or Class IV food service establishment as defined by Section
19-13-B42 of the State of Connecticut Public Health Code, Class 2, 3 or 4 food establishments as defined
by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Food Code adopted by Connecticut Public Act 17-93 or any
other facility discharging fats, oil and grease above the effluent limits in Section 5(c)(1) and (2) of the State
of Connecticut General Permit for the Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food Service
Establishments such as, but not limited to, restaurants, hotel kitchens, hospital kitchens, school kitchens,
bars, factory cafeterias, retail bakeries and clubs.

2c. “GENERAL PERMIT FOR THE DISCHARGE OF WASTEWATER ASSOCIATED WITH

FOOD SERVICE ESTABLISHMENTS”

The State of Connecticut’s Department of Energy and Environmental Protection General Permit for the
Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food Service Establishments issued October 5, 2016 and its
subsequent updates. The General Permit was formerly titled General Permit for the Discharge of
Wastewater Associated with Food Preparation Establishments.

2d. “ACTIVE GREASE RECOVERY UNIT (AGRU)”

Active Grease Recovery Unit means an interior grease interceptor that separates grease from wastewater
by active mechanical or electrical means as described in the General Permit for the Discharge of
Wastewater Associated with Food Service Establishments issued October 5, 2015 and its subsequent
updates.

2e. “SUPER CAPACITY GREASE INTERCEPTOR (SCGI)”
Super Capacity Grease Interceptor means an indoor passive unit, third party certified to retain more than
four times the amount of pounds (lbs) of grease than the flow rating in gallons per minute (GPM) and is

2
designed to separate fats, oils and grease from wastewater while allowing water to flow through and meets
specifications defined by Section 5(b)(3) of the General Permit.

2f. “GREASE TRAP/FOG INTERCEPTOR”

Grease Trap/FOG Interceptor means a minimum 1,000 gallon outdoor, inground passive unit designed to
separate fats, oils and grease from wastewater while allowing water to flow through and meets
specifications defined in Section 5(b)(1) of the General Permit and the sewer department Sewer Standard
Details S-38 Outside Grease Separator for Kitchen Waste Lines.

2g. “FOG MANAGEMENT EQUIPMENT”
FOG Management Equipment means properly designed, installed and operated equipment including
Active Grease Recovery Units (AGRU), Grease Trap/FOG Interceptors and Super Capacity Grease

Interceptors (SCGI), as approved by the Sewer Department, designed to meet the effluent limits defined
by Section 5(b){1) of the General Permit.

SEC.3 RULES AND REQUIREMENTS

3a. Food service establishments discharging wastewater to the East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission’s
collection system are subject to the following requirements which shall constitute the Commission’s Fats,
Oils and Grease Management Program (“FOG”):

Compliance with the 2015 General Permit for the Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food Service
Establishments.

Every three (3) years FSEs are required to complete FOG registration or Variance application forms
supplied by the Commission. All registrations and variances are non-transferable and must be submitted
to the Commission after any change in ownership or significant menu/facility change.

Inspections performed by the Commission to verify compliance with these requirements and those
requirements of the General Permit for the Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food Service
Establishments.

Payment of any fees associated with registration, variances, fines or violations, and reimbursement for
costs associated with any emergency services provided by the Commission.

3b. Compliance Dates

Food service establishments and facilities that are applicable to this General Permit shall comply based
on the following schedule:

All new facilities shall comply with the requirements of the General Permit before initiating such
discharge. An existing facility, which begin operation prior to the effective date of the General Permit
(September 30, 2005), shall comply with the requirements of the General Permit no later than October 1,
2026.

SEC.4 EQUIPMENT REQUIREMENTS

4a. Wastewater generated from food service establishments shall be treated at the point of discharge to
remove FOG using FOG Management Equipment to meet the requirements of the Connecticut Department
of Energy and Environmental Protection General Permit for the Discharge of Wastewater Associated with
Food Service Establishments, Issued October 5, 2015.
The FOG Management Equipment shall be connected to those fixtures or drains that would allow FOG to
be discharged. This shall include but is not limited to; pot sinks, pre-rinse sinks, any sinks into which fats,
oils, or grease are likely to be introduced, soup kettles or similar devices, wok stations, floor drains or sinks
into which kettles may be drained, automatic hood wash units, and any other fixtures or drains that are
likely to allow fats, oils and grease to be discharged.

4b. Grease Trap Options
All Class III and Class !V and FDA class Il, Ill, and IV Food Service Establishments (FSEs) are required to
install either an Active Grease Recovery Unit (AGRU), Super Capacity Grease Interceptor (SCGI), ora
minimum 1,000 gallon outdoor in-ground passive grease interceptor. Specific requirements for these
devices are outlined in the General Permit.
The following fixtures must be connected to FOG Management Equipment:

e pot sinks (3 bay sinks)

e  pre-rinse sinks;

e any sink into which fats, oil, and grease are likely to be introduced;

e soup kettles or similar devices; wok stations;

e floor drains or sinks into which kettles may be drained;

e automatic hood wash units; and

e any other fixtures or drains that are likely to allow fats, oils and grease to be discharged

4c. Active Grease Recovery Unit (AGRU)

Active Grease Recovery Units (AGRUs) are relatively small pretreatment units that can be located inside a
kitchen to remove grease at the source of generation. These units allow fats, oils, and grease (FOG) to
accumulate within the unit while skimmers or pumps remove the FOG from the unit and deposit the
material in a separate container for disposal by a grease removal contractor.

All AGRUs shall be maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. This includes a
minimum quarterly (four times per year) clean out of the entire unit. The contents of the trap must be
placed into a container for grease trap waste (brown grease). A written log and receipts of all cleaning and
maintenance of the trap must be maintained on site for review by the MDC.

4d. Super Capacity Grease Interceptor (SCGI)

Approved Super Capacity Grease Interceptors (SCGIs) are defined as indoor passive units that are
certified to retain more than four times the weight (lbs) of grease than the flow rating in gallons per minute
(GPM) and meet specifications defined by Section 5(b)(3) of the General Permit.

All SCGIs shall be maintained in accordance with the manufacturer’s recommendations. This includes a
minimum quarterly (four times per year) clean out of the entire unit. The contents of the trap must be
placed into a container for grease trap waste (brown grease). A written log and receipts of all cleaning and
maintenance of the trap must be maintained on site for review by the Commission.

4e. Outdoor In-Ground Grease Trap/Interceptor

An outdoor in-ground grease trap must be emptied at least every three months or whenever 25% of the
Operating depth of the grease trap/interceptor is occupied by grease and settled solids, which ever is
more frequent. Following a minimum one year of operation, the facility may submit a request to the
Commission to extend the cleaning beyond the three month period if the facility demonstrates that the
level of grease and solids was less than 25% during each of the three month intervals. The facility shall
maintain a written log and receipts of all cleaning and maintenance of the trap.

The outdoor grease trap/interceptor specifications are described in Section 5(b)(1) of the General Permit.
Installation of a new outdoor grease trap/interceptor shall meet the specifications as described in the
General Permit and the Commission specifications.

4f. Best Management Practices

Renderable fats, oils, and grease shall be disposed of in separate storage containers for recycling by a
renderer. No renderable fats, oils, and grease shall be discharged into grease trap/interceptors or AGRUs,
sanitary sewers, dumpsters or storm sewers.“Renderable” means uncontaminated fats, oils and grease
from the food preparation process that can be used as a source of material that is free of impurities and
can be recycled into products such as animal feed and cosmetics.

The owner/operator shall perform at least quarterly inspections of all indoor and outdoor grease
trap/interceptors.

For disposal in Connecticut, the contents of all grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs and other approved
units shall be disposed of at a regional collection/transfer/disposal site. For disposal outside of
Connecticut, the contents of all grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs and other approved units shall be
disposed of in an environmentally accepted manner.

The permittee may use hot water, steam, chemicals, or biological additives in the normal course of
facility maintenance, but may not intentionally use hot water, steam, physical means, chemicals, or
biological additives that will cause the release of fats, oils, and grease from the grease trap/interceptor.
(Please see the link below for additional information.)

No food grinder (garbage disposal) or food pulper shall discharge to any grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs
or other approved units.

All wastewater flows connected to the grease trap/interceptors shall be screened to prevent solids from
entering the treatment units. Screened solids shall be disposed of in accordance with applicable solid
waste regulations.

SEC.5 VARIANCES

5a. If a Food Service Establishment has limited potential for FOG in the discharge; an establishment may
request a variance for required equipment by submitting a Application for Variance on a form provided by
the Commission. This variance is a waiver of the treatment requirements of Sections 5(b)(1) through 5(b)3,
inclusive, of the Department of Energy and Environmental Protection’s (DEEP) General Permit for the
Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food Preparation Establishments. If a variance of equipment is
approved, the Food Service Establishment owner shall pay a variance registration fee. The variance
registration is valid for a period of three years. If there is a change of ownership, then the establishment’s
new owner must submit a new Application for Variance and pay the associated fee. Facilities that receive
a variance on the FOG Management Equipment requirement are subject to the FOG Annual Fee. The Water
and Sewer Commission reserves the right to revoke any variance issued and require grease control devices
to be installed.
5b. The applicant must demonstrate there is a limited potential for fats, oils, and grease in the discharge,
as well as minimal flow. If the Commission encounters any grease problems in the sewer infrastructure
downstream of the establishment, the Commission has the right to revoke any variances issued.

5c. Please note the applicant must reapply for a variance approval 30 days prior to the following:
e Expiration date of the three-year approval period or;

e Any significant menu or operational changes that would increase the potential for fats, oils, and
grease in the discharge or;
e Change of ownership.
5d. To be considered for a variance, the facility shall complete the Variance Application and attach a
complete menu for the establishment. The application shall include a description of the proposed
methods for minimizing grease discharged in the wastewater, including employee training, cleaning
procedures and interim grease disposal methods.

SEC.6 INSPECTIONS

6a. Food Service Establishments shall be subject to inspection by the Commission on a regular basis to
determine whether the requirements set forth in this Regulation and the General Permit are being met.
Inspections may include, but are not limited to, inspection of the FOG Management Equipment and food
preparation activities, review of records for the quarterly inspections performed by the Food Service
Establishment, grease storage, and review of cleaning and maintenance logs.

The owner or operator shall provide Commission personnel with reasonable access to the premises, all
FOG Management Equipment, and all records required by this Regulation for the purpose of conducting
inspections.

Inspections may also include verification that:

e Required FOG Management Equipment has been properly installed and is being maintained in good
working order.

e Grease traps, interceptors, AGRUs, and SCGIs are being cleaned at the required frequency or prior
to exceeding twenty-five percent (25%) of the operating depth occupied by fats, oils, grease, and
settled solids.

e Grease trap service tags or other Commission-approved maintenance identification are present
and accurately reflect the most recent cleaning and maintenance.

e Cleaning manifests, maintenance logs, disposal receipts, registration forms, variance approvals,
and any other records required by this Regulation are maintained and available for review.

e Best Management Practices are being followed to minimize the discharge of fats, oils, and grease
to the sanitary Sewer system.

6b. Failure to provide access for an inspection or failure to produce records required by this Regulation
shall constitute a violation and may result in the issuance of a Notice of Violation and assessment of
applicable fees and penalties.

SEC.7 DISCHARGE LIMITS

No Food Service Establishment shall discharge or cause any wastewater to be discharged with a FOG
concentration more than one hundred (100) milligrams per liter, as determined by the currently approved
test for recoverable fats and grease listed in 40 CFR 136.3, as may be amended.
SEC.8 VIOLATIONS

8a. In the event that a Food Service Establishment fails to meet the recordkeeping, installation,
maintenance and/or operational requirements or discharge limit, the Commission will issue a written
notice of violation for the non-compliant condition(s). The Food Service Establishment shall take
immediate steps to bring the establishment into compliance.

8b. Any violation of the Commission’s FOG Management Program will be subject to violation fees. Each
day that a violation continues shall be considered a separate occurrence.

SEC.9 RESTRICTIONS

No Food Service Establishment shall introduce any additives, including but not limited to, enzymes or
surfactants as grease emulsifiers or degradation agents, into the FOG Management Equipment unless
given prior approval by the Commission.

SEC.10 SEWER STOPPAGES AND MAINTENANCE

10a. In the event of a sewer stoppage or blockage, caused by an FOG discharge from a Food Service
Establishment, the Commission shall notify said establishment in writing, and said establishment shall
reimburse the Commission any expenses incurred by the Commission due to the sewer stoppage or
blockage. The expenses may include, but are not limited to, the cost of services of Commission employees
or contractors for any work or inspection necessary to relieve such stoppage or blockage, and damage to
any other property effected.

10b. If any FOG related stoppage requires emergency action on the part of the Commission, the Food
Service Establishment shall be charged a minimum penalty fee per event in addition to the cost of services
of Commission employees or contractors. An emergency is defined as any event which requires pipeline
cleaning, snaking, jetting or any other work required to clear the stoppage.

In the event the collection system needs to be cleaned due to the excess FOG discharged by a Food Service
Establishment, the establishment shall pay to the Commission the expense incurred by the Commission
to clean the sewer or related appurtenances.

SEC.11 FEES

The Water and Sewer Commission may establish and revise fees for the Commission’s FOG Management
Program required by this Regulation. The fees include, but are not limited to: annual registration, variance,
violation, and maintenance fees. Please see East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission — Fats, Oils, and
Great Program Fines and Fees Schedule, updated from time to time.

SEC.12 BILLING OF FOG CHARGES

The fees associated with the Commission’s FOG Management Program will be billed to the fee owner of
the property upon which the Food Service Establishment situated. The fees shall constitute a lein on the
property upon non-payment.

SEC.13 PAYING OF FOG CHARGES

The fees associated with the Commission’s FOG Management Program, either as a separate bill or
combined with the water and/or sewer bill, shall be due and payable within 30 days of the date of issue,
and the Commission is empowered to permit an extension of time of the due date up to seven days after

7
the end of the billing period. One percent (1.5%) interest will be applied monthly to the unpaid balance,
including previously applied interest, of all bills outstanding beyond the 30 days.

SEC.14 NON-COMPLIANCE

The Commission reserves the right to notify the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental
Protection of any establishment that is non-compliant with the Commission’s FOG Management Program.

SEC.15 NON-PAYMENT & SHUT OFF

15a. In the event any FOG charges remain unpaid 30 days after the date on which payment is due, such
unpaid FOG charges shall be delinquent and constitute a lien pursuant to Section 12 and such lien shall
be enforceable in accordance with the terms of this Regulation and prevailing law. The establishment
owner shall be responsible for all lien filing fees, recording fees, and lien release fees incurred by the
Commission in connection with the collection of delinquent FOG charges.

15b. The Commission reserves the right to shut off the water service to the premises where FOG charges
are combined with the water use charges and remain unpaid 30 days after the date on which payment is
due. If so shut off, the water service will not be restored without payment of all charges due.

Establishments (FSEs). This fee is associated with the review and approval of facility
