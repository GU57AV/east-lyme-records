Mr. North provided a detailed overview of the leak adjustment request:

*The property qualifies for Commission review because it is mixed-use.

*Staff completed the preliminary review- verified calculations, confirmed the leak was repaired,

reviewed the repair invoice ($1,400), reviewed appraisal data confirming mixed-use classification,

provided photos showing the leak location (underground between the commercial sign and residential units).
*Normal usage for the property is 24,000 gallons per May—November cycle.

*During the leak period, usage spiked to 71,000 gallons.

*The leak was detected after grass appeared unusually green; the radio transmitter had communication
issues, delaying detection.

*Notification to the owner occurred on 11/6, and the leak was repaired in mid-December.

NSE) Mr. North reminded everyone that the Commission's 1-in-10 policy requires:

*At least $1,000 in excess charges.

*Consideration of whether leaked water entered the sewer system.

*For mixed-use or >3-unit properties, precedent has been not to adjust sewer charges if the leaked water did
not enter the sewer system.

*Despite this, staff ran the calculation as if sewer adjustment were allowed, to show the maximum possible
benefit.

(NSS) Mr. Roberts asked for clarification on the difference between water and sewer savings.

=) Mr. North explained the calculation method:
*The 1-in-10 adjustment compares normal usage (24,000 gallons) and leak-period usage (71,000 gallons).
*The user pays the midpoint rather than the full high usage.
*Sewer charges are only adjustable if leaked water entered the sewer system.
*In this case, it did not, so sewer adjustment is not typically granted.
*The Commission must still pay New London for sewer flow, which limits flexibility.
*As a result, the 1-in-10 adjustment would reduce the customer's bill from $1,410 to approximately $740,
yielding a savings of about $740.

55) Mr. Bond asked whether the facilities are now fully on the meter and Mr. North confirmed they are. The
meter had a period during the summer when it stopped communicating. This is not uncommon- meters can be
hit, moved during construction, or disconnected.

Because the radio transmitter was not functioning, staff could not detect the leak as early as modern metering
typically allows. As a result, staff had to rely on the traditional 1-in-10 policy, which the property qualifies for
because the bill was unusually large and the customer had never received a 1-in-10 adjustment before.
