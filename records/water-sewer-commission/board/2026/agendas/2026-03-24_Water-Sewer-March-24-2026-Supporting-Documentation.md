Ms. Royce Williams excused herself from the Meeting since she is an employee of CT Water.

Danelle Dillon, a Representative from CT Water detailed some of the following:

*They'd like to establish a municipal partnership with the town for their Linebacker" Service Protection
Program.

*The program provides insurance-style coverage for service line repairs that typical homeowners' insurance
often excludes.

*There are three tiers of plans covering water lines (from curb to shut-off), wastewater/sewer lines, and
internal plumbing.

*Coverage is up to $12,000 for water services and $6,000 for wastewater.
*They employ 17 customer service reps and 24 licensed, vetted contractors.

*A $75 call-out fee applies to service requests.

*The program has operated for 26 years with only 6 price increases in that timeframe.

*They're seeking a municipal partnership where the town endorses the program.

*Marketing materials would be co-branded- (East Lyme Water & Sewer and CT Water.

*The town would provide customer contact information to be uploaded via a secure SharePoint site for digital
campaigns and mailers.

*The town would receive a $10 payment for each customer who enrolls in the program.

Mr. Roberts expressed concern regarding the necessity of sharing customer data and Mr. North
explained that it's all public information, which is all FOl-able.

Ms. Dillon also highlighted the following:

*CT Water handles all billing and risk; the town’s primary role would be endorsement and data sharing.
*They to finalize their 2026 pricing within the next week.

*While customers can cancel at any time, CT Water typically looks for a five-year commitment from the
municipality, which allows them to recoup the initial marketing costs.

*A formal license agreement is required to handle customer privacy and data security.

*There is zero cost to the Water and Sewer Commission. CT Water assumes all risk, repair expenses,
and administrative responsibility.

*The town would still receive its turn-off/turn-on fees when a service is interrupted for repair, but CT Water
waives these fees for the customers enrolled in the program.

*Coverage limits were last updated in 2015 (from $10,000 to $12,000).

*Most repairs stay well within these limits; only rare cases involving long service lines (e.g., 500+ feet) exceed
them.

Ms. Russell raised concerns about the town endorsing a single vendor without seeing other options. She
emphasized that residents would expect the Commission to have evaluated the market before giving an official
stamp of approval.

Mr. North noted that while research began 18 months ago, they are open to reviewing other competitors
if the Commission desires.

Mr. Bragaw pointed out that Linebacker is unique because it is run by a utility company rather than a
traditional insurance company (like HomeServe/Aquarion), which enables them to often provide better
customer service and technical knowledge.

Ms. Dillon noted that CT Water boasts a 93.5% satisfaction rate and handles all dispatching and
contractor management. Mr. Bragaw and Mr. North observed that by providing a quick repair solution, the
program helps reduce unaccounted-for water or leaks that residents might otherwise delay fixing due to lack of

Ms. Dillon clarified that this is not insurance, it is a service protection program. There are no claims or
reimbursements. If a customer has a leak, they call CT Water directly, and the company dispatches a vetted
contractor to fix it. If a resident hires their own plumber first, they cannot be paid back by the program.

Mr. Bond said he's a plumber and that a typical sewer snaking can cost $650 just for the visit, while a full
line repair can easily reach $6,000+. Ms. Dillon explained that Linebacker’s negotiated rates keep their
average repair cost around $3,500.

Mr. Bond detailed how he's been suggesting something like this for years and would have loved to see it
done in house.

Mr. Bragaw and Mr. North explained that the town is not a marketing machine nor a risk organization. Trying to
run the program internally would likely fail due to a lack of enrollment and the high financial risk of expensive
repairs early in the program's life. Partnering with CT Water removes the risk from the town while providing a
reliable service they can endorse to residents.

If a homeowner can't afford a repair, the leak continues, wasting the town's water and money.
Linebacker ensures fast repairs, which helps the town remain financially solvent by stopping leaks quickly.

Ms. Dillon offered these final comments:

*CT Water is transparent in what they do and do not cover.

*They're a utility with a local presence and a reputation to maintain.

*Customers prepay annually.

*They use local contractors (like Schumacher and B&L) that the town is already familiar with.

*If approved, mailers and e-blasts co-branded with the town, would likely begin in late Q1 or early Q2.

*Coverage officially begins 30 days after payment is received.

*CT Water handles all billing directly; the town has no administrative involvement.

*The basic water plan is likely increasing from $100 to $109 per year while the water & wastewater plan is

Water Leak Billing Policy: 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment

1. General Billing Policy

All East Lyme water customers are responsible for paying for all water that passes through
their water meter, whether used intentionally or lost due to leaks, accidents, or neglect.
This is in accordance with the current water rate schedule. No billing adjustments will be
made, except as described in this policy.

2. Addressing High Water Bills
If you believe your water bill is unusually high, follow these steps:

Step 1: Contact the Department

Call the Water Department at 860-691-4104 or email us at watersewer@eltownhall.com. A
customer service specialist will begin by reviewing your account and comparing your
current usage to past usage. Due to our Advanced Metering Infrastructure (AMI) smart
meters, we can see your usage and help you determine what is occurring.

Step 2: Identify the Cause
A. If you already know the cause (e.g., major leak, broken pipe), skip ahead to Section 3.

B. If you're unsure of the cause, you and the Department may take the following actions:

1. Toilet Leak Check: A leaking toilet flapper is a common cause of high usage. To
check, place food coloring in the toilet tank. If the color appears in the bowl without
flushing, the flapper needs repair or replacement.

2. Site Visit: lf no leaks are found inside, a field technician may visit your property. The
Department will follow up once the inspection is complete.

3. Meter Issues: If the meter is found to be faulty, it will be repaired or replaced.

4. Leak Found: If a leak is discovered, the customer is responsible for timely repairs.
You may be eligible to apply for a 1-in-10 Leak Adjustment (see Section 3).

5. No Issue Found: If no leaks or meter issues are identified, the full amount of the bill
is due.

3. Applying for a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment
Once a leak is confirmed and repaired, the property owner may apply for a 1-in-10 Leak
Adjustment—available once every ten years per property with the same owner.
Eligibility Criteria:

A. No previous leak adjustment granted in the last ten years.

B. Water usage must exceed the average of the two prior billing cycles by at least 50%,
resulting in a potential bill adjustment of:

e $150+ for a single-family residence
e $500+ for a multi-family residence

e $1,000+ for non-residential properties
Adjustments will not be made for smaller overages.

C. Applicant must be the property owner and agree to the terms of this policy.
D. Applicant must provide written proof that the issue has been resolved:

e lfrepaired by a professional: Include the plumber or contractor invoice.
e If self-repaired: Provide a signed letter explaining the repairs made.

E. Properties with irrigation systems are not eligible. Usage patterns will be reviewed to
verify this.
F. Adjustments will not be granted due to:

e Changes in property use
e Changes in occupancy
e Use of unapproved service lines

G. Commercial or Residential accounts with more than three units will only be granted
relief on the water portion of the bill. The sewer portion will remain unadjusted.

H. If a leak is detected via smart meter, the Department will notify the customer.
Customers must take action within 30 days of notification. If a leak survey is requested, the
30-day period restarts from the survey date.

|. Properties without smart meters or radio-read systems are not eligible for adjustment.
J. If the water did not enter the sewer, the sewer portion of the bill may also be adjusted. If
it did enter the sewer, only the water portion is adjusted.

K. Applications must be submitted within 60 days of the date on the high bill via email,
certified mail, or in-person.
4. How to Apply

Applications can be found on the Town of East Lyme website under the Water Department
section, or picked up at the Department office (Lower Floor, Town Hall, 108 Pennsylvania
Ave).

There are two types of applications:

e Residential (up to 3 units per meter)

e Residential over 3 units / Mixed Use / Non-Residential

5. Application Review Process
A. Residential (up to 3 units):

e Staff will approve or deny the application.
e Adecision will be made within 30-60 days.

e During this review period, no interest or penalties will accrue on the disputed
amount.

B. Residential (over 3 units), Mixed Use, Non-Residential:
e The application will be reviewed by the Water & Sewer Commission.
e The applicant must attend the Commission meeting to present their case.

e The Commission’s decision is final.

6. How Adjustments Are Calculated

A. Excess water is determined by subtracting the average usage of the prior two billing
cycles from the excessive bill. Half of the difference is considered “excessive use” and
used to calculate the adjustment.

B. If the excess water went into the sewer, no sewer adjustment is made.

C. If it did not enter the sewer and this can be proven, the sewer charge may be adjusted.
D. If a leak was detected and fixed during a partial billing period, a pro-rated adjustment
may be made.

E. lf a customer has owned the property for less than two years, the department may elect
to monitor usage for 30 to 60 days to gain an accurate representation of usage.

F. Once approved, the adjusted bill must be paid within 30 days or it will become
delinquent.
G. If the bill was already paid, the Department may issue a refund or apply a credit to the
account.

G. For non-residential/mixed-use/over 3-unit properties, staff will calculate a suggested
adjustment, but the final amount is at the Commission’s discretion. Accounts under this
criteria will be adjusted for water use only. The sewer portion of the bill shall not be
adjusted.

7. Appeals Process

A. Ineligible Applicants: If denied due to eligibility, customers may appeal to the Water &
Sewer Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. The applicant must attend the
hearing or the appeal will be denied. The Commission’s decision is final.

B. Disagreement with Staff Decision:

If a residential (up to 3 units) applicant is denied or disagrees with the adjustment, they
may appeal to the Commission within 30 days of the denial decision. Non-
residential/mixed-use applications already go to the Commission and therefore have no
further appeal.

8. Applicant Acknowledgement
By submitting a 1-in-10 Water Leak Adjustment application, the applicant agrees to comply
with all terms and provisions in this policy.

EAST LYME WATER & SEWER DEPARTMENT
ISSUES WITH OUR RATE STRUCTURE

WHAT IS THE PROBLEM & POTENTIAL SOLUTIONS?

e Residential irrigation customers on sewer are dropping into either 40,000-150,000 gallons per 6 months or
the 150,000 -2,500,000 gallons per 6 months. Between the higher tiered water rates and the sewer usage,
this is creating some really large water and sewer bills for these customers. If these customers don’t have a
submeter for their irrigation system, they are charged for sewer usage for all the irrigation water even though
the irrigation doesn’t go into the sewer lines. We have had many customers complain to staff that this isn’t
fair.

e This is the first year that we have a specific water irrigation rate. If a customer wanted to go this route, they
would have to pay for the submeter, a radio and the installation (about $800-1000 at a minimum). Based on
our current rates, if you added up the water irrigation rate from the submeter with their regular domestic
water usage rate, it would be higher than if they did nothing so there is currently no incentive to submeter
your irrigation system. We really need to find a way to fairly charge irrigation customers that gets the revenue
for irrigation to the water department instead of sewer.

e We don’t know exactly how many of our customers have irrigation systems, what portion of their bill is
irrigation and what portion is for their domestic needs. We believe that it could be up to 500 accounts
assuming that usage above 60,000 gallons in a 6-month period is used for irrigation. It appears that 92% of
our residential customers do not irrigate using this criteria..

e We hear a lot that our bills are starting to become very costly for people on fixed incomes, so we are looking
to introduce a lower-tiered water and sewer rate for customers that use very little water (say 10,000 gallons
or less).

e Apartment buildings with master meters on buildings are getting charged at a higher water rate tier, as they
are being considered high water users. By placing apartment buildings in a non-residential usage category,
this would help alleviate this issue.

e With the upcoming capital needs for water and sewer greatly increasing, the rates need to go up significantly
to help fund all the projects. This is going to be very unpopular with our customers. We need to explain that
these capital needs are for complying with new drinking water standards regarding PFAS and for various
sewer construction projects, such as Pattagansett upgrades, a new force main under the Niantic River, and a
new Bride Brook Sewer Pump Station along with paying for capital in Waterford and New London.

POTENTIAL CHANGES TO THE RATE STRUCTURE (which were endorsed by the Finance Subcommittee)
WATER

e Have separate rates for residential and non-residential that would allow us to have a competitive irrigation
rate. Customers would then be incentivized for installing an irrigation deduct meter so that customers are not
charged for sewer usage for their irrigation usage.

e Have a lower-level rate tier to help customers on fixed incomes and very low water users — 10k or less.

e Increase the manual meter reading fee from $175 to $200 try to get 100% compliance with radio installs.

e Change the name of the meter maintenance fee to just meter fee as we get a lot of questions about what this
is for

e Change the minimum water charge to not include the first 3,500 gallons. Including the first 3,500 gallons of
water used just gets confusing to calculate and explain to customers.

e All non-residential irrigation and newly installed residential irrigation systems would need to have an
irrigation deduct meter paid for by the customer.

SEWER

e Like water, sewer would have a lower-level rate tier to help customers with fixed incomes.
e Increase the highest sewer rate as these customers are putting a large demand on our system.

MOVING FORWARD

1. With regards to these potential water/sewer rate changes, we would like to hold an informational session during
the March W&S Commission meeting to let our customers hear what we are proposing and to get their input. In
addition to our normal ways of advertising, we would post on Facebook and the Town’s website so that all of our
ratepayers have a chance to learn and be heard.

2. We will continue to meet with the Finance Subcommittee to discuss any proposed rate changes that come from
input from the Commission and the public.

3. Staff will be ready to propose draft operating budgets and rate structures for Water and Sewer at the March W&S
Commission meeting.

4. We will have a public hearing about the budgets and rates at the April W&S Commission meeting.

5. The new rates would go into effect as soon as the appeal and posting period ends, but for most customers, they
would not see a change until the November 2026 bill.

6. We also discussed with the Finance Subcommittee to hold off on moving to quarterly billing for the foreseeable
future as this would have administrative cost implications and is not something that needs to be done at this time.

1. Chairman Cunningham called meeting to order at 10:35 am.

2. Ben North opened by clarifying the need for a potential change to the existing rate structure because of
irrigation. He noted that not everyone that irrigates is on sewer. However, those residential irrigation
customers on sewer are dropping into higher tiers creating large/unexpected bills for the sewer portion
of their bill. Without a submeter, they are charged sewer rates for all water consumed including
irrigation. And, as a result Sewer gains revenue that essentially belongs to Water.

The first attempt at an irrigation rate was offered this year. Customers were required to cover the cost
of the meter, radio, and installation. In doing so, they saw no immediate cost savings and no incentive
to enroll.

As one potential solution, Ben North proposed creating a separate rate structure for residential and
non-residential properties. The span between tiers would be changed and/or additional tiers added.
Having a lower-level tier would help those using minimal water as well as those on a fixed income. To
eliminate confusion on the bills, “meter maintenance fee” would be changed to “meter fee” and base
amount (first 3,500 gallons) would be removed.

Another solution would be to implement a Mandatory Irrigation Program. A volume range would be
set and customers that fell into that category would be required to install a deduct meter. The rates
would be restructured based on the volume criteria. Regardless of solution, customers interested in
irrigation meters would utilize Sensus Ally meters so that the Water & Sewer Department could
automatically shut off the meters during times of draught, etc.

Michelle Williams felt that using block tiers would be less complicated than adopting the mandatory
program. Dave Bond suggested making Irrigation Program voluntary and offer submeters to those that
were interested. Committee liked the block concept more and wanted to see what 0-10,000 and 0-
20,000 tiers would look like. Committee was also interested in a cost estimate to cover deduct meters
for customers initially.

The concept of customers policing their own usage was briefly discussed. Ben North reported he
recently tested a potential software system that would give customers current readings through the
online portal.

As for generating revenue, quarterly billing and implementing a sewer flat fee or minimum charge
were mentioned and tabled for later discussion.

3. Next committee meeting set for Friday, February 13, 2026, at 10:00 am.

2. Ben North opened the meeting by reflecting on his email to the Subcommittee members dated

2/13/2026. Due to consensus on the rate structure, he pivoted to discussing the proposed water budget
for FY¥27. The highlights of the proposed budget included an increase in health insurance premiums
(15%) and the request for an additional distribution operator. Mr. North had mentioned that there was
an immediate need for a distribution operator last fiscal year, and a treatment operator had left to
explore other opportunities. Instead of filling this position, it was deemed better to leave it open and
fill the vacancy in the water distribution division of the department. This filled the immediate need but
left a gap in coverage in water treatment to a staff of two. In December 2025, one of the treatment
operators had taken an extended period of absence and has not returned to work to date. This created a
large void in the water treatment division, and so a fill-in distribution operator volunteered to move
into treatment. Given the uncertainty of the timing and return of the previous operator, and the
vulnerability of relying on only two, Mr. North is proposing to add a position for better coverage.
These notable changes result in a proposed increase to operational expenditures on the water
department by 6.7%. In addition, Mr. North also identified the continued need to increase the water
fund balance to cover the expected debt loading that will be incurred when the Water Department’s
two PFAS projects are completed sometime in FY28 or FY29. This results in an overall Water
Department rate increase and budget increase of 12.2%, which had been discussed with the finance
subcommittee previously.

Joe Bragaw presented the Sewer Department budget for FY27. He noted that most of the increase in
the Sewer Department was due to. increased cost of treatment in New London and an increase in
capital requirements in both New London and Waterford, as East Lyme shares infrastructure costs with
these shared-use assets. Treatment costs are already the largest budget item in the Sewer budget and
will rise by 15.3%. Due to the same increases in health insurance, that cost will also rise. And due to a
new union contract, that included revisions to retirement benefits, both water and sewer departments
would see higher costs related to same. Overall, Sewer Department Rates and Budget are proposed to
rise by 5.6%. Due to the creation of the 0-10k usage tier for residents, about 22% of residents would
see a rate increase of roughly 5% while another 65% of residents would receive a roughly 9% increase
to their overall water and sewer usage bill.

owners to self-check usage and he included money in the sewer budget to pay for the up-front
implementation costs of that initiative, which would total about $30k. Since the rate structure would

would need to be informed of these changes by the March Commission meeting so that it could be
