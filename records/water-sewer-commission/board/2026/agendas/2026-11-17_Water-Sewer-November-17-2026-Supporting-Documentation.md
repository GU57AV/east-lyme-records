FOG Management Program

Proposed Phased Implementation, Inspection and Electronic Reporting Plan
November 2026

Purpose and Recommended Approach

Protect the sanitary sewer system from grease blockages and overflows while minimizing staff demands and
unnecessary costs to food establishments. Adopt a phased, risk-based approach: one comprehensive initial
municipal inspection, quarterly facility self-inspections and electronic service reporting, then ordinarily one
municipal inspection annually for compliant establishments. Additional municipal inspections would be
triggered by documented risks or noncompliance.

What Connecticut Requires — and What It Leaves to East Lyme

e = The 2025 DEEP General Permit, §5(g)(1), requires sufficiently frequent municipal inspections to ensure
compliance; the authorized agent determines that frequency. It does not mandate quarterly municipal
visits.

e Under 85(d)(2), each facility must inspect its FOG management equipment at least quarterly. That
obligation continues even when municipal inspections become annual.

e Under 85(d)(7), outdoor grease interceptors and SCGls ordinarily require complete pumping at least every
three months, or sooner at the 25% grease-and-solids threshold. After at least one year and four qualifying
quarterly inspections, the authorized agent may approve a longer cleaning interval in writing.

e Under 85(f), facilities must retain required inspection, cleaning and maintenance records for five years and
make them available upon request. Digital submissions can supplement, not replace, this obligation.

Three-Phase Implementation

Phase 1 | Initial Outreach and Compliance

Establish a complete inventory of affected Food Service Establishments (FSEs), provide educational materials,
and conduct comprehensive initial inspections to identify equipment, maintenance practices, and deficiencies.
Work with establishments to achieve compliance as soon as possible.

The Commission and selected software provider will assist FSEs and waste haulers with registration and use of
the electronic reporting system. Initial compliance efforts may overlap with Phase 2.

Phase 2 | Quarterly Inspections and Service History (First 12 Months)

The East Lyme Sewer Department will conduct quarterly inspections to supplement required quarterly FSE self-
inspections. These inspections will verify compliance, review maintenance records, and establish operating
histories to evaluate appropriate equipment cleaning frequencies.

FSEs remain responsible for scheduling required cleaning with waste haulers, performing quarterly self-
inspections, and maintaining logs. Waste haulers will submit service records electronically.

Required cleaning frequencies will remain in effect unless an alternative schedule is approved in writing in
accordance with the Connecticut DEEP General Permit.

Phase 3 | Annual Inspections and Ongoing Compliance (Thereafter)

Following the initial 12-month inspection period, compliant establishments will transition to one
comprehensive municipal inspection annually.

FSE quarterly self-inspections, required cleaning, recordkeeping, and electronic reporting will continue.
Additional municipal inspections may be conducted for violations, missed cleanings, excessive grease
accumulation, or other compliance concerns.

The department will periodically evaluate inspection results to ensure continued compliance with the
Connecticut DEEP General Permit.
Electronic Reporting, Notifications and Staffing

The department is evaluating electronic FOG management and compliance tracking systems and obtaining

vendor proposals for consideration. Staff will assess available options addressing system capabilities, costs,

and implementation. Vendor evaluation shall compare implementation and annual costs, customer and hauler

usability, automated reminders, corrective-action tracking, data export, security, training and support, and

contractor adoption.

The proposed system would maintain the FSE inventory, track cleaning and inspection schedules, receive

electronic cleaning and hauling reports, flag missing or late submissions, retain inspection history, and notify

establishments of upcoming or overdue cleanings, violations and corrective-action deadlines.

e SEs remain responsible for scheduling required cleaning and maintenance with waste haulers,
conducting quarterly self-inspections, and maintaining inspection, cleaning and maintenance logs.

e Waste hauling contractors would register for the online portal and submit service documentation following
completed cleaning or pumping.

e The Commission and selected vendor would provide educational and registration materials for
establishments and contractors.

e Department staff would monitor exceptions, review outstanding corrective actions and conduct municipal
inspections; software reminders do not transfer compliance responsibility away from the FSE.

Staff Training and Program Administration

The department will provide training for personnel responsible for administering, inspecting, and enforcing the
FOG Management Program. We have consulted with the Connecticut Rural Water and Wastewater Association
(CT RWWA), which is developing an in-depth course for collection system operators covering FOG program
management. Low-cost online training and certification opportunities will also be evaluated as supplemental
resources.

Although the 2025 Connecticut DEEP General Permit does not specify a separate FOG inspector license or
certification, staff training will support consistent program administration. Topics will include FOG Management
Equipment, inspection methods, grease and solids measurements, cleaning requirements, recordkeeping,
electronic reporting, violations, corrective actions, and enforcement procedures.

The department will also seek training from the selected software provider on portal administration, contractor
reporting, automated notifications, compliance tracking, and reporting. Training options and associated costs
will be considered as part of the implementation proposal.

Reducing the Burden on Businesses

e Explain that the state permit already imposes core FOG maintenance duties; the local program makes
compliance visible and consistent.

e Offer a baseline educational visit, written checklists, clear correction deadlines, and straightforward portal
onboarding and digital reporting options.

e Provide a documented path to less frequent cleaning for qualifying outdoor interceptors and SCGls after
the state-required operating history; this does not reduce quarterly self-inspections.

¢ Apply additional municipal inspections and enforcement primarily where evidence warrants them.

Decisions Requested from the Commission

1. Endorse phased implementation and an annual municipal inspection as the proposed default for
compliant facilities.

2. Direct staff to align the proposed regulation with the October 5, 2025 General Permit and establish written
criteria for increased inspections and extended cleaning intervals.

3. Support appropriate staff training and review associated costs as part of the program implementation
budget.

4. Review a documented fee proposal and outreach plan before final adoption.

SEC. 1 PURPOSE OF REGULATION

The purpose of this section of the Sewer Regulation is to aid in preventing the accumulation of fats, oils
and grease in the East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission’s sanitary sewer system, thus reducing the
potential for sanitary sewer overflows. This Sewer Regulation outlines the wastewater pretreatment
requirements for food service establishments and other commercial facilities that have the potential to
discharge fats, oils and grease in their wastewater flow. All new and existing facilities that discharge fats,
oils, and grease in their wastewater are subject to this program. This Section of the Sewer Regulation
shall be an integral part of the Commission’s Regulation in the application, administration and
enforcement thereof.

SEC. 2 DEFINITIONS

The following terms shall have the meanings indicated hereafter where used in this Regulation and are
in addition to those defined in Part 1, General Sewer Regulation, Section S1b, Definitions:

2a. “FOG — FATS, OILS AND GREASE”

Any fats, oils and grease generated from the food preparation process as identified per the most current
EPA method as listed in 40 CFR 136.3, as may be amended from time to time.

2b. “FOOD SERVICE ESTABLISHMENT (FSE)”

“Food Service Establishment” means a food establishment covered by the General Permit, including
Class Il, III and IV food establishments under Connecticut General Statutes §19a-36g and other facilities
meeting the definition in Section 2 of the General Permit, including facilities in a formally designated
problematic FOG area.

2c. “GENERAL PERMIT FOR THE DISCHARGE OF WASTEWATER ASSOCIATED WITH FOOD
ESTABLISHMENTS”

The Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection General Permit for the Discharge
of Wastewater Associated with Food Establishments, effective October 5, 2025, and any subsequent
amendments, renewals, or replacement.

2d. “ACTIVE GREASE RECOVERY UNIT (AGRU)”

Active Grease Recovery Unit means an interior grease interceptor that separates grease from
wastewater by active mechanical or electrical means, as defined in Section 2 of the General Permit.

2e. “SUPER CAPACITY GREASE INTERCEPTOR (SCGI)”

Super-Capacity Grease Interceptor means an indoor or outdoor passive unit, third-party certified to
retain more than four times as many pounds of grease as its rated flow in gallons per minute, meeting
Section 5(b)(3) of the General Permit.

2f. “GREASE TRAP/FOG INTERCEPTOR”

Grease Trap/FOG Interceptor means a minimum 1,000-gallon outdoor in-ground passive unit designed
to separate fats, oils and grease from wastewater while allowing water to flow through and meets
specifications defined in Section 5(b)(1) of the General Permit and the Sewer Department Sewer
Standard Details S-38 Outside Grease Separator for Kitchen Waste Lines.

2g. “FOG MANAGEMENT EQUIPMENT”

FOG Management Equipment means properly designed, installed and operated equipment approved by
the authorized agent, including AGRUs, outdoor grease interceptors, SCGIs, and other equipment
approved under Section 5(b)(4), designed to meet the effluent limits of Section 5(c) of the General
Permit.
SEC. 3 RULES AND REQUIREMENTS

3a. General Requirements
Food Service Establishments (FSEs) discharging wastewater to the East Lyme Water and Sewer
Commission’s collection system shall comply with the following requirements of the Commission’s Fats,
Oils, and Grease (FOG) Management Program:
1. Compliance with the General Permit for the Discharge of Wastewater Associated with Food
Establishments, effective October 5, 2025, as amended or replaced.
2. Completion of FOG registration or variance application forms supplied by the Commission
every three (3) years. Registrations and variances are non-transferable and must be resubmitted
following a change in ownership or significant menu or facility change.
3. Inspections by the Commission to verify compliance with these Regulations and the General
Permit.
4. Payment of applicable registration, variance, and violation fees, and reimbursement of costs
associated with emergency services provided by the Commission.

5. Maintenance of current business-hours and after-hours emergency contact information with
the Commission, including prompt notification of any changes.

3b. Compliance Dates

New facilities shall comply with the General Permit before initiating discharge. Existing authorized
facilities shall comply with the General Permit effective October 5, 2025, subject to the transition
provisions of Section 3(d) of the General Permit.

3c. Equipment Operation and Maintenance

Each FSE shall be responsible for the proper operation, inspection, cleaning, maintenance, and repair of
its FOG Management Equipment in accordance with these Regulations and the General Permit.

3d. Recordkeeping and Electronic Reporting

Each FSE shall maintain on-site records of all FOG Management Equipment inspections, cleaning,
pumping, maintenance, and disposal, including the information required by Section S(f) of the General
Permit. Such records shall be retained for at least five (5) years and made available to the Commission
upon request.

The Commission may require submission of these records through a Commission-approved electronic
reporting system. Electronic submission shall not replace the on-site recordkeeping requirement.

SEC.4 EQUIPMENT REQUIREMENTS

4a. Wastewater generated by covered food establishments shall be treated using approved FOG
Management Equipment meeting Sections 5(a) and 5(b) of the General Permit, effective October 5,
2025, and the discharge limits in Section 5(c).

4b. Grease Trap Options

Covered food establishments shall use approved FOG Management Equipment under Section 5(b),
including an AGRU, SCGI, outdoor interceptor (minimum 1,000 gallons), or other equipment approved in
writing under Section 5(b)(4), unless a written de minimis waiver is granted under Section 5(b)(5).

The following fixtures must be connected to FOG Management Equipment:
e pot sinks (3-bay sinks);
e _pre-rinse sinks;
¢ any sink into which fats, oils, and grease are likely to be introduced;

¢ soup kettles or similar devices; wok stations;

e floor drains or sinks into which kettles may be drained;

¢ automatic hood wash units; and

¢ any other fixtures or drains that are likely to allow fats, oils and grease to be discharged.

4c. Active Grease Recovery Unit (AGRU)

Active Grease Recovery Units (AGRUs) are relatively small pretreatment units that can be located inside
a kitchen to remove grease at the source of generation. These units allow fats, oils, and grease (FOG) to
accumulate within the unit while skimmers or pumps remove the FOG from the unit and deposit the
material in a separate container for disposal by a grease removal contractor.

AGRUs Shall be inspected by the facility at least quarterly and cleaned and maintained in accordance
with manufacturer recommendations and Section 5(d) of the General Permit. Quarterly inspection does
not by itself establish a universal quarterly complete pump-out requirement for AGRUs. Required
maintenance records shall be retained for five years.

4d. Super Capacity Grease Interceptor (SCGI)

Approved SCGIs may be indoor or outdoor passive units, as defined in Section 2 and regulated by
Section 5(b)(3) of the General Permit.

SCGIs shall be inspected at least quarterly and pumped at least every three months or when grease and
settled solids reach 25% of operating depth, whichever occurs first, unless a longer interval is approved
in writing under Section 5(d)(7)(B).

4e. Outdoor In-Ground Grease Trap/Interceptor

Outdoor grease trap/FOG interceptors shall be inspected at least quarterly and pumped at least every
three months or when grease and settled solids reach 25% of operating depth, whichever occurs first.
Longer pumping intervals require written approval under Section 5(d){7)(B), after the specified one-year
operating and inspection demonstration. Quarterly inspections continue notwithstanding any extension.

The outdoor grease trap/interceptor specifications are described in Section 5(b)(1) of the General
Permit. Installation of a new outdoor grease trap/interceptor shall meet the specifications as described
in the General Permit and the Commission specifications.

Af. Best Management Practices

Renderable fats, oils, and grease shall be disposed of in separate storage containers for recycling by a
renderer. No renderable fats, oils, and grease shall be discharged into grease trap/interceptors or
AGRUs, sanitary sewers, dumpsters or storm sewers. “Renderable” means uncontaminated fats, oils and
grease from the food preparation process that can be used as a source of material that is free of
impurities and can be recycled into products such as animal feed and cosmetics.

The FSE may use hot water, steam, chemicals, or biological additives in the normal course of facility
maintenance, but may not intentionally use hot water, steam, physical means, chemicals, or biological
additives that will cause the release of fats, oils, and grease from the grease trap/interceptor. No food
grinder (garbage disposal) or food pulper shall discharge to any grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs or
other approved units.

All wastewater flows connected to the grease trap/interceptors shall be screened to prevent solids from
entering the treatment units. Screened solids shall be disposed of in accordance with applicable solid
waste regulations.
4g. FOG Storage Containers and Housekeeping

All FOG generated or collected by an FSE shall be stored in dedicated, leak-resistant containers that
prevent overflow or uncontrolled discharge. Containers shall be kept closed when not in use,
maintained in good condition, and serviced as needed.

The FSE shall keep FOG storage areas clean and promptly contain, clean up, and properly dispose of
spills or leaks. No grease or contaminated wash water may be discharged unlawfully to sewers, storm
drains, or surrounding property.

For disposal in Connecticut, the contents of all grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs and other approved
units shall be disposed of at a regional collection/transfer/disposal site. For disposal outside of
Connecticut, the contents of all grease trap/interceptors, AGRUs and other approved units shall be
disposed of in an environmentally acceptable manner.

4h. Equipment Modifications, Ownership Changes, and Inspection Requirements

Ownership and Operational Changes. Upon a change in ownership or renovations that alter FOG
discharge volume or characteristics, the Commission may require FOG Management Equipment to be
modified, supplemented, or replaced in accordance with General Permit §5(a)(4). Registration and
variance resubmission requirements remain in §3a and §5.

Equipment and Plumbing Changes. The FSE shall notify the Commission when new or modified FOG
Management Equipment is ready for inspection and acceptance and of wastewater plumbing changes
requiring reinspection, in accordance with General Permit §§5(a)(5)-(6).

SEC.5 VARIANCES

5a. A facility with limited potential for FOG discharge may request a de minimis treatment waiver from
the authorized agent under Section 5(b)(5) of the General Permit. The waiver concerns treatment
requirements in Sections 5(a) and 5(b), but does not waive Section 5(c) effluent limits. Local variance
registration, fees, and renewal procedures remain separate municipal requirements.

5b. The applicant must demonstrate there is a limited potential for fats, oils, and grease in the
discharge, as well as minimal flow. If the Commission encounters any grease problems in the sewer
infrastructure downstream of the establishment, the Commission has the right to revoke any variances
issued.

5c. Please note the applicant must reapply for a variance approval 30 days prior to the following:
e Expiration date of the three-year approval period; or

° Any significant menu or operational changes that would increase the potential for fats, oils, and
grease in the discharge; or

e Change of ownership.

5d. To be considered for a variance, the facility shall complete the Variance Application and attach a
complete menu for the establishment. The application shall include a description of the proposed
methods for minimizing grease discharged in the wastewater, including employee training, cleaning
procedures and interim grease disposal methods.

SEC.6 INSPECTIONS

6a. Food Service Establishments shall be subject to inspection by the Commission on a regular basis to
determine whether the requirements set forth in this Regulation and the General Permit are being met.
Inspections may include, but are not limited to, inspection of the FOG Management Equipment and food
preparation activities, review of records for the quarterly inspections performed by the Food Service
Establishment, grease storage, and review of cleaning and maintenance logs.

The owner or operator shall provide Commission personnel with reasonable access to the premises, all
FOG Management Equipment, and all records required by this Regulation for the purpose of conducting
inspections.

Inspections may also include verification that:

¢ Required FOG Management Equipment has been properly installed and is being maintained in
good working order.

¢ Grease traps, interceptors, AGRUs, and SCGls are inspected and maintained at the frequencies
applicable to each equipment type under §4 and the General Permit.

¢ Grease trap service tags or other Commission-approved maintenance identification are present
and accurately reflect the most recent cleaning and maintenance.

¢ Cleaning manifests, maintenance logs, disposal receipts, registration forms, variance approvals,
and any other records required by this Regulation are maintained and available for review.

¢ Best Management Practices are being followed to minimize the discharge of fats, oils, and
grease to the sanitary sewer system.

6b. Failure to provide access for an inspection or failure to produce records required by this Regulation
shall constitute a violation and may result in the issuance of a Notice of Violation (NOV) and assessment
of applicable fees and penalties.

6c. Municipal inspections, reviews, approvals, or a failure to identify a violation during an inspection do
not relieve an establishment of its continuing obligations under this Regulation and the General Permit,
and do not constitute a guarantee of equipment performance or ongoing compliance. This provision
shall not be construed to waive any duty or liability imposed on the Commission by applicable law.

SEC. 7 DISCHARGE LIMITS

No covered food establishment shall discharge wastewater from FOG Management Equipment, before
mixing with other wastewater, with a FOG concentration exceeding 100 mg/L or a pH below 5.0 or
above 10.0 standard units, as provided in General Permit Sections 5(c)(1) and (2). FOG analyses shall use
the current method under 40 CFR Part 136 or another method approved in writing by DEEP.

SEC.8 VIOLATIONS

8a. Notice of Violation

In the event that a Food Service Establishment fails to meet the recordkeeping, installation,
maintenance, operational requirements, or discharge limits of these Regulations or the General Permit,
the Commission will issue a written Notice of Violation (NOV). The establishment shall take immediate
steps to correct the non-compliant condition.

8b. Violation Fees

Violations of the Commission’s FOG Management Program shall be subject to applicable fees as
established in the adopted FOG Fines and Fee Schedule. Each day that a violation continues shall be
considered a separate occurrence.

8c. Emergency Notification and Corrective Action

An FSE shall immediately notify the Commission by telephone at 860-739-3419 upon discovering a FOG-
related sewer blockage, backup, overflow, or equipment failure likely to cause such an event. Electronic
reporting submissions do not satisfy this notification requirement.
Where a General Permit violation causes a partial or total sewer blockage, the FSE shall immediately
cease the discharge and notify the authorized agent in accordance with Section 5(h)(2) of the General
Permit. Applicable Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental Protection (DEEP) notification
and reporting obligations, including Sections 5(h) and 6(a), remain in effect.

Where a FOG-related condition presents an imminent risk to the sanitary sewer system, the Commission
may require immediate protective or corrective action to the extent authorized by law.

SEC.9 RESTRICTIONS

No Food Service Establishment shall introduce any additives, including, but not limited to, enzymes or
surfactants as grease emulsifiers or degradation agents, into the FOG Management Equipment unless
given prior approval by the Commission.

SEC. 10 SEWER STOPPAGES AND MAINTENANCE
10a. Responsibility for Costs

Where a Food Service Establishment (FSE) is determined to have caused or contributed to a FOG-related
sewer blockage, stoppage, overflow, or accumulation requiring cleaning or maintenance of the
collection system, the FSE shall reimburse the Commission for documented costs attributable to its
discharge. Such costs may include investigation, labor, contractors, equipment, cleaning, jetting, CCTV
inspection, waste disposal, repairs, and restoration.

10b. Emergency Response

The Commission may undertake necessary emergency measures to protect public health, safety, the
environment, or the collection system without awaiting corrective action by the FSE. An FSE determined
to be responsible for a FOG-related incident shall also be subject to applicable emergency response fees
under the adopted FOG Fines and Fee Schedule, in addition to reimbursement of costs under Section
10a.

10c. Determination of Responsibility and Other Remedies

In determining the source and cause of a FOG-related incident, the Commission may consider inspection
findings, FOG Management Equipment condition, grease and solids measurements, maintenance and
pumping records, CCTV footage, sampling, operational records, and other relevant evidence.

Nothing in this section shall limit other remedies lawfully available to the Commission.

SEC. 11 FEES

The Water and Sewer Commission may establish and revise fees for the Commission’s FOG Management
Program required by this Regulation. The fees include, but are not limited to: annual registration,
variance, violation, and maintenance fees. Please see East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission — Fats,
Oils, and Grease Program Fines and Fees Schedule, updated from time to time.

SEC.12 BILLING OF FOG CHARGES

The fees associated with the Commission’s FOG Management Program will be billed to the fee owner of
the property upon which the Food Service Establishment is situated. The fees shall constitute a lien on
the property upon non-payment.

SEC. 13 PAYING OF FOG CHARGES

The fees associated with the Commission’s FOG Management Program, either as a separate bill or
combined with the water and/or sewer bill, shall be due and payable within 30 days of the date of issue,
and the Commission is empowered to permit an extension of the due date up to seven days after the
end of the billing period. One and one-half percent (1.5%) interest will be applied monthly to the unpaid
balance, including previously applied interest, of all bills outstanding beyond the 30 days.

SEC. 14 NON-COMPLIANCE

The Commission reserves the right to notify the Connecticut Department of Energy and Environmental
Protection of any establishment that is non-compliant with the Commission’s FOG Management
Program.

SEC.15 NON-PAYMENT AND SHUT OFF

15a. In the event any FOG charges remain unpaid 30 days after the date on which payment is due, such
unpaid FOG charges shall be delinquent and constitute a lien pursuant to Section 12 and such lien shall
be enforceable in accordance with the terms of this Regulation and prevailing law. The establishment
owner shall be responsible for all lien filing fees, recording fees, and lien release fees incurred by the
Commission in connection with the collection of delinquent FOG charges.

15b. The Commission reserves the right to shut off the water service to the premises where FOG charges
are combined with the water use charges and remain unpaid 30 days after the date on which payment is
due. If so shut off, the water service will not be restored without payment of all charges due.

LOCAL NEWS BRIEF
Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) Program

FITCHBURG, WI —In an effort to reduce sanitary sewer backups and potential sewer overflows into our
creeks and rivers, the City of Fitchburg maintains a Fats, Oil, and Grease (FOG) program. FOG is the
major contributor in causing sewer backups and overflows, as it reduces the interior diameter of the
sewer pipe and/or plugs the sewer completely.

Sewer overflows and backups can cause health hazards, damage home interiors, and threaten the
environment. A common cause of overflows is sewer pipes blocked by grease. Grease gets into the
sewer from household drains, as well as from poorly maintained grease traps in restaurants and other
businesses.

Grease is a byproduct of cooking from meat fats, lard, oils, shortening, butter, margarine, food scraps,
baked goods, sauces, and dairy products. When washed down the sink, grease sticks to the insides of
sewer pipes (both on your property and under the street). Over time, it can build up and block entire

portions of your home or business plumbing system.

The City has ordinances that require grease traps or oil interceptors at restaurants, large buildings, and
other commercial establishments. Grease traps must be properly designed to handle the amount of
grease that is expected, be installed correctly, and be cleaned and serviced on a frequent basis. Triple
(oil) separators & catch basins (mechanic’s bays, car washes, large garages), plaster/solids traps,
neutralizing basins and more must also be cleaned and maintained on a regular basis.

Residents can help by:

e Never pouring grease or oils down sink drains or into toilets.

* Scraping grease and food scraps into a can or the trash for disposal (or recycling).
Putting baskets/strainers in sink drains to catch food scraps and other solids, and then
emptying them into the trash.

¢ Speaking with your friends and neighbors about how to keep grease out of sewers.

service and/or clean devices where grease traps, oil separators, catch basins, or similar are required.

Joe Wren of Indigo Design came forward representing the Applicant and detailed some of the following
during his presentation:
*The property is over 20 acres and intended for one single-family home.
“The lot has very limited frontage on Mohawk Drive.
*Extensive wetlands, vernal pools, and environmental restrictions limit where a house and driveway can be
built.
“The owners explored alternative access routes but found no practical way to avoid crossing wetlands.
*A driveway approximately 900 feet long is needed to reach a suitable building site.

*The property can support a well and septic system.
*There is sufficient space and suitable soil conditions.
*Connecting to public water and sewer would require digging a deep trench for hundreds of feet, removing
large amounts of groundwater, managing more than 30,000 gallons of water during construction and potential
environmental impacts to wetlands and neighboring properties.

- *The property is unusual because of its size, wetlands, and limited access.
*The request is based on environmental and practical concerns, not simply cost savings.
*Once water and sewer pipes are installed, and properly backfilled, long-term groundwater issues should be

“The major concern is during construction, when deep trenches would fill with groundwater and require
constant pumping. Also, utility trenches can sometimes redirect groundwater toward roads and sewer lines,
potentially creating future drainage issues and avoiding underground utilities altogether would eliminate those
concerns.

Mr. Bond asked how electrical service would be provided to the property.

Mr. Wren explained that electric service would likely use utility poles through part of the site and then
transition underground closer to the house. Power lines do not require the same deep trenching as water and
sewer because they are not affected by freezing. He added that the Inland Wetlands wouldsion would likely
prefer the option with the least ground disturbance.

Mr. North noted that the request involves both sewer and water exemptions and said that the Inland
Wetlands Commission should determine whether crossing wetlands is feasible before concluding that utility
installation is too difficult.

Mr. North expressed concern about allowing a property owner to opt out of public water and sewer
services when infrastructure is already available. He stated that taxpayers have invested substantially in the
public utility system and that connections were generally intended to be mandatory, not optional. He also
inquired about fire protection if the property relies on a private well rather than public water.

| The Commission continued evaluating whether the property should be exempt from connecting to public
water and sewer. Mr. Wren maintained that wetlands, vernal pools, groundwater impacts, and construction
difficulties support allowing a private well and septic system. Mr. North expressed concerns about setting a
precedent and Commission Members also questioned whether wetlands approvals and septic feasibility should
be resolved before making a final decision.

= Mr. Cunningham asked for input from the Town Attorney.
Attorney Bleasdale came forward and highlighted the following:
*The Commission's role is to determine whether strict enforcement of the water and sewer connection
requirement would create a practical difficulty or hardship.
*An Inland Wetlands approval is not required before the Water & Sewer Commission acts.
*The Commission has the discretion to approve or deny the request.
*The Commission could deny the request “without prejudice,” allowing the Applicant to return later with
additional information, or approve the request.

‘ Mr. North explained that engineering measures could be used to control groundwater and drainage
issues during construction, while Mr. Wren again emphasized that the main concern is the large volume of
groundwater that would need to be pumped during the installation process and the potential impact on
neighboring properties.

Mr. Wren argued that granting the exemption would not set a precedent because similar requests are
rare and the property's wetlands, long driveway, and environmental constraints make it unique. He added that
that sewer assessments have already been paid and water frontage fees would still be paid even if the property
does not connect and they would be happy to install any required fire protection measures deemed prudent.

Ms. Russell asked about potential environmental concerns and Mr. Wren said that any well and septic
system would be designed to meet all state health regulations and that a single home on a 20-acre parcel
poses less risk than many approved residential developments that rely on private wells and septic systems on
much smaller lots.

Commission Members discussed whether the Commission's decision should focus solely on whether
requiring the property to connect to public water and sewer would create a practical difficulty or unnecessary
hardship for the Applicant. It was noted that while other issues, such as wetlands impacts and infrastructure
concerns, are important, the Commission's legal standard is limited to evaluating hardship.

Attorney Bleasdale advised that each exemption request is unique and decided on its own facts, so
approving this request would not create a binding precedent for future applications.

Mr. Wren argued that denying the request would force the project to move forward assuming water and
sewer connections are required, potentially weakening future wetlands applications by requiring extensive
trenching and dewatering activities. He reiterated that the property's unique conditions, including extensive
wetlands and the need to relocate significant amounts of groundwater during construction, create a genuine
hardship.

5. Discussion on implementation of Fats, Oils, and Grease (FOG) Reduction Program
Regulations and Set Public Hearing

see attachment.
@ Eats Oils.pdf

The Commission discussed proposed regulations aimed at controlling fats, oils, and grease from
restaurants and other food service businesses to help prevent sewer system problems. Attorney Bleasdale
explained that the Commission has the legal authority to adopt these regulations under both state law and a
DEEP permit that requires local sewer authorities to monitor and enforce grease control requirements. He
noted that DEEP sets the statewide standards, while local sewer commissions are responsible for making sure
businesses follow them.

- Commission Members also asked whether the proposed fines and fees would need approval from the
Board of Selectmen. Mr. Bleasdale explained that these are commission regulations, not town ordinances, and
that the Commission has the authority to adopt and enforce them on its own.

The Commission discussed how the proposed grease regulations would be enforced once adopted. Mr.
Staff explained that employees would receive training to inspect grease traps and ensure restaurants are
properly maintaining them. The process would begin by identifying restaurants that already have grease traps
and collecting information about their operations. Restaurants would be required to submit cleaning and
maintenance records on a regular basis, with the frequency depending on the volume of grease they generate.

Commission Members expressed concern about the amount of staff time needed to perform inspections
and review records. It was estimated that the workload should be manageable because inspections would be
spread out over the year and the town has approximately 100 restaurants.

Commission Members also discussed whether inspection fees would fully cover the cost of the program. Mr.
North indicated that the proposed fees were not intended to cover all expenses and that the town would likely
see savings through reduced sewer maintenance and fewer grease-related blockages.

The importance of properly training inspectors so they can identify maintenance issues and violations
was emphasized. While Mr. Bond expressed concern about adding another expense for local businesses, the
Commission generally agreed that a grease control program is necessary to protect the sewer system and
could reduce costly maintenance problems in the future.
