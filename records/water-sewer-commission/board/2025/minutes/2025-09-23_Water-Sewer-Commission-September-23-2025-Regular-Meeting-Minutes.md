7. Discussion & Possible Action Regarding a Request to Relocate the Sewer Shed Line for
Parkers Place LLC

  Note see attachment.
       Parker's_Place_attachments.pdf

  Note see attachment.
       Mulholland_Letter.pdf

  Note Mr. Heller introduced himself again and stated he was representing Parker’s Place, LLC, owner of
 approximately seven acres located on Park Place in Niantic.

  Note Mr. Heller shared some of the following:
 *The property is bisected by the sewer shed line, running north to south through the parcel.
 *The eastern portion of the property lies within the sewer shed, while the western portion lies outside of it.
 *This application was originally submitted along with a sewer capacity allocation request that had previously
 been denied.
 *The denial resulted in a stipulated judgment entered in Superior Court, which now frames the basis for the
 current reconsideration or modification request before the Commission.

 Note *The original capacity allocation proposal envisioned 60 residential units, split between two 30-unit
institutional-type buildings, all located within the designated sewer shed area.
*During preliminary meetings with the Town’s Land Use Department, it was recommended that the applicant
consider relocating the sewer shed line to allow for a design more aesthetically pleasing and better integrated
with surrounding development.
*As a result, approximately a year and a half ago, the development team reformulated their project concept.

Note *The new concept proposes townhouse-style buildings arranged in smaller clusters or pods with green
space interspersed, offering improved stormwater management and a more community-oriented layout.
*No increase in total sewer capacity or total number of housing units (still 60 units).
*No additional strain on the sewer system beyond what was approved in the prior stipulated court judgment.
*The requested change is limited to adjusting the sewer shed line.

Note *The current line is based on old, inexact maps, not scientific data.
*He believes it's poor planning to split a property with this line, and moving it would make planning more
consistent, align better with Land Use rules, and improve the overall quality of the development.
*This change would benefit the project and the community without stressing the sewer system.

Note The Commission briefly reviewed the map and sewer shed line with Mr. Heller.


Note Mr. Heller reiterated that when the original sewer shed lines were drawn, modern GIS (Geographic
Information Systems) technology did not exist. Today’s tools allow for more precise and logical boundary
setting, which would better align with property lines and real-world geography.

Note Mr. North clarified that the Applicant is not seeking additional capacity, only relocation of the sewer shed
line, and this isn’t an attempt to backdoor new units, but rather a redesign prompted by the Zoning department
to create a more cohesive layout.

 Note Mr. Bond and Mr. Spencer said they're comfortable with moving the sewer shed line to the edge of the
property so long as the unit count remains the same (60 units).

Note Ms. Bollenbach stated she's comfortable as long as the capacity remains the same.


Note Ms. Royce Williams noted she has mixed feelings:
*She's concerned about setting precedent for future developers to request moving the sewer shed line.
*She acknowledged that bisecting lots with arbitrary sewer shed lines isn’t ideal.
*She recalls earlier assurances that the project could function within existing capacity.
*She's still undecided but weighing both fairness and prudence.

Note Ms. Russell warned of the need for caution in future cases to avoid opening the door to larger
developments requesting sewer shed expansions after initial approvals. She thinks we need clearer rules and
official standards going forward.

 Note Mr. Heller reiterated:
*The request originated from the town’s Zoning Department, not the Developer.
*The Developer would have been content with the prior two-building design.
*The change looks better and improves the site design, but it won't change how much wastewater is
produced.

Note Mr. Cunningham asked Attorney Heller to display the two maps for comparison.

8. Discussion & Possible Action Regarding the Purchase of Manhole Assemblies from the
Sewer Assessment Fund

  Note see attachment.
       Manhole_frames.pdf

  Note Staff explained that during a recent Hazard Mitigation meeting, the town identified areas where saltwater
 and storm inflow were entering the sewer system — particularly along Atlantic Avenue near McCook's Beach.

  Note The plan is to install new sealing manhole covers equipped with cam-lock mechanisms that tightly
 secure the lid and prevent infiltration of saltwater, sand, and debris. These covers are designed for flood-prone
 coastal zones to reduce system stress during storms. The cam locks fasten at each corner, fully sealing the
 manhole structure and preventing surface runoff from entering the sewer line.

  Note Mr. North noted the following:
 *They plan to purchase 20 total frames and covers.
 *8 will be the new sealing type, which cost about three times more than standard covers,
 the rest will be traditional models.
 This is a pilot test to assess long-term performance.
 *This pilot project will help identify other high-risk inflow and infiltration (I&I) zones for future upgrades.

 Note Mr. Bragaw shared that Public Works will coordinate with the Town Highway Department as well as
 Water & Sewer next year, to repave and perform work in areas like Attawan Heights and Crescent Beach.
 These are manhole-intensive areas that require realignment and replacement.

  Note Mr. Bragaw mentioned that crews were already working on-site earlier that day and are repairing covers
 using the new manhole cutter equipment. This work is part of a priority sewer maintenance area for the
 upcoming year.

  Note Mr. Cunningham observed that sealed manhole covers are critical to protect pumps and system
 components from saltwater intrusion, which can corrode and destroy mechanical parts.

  Note Mr. Bragaw and Mr. North added the following:
 *East Lyme’s manhole covers are unusually large, which increases wear when vehicles drive over them.
 *Large diameter lids create greater “moment” (mechanical leverage), causing faster deterioration on busy state
 roads.
 *Replacing this in-house will save significant costs — about $5,000 per manhole if contracted out (excluding
 traffic control and materials).
 Being able to do this in-house is a bit unique to East Lyme.
 *Performing this work internally saves both time and taxpayer money.

  Note The Commission commended Staff on their proactive maintenance.

 Mr. Spencer seconded the motion.
