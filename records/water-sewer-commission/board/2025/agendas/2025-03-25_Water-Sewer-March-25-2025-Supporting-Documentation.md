5. Discussion and Possible Action on Fluoride Dosing in Drinking Water

(35) see attachment.
G 1560_001.pdf
(5) Mr. North discussed the ongoing issue of fluoride dosing in the community's water supply. He gave a
brief overview, including communications with the Department of Public Health and legal counsel.

Mr. North shared that the cost of fluoridation is approximately $20,000 and he and the Commission discussed
how the decision is not primarily financial, that they aim to follow scientific evidence but acknowledge the
complexity of the issue and the need for expert as well as public input.

(SEI The Commission is considering whether to continue fluoridation or not, given recent studies and the fact
that they are not legally required to fluoridate due to population size; Connecticut State Statutes require
communities with populations of 20,000 or more to fluoride dose their water.

The Commission discussed the possibility of setting up a public hearing to gather more input on the issue. Mr.
Bond considered timing options, weighing the need to include seasonal residents in the discussion since our
population doubles in the summer. He also noted that it would be beneficial to have expert speakers who are
both in favor of and against dosing, as individuals from both sides feel strongly about their positions.

Ij Mr. Roberts joined the meeting at 7:09 p.m.

(NE) Ms. Russell supplied a handout which gave an overview of the water fluoridation timeline from 2003 to
2018. See attachment.
G 1582_001.paf

SE} Ms. Russell discussed her handout and detailed some of the following:

The EPA’s maximum contaminant level for fluoride was 4.0 mg/L in 2003.

In 2015, the CDC recommended 0.7 mg/L for voluntary water fluoridation.

A 2018 court ruling ordered the EPA to reassess its maximum contaminant level goal for fluoride.

She thinks they should consider temporarily suspending water fluoridation due to concerns about potential
health risks, particularly impacts on IQ, until more information is available.

EE] The Commission discussed the need for public input before making a decision on fluoridation. They
debated the merits of suspension versus maintaining current levels. The unique position of their water system,
being close to the mandatory fluoridation threshold, was highlighted. The complexity of the issue and the
presence of conflicting scientific evidence was also acknowledged.

{EI Ms. Russell emphasized the importance of public input and moving forward in a more expeditious
manner. She does not believe the Commission has the expertise to determine if a fluoride reduction ts
beneficial. She noted that they need a firm answer from the CDC regarding their recommendation. They can
suspend dosing until that information is received, and it is very easy to both stop and restart the process.

[2] Mr. North expressed his appreciation for Ms. Russell's synopsis and summary, noting that this issue has
persisted for a long time. He mentioned his industry experience, observing changes from the 0.8 to 1.2
threshold and now to the 0.7 threshold. He sought to clarify that in the most recent case, which is the current
focus, Judge Chen ordered the EPA to review the information. The data under review pertained to a 1.5
milligrams per liter dose rate, which the study acknowledged, but it did not consider the current residual of 0.7.

SE] Mr. Spencer said he would like input from the customers actually consuming our water and Mr. Roberts
concurred.

‘SEI Mr. Bond stated that he has heard a lot about overdosing and inquired about the actions of other
communities. Mr. North replied that he is unaware of any other community currently addressing this issue.

EI Mr. Cunningham said it's a practical consideration, sticking with the 0.7 level or stopping, but it won't be
long before our population reaches 20,000. He thinks they should pursue the debate a little bit and delay the
public hearing in the hope that summer residents will participate.
[{55) Ms. Russell again stressed the importance of getting updated information from the EPA and CDC.

[E%=) MOTION (2)

Mr. Spencer moved to table the fluoride dosing in drinking water discussion until the May meeting and to
discuss moving it forward to a public hearing at that time.

Mr. Roberts seconded the motion.

(NSE) Ms. Bollenbach pointed out that the DPH made the recommendation to continue adding the fluoridation
to the water, which is significant.

{55 Ms. Russell added that the Department of Public Health, when we first communicated our concerns due
to the ruling, said they were monitoring the situation. When they sent out their most recent comments
encouraging us to continue water fluoridation, they did not reference the lawsuit or consider the serious
unresolved issue regarding whether the 0.7 level may have adverse health effects that were not realized when
it was set. She also noted that this court decision was significantly impacted by the National Toxicology
Program's strong statement that at 1.5 milligrams per liter and above there is strong evidence of potential
effects.

She visited the National Toxicology Program website and found that eight of nine high-quality studies
examining cognitive or neurodevelopmental outcomes indicated problems. Additionally, 72 studies assessed
the association between fluoride exposure and IQ in children; 19 of these were considered high-quality, and 18
of those reported an inverse association between estimated fluoride exposure and children's IQ. This report
from the National Toxicology Program was a key factor in the judge's evaluation.

EE) Mr. Bragaw and Mr. North discussed how water production is down 18% from previous years and
22% from typical production. This reduction is attributed to system improvements, proactive leak detection,

conservation efforts.

APPLICATIONS FOR DETERMINATION OF ADEQUACY OF
SEWER CAPACITY PURSUANT TO GENERAL STATUTES §7-246a(a)(1)

Sewage treatment for the Town of East Lyme is limited. Pursuant to an agreement with
the City of New London and Town of Waterford, East Lyme is currently entitled to a
maximum of 1.5 million gallons per day of sewer treatment capacity at the New London
Regional Water Pollution Control Facility. In order to ensure that there is adequate
capacity for all customers, the Commission adopts the following regulation for
applications for sewer treatment capacity pursuant to General Statutes §7-246a(a)(1).

|. Application. For all development projects that either (a) request a connection for
more than 20 residential units or (b) require more than 5,000 gallons per day
of sewage treatment capacity, an application, pursuant to General Statutes
§7-246a(a)(1), for determination of adequacy of sewer capacity related to a
proposed use of land, shall be submitted to the East Lyme Water and Sewer

Commission (“Commission”) on a form satisfactory to the Commission, and
shall include all of the following:

1. Aclass A-2 survey of the property to be developed, showing the general
layout of the proposed use of land:

2. Proof that the applicant owns the property to be developed, or has the right to
develop the property, and

3. Documentation supporting the amount of capacity being requested.

a. Documentation related to a proposed residential development shall
include the number of residential units, the numbers of bedrooms per

unit, and the methodology used in calculating the amount of capacity
being requested.

b. Documentation related to a proposed non-residential or commercial
development shall include the methodology used in calculating the
amount of capacity being requested, and any special circumstances
(i.e. the type of sewage being treated, design specifications, etc.) that
would affect the amount of capacity being requested.

c. The Commission reserves the right to request from an applicant such
other information that it deems necessary.

4. Anon-refundable application fee of $500.00 shall be paid when an application

is submitted. An additional public hearing fee of $450.00 may be assessed if
applicable.

1. Duration.

1. Within 12 months after the expiration of the appeal period of a capacity
allocation, the applicant shall (1) apply for all necessary land use approvals
for the proposed use of land, and (2) provide proof of all such applications to
the Commission. If an applicant fails to apply for all necessary land use
approvals, or fails to provide proof of such applications to the Commission
within this 12-month period, the sewer capacity allocated to the applicant shall
terminate and be considered null and void.

2. \f the applicant fails to obtain all land use approvals required for the proposed
use of land, the sewer capacity allocated to the applicant shall terminate and
be considered null and void.

3. The Commission will notify an applicant in writing when an allocation has
terminated. The failure of the Commission to provide written notice in a timely
manner shall not constitute or be construed as a waiver of the Commission's
right to declare a terminated allocation null and void.

4. A capacity allocation shall be in effect for a period not to exceed 48 months
from the expiration of the appeal period of the applicant’s last land use
approval with no appeal having been taken therefrom or an unappealed
decision of a court of competent jurisdiction adjudicating such land use
appeal. The Commission may extend an allocation of sewer capacity beyond
4 years if it determines, in its sole discretion, that good cause exists.

5. lf the amount of sewer treatment capacity needed by an applicant decreases

during the land use approval process, the applicant shall notify the
Commission immediately.

Ill. Public Hearing. The Commission may, in its sole discretion, hold a public hearing

on any application. Any such public hearing shall be in accordance with the
provisions of General Statutes 8-7d.

EXTENSION OF SEWER TREATMENT CAPACITY ALLOCATION
MARCH 25, 2025

WHEREAS, on January 19, 2023, Pelletier-Niantic, LLC (“Applicant”) filed with the East
Lyme Water and Sewer Commission (“Commission”) an application for determination of
sewer capacity pursuant to General Statutes 7-246a(a)(1) and the East Lyme
Regulation entitled “Applications for Determination of Sewer Capacity Pursuant to
General Statutes §7-246a(a)(1)” (“Regulation”), seeking an allocation of 110,000 gallons
per day (“gpd”) of sewer capacity for a proposed multi-building designed for assisted
living and associated medical facilities to be located on property at 200 Pennsylvania
Avenue (“Application”); and

WHEREAS, on March 28, 2023, the Commission granted the Application and allocated
capacity to the Applicant in the amount of 75,000 gpd pursuant to the Regulation; and

WHEREAS, Section II.1 of the Regulation states that a successful applicant must apply
for all necessary land use approvals and provide proof of such application within 12
months of the expiration of the appeal period of a capacity allocation, and that failure to
comply with that section results in termination of the allocation and that the allocation
shall thereafter be considered null and void; and

WHEREAS, at the Commission meeting of February 27, 2024, the Applicant, through its
counsel, requested an extension of one year to secure, and not merely apply for, all
necessary land use approvals pursuant to the Regulation. The Applicant acknowledged
that the purpose of the one year extension was to allow the Applicant to secure all
necessary land use approvals, and that if it failed to do so the allocation would terminate
and be considered null and void; and

WHEREAS, at the February 27, 2024 meeting the Commission approved the
Applicant's request for a one year extension, until March 28, 2025, to secure all
necessary land use approvals; and

WHEREAS, on March 3, 2025, the Applicant, through its counsel, wrote to the
Commission requesting a further extension of one year through April 1, 2026, stating
that medical issues prevented the Applicant from complying with the one year extension
granted on February 27, 2024; and

WHEREAS, as of March 3, 2025, the Applicant has secured no land use approvals, has
applied only for a text anendment to the East Lyme Zoning Regulations, and has not
applied to the Inland Wetlands Agency or to the Zoning Commission for site plan
approval; and
WHEREAS, the March 3, 2025 letter is the only communication received by the
Commission or its staff from the Applicant since the February 27, 2024 extension was
granted; and

WHEREAS, based on the foregoing the Commission finds that:

1. The Applicant has not applied for all necessary land use approvals in
compliance with Section {1.1 of the Regulation,

2. The Applicant has not complied with the terms of one year extension granted
on February 27, 2024, and which ends on March 28, 2025.

3. Based on the representations in its March 3, 2025 letter, the Applicant cannot
comply with the terms of the February 27, 2024 extension by March 28, 2025.

4. The Commission finds that good cause exists to grant the Applicant a further
extension of time to secure all necessary land use approvals.

BE IT THEREFORE RESOLVED, that the East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission,
acting as the Town’s Water Pollution Control Authority, pursuant to the Regulation and
based on a review of evidence in the record as set forth herein, hereby grants an
extension of one year, until March 30, 2026, for the Applicant Pelletier-Niantic, LLC, to
secure all necessary land use approvals as required by Section II.1 of the Regulation. If
the Applicant fails to secure all necessary land use approvals on or before March 30,
2026, the allocation shall terminate and be considered null and void effective March 31,
2026.

SUBJECT: Purchase Water Utility Truck with a Loan from Sewer Assessment Funds

Summary of Agenda Item:

The water department is in need of a new vehicle as part of its vehicle acquisition program. In
the past, the department would submit its new vehicle requests to the town to be included in the
town-wide vehicle acquisition program to receive a 5-year loan. The town has discontinued this
practice, and is paying for vehicles outright in cash. Due to the pending funding obligation to
treat PFAS ‘at two well facilities, the water department will not be in a position to pay for the
vehicle in cash outright at this time. Instead, staff are requesting that a 5-year loan be acquired
from funds available in the Sewer Assessment Fund to pay for the vehicle over time with a 4%
interest rate, which is in line with the rate the fund has been receiving through the CT STIF
account.

Action Needed:

Move to authorize a loan of up to $85,000 from funds available in the Sewer Assessment Fund to
purchase a utility truck for the water department. The water department will pay the loan back
with an initial principal installment early in FY 2026 and four additional installments thereafter
at a rate of 4% per year of the remaining balance until the last payment in early FY 2030.
