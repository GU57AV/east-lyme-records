5. PFAS Litigation- Ratification

(Nei) Attorney O'Connell reminded the Board of the background and history of this matter for the record, and of the
action the Board previously took at the November 19th, 2024, meeting. He addressed the presence of PFAS
chemicals in water sources and the class action settlements involving BASF and Tyco Fire Products, noting these
chemicals are non-biodegradable. The Commission, serving 12,245 individuals with drinking water, was included in
the settlement class until it opted out at the previous meeting. The initial line of thinking had been that being part of
the settlement relieves these companies of all liabilities, transferring responsibility to the water supplier and that
opting out retained the Commission's right to sue.

(SE) Attorney O'Connell shared that they received a notice from the settlement lawyers stating the judge in this
settlement litigation had expressed great concern about the small water companies opting out of the settlement and
that we should stay in the settlement class. They were referred to certain pages in the fairness hearing transcript,
which delineated the extreme costs of litigating against the manufacturers, detailing the enormous expense of trying
to get a court judgment against these manufacturers.

After reading these parts the transcript, they received a notice of an option to stay in the class rather than continue to
opt out. We have been going back and forth on this issue. After reviewing it, Mr. North and he thought that although it
requires us to reverse our appeal, it would be better to publicly acknowledge that we to reverse our previous advice
to the Commission, and that the better course of action in this instance is to stay in the settlement class rather than
remain out of it. He believes there were a couple of bellwether cases involving Tyco and BSAF in other areas of the
country that did not go well for the small water companies. Another problem that is worsening is the expense and
trouble of identification, which falls on us as plaintiffs; we would be responsible for proving the presence of the
problem chemicals directly came from the specific products supplied by these companies.

-| Mr. Roberts arrived at 7:13 p.m.

Attorney O'Connell and Attorney Collins further explained that opting to stay in one class action does not mean
that you are holding the others. Mr. North said they chose to stay out of the two larger class action lawsuits because
they believed, and still do, that DuPont and 3M were the primary culprits responsible for producing the chemicals in
question. DuPont and 3M produced a wider range of chemicals, whereas Tyco and BASF were limited to specific
types of chemicals with certain signatures. After learning this new information, it became apparent that it would be a
difficult battle to prove that Tyco, rather than 3M or DuPont, was responsible for the contaminants. Therefore, it made
sense to opt back into the settlements with BASF and Tyco based on this understanding.

Ms. Collins said opting in will prevent the East Lyme Water & Sewer Commission from having to pay high costs out of
pocket.

Ms. Collins noted that if they were to opt out, the challenge lies in identifying the product, having it on site, and the

makes good sense to join in and see where we end up.

SUBJECT: Fluoride Dosing in Drinking Water

Summary of Agenda Item:

The Water and Sewer Commission is currently deliberating on the continuation to fluoridate the
Town’s drinking water. While the benefits of fluoridation to dental health are well known, a
recent court ruling is requiring that the EPA revisit fluoride levels in drinking water. EPA
appealed this ruling in January. State health regulations require fluoridation in communities
larger than 20,000 people, but East Lyme does not fall under this requirement and is fluoridating
voluntarily. The commission has discussed the possibility of temporarily suspending fluoride
dosing until EPA revisits and revises the practice of fluoridation to ensure there are no adverse
health effects to its use. It is advised by Town Counsel that the Commission should hold a public
hearing to ensure that all members of the public have an opportunity to weigh in on this
important matter.

Action Needed:

Move to authorize the Water and Sewer Commission to set a public hearing to further discuss
water fluoridation dosing in the Town’s drinking water.

Re: CESSATION OF FLUORIDE ADDITIONS TO TOWN WATER SUPPLY
Dear Mr. North:

You have advised us that the Water and Sewer Commission (the “Commission”)
is presently considering ending its practice of adding fluoride to the public water supply,
and you have requested the guidance of the Town Attorney’s Office concerning the
procedural process that should be followed in determining whether to move forward with
ending the practice.

You have advised us that the Town has been adding fluoride to the water supply
for many years. This practice is not based on a requirement contained in the Town
Charter or in any Town ordinance or regulation. You have advised us that the practice
is longstanding, predating the current staff of the department, and the current
Commission membership. As a result, the exact circumstances and process by which
the practice of adding fluoride began is not known.

State law does provide for the addition of fluoride to public water systems under
certain circumstances. General Statutes § 19a-38 requires that fluoride be added to
any public water system serving twenty thousand (20,000) or more people. East Lyme’s
public water system serves fewer than twenty thousand people. Based on these facts,
you had advised us of your conclusion that the statute does not apply to East Lyme.
We agree with your conclusion. The fact that the statute does not apply means that the
Town's practice of adding fluoride to the water is entirely voluntary and there is no legal
obligation for the Town to continue doing so.

You have also advised us that you have contacted the state Department of
Public Health (“DPH”) regarding this issue. This was an important step for you to have
taken as state law places public water systems under the jurisdiction of the DPH and
DPH monitors matters of public health such as the fluoridation of public water systems.
DPH has provided a letter to you dated December 11, 2024, agreeing with the above
conclusions we have reached concerning the lack of a legal obligation to continue
adding fluoride to the water. The letter expressly recognizes that § 19a-38 does not

§ 19a-38. Water company to add fluoride to water supply

Currentness

A water company, as defined in section 25-32a, shall add a measured amount of fluoride to the water supply of any water system
that it owns and operates and that serves twenty thousand or more persons so as to maintain an average monthly fluoride content
that is not more or less than 0.15 of a milligram per liter different than the United States Department of Health and Human
Services' most recent recommendation for optimal fluoride levels in drinking water to prevent tooth decay.
