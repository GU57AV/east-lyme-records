Chairman Nickerson noted that this is not a Public Hearing and that it is only for informational purposes.
Mr. Bragaw and Mr. Scheer would be providing information.

Mr. Scheer explained the letter and map (Attached) and the Sewer extension noting that the alternate
route allows for gravity feed, a road upgrade and the developer would pay for the sewer line. It would be
an ‘opt-in’ system meaning that people would be required to tie-in if:

= The septic fails and LLHD deems it a health hazard

= There is a change of use of the property — it is enlarged, added to, etc.

In order for this to go forward an easement from a property owner would be necessary. The developer
would negotiate this easement at his expense.

Attorney O’Connell explained the residential assessments and the history of them — starting with a basis
of $7800 per lot that was established in the 1980’s. The assessments are calculated over the years
based on the original $7800. He noted that the recent Pine Grove project was a necessary tie-in and
not an ‘opt-in’. When there is no immediate public health need, they have an ‘opt-in’. The most recent
system at the Orchards was assessed at $12,066 (per lot) which compared exactly to the original $7800
per lot in the 1980’s. The assessment is for the benefit of having a sewer system and the infrastructure
for it such as the New London treatment plant. He lastly noted that the red line on the map was the
subject of a public hearing some years ago and does not need another public hearing. The green line
would require a public hearing. The assessment is paid in installments usually over a period of 20
years. Current interest rates are very low.

Questions and discussion followed.
The informational session was adjourned at 7:40 PM.

Dear Resident,

The developer of Village Crossing on Park Place, Niantic, is preparing to install a new
sewer line this spring that would run southwesterly along Park Place from the development to the
existing sewer main on West Main Street. The East Lyme Water and Sewer Commission is
considering an alternative route that would take the new sewer line in an easterly direction along
Park Place to the existing sewer main located behind house numbers 27, 29, and 31 Park Place

(see enclosed map).
There are several advantages to the alternate route:

1.) The sewer line would be constructed by the developer in conjunction with the project
and offer an opportunity to residents on Park Place to connect to the sewers if so
desired. A sewer benefit assessment would be levied on the property but the
obligation to pay the assessment would not occur until such time connection is made
to the sewer. A property owner would not be required to connect to the sewer except
in instances where the Director of Health has determined that the subsurface sewage
disposal system has failed or should a change in use of the premises require an
expansion or improvement to the septic system.

2.) The need for a sewer pump station can be eliminated. As originally planned, if
sewers are ever extended in the future into Park Place, a sewer pump station would be
required. The developer was required to provide a small parcel of land in front of 47
Park Place for a future pump station site. The alternate route would allow the
development and the rest of the neighborhood to benefit from a completely gravity
sewer system. Eliminating the pump station would save operation and maintenance

costs for the sewer department and have greater aesthetic appeal to the neighborhood.
