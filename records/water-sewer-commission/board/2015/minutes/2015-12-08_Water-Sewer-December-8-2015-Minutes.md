4. Cardinal Road Water Main Extension
Mr. Seery said that Jim Levandoski, the Flanders Fire Chief was present this evening and would explain
the issues surrounding the fire in July on Cardinal Road.

Flanders Fire Chief Levandoski said that when they fought the fire in July on Cardinal Road that the
issue was not the water — there were huge amounts of water available with more pressure than they
would need. As far as having water run up the road and more hydrants, the real issue depends on the
timing and the equipment and people. He said that they believe that the fire on Cardinal Road had an
hour and a half head start on it by the time it was noticed and someone called the department. This
really is a timing issue as there was more than enough water. He noted as an example that there was a
fire on Morris Lane and it had the same result even though they also had lots of water and equipment. It
is not where the hydrants are as they have the hose to lay to get to the fire and it can be put down in
minutes, 500 feet, 1000 feet, they have the hose. The 5” hose that they laid for the Cardinal Road fire
did not tax the capability that they have with respect to hose.

Mr. Bragaw noted that the logistics of the hydrant being across the street; across Chesterfield Road
which is busy was something that they felt needed to be addressed.

Mr. Levandoski said that he is a volunteer fireman but his job is a policeman and that the hydrant being
across the street has its own traffic issues as they cannot have people driving over it and big trucks that
cannot stop in time can potentially cause the hose to burst with their weight. The hydrant being placed
across Chesterfield Road on Cardinal Road makes it much better and makes it such that there is no
timing delay. He said that he timed it and that it took six minutes to get to the fire and that as with any
fire, their first priority is to get any people out and then attend to the property.

Mr. Seery asked that they address the bag on the new hydrant that was installed on Cardinal Road.

Mr. Kargl said that they have flushed the system there and tested the water and that it is at the lab now.
They expect the results back in a few days and expect that the hydrant will be in service by the end of
this week.

Ms. Russell asked Mr. Levandoski, when considering extending the water into the neighborhood what
the optimal number of hydrants would be for that area or is the hydrant that is there enough or would
they want more, closer together.

Mr. Levandoski said that they have the ability to use their equipment and people with the right timing to
get to a fire and do what they need to do. Using Grassy Hill as an example, he said that with the one
hydrant and all things aligning they can get to the fire and have water and do what they need to do.
With the new hydrant installed across the street on Cardinal Road he said that he could not see any net
gain in time as the issue of the traffic would be gone. Further he said that they had Flanders Fire,
Niantic, Oswegatchie Fire and Chesterfield Fire at this fire and with the head start that it had, there was
no way to save the property.

Mr. Murphy said that he thinks that what he is saying is that the hydrants would just be an added benefit
to the properties.

Mr. Seery said that he has spoken with Mr. Kargl and Mr. Nickerson about this and if there is continued
interest they would hold an informational session with more detailed information and dollar costs.

Mr. Bragaw said that in the survey that they sent out that they had provided information on the costs
which came from a contractor who does this work.

Mr. Kargl noted and explained the preliminary design that was done and said that it appears feasible

and the hydrants would be every 700’ or so. Septic systems and wells would have to be located and
they would have to figure where exactly the line would be run.
