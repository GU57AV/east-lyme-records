5. PFAS Litigation- Ratification

   Note Attorney O'Connell reminded the Board of the background and history of this matter for the record, and of the
  action the Board previously took at the November 19th, 2024, meeting. He addressed the presence of PFAS
  chemicals in water sources and the class action settlements involving BASF and Tyco Fire Products, noting these
  chemicals are non-biodegradable. The Commission, serving 12,245 individuals with drinking water, was included in
  the settlement class until it opted out at the previous meeting. The initial line of thinking had been that being part of
  the settlement relieves these companies of all liabilities, transferring responsibility to the water supplier and that
  opting out retained the Commission's right to sue.

   Note Attorney O'Connell shared that they received a notice from the settlement lawyers stating the judge in this
  settlement litigation had expressed great concern about the small water companies opting out of the settlement and
  that we should stay in the settlement class. They were referred to certain pages in the fairness hearing transcript,
  which delineated the extreme costs of litigating against the manufacturers, detailing the enormous expense of trying
  to get a court judgment against these manufacturers.
  After reading these parts the transcript, they received a notice of an option to stay in the class rather than continue to
  opt out. We have been going back and forth on this issue. After reviewing it, Mr. North and he thought that although it
  requires us to reverse our appeal, it would be better to publicly acknowledge that we to reverse our previous advice
  to the Commission, and that the better course of action in this instance is to stay in the settlement class rather than
  remain out of it. He believes there were a couple of bellwether cases involving Tyco and BSAF in other areas of the
  country that did not go well for the small water companies. Another problem that is worsening is the expense and
  trouble of identification, which falls on us as plaintiffs; we would be responsible for proving the presence of the
  problem chemicals directly came from the specific products supplied by these companies.

  Note Mr. Roberts arrived at 7:13 p.m.


   Note Attorney O'Connell and Attorney Collins further explained that opting to stay in one class action does not mean
  that you are holding the others. Mr. North said they chose to stay out of the two larger class action lawsuits because
  they believed, and still do, that DuPont and 3M were the primary culprits responsible for producing the chemicals in
  question. DuPont and 3M produced a wider range of chemicals, whereas Tyco and BASF were limited to specific
  types of chemicals with certain signatures. After learning this new information, it became apparent that it would be a
  difficult battle to prove that Tyco, rather than 3M or DuPont, was responsible for the contaminants. Therefore, it made
  sense to opt back into the settlements with BASF and Tyco based on this understanding.
  Ms. Collins said opting in will prevent the East Lyme Water & Sewer Commission from having to pay high costs out of
  pocket.

   Note Mr. North noted that he was able to opt back in online and Ms. Royce-Williams inquired about the Attorney
  General's Office and their thoughts on this issue. Mr. North replied that he has yet to receive a response from their
  office and Ms. Collins suggested that she contact them in a more formal manner.
  Ms. Collins noted that if they were to opt out, the challenge lies in identifying the product, having it on site, and the
  mass authority litigation that always makes it difficult to show whose product was on site at the time. It is a very costly
  and difficult endeavor to prove that. Therefore, small municipalities simply could not manage it. Therefore, it probably
  makes good sense to join in and see where we end up.

6. Discussion & Possible Action RE Metering at Windward Apartments

  Note see attachment.
       Winward_Village_Apartment_Conversion_to_Condominiums.pdf

   Note Mr. Cunningham noted he would be abstaining from any vote on this topic since he has family members that live
  at Windward Village.

   Note Johnny Diaz from Alpha Capital proposed converting a building at 468 Main Street into condominiums and
  requested approval for a single water meter for the building. He detailed how they would have a year's worth of
  payments in escrow at all times as a safeguard, and always have an HOA associated with the property. He pointed
  out how this would streamline billing for them and converting the apartments into condos will increase the tax revenue
  for the town. Mr. Diaz added that this gives residents the opportunity for ownership without requiring additional
  resources from the town. Their goal is to work collaboratively with the town.

   Note Ms. Russell asked if there's a specific problem with installing individual meters for the units and Mr. Diaz replied
  that the high cost would likely prevent them from going this route if they're required to install individual meters.

   Note Mr. North explained that the regulations, which have been in place since 1981, prohibit a single meter for
  condominiums. He used Churchwood Condominiums as an example and explained how they cannot levy fees if only
  one meter is installed. This situation creates difficulties because it is unclear who is paying and who is not, and most
  condominium residents prefer to manage their own utilities. He does not believe that having individual meters is
  sensible.

   Note Ms. Russell inquired if any exceptions had been made to this regulation since it was enacted, and Mr. North
  stated that there had not. Mr. Diaz pointed out that there had also not been any requests until now.

  Note Mr. North expressed concern about the potential financial risk if the HOA fails to pay bills.


  Note Mr. Bond asked Mr. North what would make him comfortable, and Mr. North replied, "individual meters."


   Note The Commission briefly discussed how there are 60 units in the building and Ms. Russell observed that she
  didn't see any legal exception for allowing this proposal, which Attorney Zamarka confirmed.

   Decision MOTION (6)
  Ms. Royce-Williams moved to deny Alpha Capitals' request to receive an exception to East Lyme Water and Sewer
  regulations and bill an HOA as entity representing the homeowners for water usage at 468 Main Street.
  Ms. Russell seconded the motion.
  Motion carried, 8-0-1.
  Mr. Cunningham abstained from the vote.

10. Discussion on Water Fluoridation Moratorium

  Note see attachment.
      CommunityWaterFluoridation.pdf

  Note The Commission discussed fluoridation as a measure for water treatment. It's a low-cost measure with proven
 benefits, but it requires careful dosing. The cost is about $15,000 a year and dosing levels have been revised by the
 EPA.

  Note Ms. Russell inquired about the complexity of implementing a moratorium and reversing it if necessary in the
 future. Mr. North responded that it should be a public and fully transparent process, including a public hearing. He
 suggested sending out a letter, as public hearings typically do not receive substantial participation.

  Note Mr. Murphy observed that the reading indicates that fluoride is more beneficial for young children.

11. Project Updates
  11-1. Phosphate dosing at Well 1A/6 and BBWTP

     Note Mr. North stated that this was a preventive measure implemented at two facilities, allowing us to achieve
     adequate dosing throughout the entire distribution system due to their significant size. We have recently initiated
     this process and have reached our desired dosing level of approximately 1 mg. The cost is comparable to fluoride
     dosing, estimated at around $10,000 to $15,000 per year.

  11-2. Jean Drive Water Booster Station Upgrade

      Note Mr. North mentioned that our distribution system in that area is a smaller hydropneumatic tank system,
     serving approximately 30 homes. About 8% of these residents live in that area. To boost pressure, we opted for
     vertical turbine pumps, specifically the smaller cartridge style. The system will be updated to include dual pumps.
