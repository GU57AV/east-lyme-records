= Regional Interconnection

Mr. Kargl said that Mr. Kenney has been doing a good job working with the contractors and noted the
copy of the letter on the pressure reducing valves (PRV’s) that was sent to people. They have been
working with the property owners and will start installing them soon. He said that they have also found

one is the Aces High Campground and another is St. Matthias Church. He noted that Aces Highs has a
6” service.

them, it would allow Annello the ability to purchase the materials necessary to do both of the jobs.

9. Review of Hydrant Fees
Mr. Formica said that this has been mentioned as a topic for discussion.

Ms. Russell provided everyone with copies of some information that she had provided Mr. Formica with
a few weeks ago. (Copies attached at end of Minutes) She reviewed the sheets noting the analysis of
public hydrant revenue to the East Lyme Water from the Town over the years and what she had
determined was lost revenues especially when comparing our hydrant fee to those of other Towns and
Public Water Companies in the area. She said that we are at the low end of the spectrum and that she
thinks that we should seriously increase what the Town pays the East Lyme Water company for
hydrants. She noted that the charges with the others cited included the flushing, maintenance, etc of the
hydrants, She said that she thinks that our Water Dept. is in a situation where is could use the extra
revenues.

Mr. Mingo said that while he agrees with what has been said; he recalled to everyone that when the
water mains were put in that the Town paid 30% of the cost to run the line. They are a business
however he feels that politically increasing the water rates will cause people to scream — whereas a one
mil increase to everyone would be better absorbed. He said that he thinks that is a more palpable
method of doing this to the public as the public benefits from having the hydrants and they save on
taxes and their insurance because they are there.

Ms. Russell said that she thinks that the simpler way to do this is for the rate payers to absorb it.

Mr. Formica asked if they have a recommendation of what the fee should be.

Ms. Russell said that even if they go back to the 1998 level of $74,708 that would be better than where
they are now. If they were to go to $150 per hydrant with approximately 800 hydrants that would be
$120,000.

Mr. Formica asked Ms. Russell if she wanted to charge the $150 per hydrant.

Ms. Russell said maybe eventually but not all at once.

Mr. Mingo said that they need the money for the pump station upgrades.
Mr. DiGiovanna said that they are not going to get all of their revenues from hydrant fees and that it is
unrealistic to think that way.

Mr. Bond said that they are $60,000 off from what they were in 1998 and asked what percentage that
would come to in a rate increase on the water side to generate that amount.

Mr. Kargl said that it would be from 3% to 5%.

Mr. Bond said that while they are in need of money that he looks at it from the view that they are a
municipality as a group and not separate and that when the Water & Sewer truck comes and plows the
road it is the same as a Public Works truck doing it — they are all in this together and they would only be
taking from one pocket to put it in another.

Mr. Spencer said that even if they go back to the $74,708 — it would still be the cheapest of the others
listed. He thinks that in considering fire protection that there is some benefit to the Town even if they
were charging the $150 per hydrant.

Mr. DiGiovanna asked if the current hydrant fees are spread out amongst all the taxpayers — as while
he is in favor of raising the rates — he would not be in favor of doing it all at once.

Mr. Formica said that they do raise them — at 10% each year.
Ms. Russell said that is at a glacial pace.

Mr. DiGiovanna asked how this would be done per hydrant.
Mr. Kargl said that they would have to go out and check to make sure of how many there are, etc.

Mr. Bond asked where the money would go.
Mr. Kargl said that it goes into general revenues.

Mr. Mingo said that he thinks that it is easier to pass it on to the taxpayers in the form of a one mill tax

support them doing that. However — he wants it known that he is against this. He asked if they want to
establish a committee.

Mr. Mingo said that he wants to wait and see what the budget looks like first. He also recalled that they
were supposed to have new meters years ago.

Mr. Bond said that they should continue discussing this as he does not agree with hydrant fees — he
would rather see them charged for using this room as that is something that is done in other Towns —

added that he pays more taxes for his house because he has access to city water and sewer in front of
