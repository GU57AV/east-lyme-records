                                                RECITALS

WHEREAS, Miami Beach Association, Miami Beach Association Water Pollution Control
Authority, The Old Lyme Shores Beach Association, The Old Lyme Shores Beach Association
Water Pollution Control Authority, Old Colony Beach Club Association, and The Old Colony
Beach Club Association Water Pollution Control Authority, and East Lyme are the Parties to the
Agreement (for purposes of this Amended Agreement, they shall be called the Original Parties);
and
          WHEREAS, the Agreement sets out the terms by which the Beach Associations would
have the right to transmit sewerage generated within the limits of each Beach Association through
a connection with the East Lyme Wastewater Conveyance System (East Lyme System) ultimately
to be treated at the City of New London Sewage Treatment Facility; and

       WHEREAS, pursuant to paragraph 7 of the Agreement, East Lyme agreed to convey
120,000 gallday of sewerage from the Beach Associations' System and gave the Beach
Associations an option to reserve an additional 180,000 gallday of flow; and
        WHEREAS,paragraph 14 of the Agreement provides that while the sewerage transmitted
from the Beach Associations to East Lyme was to be generated solely from the Beach Associations
and from no other source, the Original Parties, by mutual consent, which consent was not to be
unreasonably withheld, could "extend" the Agreement to allow for sewerage generated from
additional communities to be conveyed via the East Lyme Wastewater Conveyance System (East
Lyme System); and

        WHEREAS, the Original Parties recognize that Old Lyme and the Old Lyme WPCA
require assurances from them that they will have the right to convey up to 50,000 gallday of
sewerage to be generated from areas within the Town but outside the limits of the Beach
Assocations, in order to proceed with plans that have been underway for some time to potentially
sewer those areas and comply with governmental orders; and

       WHEREAS, Old Lyme and the Beach Associations are parties to a Cost-Sharing
Agreement dated October 22,2020 (CSA), pursuant to which, among other things, they have
agreed to share the costs of consttucting a sewerage conveyance system and connecting it to and
conveying sewerage through the East Lyme System.

        NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the foregoing premises and the mutual
covenants, representations, wamanties and agreements contained herein, and other good and
valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged by the
Parties to this Amendment, they hereby agree as follows:

       1. Pursuant to paragraph 14 of the Agreement, the Original Parties hereby mutually
consent to extending the Agreement to allow Old Lyme to convey 50,000 gallday of sewerage
generated from areas within the Town of Old Lyme but outside the limits of the Beach Assocations
through the East Lyme System. The Original Parties firrther agree that the 50,0009allday shall be

Explanation: Lower rainfall, combined with high water demand, has led the State of
Connecticut's interagency Drought Working Group to declare that all eight counties in the state
are experiencing Stage 2 Drought conditions. These conditions can lead to a drought event and
can impact water supplies, agriculture, and the environment. Considering these conditions, East
Lyme Water is reminding its customers about the importance of water conservation.

Homes and businesses with automatic sprinkler irrigation systems often use large volumes of
water - about 40olo more than properties without the systems. The Environmental Protection
Agency (EPA) and other experts identiff the overuse of outdoor irrigation as a major source of
strain on local water supplies while providing no benefit to lawns and gardens.

Research shows that lawns and gardens can ttuive on reduced watering. By encouraging roots to
grow deeper into the soil, they're able to absorb more moisture and nutrients, even during dry
spells.

The mandatory twice-weekly irrigation schedule is in effect until further notice.

Connecticut continues to experience the impacts of climate change with this exceptionally dry

severe of those conditions right now in the areas of New London and Windham counties,"

To avoid further stressing water supplies and other impacts of drought, the Governor has requested
State and local governments, residents, and businesses to voluntarily observe the following
measures in addition to the mandatory ones currently in effect:

   o Reduce, to the extent possible, the watering of lawns, recreational and athletic fields,
       gardens, or other landscaped areas (if watering is essential, late evening hours are best)
   o Avoid buming in or near woodlands or brushlands
   o Report dry fire/inigation ponds or private wells to municipal drought liaisons or regional
       emergency management liaisons
   o   Postpone the planting of any new lawns or vegetation
   o   Minimize overall water use by fixing leaky plumbing and fixtures
   o   Take shorter showers
   o   Run dishwashers and clothes washing machines with full loads
   o   Shut off water while washing dishes, shaving, brushing teeth, and lathering up to wash
       hands, rather than running the water continuously
   .   Avoid washing vehicles or power-washing homes and other buildings
   o   Do not use water to clean sidewalks, driveways, and roads
   o   Do not use public water to fill residential swimming pools
