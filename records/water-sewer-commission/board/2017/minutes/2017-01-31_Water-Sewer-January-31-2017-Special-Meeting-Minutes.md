4. Billing Adjustments - 11 Freedom Way (Building A, Unit 1 & Unit 2)

Mr. Kargl said that the owner would have liked to be here this evening but was unable to make it due to
the weather. He explained that one unit had a defective toilet flapper and that during the same billing
period another unit had installed a hot water tank above the bathroom ceiling without their knowledge
and the unit broke emptying excessive water into the back parking lot where the drain pipe was
emptying to. The entire parking lot was flooded before they found out what had happened.

Mr. Spencer said that this is outside of the actual 1 in 10 as it is a commercial property.
Mr. DiGiovanna said that it is negligence that caused the second referral and that frankly he has

difficulty approving any of this. In considering the latest letter, he would put the onus on the tenant and
he feels that the owner should go after them.
Mr. Spencer added that he also has an issue with them already getting a stab at this and then coming
back for another one. The fact of the matter remains that they still lost a half million gallons of water due
to the negligence.

**MOTION (2)
Mr. Spencer moved to table further discussion on this item until the meeting next month.
Mr. DiGiovanna seconded the motion.

Mr. Murphy asked if they have a policy of sending our people out to look at this and if we have a
disclaimer.

Mr. Bragaw said that the customer is responsible for fixing the leaks and providing proof that all of the
work has been done.

Vote: 5-0-0. Motion passed.

7. Policy Review

= Billing Dispute Resolution
Mr. Bragaw explained the policy revision that they were proposing. He said that they review the dispute
as sometimes it is just the rate increase that was approved and that went into effect that is causing the
difference. If the meter is to be tested/replaced he said that they cannot continue doing that for free.

Mr. DiGiovanna asked what is involved in looking up the customer history and how long it takes.
Mr. Bragaw said that it takes around 15 minutes to pull the information needed for review.

Mr. DiGiovanna said that this is for residential.

Mr. Murphy said that the 1 in 10 policy is meant for residential.

Mr. Spencer said that they have at times allowed others.

Mr. Murphy suggested that if they were going to consider commercial that all of them would have to

come before the Commission.

Ms. Russell agreed that all commercial should come before the Commission as there are a number of
issues. She also suggested phasing out the leaking toilet allowance completely as a reason for an
adjustment. They should warn the customers that it is going away and then do away with it. Customers
have to have some responsibility for these items as they are homeowner issues.

Mr. Bragaw said that they could provide two different applications and that all commercial comes before
the Commission. He noted that once they fine tune the policy that it would have to come before the
Commission for a vote.

Mr. DiGiovanna said that whatever is causing the leak has to be fixed — they cannot continue to let it go.

Mr. Bragaw said that he hopes that this becomes a bridge to the new meters — at which point it would
go away.

Mr. Nickerson said that he wants them to move on the new meters right away and that it will be on one
of their next agendas.

= Delinquent Interest Fees
Mr. Bragaw explained that they were looking into trying to make an adjustment on delinquent interest
fees under certain circumstances however the Town Attorney said that they could not do that except
(after investigation/review) it is found that the error in interest is a clerical one made by department staff.

Mr. Spencer said that those with high delinquent interest should not get a free pass as there are those
customers who pay current all the time and get nothing for it.

= Fire Hydrant Fees
Mr. Nickerson said that Ms. Russell wanted to discuss this item.

Ms. Russell said that in 1999 the Town paid $74,708 in hydrant fees and that in 2000 it paid $12,000
and that it stayed at $12,000 until FY 2012 when they had 10% increases each year. They are just
about at $20,000/year now.

Mr. Bragaw corrected that amount saying that they are at $33,000 now

Ms. Russell said that in 1999 there were 700 to 724 hydrants in Town so it was just about $150 per
hydrant then — so now they are losing money.

Mr. Nickerson said that the Commission voted on that at the time.

Ms. Russell said that they cannot afford it anymore.

Mr. Nickerson asked Ms. Russell where they would get the additional amount that she wants to charge
per hydrant and how she proposed to do it. He asked with all the research she had done — where is the
research to show where the money is going to come from.

Mr. DiGiovanna said that they would have to raise taxes to all of the taxpayers to get the additional
money.

Mr. Bragaw explained that for instance - Groton Utilities is a separate organization — but here if you
raise the hydrant fees by $50,000 the Town does not have the money either and it would mean laying

off an employee to get it.

Mr. Nickerson asked again — where is the money going to come from. He said that they will give 10%
more this year than last year so it is getting increased — they can’t just give a large lump sum.

Mr. Spencer said that he thinks that they have debated this forever — so if they look at Water & Sewer
and the offices — we don’t pay the Town for that or the trucks, pay loader, etc that are used for breaks
and other work. We also use Public Works staff — so they could go back and forth on this forever. The
decision was made some 20 years ago and they have been working on it.

Mr. DiGiovanna said that he thinks that they are taking a good gradual approach to it.

Mr. Nickerson and Mr. Bragaw said that it has gone up and they are making progress. They also noted
that the engineer salary that was 40% paid by Water & sewer is now completely on the Town side.
Everything has to be done incrementally.

Ms. Russell said that she wants Montville and Waterford to pay on the hydrants they have there with the
interconnection.

Mr. Bragaw said that the businesses there are paying for private hydrants and that is part of the
agreement.

Mr. Nickerson said that he would speak with the Mayor of Montville and that the 10% will stand this year
and in successive years.
