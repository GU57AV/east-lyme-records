4. Billing Adjustments

Mr. Kargl explained that he had provided them with paperwork on the 15 Clark Street billing adjustment
request. He said that based on the evaluation that they were not able to identify a leak that could have
contributed to the higher than normal bill and therefore could not make the adjustment in accord with
the 1 in 10 policy. They also put a new meter in and could not find any evidence of a leak — they
watched the meter and there was no movement signifying no leak. Mr. Johnson is here this evening for
consideration.

John Johnson, 15 Clark Street said that he lives alone and travels so he could not see where anything
would be different from previous billings. He said that he thinks that the person reading the meter may
have read it incorrectly as it is behind bushes in a corner.

Mr. Zoller said that if that was so, then other readings would also be inconsistent. It seems that he just
used more water and that was it.

Ms. Russell asked Mr. Karg] if this is the kind of issue that old meters have.
Mr. Kargl said that the outside meter would slow down and fail — but as soon as the meter was changed
it was the same so the old meter was working fine and communicating property with the outside box.

Mr. Mingo agreed that when a meter fails that it typically slows down so it is the Town that takes the hit
on the water usage reading.
Mr. Kargl said that was correct.

Mr. Bond asked if there is any situation where a meter would behave this way.

Mr. Kargl said no and that was why they waited and tested it so much here. He added that the 1 in 10
adjustment is not that forgiving with a leaking toilet as they do not do an adjustment on the sewer side
so the water difference would be rather disappointing.

Mr. Bond asked what the 14,000 gallons would amount to.

Ms. Johnson said that it was actually 12,000 gallons.

**MOTION (3)
Mr. Bond moved to credit 12,000 gallons of water on the next bill.

Mr. Nickerson seconded the motion.

Vote: 2-6-0. Motion failed.

8. Sewer Project Updates

= Niantic Pump Station Emergency Upgrades

Mr. Kargl went over the expenditures to date of $52,706.25 and the additional project expenses as
provided by Weston & Sampson.

Mr. Spencer said that he did not see any incentive payment from Eversource for going to a cost
effective pump and would like to see it listed there by Weston & Sampson.

Mr. Kargl and Mr. Bragaw said that they have requested that of Weston & Sampson and that they will
request it again.

Ms. Russell asked how the Eversource Incentive Program works.

Mr. Bragaw explained that they figure out how much they will save based upon what was previously
being used. He said that they would lump it in with the other projects that we have in Town and they will
send a check for that amount. It is straight dollars. Weston & Sampson has to give Eversource the data.
They will ask them again to do that.

= Cini Park Water and Sewer Connections
Mr. Kargl said that they are looking at this like running a ‘service’ (both water and sewer) for the
bathrooms and having them operate on a seasonal basis.

Mr. Bragaw further explained that the State also wants to re-pave Route 156 which adds another
dimension to this. The plan would be for a portable trailer system so that they could be hauled out in the
event of a major storm. This would help with the FEMA related issues that they are facing.

Mr. Bond asked who is paying for this.
Mr. Bragaw said that they are doing it with a STEAP Grant.

11. Safe Drinking Water Assessment Fee

Mr. Kargl said that the most recent State budget included $2.5M to be paid by water companies to
secure EPA designated primacy. This comes to $3 to $4 per customer and will be charged to the
customer — they can put it in with the bill. He suggested that they would want to look at this also with
regard to the rates as it will affect the customers. It is some $25,000 and we have 6500 customers.
