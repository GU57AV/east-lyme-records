8. Draft Sewer Regulations
Mr. Nickerson said that Attorney Zamarka was present to update them on this.

Attorney Zamarka said that he had provided the Commissioners (only) with a cleaned up draft copy and
proceeded to note the changes that had been made. He suggested that in Section 4.8 — 4.10 that it
read July 1, 2017 forward rather than retroactively.

Discussion ensued regarding whether or not to leave it as an ordinance or change it to a regulation. As
an ordinance the penalty fee would be allowed and it also adds another level of over sight. It was noted
that in the 25 years that the ordinance has been in existence that there has been no need to fine
anyone.

Ms. Russell asked if during that time there were situations where a fine could have been levied or if it
reached that level.
Mr. Kargl said no.

Ms. Russell said that she was also considering what if there was ‘buyer's remorse’ if they changed it
from an ordinance to a regulation.

Attorney Zamarka said that if they move forward with the regulation then they would simply repeal the
ordinance and it would not be necessary to hold a Public Hearing. If they keep it as an ordinance then it
adds another layer to it.

Mr. Mingo recalled that as an ordinance that the levy of the fine is under the purview of the Board of
Selectmen.

Attorney Zamarka said yes, as an ordinance once the Board of Selectmen grants them the power to
levy a fine, the Commission can levy it.

Mr. Zoller asked if they adopted it as a regulation if the paragraph on the $100 fine would come out.
Attorney Zamarka said yes.

Mr. Nickerson asked Mr. Kargl what happens if this is a regulation and they have a violation — what is
the leverage on this.

Mr. Kargl said that would lie on the Certificate of Occupancy — that is the leverage — it would not be
issued.

Mr. Bragaw noted that a $100 fine does not mean anything to a developer.

Ms. Russell asked what happens if something has to go to the courts and if a regulation weakens our
position.
Attorney Zamarka said that it would not make any difference.

Mr. Jacques asked if the difference between a regulation and ordinance factors into Section 8 —
Penalties.
Attorney Zamarka said yes, section 8.3 would have to be removed.

Attorney Zamarka asked what their pleasure was — to move forward as a regulation or keep it as an
ordinance.

= Well 1A and 6 Treatment Plant Modifications and Upgrades

Mr. Kargl reported that they submitted the eligibility application to the DPH for DWSRF loan funding for

registering issues are being worked on.
