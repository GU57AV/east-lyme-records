EAST LYME WATER AND SEWER COMMISSION

PUBLIC HEARING ON SEWER RATES

Present: Kevin Seery, Actirig Chairman, Steve DiGiovanna, Dave Jacques, Joe

Also Present: Joe Bragaw, Public Works Director

1, Call to Order. Acting Chairman Seery called the October 24, 2017 Public Hearing on
Sewer Rates to order at 6:40 p.m.

Mr. Seery reported the proposed water rates will be a 5% increase. Neighboring towns
water rates were checked on and it was felt our rates are lower than neighboring towns.
See attached talking points from Mr. Bragaw.

Mr. Seery called for comments from the public.

Wes Firman of 178 Boston Post Road asked where are the cuts to the Water and Sewer
Department coming from? Mr. Bragaw replied we are four months into the year and
have delayed programs to help reduce rates.

John Drabik of 18 Drabik Road stated four years ago we looked into Lake Kanomic. We
were required to hire two engineers. He asked how many engineers do we have at the
present time? He felt the blue collar workers are doing a good job, but felt upper
management needs to be cut.

Mr. Seery stated the only full time engineer the town has is Brad Kargle. We also have
Bill Sheer 25% of the time. He is working on our water supply report. A consultant
would be much more. We are barely keeping up with the projects we have.

John Drabik felt there is a management problem. Many people are moving out of town.
The increase is unfair.

MOTION (1): Mr. DiGiovanna moved to close the Public

Hearing on Sewer Rates at 6:50 p.m.
Seconded by Mr. Zoller. (8-0) Unanimous.

PROPOSED FY 17-18 SEWER RATES

Prepared for the W&S Commission Rate Hearing on 10/24/17

By Joe Bragaw, Director of Public Works

TALKING POINTS

1,

The operating budget (revenue and expenditures) is approved in April and any proposed changes to rate
structure are voted on at the October W&S meeting. Any new rates become effective as of November
an

At the April 2017 W&S meeting, the FY 17-18 sewer budget was approved with a 1.16% decrease over
the adopted FY 16/17 budget. At that time, we had the following assumptions;

a. We indicated to the W&S Commission that even with a 1.16% decrease in our operating budget;
we may be looking at a rate increase of around 6% for most customers and a 10% increase to our
top tier (over 2,500,000 gallons used over a 6 month period). These increases in rates were
projected based on declining flows and revenues from our biggest customer, the state prisons.

b. We assumed flat water usage. The November 2017 billing was 12.76% less than the November
2016 billing. There was no way for us to predict a 12.76% decrease as this was mainly driven by
a wet May thru early summer.

c. The prison and town metered flows make up 96% of sewer revenues, so if those revenues are
down it has a major impact on our departmental cash flow and budget.

d. Our system is well over 20 years old without a major upgrade to date. Because of this,
maintenance costs consume a sizable amount of our budget but they are necessary to keeping the
system operational.

The largest water customer in our system, the State prisons has seen metered sewer flows drop by a
significant amount of the last few years. Over the last two years, the revenue from the prisons has gone
from $638k in FY15-16 to $431k in FY 16-17 as their prison population has decreased. This drop of
over $207k in the last two years is putting significant pressure on the rest of our operating budget. We
are seeing savings in the costs we have to pay for less flows (i.e. less electric and NL and Waterford
treatment costs) and that is why we were able to reduce the operating budget by 1.16% this year even
though we had increases in contractual and benefits costs.

We have been trying to save money anywhere we can and so far this year we are doing pretty well, so at
this time we are proposing only a 3% rate increase instead of the 6% rate increase we predicted back in
April. To get to the 3% rate increase, we will need to further cut the existing operating budget by $63k
to balance projected revenue with expenditures. This will be difficult to do but is necessary to help
contain rising rate costs for our customers. If we had the same rate increase for all customers and didn’t
cut the existing budget, it would take an 11.6% rate increase across the board to balance the budget. Last
November we created a top tier user (over 2,500,000 gallons per 6 months) and we are proposing to
increase this top tier by 10%.

Besides sewer usage, we are proposing 5% rate increases to the application fees and 5% rate increases to
all service fees that are tied to increases we have in labor and benefits.
