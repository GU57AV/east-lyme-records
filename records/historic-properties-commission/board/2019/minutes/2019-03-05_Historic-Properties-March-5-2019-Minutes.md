                         EAST LYME TOWN HALL

CORRESPONDENCE: None

OLD BUSINESS: Steve pointed out that the terms "no news or no report" imply that
things are not getting done when it fact incremental progress is being made but the
issues are complex and a report to the commission is premature at the time. Agreed
that we will state "under discussion" or " to be continued" rather than "no report" going
forward.

a   Budget: Selectmen meeting Wednesday, March 6 at 7PM. lncludes $400 for town
    resource digitization and $2500 for Samuel Smith Homestead.

o   Historic Properties / Library Collaboration: Barb is working with East Lyme
    Public Library Director Lisa Timothy to develop a plan for digitally organizing our
    historic resources. She and Lisa are writing two CT Humanities grants for this
    purpose. The first is a capacity building grant. The second is a quick grant for the
    purpose of developing a short "story corps" style video and physical exhibit in
    celebration of the 50'n anniversary of East Lyme High School.

POCD Update: The Historic Properties team discussed East Lyme's historic property
needs over the next ten years and makes the fotlowing tentative recommendations to
POCD to address them.:
o  Combine the town Planning Commission and the town Zoning Commissions to
   reduce / remove duplication of effort and resolve conflicts between the two groups in
   a more timely and efficient manner than exists under the two-commission structure.
.  lnclude the town's historic properties / museums in its marketing program.

. Consider signage designating "historic neighborhoods" in areas where there is a
   concentration of older homes ( Smith Street / Smith Avenue/ Niantic Village,
   Crescent Beach, Flanders Baptist Church areas, for example)
.  Hire a grant writer.
Steve Marks-Hamilton has agreed to represent the HP Commission on the POCD
Review team this year.

NEW BUSINESS: None

REPORTS

o Bike and BBQ: M. Christensen reported that the new bike route is mapped out, and
    that they currently have three sponsors. The event will again be held at the
     Brookside Farm Museum. Parks and Recreation Department still needs to clarify in
    writing whether the $5.00 per head fee is inclusive of - or in addition to - any
    charges for labor and materials prior to and on the day of the event.
o    Brookside Farm Museum-Budget presentation tomorrow night.
o   Gemetery Association - Working on fixing headstones at Old Stone Church
    Cemetery.
o   The Samuel Smith House -Budget presentation tomorrow night. Two Central CT
    State University Public History students are making posters for SSF's participation in
    the Connecticut Community lnvestment Act Lobby Day in April. Working on
    obtaining written proposals for restoration of house and barn. Working with First
    Selectman to be able to accrue annual funding year to year and to thereby address
    more expensive repairs than are possible with our $2500 annual allotment.
o   Thomas Lee House: Researching Lee family at Lyme Town Hall has revealed that
    Thomas Lee was given 40 acres in 1673.
o   National Register of Historic Properties / Historic House Inventory - Town has
    over 200 buildings built prior to 1900 though not all are in reflective of the period. As
    many are concentrated in either the Smith Street / Smith Avenue area, the Crescent
    Beach area or the Flanders School area, HP team suggests the Town might
    consider naming these areas as historic neighborhoods, but not historic districts, as
    this term may carry with it architectural/ construction restrictions.
.   Flow Chart - in process.
.   Destination Town Brochure - in process.
