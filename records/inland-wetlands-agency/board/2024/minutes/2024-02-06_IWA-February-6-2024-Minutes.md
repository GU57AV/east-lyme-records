    A. Roles and Responsibilities of Local Land Use Officials.
         Mark Zamarka, Town Land Use Attorney addressed the Agency on their roles and
         responsibilities. He said the Agency is a statutory creation of the state and derive their powers
         from state statute and their regulations.
         There are five functions of the Wetlands Agency
            o Pass and amend regulations.
            o Determine boundaries, of inland wetland and water courses, URA.
            o Rules on permits for regulated activities
            o Enforce regulations.
            o Advisory function
         There are six types of activities that are exempt from wetlands review.
            o Agricultural review
            o Residential home on a subdivision lot that was approved prior to IWA regulations.
            o Boat anchorage and moorings
            o Some uses that are incidental to the enjoyment and maintenance of residential
              property
            o Construction of commercial or municipal water supply systems
            o Maintenance of water pipes on residentially owned lands which became effective before
              IWA regulations.

         The jurisdiction of the agency is limited to regulated activities in wetlands or watercourses or
         operations that affect them as defined by the statute. The jurisdiction also extends to the URA,
         (Upland Review Area). East Lyme has a 300 ft. URA. According to the Connecticut Supreme
         Court, you can regulate outside of the wetlands or the URA only those activities that are likely
         to affect the physical characteristics of the wetlands or watercourses. The jurisdiction does not
         extend to any wildlife that may use or be supported by those wetlands.
         M. Zamarka explained the timelines for applications.
         The acceptance of an application is either the first meeting after the application is brought to
         the town hall or within 35 days, whichever comes first according to statute 8-7d.
         A public hearing must be held within 65 days of the day of receipt. A public hearing is held if
         25 or more people petition for a public hearing, there is significant impact to the wetlands or
         public interest in the application. The public hearing must be completed within 35 days unless
         the applicant asks for an extension. Once the public hearing is closed the Agency has 65 days
         to make a decision. If the Agency fails to act there is no inferred approval.
         At the public hearing the applicant presents the application, the Agency can ask questions of
         the applicant. The public has an opportunity to speak to the application. Once the hearing is
         closed the Agency can no longer take testimony or receive documentation unless it is from
         town staff.
         If there is a member of the Agency who has specialized knowledge which pertains to the
         application, they must inform the public and applicant on the record at the beginning of the
         public hearing.
         A member can be disqualified from hearing an application if they have a predetermined
         opinion on the application. If the member has a conflict of interest such as a financial conflict
         they should recuse themselves.
         M. Zamarka stated an impact on the URA is not a sufficient reason to deny an application, the
         impact must be to the wetlands. Any reason for denying an application must have reasons
         stated on the record. If experts testiff, their testimony cannot be disregarded without some
         factual basis in the record to rebut the testimony.
