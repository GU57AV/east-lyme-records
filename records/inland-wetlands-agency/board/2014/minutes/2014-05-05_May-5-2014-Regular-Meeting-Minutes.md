V. Pending Applications

A. Application of the Town of East Lyme, for Atlantic Street Culvert/Drainage Improvements in the
area of Little Indian Pond

Ms. Lozanov called upon Mr. Benni, Town Engineer to explain this.

Mr. Benni said that the plan/proposal is to do a pipe upgrade from Little Indian Pond to LI Sound. The pipe

that is currently there is a 15” corrugated pipe that has historically been clogged by vegetation, silt and

debris. It also has angle points in it that allow debris to be trapped and it may also have breaks due to the

age of it. The new pipe will be 24” and will be fitted with a rash rack that will prevent debris from entering and

clogging the storm drainage system and help with the removal of sand and silt. It will not be able to stop any

of the flooding from LI sound but they do hope that it will allow the area to drain out much faster from the

flooding. The project is for 25° of pipe replacement and 116’ of pipe following the pre-existing pipe. He said

that the permit review for the outlet point at LI sound will be considered by the CT DEEP through a LI Sound

application and the Amy Corps of Engineer will also provide comment in that application process.

Ms. Lozanov asked the time frame for the work to be done.
Mr. Benni said that they would probably do it in late August to fall but it also depends upon the work being
done at #22 Atlantic as they want them to finish as they have to work in that area.

Ms. Lozanov asked how long it would take to do the work.
Mr. Benni said that it would probably take a couple of days but they are hoping to remain connected to the
existing pipe as they are working on the new one.

Mr. Mingo said that he does not think that they have jurisdiction here.
Mr. Goeschel said that he thinks that they do down from the pond — however they have already issued a
permit for work in that pond area previously.

Mr. Hall said that he thought that they should get approval from the DEEP first and then come here — as he
was concemed about what would happen if they should not approve their permit.

A. Application for Determination of Permitted/Non-Regulated Activity at property at 249 Old Black
Point Road for wetlands restoration/enhancement and site improvements including new
buildings

Mr. Goeschel said that Mr. Fortunato is present this evening to explain this.

Don Fortunato, Soil Scientist, Plants Dam Road, East Lyme recalled that at their last meeting that Mr. Mingo
had said that what they are proposing was an ‘as of right’ use. They plan to get rid of the phragmites, dig out
the mucky soil and plant with a wetland mix. The area is 3200 sq. ft. and they will provide a report on the
area for five years. Machnik would be doing the work and they will provide a letter regarding NOT re-using
the material that is removed here as top soil in some other location.

Mr. Mingo said that he still thinks that this is an ‘as of right’ use and not a big deal. He added that if they do it
during the summer that they would probably have to irrigate it to get it going.

Mr. Goeschel said that his assessment previously was that it needed a permit — but there is a section in the
regulations that does relate to if it is ‘significant’ or not.

Mr. Mingo said that 3200 sq. ft. is not significant.
Mr. Fortunato said that the material is coming out but wetland material is going back in.

Ms. Lozanov asked if they think that this is significant or not.
Mr. Clarke said that he would not see it as significant and that he would see it as a landscaping maintenance

item.

**MOTION (8)

Mr. Mingo moved regarding the property at 249 Old Black Point Road for wetlands
restoration/enhancement that the work being done is ‘insignificant’ in nature and falls under
landscaping maintenance and is an ‘as of right’ use not requiring a permit.

Mr. Clarke seconded the motion.

Mr. Fortunato said that he would update them on this with yearly reports for five years.

Vote: 6-0-1. Motion passed.
