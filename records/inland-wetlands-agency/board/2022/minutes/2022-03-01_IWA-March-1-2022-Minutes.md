       A. Land Use 101-Brief overview of Inland Wetlands Agency Statutory Roles and Responsibilities,
          etc., Town Attorney Mark S. Zamarka, Esq.
          M. Zamarka informed the members they are a uealion of state statute and get their powers from the
          statutes and their regulations. The basic premise of the Wetlands Agency is to supervise regulated
          activities which is described in section 2.1 of the regulations. Section 2.1 also describes what is a
          wetland and a watercourse.
          The Agency performs five basic functions:
              o Passes and amends regulations
              o Determines the boundaries of wetlands and watercourses
              o Passes on permits to conduct regulated activities
              o Enforces wetlands regulations
              o Occasionally makes advisory reports
          M. Zamarka also explained, "as of right" activities to the Agency. He explained the jurisdiction of the
          agency which includes the Upland Review Area (URA), which at this time is 300' fi'om the
          watercourse boundary and is under judicial review. The agency can regulate the URA only when
          activities adversely affect the physical characteristics of the wetlands or watercourses, an effect on the
          wildlife is not sufficient to regulate within that area, it must affect the wetlands or watercourse.

       When an application comes before the Agency there are timelines the Agency needs to follow. The date
       of receipt is the next regularly scheduled meeting or 35 days after it is received in the office.
       The Agency is not required to hold a Public Hearing but may do so under three circumstances:
           . Significant impact on wetlands
           o A petition of 25 residents calling for a public hearing
           o Public interest
       During a Public Hearing the applicant usually goes first, then the public is given a chance to speak, the
       applicant can respond to what was said during public comment. The Agency has an opportunity to ask
       questions during the Public Hearing. A member must disquali$' themselves if they have a
       predetermination or a conflict of interest.
       M. Zamarka stated that unlike other land use agencies, if the Wetlands Agency does not decide on an
       application during the statutory time frames, the application is not considered approved, there is no,
       "inferred approval."
       Any decision the Agency makes must be supported by substantial evidence provided on the record
       during the public hearing or applicationprocess. The only information which can be considered after
       the public hearing is closed is information given by staff. The decision of the Agency must be stated for
       the record and take into consideration six factors:
           . Environmental impact
           o Prudent and feasible alternatives considered
           o Short term and long-term impacts of the regulated activities
           o Irreversible and irretrievable loss of wetland or watercourse resources which would be caused
               by the proposed regulated activity,
           o the character and degree of injury to, or interference with, safety, health or the reasonable use of
               property which is caused or threatened by the proposed regulated activity
           o impacts of the proposed regulated activity on wetlands or watercourses outside the area for
               which the activity is proposed and future activities associated with or reasonably related to, the
               proposed regulated activity which are made inevitable by the proposed regulated activity, and
               which may have an impact on wetlands and watercourses.
       M. Zamarka informed the members that as an Agency of the Town they are subject to FOIA rules and
       regulations and should avoid discussing Agency business outside of the meeting and not to use text or
       email for communication.

        general permit to do maintenance work on town owned rights of way was in 2006 and have been
        renewing the permit since then. He said the permit has now run out and they are putting in a new
        permit that is the same as the old one and modeled on the state DOT general permit. A. Klose said

           o Excavation of accumulated sediment or removal of brush from the inlet and outlet sides of a
              drainage pipe or bridge
           o Cleaning or reshaping of a man-made drainage ways or sediment basins.
