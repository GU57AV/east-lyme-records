MOTToN (1)

the current circumstances.

MOTTON (2)
Mr. Vilcheck MOVED to increase Mr. Olenick's rent by $450, adjusted rent is $1,500 as of
April 1, 2025, through July 1, 2025;
Seconded by Mr. Bekech. Motion passed 4-0.

DISCUSSION: Ms, Mountzoures testified that this large of an increase would significantly
impact her ability to pay her other bills and buy groceries.

Mr. Horgan MOVED to raise Ms. Mountzoures'rent to $1650 or $1700 per month. MOTION
WITHDRAWN.

MOTTON (3)

January I,2025 through December 31,2025. The retroactive credit of $600 should be paid
to Alpha Niantic LLC over a three month period,
Seconded by Mr. Horgan. Motion passed 4-0.

Synthesi$ sf and Alphil Capital Funds
                              Synthesis of Alpha's Business Strategy
Alpha refers to their property investment strategy as "off-market, value-add multifamily
properties." In practice this means: (1) Alpha targets properties owned by local "mom-and-pop"
landlords who have kept rents at a reasonable price (which those owners are able to do because
they either own the property outright or it is well financed); (2) they pursue those owners,
pushing them to sell; (3) they purchase that property for a certain amount, believing it is
undervalued; (3) then they raise rents to increase the income of the property, and, in turn, its
value on paper; and (4) finally they either sell it offat a higher price or refinance for a higher
loan amount.


As evidenced by the post below, this investment strategy was guiding the decision to purchase
468 Main Street, East Lyme. Alpha purchased 468 Main Street from a local landlord for $8.4
million. The property was not on the market, they pursued the owner for two years. ThAy
financed the purchase of 468 Main Street with a 24-monlh bridge loan of $7.75 million with an
interest rate of l0.gg%. They targeted this property because the existing rents were low. That is,
they believed the property to be undervalued and explicitly planned to raise rents exorbitantly
upon taking ownership to increase the property:s incorne. This is also why they took out a
high-risk, high-interest loan. Their intent, after increasing the property's income solely by
increasing rents, is to refinance for a higher loan amount (pocketing the difference) or sell.

Instead of putting more money down up fron! Alpha is shifting the cost of financing their
purchase to tenants - this is not appropriate. Because when they tum around and sell or refinance
for a higher loan and pocket the difference, tenants are not getting any of that investment value.
Alpha does not have an operating expense issue, they have a purchasing expense issue resulting
from their own intentional strategy - as such, we believe the FRC should give less weight to the
element of debt service in circumstance 7 of the considerations laid out in 7-148c.

Background

Alpha Capital understands tenant concerns but wants to provide the full context of its mission
and business approach. We are a small Connecticut-based company, not a corporate giant, with
deep community ties and a commitment to fair, responsible property management. Founder
Tyler Smith, a North Haven native, built Alpha Capital from the ground up after facing personal
setbacks, driven by a passion for real estate as a means to serve others.

Since 2017, Alpha Capital has grown from one building to 364 units, reinvesting locally to
improve housing and support community initiatives like toy drives and school supply programs
Unlike large, out-of-state firms, we operate with transparency, maintain fair rents, and prioritize
tenant needs. While we don't control market rents, we keep renewal rates below market when
possible and ensure well-maintained properties.

Alpha Capital has actively sought to expand affordable and senior housing but has faced zoning
resistance, despite Connecticut's housing challenges. Municipal collaboration is essentialto
address this issue. Claims that we are part of a "greedy landlord" narrative are inaccurate-we
strive to balance affordability with sustainability,

Regarding rent increases, we inherited a property where previous owners kept rents artificially
low while selling at today's market value. lnstead of imposing abrupt changes, we engaged
tenants, offered flexibility, and implemented gradual adjustments. Most tenants found no more
affordable alternatives, and many chose to stay. Our rent strategy considers market conditions,
property improvements, and fair rental practices.

Alpha Capital remains dedicated to fair housing, responsible ownership, and community
engagement. We will outline how our approach aligns with East Lyme's fair rent guidelines and
why our rental adjustments are necessary and justified.




Rental Comparables & Market Realities

Attached are rental comparables in East Lyme, CT, showing that our renewal rates remain
below market. Nine new tenants currently rent one-bedroom units at $1,900+ per month. These
apartments offer central air, balconies, an elevator, and a secure building in a prime
location-market-rate, not subsidized or deed-restricted. Please refer to exhibits E through M.

Affordable housing, per Connecticut laq applies to government-assisted or deed-restricted units
for those spending 30o/o or less of their income on rent. ln East Lyme, the area median income
(AMl) is $96,023, making 80% AMI $76,818 and 50% AMI $48,011. AHEPA Senior Living, a
subsidized complex, charges $1 ,200-$1 ,250 monthly. A 10% increase on a $1 ,100 rent still falls
below subsidized housing rates and does not cover maintenance costs.

The Nonruich-New London AMI is $75,000, with 80% AMI at $60,000 and 50% AMI at $37,500.
Low-income rental rates are unsustainable without government support. Programs like the
Low-lncome Housing Tax Credit (LIHTC) and Section 8 exist because very-low-income rents do
not generate enough revenue to cover expenses like taxes, insurance, labor, and maintenance.

East Lyme has failed to meet Connecticut's 10% affordable housing target, shifting the burden
onto private landlords without providing tax breaks or incentives. While the state acknowledges
affordable housing needs government support, East Lyme expects private landlords to fill the
gap at a financial loss. This approach leads to declining housing quality, reduced private
investment, and exacerbated shortages.

Sustainable rental rates are essential for maintaining properties, ensuring quality housing, and
encouraging investment. Rent caps mirroring very-low-income rates without incentives are
impractical and unfair. Fair rent laws should balance tenant protections with landlords' ability to
sustain their propedies, making reasonable returns while keeping housing in good condition.
