2.      Training Session by Connecticut Legal Services

Ms. Konakowitz stated that tonight's meeting is solely for training and informational
purposes, and that there is no public comment or dialogue on tonight's agenda. She
introduced the presenters; Raphael Podolsky, Esg., Connecticut Legal Services and Sarah
White, Esq., Connecticut Fair Housing Center. They presented information on the Fair Rent
Commission to assist our members in understanding the process and their roles. Their
presentation is attached hereto as Exhibit #1. The full video of the presentation and
subsequent questions and answers segment can be found at Videos - 2025 - Town of East
Lyme. Some of the issues discussed:
-    Can a tenant submit a complaint before they receive a notice to raise rent?

     In the case of a perceived increase, the tenant must wait until the increase notice is
     received; notification can come in any form such as verbal, written, electronic means.
     However, if a landlord has made some sort of announcement that rents will be going up
     across the board, but you have not received your notice personally, that tenant is safe
     to assume that they will also receive notice of increase so may file a complaint. It was
     noted that the Commission's role will be to hear complaints about rents in general, and
     not always necessarily because of an increase; it could be because the elevator hasn't
     been working, or any issues with the rental property itself. The final note is that in the
     case of an increase, it is important to file the complaint as soon as possible before the
     increase takes effect.

     What information should the FRC request from the landlord and tenant in preparation for
     the public hearing?

     The FRC should request both the landlord and tenant bring as much information with
     them to the hearing to help you decide; including but not limited to, income, expenses,
     landlord's operating expenses, etc.

     a fixed income,  etc.?

     How long does the FRC have to decide a case?

     It is expected that the case will be decided that same night at the public hearing. Cases
     should not be continued to a future public hearing if at all possible.

     What if there are multiple renters complaining about the same landlord?

     Since the landlord will most likely have a similar response for all tenants, it will make
     more send to include all renters from a single landlord complaint to the same public
     hearing. It would be helpful if the tenants could pool together and articulate what issues
     apply to all tenants involved and have one representative speak on that. The
     commission may also request that an individual's case be heard on its own for more
     information.

     What happens if one of the parties does not show up to the public hearing?

     The public hearing should move forward if the commission wants to regardless if either
     the tenant or landlord does not show up. The FRC also has the right to contact the
     missing party to give them an opportunity to attend or reschedule,

     What happens if the landlord and tenant reach an agreement before the public hearing
     date?

     The parties should devise a written agreement and have that signed off on by the Fair
     Rent Commission. For this step, neither party needs to be at the meeting in order for
     the Commission to accept their agreement, The tenant could also withdraw the
     complaint, but this may lead to problems down the road if either party does not hold
     their end of the agreement. If the complaint is withdrawn the renter will have no
     recourse except to start over, If an agreement is made during the public hearing itself,
     that agreement should be ratified by the FRC at that meeting.
