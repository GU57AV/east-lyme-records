A. Discussion re: Presentation on the Draft 2014/2015 Budget for East Lyme Public Schools
Dr. Lombardo gave a PowerPoint presentation of the draft 2014/2015 Budget for East
Lyme Public Schools. A total budget of $43,989,614; representing a 4.9% increase over the
current-year budget was presented as follows:

0.88% - Federal Mandates (Affordable Care Act - $372,000)

1.08% - State Mandates (Administrator & Teacher Evaluation Plans -
$296,587) and new initiatives

2.92% - General Budget

4.88% Total Budget increase for 2014-2015

The average budget increase between FY 11 and FY 15 is 2.4%.

= The budget priorities align with the district’s long range plan.

= The major increases in the budget are reflected in salaries, benefits and insurance,
natural gas, technology lease, support for evaluation, immediate projects, and
maintenance.

Staff changes include the following:

Enrollment reflects a projected overall district decrease of 25 students.

The Board will continue discussion and deliberations and expect to adopt a final budget
proposal NLT March 10. The adopted budget will then be forwarded to the Board of
Finance. Board Chair Tim Hagen invited the public to submit questions and comments to
elps.budget@eastlymeschools.org. The PowerPoint presentation, budget questions,

clarifications and replies will continue to be posted to the website.

. Receive public comment relative to the draft 2014-2015 school district budget

1.

Mike Schultz, Lovers Lane, welcomed the new Board members and thanked all members
for their service. Mr. Schultz questioned whether the quality of education in the
classroom is really compromised if class sizes are increased. He requested an update
regarding the status of the change in medical insurance carriers and asked why there is an
increase in insurance costs. He suggested the District pass some of the extra-curricular
costs onto parents of participating students. He stated the contractual obligations for
personnel extend too far out, and suggested this be considered in future negotiations.

Dr. Lombardo stated the budgeted insurance costs are based on the existing provider. The
proposed switch to Aetna will result in a cost savings to the district. The uncertainty
surrounding the Affordable Care Act and the unfunded, state-mandated evaluation process
present a challenge regarding budget planning.
