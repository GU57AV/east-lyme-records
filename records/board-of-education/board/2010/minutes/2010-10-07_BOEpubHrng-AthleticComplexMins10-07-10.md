III. DISCUSSION/ACTION ITEMS
     A. Presentation of the Proposed East Lyme High School Athletic Complex
        Chairman Tim Hagen began the meeting by providing background information to the public.
        In May, Gale Associates was contracted to provide the Board of Education with a
        feasibility/preliminary design study of the proposed East Lyme High School Athletic
        Complex. The study includes options for a possible artificial turf field, track and bleacher
        renovations and updated lighting. Several meetings were held with Dr. Lombardo, the
        Athletic Complex Renovation Committee and Gale Associates, Inc. The results, conclusions
        and recommendations of the study were jointly presented to the Board of Education on
        September 27 by Bill Seymour, P.E. of Gale Associates and Athletic Complex Renovation
        Committee Chair Carl Reichard. The presentation was repeated and the Board invited the
        public to comment. No action was taken as the board continues deliberations. Further
        discussion will take place at future board meetings. The board recognized and thanked
        committee members Carl Reichard (Chair), Kevin Seery (BOE member), Tim Hagen (BOE
        member), Dr. Lombardo (Superintendent), Don Meltabarger (ELPS Finance and Facilities
        Director), Mike Susi (HS Principal), Jack Biggs (former HS Athletic Director), Steve Hargis
        (HS Athletic Director), Dave Putnam (EL Parks and Recreation), and John Rhodes (Town
        Building Committee).

      B. Receive Public Comment Relative to the Proposed East Lyme High School Athletic Project
         1. Joe Mingo, 397 Boston Post Road, reviewed the history of the bleacher, press box and
            lighting projects as well as lumber replacement and upgrades noting contributions from
            the ELHS band parents. He acknowledged the need for facility upgrades; however,
            expressed concerns regarding economic constraints. He questioned the species of grass
            to be used on a natural turf field and noted that artificial turf requires regular disinfecting.

2. Judy Engel, 3 Spring Rock Road, spoke in support of the project and made reference to
    child obesity concerns and the importance of keeping children active and the importance
    it plays on their well being. She also supports community fund raising events to help
    with the costs of the project.
3. Wayne Wondell, 13 Stones Ranch Road, spoke in support of a multi-use facility. He
    expressed safety concerns regarding band practices in the parking lot.
4. Ruth Reluga, 10 Bronson Street, of the EL Commission for the Conservation of Natural
    Resources, submitted a written list of questions and concerns related to the synthetic turf
    portion of the proposal and asked the Board that additional environmental studies and
    “green choice” considerations be made before pursuing the project. She also referred to a
    letter to Former Board of Education Chair Kevin Seery from the Commission dated
    November 22, 2009 re: the proposed artificial turf and runoff.
5. Bruce Cohen, 41 Cardinal Road, President of the EL Lacrosse Association, submitted a
    letter in support of the synthetic turf field and expressed concerns regarding the non user
    friendly and deteriorating bleachers, as well as the poor track and field conditions. A
    new facility will foster community spirit and will provide a vision for East Lyme.
6. John Rhodes, 6 Seebeck Road, spoke in support of the project and noted an artificial turf
    field will provide the town the equivalent of 2-3 fields. As a facilities supervisor in
    another district, he noted the present facilities have reached their life expectancy and
    stressed the economical reasoning of a new lighting system. He commended Track
    Coach Carl Reichard for his work.
7. Shawn Greeley, 11 Scott Road, President of the EL Youth Soccer Program, spoke in
    support of moving the project to the next step. His concerns included the wear and tear
    the high school athletic teams are taking on the Bridebrook fields, as well as the need for
    a practice area for the band. He also noted safety issues with the current track.
8. Rich Hansen, 17 Romania Road, recommended moving ahead with the project and asked
    the board to consider more efficient lights for extending public use of the track.
9. Fran Shields, 30 Parker Drive, informed the Board of his first hand knowledge of the CT
    College turf field which was installed in June 2005. To date there have been no issues
    with bacteria or heat. The artificial turf maximizes student athlete participation and he
    applauded the town for understanding the need for this type of field. His experience has
    shown a decline in injuries, elimination of game cancellations due to field conditions and
    encourages East Lyme to invest in excellence by providing a cutting edge facility for its
    students.
10. Alison Woods, 4 Ferro Court, supported wholeheartedly the project as presented. She
    expressed concerns regarding offsite practices and encouraged the community to invest
    in town assets, our kids and our community. She also encouraged the Board to research
    and pursue funding opportunities, and as a taxpayer, she is willing to pay for the facility
    in the many years to come.
11. Rajesh Phanabal, 21 Goldfish Terrace, ELHS drum major, advocated for the project as
    presented and spoke of safety concerns for band members practicing on the parking lot as
    well as limited time to practice on the field. He also expressed concerns regarding the
    bleachers, track and fence.
12. Mark Nickerson, 15 Jean Drive, Board of Selectmen, had no opinion on the subject;
    however, encouraged the Board to send the project to the taxpayers for decision.
13. Steven Hinkle, 21 Dean Road, HS track student, spoke in support of the project as
    presented. He spoke of track and fencing safety issues and how the town would benefit
    from a new facility.
14. Jerry Fortier, 13 North Ledge Rock Road, informed the Board that the same type of
    project was presented to the Board five years ago at a much lower cost; however, now is
    the time to act while the rates are low.
