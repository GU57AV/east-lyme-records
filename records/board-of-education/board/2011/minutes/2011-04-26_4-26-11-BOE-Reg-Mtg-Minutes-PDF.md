. Discussion re: FY 12 Budget Process Update

Initial discussion took place regarding the Board of Finance’s reduction of $460,000 to the Board of
Education’s proposed FY 12 budget. Discussions continue among administrators on where to cut the budget
if we need to. New information has been received resulting in reductions in health insurance (approximately
$130,000), reducing the FY 12 budget to a 1.44% increase. The Board of Finance suggested that technology
items be removed from the general fund budget and transferred to the capital fund budget to be financed by a
5-year lease. The budget process was described as a collaborative effort between the Boards’ of Education,
Selectmen, and Finance, that has resulted in a limited impact on students and taxpayers. The town
referendum is scheduled for May 19 and reductions are expected to be identified at the May 23 Board of

Education meeting.

. Proposed Steps to Develop a Consensus on the Best Solution to Address ELPS Facilities Needs

The Finance, Facilities and Organizational (FFO) Committee recently reviewed a rough outline and timeline
for proposed steps to develop a town consensus on the direction to proceed to address the long term facilities
needs of the East Lyme Public Schools based on the district’s feasibility report. The Facilities Vision
Committee would be established and charged with developing and presenting to the Board of Education a
vision and a plan to address the identified facilities needs of the district. Committee membership would
include Board of Education representatives, community members, parents, teachers, and administrative
representatives from the school district and the town. The committee would provide its recommendations to
the Board in February 2012 in order to accommodate budget and capital planning for the 2012-2013 fiscal
year. Robust discussion ensued with suggestions for town inclusiveness of the process, tapping the
experience from the EL Middle School project, ensuring the committee has close communication with the
Board of Education, consideration for the elementary students, and interweaving the vision, long range plan

and the work of the task forces.

Al Littlefield moved to establish the ELPS Facilities Committee to develop and present to the Board of
Education a plan on the best solution to address the East Lyme Public Schools facilities needs; Marlene

Nickerson seconded.

4. Discussion re: Presentation of New England Association of Schools and Colleges ASC) Report for
East Lyme High School — November 7 — 10, 2010 Visit
ELHS Principal Mike Susi reviewed the three-fold NEASC accreditation process to include: 1) the self-study
conducted by the local professional staff; 2) the on-site evaluation conducted by the Commission on Public
Secondary Schools visiting committee; and 3) the follow-up carried out by the school to implement the
findings of its own self-study and the valid recommendations of the visiting committee and those identified

by the Commission in the follow-up process.

Following the on-site evaluation conducted by the Commission on Public Secondary Schools visiting
committee, the NEASC Report was prepared and submitted to the district/school. It is a professional
appraisal of the school as it appeared to the visiting team and it provides a blueprint for the administration,
faculty and officials to use to improve the quality of programs and services for the students in East Lyme
High School. The committee found the quality of education and standards very high, despite the significant
flux in leadership. It commended the contributions of the entire faculty and staff for their dedication. Some
significant commendations and recommendations were also noted:

* Focus on being mission driven — The school mission should be updated every few years

¢ Curriculum development and use of rubrics with focus on data feedback

¢ Equal access and equity of education throughout all levels

* Professional development

Responses and plans for addressing recommendations are being compiled. The NEASC Report will be
forwarded to the Commission which will make a decision on the accreditation of East Lyme High School. To
monitor the school’s progress in the follow-up program, the Commission requires routine Two- and Five-
Year Progress Reports documenting the current status of all evaluation report recommendations. Continued
accreditation requires that the school be reevaluated at least once every ten years and that it shown continued
progress addressing identified needs. Principal Susi publically thanked the high school staff for their time
and effort over the last two years and for the work they continue to do. The report will be available next

week for viewing.

5. Discussion re: 2009-2010 Strategic School Profile for East Lyme Public Schools

Dr. Lombardo highlighted significant district wide findings of the CT State Department of Education’s 2009-
2010 Strategic School Profile Report for East Lyme Public Schools. The ELPS district remains competitive
at the state level and within our DRG as providing a quality education for a fair cost. Some features of the
report were noted as being watched, including CMT scores of 3“ grade students, declining additional
expenditures (per student) for land/buildings/debt service, a slightly higher SPED population (and associated
SPED costs) relative to DRG and state. The profile is available on the district website.
