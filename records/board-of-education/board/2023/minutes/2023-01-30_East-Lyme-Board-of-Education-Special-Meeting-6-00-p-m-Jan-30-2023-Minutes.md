1. OPENING OF MEETING
Procedural:

Procedural: C. Public Comment
Emily Shrader
, 15 Applewood Common. It is important to maintain the least impact on students and the support that our students need to increase in achievement. The academic achievement and mental health of the students have been impacted due to COVID. I strongly advise the Board to increase the percentage and let the parents advocate for the appropriate funding and let the votes decide at the budget referendums and hearings.
Pat Hesney
, President of the Secretarial Union, wanted to share the impact of the 18.5 layoffs that are being proposed. The four library assistants being cut will have a negative impact on the staff, teachers and students. They are a huge help when it comes to our students, and this will negatively impact everyone. The morale and engagement in learning among staff and students will be affected and I encourage you to reconsider the budget reductions.
Scott Mahon
, President of the ELTA Union, wanted to share the effect of the budget reductions of the teachers at ELPS. The needs of students’ achievement and mental health will be affected. We are going to lose highly educated teachers. We are struggling to reach learning achievement gaps due to COVID and this will make it even worse. We need to consider that each of these teachers are people and need to do everything we can to support keeping them a part of ELPS.
Chris Majchrzak
, President of the AFSCME-Paraprofessional Union, wanted to share that paraprofessionals are a huge part of maintaining classrooms with the teachers. There are increased special education students (1:1), and increased behaviors. If paraprofessionals are reduced, the district morale will significantly decrease. It will also affect the challenges that students and teachers already deal with on a daily basis with less help. I urge you to reconsider these reductions.
Bryan Zahnstecher
, 65 East Pattagansett Rd, wanted to share that we appreciate the teachers and resources that East Lyme has had to share. We moved from California where class sizes were extremely high. We need to emphasize education and not health insurance. We need to have quality education and focus on teachers’ and students’ needs.
Sarah Susco
, 25 Willow Lane, wanted to thank the Board members and Superintendent Newton for the budget that was proposed although would like them to review it further. With the increasing special education population, why would we cut paraprofessionals? It is very concerning with all special education students at the elementary level is only at LBH. It is unsustainable. I also want the board to reconsider the budget reductions.
Kyle Douton
, 5 Rockwell Street, wanted to share that we need to work together as a group to plan to attend not only BOE meetings but to go to BOS and BOF meetings to push for those Boards to look at their budgets. The education of our students is most important. There is a changing demographic in East Lyme with the housing developments that are being built. We need to look at the costs of the increasing population.
2. DISCUSSION/ACTION ITEMS - OTHER
Discussion:
A. ELPS Superintendent's Proposed Budget for 2023/2024 FY
Superintendent Newton and Dr. Lund (Director of Facilities) shared information on the current and projected Capital Improvement Plans (CIP) for the East Lyme Public Schools. There will be a Facilities Asset Management Committee Meeting on February 7, 2023, at 4:30 p.m. to discuss this further.
Superintendent Newton and Chairman, Mr. Bauman began discussions with the Superintendent’s proposed budget for the 2023/2024 FY that was presented on January 9, 2023. The administration worked together to find the least impactful budgetary cuts that would have an effect on the students.
Save
Budget Reduction Reasoning:
Elementary Teachers:
All class sizes will remain approximately the same with the reduction of teachers due to the decrease in enrollment.
Technology Education Teachers:
It is a great addition to have more support in technology to support the increased use of technology, but we need to have priorities of class size remaining the same.
Instructional Tech Coach:
It is a great addition to have additional support in technology, but reductions need to be made and this is an area we considered.
Reduction in Paraprofessionals:
The paraprofessional structure will shift as we have classroom paraprofessionals who can be moved into more of a 1:1 support. Other special education students moving out of the district will allow the administration to move paraprofessionals.
Library Assistants:
The library assistants are a great addition to the support of the librarians, staff and students but are not a direct impact on student learning.
Part-Time Salaries:
This impact was on the custodial salary that was able to be reduced.
Discussion ensued with the Board and Administration on the proposed budget.
Discussion:
B. Discussion/Information: 2022/2023 Budget Deficit
Superintendent Newton, Mrs. Stevens (Director of Finance) and Chairman, Mr. Bauman shared that the budget deficit was at approximately $700,000 for the 2022/2023 FY due to the increased health benefits, inflation, utilities/natural gas and other fixed rates. We have decreased the budget deficit to around $300,000. There have been some discussions of using the remaining LEARN funds of approximately $16,000 to decrease even more. It is our hope that we can continue to decrease the budget deficit and will keep the Board apprised.
Discussion ensued with the Board and Administration.
