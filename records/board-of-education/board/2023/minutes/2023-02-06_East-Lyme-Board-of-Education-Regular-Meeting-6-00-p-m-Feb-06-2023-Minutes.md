Pat Hesney, President of the Secretarial Union, wanted to share the impact of the layoffs that are being proposed. If the Library Assistants are cut from the budget, it will have a negative impact on the staff, teachers, and students. The four Library Assistants have been a part of ELPS from 3 years to 30 years. They are a huge help for our students and staff, and I believe weekly operations would suffer at each school from the proposed cuts. They tirelessly work and help everyone ensure everything is taken care of daily and weekly. I am asking to please reconsider the proposed cuts.
Lisa Opshal,
7 Wilbur Lane, wanted to thank everyone for listening and their support. I am currently the Library Assistant for the three elementary schools. I am also a community member and parent. As a Library Assistant, we are responsible for so many daily duties to assist in the library with students, helping the librarian, circulating books, cataloguing, labeling and so many other ongoing tasks. If these positions are cut, the librarians and students will be impacted, and I urge you to reconsider.
Christine Maxfield, 14 Green Cliff Road, a Library Assistant at East Lyme High School. I wanted to share that it is all about numbers and shared the amount of books that she circulated books, catalogued, labeled. She also helps with the reservation of the library, calls parents, assists students and staff, helps with covering classes and many other tasks that will now fall on just the librarian who will not be able to focus on just the students now. I hope these words will help you to understand how valued all of us are to staff and students and reconsider the budget cuts that are being proposed.
Chris Majchrzak, 78 Chesterfield Road, President of the Paraprofessional
Union
and Teaching Assistant. Our students need all of us to advocate for them. As a Teaching Assistant at East Lyme Middle School, it is difficult to maintain and keep students on task especially with increased behaviors, student needs with special education needs or not. The increasing
SEL
(Social & Emotional Learning) that needs to be addressed daily and using restorative practices to provide a safe climate for students can be challenging. If we decrease our paraprofessionals along with other valuable staff, we will be doing a disservice to our students. The proposed cuts will most definitely impact on the staff morale in each school building. I urge you to bring back the positions and go to the Board of Finance with a higher percentage because we need the staff.
Kyle Douton, 5 Rockwell Street, shared that the administration always wants to share statistics on how the students are progressing in school with data presentations. We put students in as numbers. It is like supply and demand and the education budget are out of balance. We put so much money into technology but now will not have the support to learn and progress. We need to look at the big picture, it is not technology necessarily the students need, it is the teachers and staff. They are what matters. We need to make smart decisions because these proposed budget cuts do not make sense.
Amy Farrior, 91 Holmes Road, parent of two students at Flanders School. This is the first normal school year since the pandemic, and
I believe that small class sizes
help bring better socialization and will help to focus on helping our students with SEL. The pandemic is over and cutting teachers hurts our students. Students and staff need technology and the staff that support them daily. If we decide to cut staff in this specialty, it will set back our students. I urge you to look beyond cutting teachers and staff and be fiscally responsible.
Chris Faulkner, 19 Wintergreen Drive, Waterford, teacher at Lillie B. Haynes School for 23 years. I have taught in elementary schools, middle school and assisted with after school activities. I want to thank the Board and Administration, but I urge you to take another look at how we can make budget cuts. We need technology to prepare our students to learn young so when they move the middle and high school, they are prepared. We want our students to be prepared when they enter the workforce or college.
Julian Evans, 104 Bushy Hilld Road, Ivoryton, teacher in district for the last 23 years and primarily the last 2 years as the Technology Education Teachers. We cannot function and work currently without technology. We live in a digital world, and we need to prepare our staff and students to be prepared as they continue to progress over the upcoming years. I urge the Board to keep the Technology Education Teachers. We must help our students and teachers who have dedicated their time to learning. Also, the Paraprofessionals are essential to our schools and should focus on the help that they provide daily to assist students,
staff
and teachers. I hope you will make a smart decision to keep all our staff and think of our students.
Kyle Wilkinson, 16 Walnut Hill Road, teacher at East Lyme Middle School and Minecraft Club Advisor. I moved here and believe that East Lyme Public Schools has a phenomenal education. We provide so many resources and make such an impact on our students' lives, including my own. The Teachers, Library Assistants and Paraprofessionals are such a big part of the school. I see the budget deficit, but we need to keep our support here in East Lyme and not cut. We need to push back to the Board of Finance to fix this and help support the education budget.
Scott Mahon, 11 Birch Hill Road, President of the
ELTA
who wanted to share we are at capacity for the meeting room, auditorium and on Zoom. It says a lot about the concern about this budget. I left the meeting last time optimistic during these hard times. We ask for you to read and listen to our staff and students. We need to put our students first; they are what matters. These budget cuts will have a direct impact on student learning.
Anne Campbell, 6 South Cobblers Court, I wanted to thank the Teachers,
BOE
, and Administration. I wanted to share a few thoughts regarding the proposed budget. I was going to have my children come to the meeting to advocate for themselves but decided best to come from me. Teachers are the reasons the student's needs, and academic achievement goals are met. It does not matter if you have accommodation or not. Every child has a different learning style, and it is so important to keep the staff we have in place. We cannot achieve academic success with limited staff. I ask you to please keep the support of our teaching staff in place.
Andrea Montavani, 41 Seabreeze Avenue, I wanted to share my thoughts on the proposed budget. I am an Assistant Principal in another district, my husband is a teacher at Flanders School, and I have two children at ELPS. I have been there with the budget concerns, but our teachers are not feeling appreciated. After the pandemic, there are major learning gaps, and it is important to keep our staff and keep the climate going in a positive direction. Our students need to come first and achieve the best school experience they can get through learning and small class sizes. I am asking the Board and Administration to look for other ways regarding the proposed budget.
Theodore Bergman, 15 Fawn Run Road, Salem, I am a student at East Lyme High School and the Salem Student Representative for the Board of Education. I knew that I needed to be here at the meeting to support and stand behind our teachers at our school. They exude kindness and respect for all students and staff. I plan to keep fighting for the budget cuts to not include cutting our great teachers. Thank you for all you do for our district but protect our students. We stand behind our teachers and you need to as well.
Nick Menapace, 38 Hope Street, Niantic, I just do not understand how we can go from calling our teachers heroes not too long ago during covid to now taking their jobs. The teachers and staff in our district are invaluable and we need to work to find another way.
Annette McDonald, 212 Pennsylvania Avenue, Niantic, there are a lot of rumors out there spreading around so I wanted to come here tonight. Teachers are important and sports are important. The pandemic was traumatic for everyone with the losses that transpired. Students need sports in their lives, there are many studies that show they reduce drug use and help ease the effects caused by trauma. The Board of education needs to stop taking away from the kids. There must be other things that we can do.
Sarah Susco, 25 Willow Lane, wanted to thank the Board and Superintendent for everything they have done. I had many questions that were answered but someone told me that the budget is a story.
