              & Beverage Exemption
              The East Lyme Board of Education will allow the sale to students of food
              items that do not meet the Connecticut Nutrition Standards and
              beverages not listed in Section 10-221q of the Connecticut General
              Statutes provided that the following conditions are met:
              1) the sale is in connection with an event occurring after the end of the
              regular school day or on the weekend;
              2) the sale is at the location of the event; and
              3) the food and beverage items are not sold from a vending machine or
              school store.
              An “event” is an occurrence that involves more than just a regularly
              scheduled practice, meeting, or extracurricular activity. For example,
              soccer games, school plays, and interscholastic debates are events but
              soccer practices, play rehearsals, and debate team meetings are not.
              The “regular school day” is the period from midnight before to 30
              minutes after the end of the official school day. “Location” means
              where the event is being held and must be the same place as the food
              and beverage sales.

                           Generative AI Guidance

This document highlights the potential of generative AI to enhance teaching and learning within
ELPS. Its primary goal is to offer practical guidance to instructors and students on effectively
integrating AI while maintaining academic integrity and respecting individual expertise.
Generative AI refers to a type of artificial intelligence that creates new content, such as text,
images, or data, often using models trained on large datasets to produce unique outputs. This
guidance strikes a balance between fostering innovation and ensuring foundational skills,
adapting as technology evolves and welcoming feedback from the educational community.

Principles

    1.​ Academic Integrity: We prioritize academic integrity, ensuring that students’ work
        reflects their own understanding, effort, and knowledge, supported by AI where
        appropriate.
    2.​ Innovation and Skill Development: Generative AI tools can enhance learning,
        creativity, and critical thinking. Our use emphasizes ethical practices while reinforcing
        students’ content understanding and skill development.
    3.​ Teacher Expertise: Teachers’ expertise and knowledge are paramount. Generative AI is
        a supportive tool and should be aligned to the district’s model for High Impact Teaching
        (i.e. explicit instruction). Teachers’ professional judgment guides its effective classroom
        integration.
    4.​ Transparency and Accountability: Students are required to acknowledge AI use in
        their work, with clear, positive guidance on how to credit AI tools for specific purposes,
        such as brainstorming or grammar assistance. Instructors will model appropriate use.
    5.​ Balanced Skills Development: Foundational knowledge and essential skills are taught
        with and without AI, ensuring balanced education that fosters strong, independent
        capabilities.

Objectives

    ●​ Skill Building and Knowledge Fundamentals: Explicit instruction of foundational skills
       remains essential. AI will complement—not replace—the teaching and learning of
       discipline-based skill development, knowledge building and critical thinking. AI
       complements the continuum of student learning outcomes across all disciplines.




                                                                                               Page 27 of 36
  ●​ Student and Teacher Agency: Teachers and students are encouraged to explore AI
     thoughtfully, aligning its use with classroom objectives and district learning goals.
  ●​ Promoting Inclusivity: AI tools are viewed as a means to create a more accessible and
     inclusive learning environment for all students, helping meet diverse needs.
  ●​ Real-World Applications: Recognizing AI’s relevance in the workforce, we aim to
     prepare students with applicable AI literacy that aligns with future career pathways.
  ●​ Feedback and Responsiveness: Regular feedback will be gathered from the
     community through channels such as Satchel Pulse surveys, allowing for ongoing
     improvement of AI practices.
  ●​ Security and Privacy: ELPS is committed to maintaining industry-standard data
     safeguards, complying with Connecticut student privacy laws to protect data and ensure
     student safety.
  ●​ Bias Awareness and Ethical Use: We expect educators and students to critically
     evaluate AI outputs for bias and misinformation, promoting responsible and inclusive
     practices to prevent harm or discriminatory outcomes.

Applications and Boundaries

  1.​ Full Assignment or Exam Completion: Generative AI tools may not be used to
      independently create or complete the primary components of assignments or
      assessments. This includes any significant portions of work such as written responses,
      problem-solving steps, or analysis that demonstrate core learning objectives. AI can be
      used to assist with minor tasks, like brainstorming ideas or checking grammar, but the
      essential work must be done by the student.
  2.​ Authorized Use Only: Any AI use must adhere to classroom and/or teacher-specific
      expectations and district academic integrity guidelines.
  3.​ Negative Consequences: While we recognize AI’s learning potential, it is essential to
      consider the negative outcomes of AI misuse. AI will supplement, not replace, teacher
      expertise.
  4.​ Customization and Flexibility: Recognizing individual learning needs, AI integration will
      vary to support unique student capabilities and instructional goals.
