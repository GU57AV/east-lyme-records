                      School Reorganization Update (30 Min)


I. Why This Conversation, Why Now

East Lyme's elementary schools are out of balance. Enrollment has shifted significantly across
Flanders, Haynes, and Niantic Center — and projections show that gap widening. At the same
time, the 2025–26 budget cycle made clear that the district faces real fiscal pressure: declining
enrollment means fewer students, fewer sections, and harder budget math. These two
pressures together — equity of access and long-term sustainability — led the Board to form a
school reorganization committee in early 2026.


II. What the Committee Was Asked to Do

The committee's charge was to ensure that class sizes and teaching staff are appropriately
aligned across grade levels — maximizing use of existing building space, streamlining fiscal
obligations, strengthening student transitions, and continuing to meet the academic and
social-emotional needs of students and families. This was not a charge to make a
recommendation — it was a charge to gather data and bring options back to the full board.


III. What We Learned

The committee engaged MP Planning Group to analyze enrollment trends, building capacities,
staffing structures, and grade configurations. Key findings: current utilization is uneven across
buildings; demographic trends do not reverse on their own; and the district has more
elementary school capacity than its enrollment requires. The committee also explored grade
configuration — specifically, whether moving to a different K–8 structure could address
enrollment imbalance and ease fiscal pressure at the same time.

IV. The Options on the Table

The committee is not bringing a recommendation tonight — it is bringing a menu:


   ●​ Status Quo+ — Redistrict only. Rebalance attendance boundaries without changing
      buildings or grade structure.
   ●​ Scenario A — Flanders becomes a K–3 school; Haynes becomes a PK–3 school with all PK
      programs centralized there; Niantic Center repurposed; Grades 4–5 move to a new
      Intermediate Academy co-located on the MS campus alongside a reorganized 6–8
      program.
   ●​ Scenario B — Two PK–3 schools with split PK programs; Niantic Center repurposed;
      same Intermediate/Upper Academy structure at MS campus; greatest projected FTE
      savings.
   ●​ Scenario C — Two K–4 schools; all PK moves to Niantic Center (feasibility study
      required); no change to upper-grade structure; least savings.


V. What Comes Next

A complete data packet will follow. From there, the proposed path includes deeper board
discussion in August, public forums in September and October, and a board decision in
November 2026 — in time to implement changes for 2027–28 if the board chooses to move
forward.
