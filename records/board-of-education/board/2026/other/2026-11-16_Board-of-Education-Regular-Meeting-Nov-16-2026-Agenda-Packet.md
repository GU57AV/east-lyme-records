BUILDING DESCRIPTION
Niantic Center School is an Elementary school located at 7 West Main Street, Niantic CT.
The school is in the 5A American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air Conditioning
Engineers (ASHRAE) climate zone. The building consists of classrooms, offices, lounges,
a Cafeteria and a gymnasium.
The HVAC system consists of hot water boilers, energy recovery units, air source heat
pumps, rooftop units and exhaust fans.

SCOPE OF WORK
Silver Petrucelli and Associates (SP+A) completed a building walkthrough and noted all
systems of heating, ventilation and cooling throughout the school. The systems were
assessed based on their age, visual condition, capacity, control, and energy efficiency.
The assessment consisted of a visual review of accessible mechanical equipment and
available system documents. Destructive testing and concealed conditions were not
investigated in this scope.
The Testing and Balancing (TAB) contractor measured all areas of ventilation throughout
the building for their outdoor air capabilities as well as their damper controls.
Based on the information gathered, and a comparison with the 2021 International
Mechanical Code, 2021 International Energy Conservation Code, and ASHRAE 62.1,
SP+A made several recommendations for consideration to correct the indoor air quality
and increase energy efficiency of the building.
SP+A has reported on the condition of the school and equipment at the time of the site

Heating System Recommendations
As they approach the end of their useful lifespans, SP+A recommends replacing the
boilers with propane fired ultra high efficiency condensing hot water boilers. Condensing
boilers work by passing the flue gases of the boiler over a secondary heat exchanger and


East Lyme Board of Education 2026 IAQ & HVAC Data:
Niantic Center School                       8
Silver Petrucelli & Associates, Inc. ©                                                 Page 26 of 56
    preheating the return water before it goes through the boiler. This occurs when the hot
    gases condense across the heat exchanger and release both latent and sensible heat.
    With the recovery of the heat of the flue gases, less energy is required from the
    combustion process to heat the water to the setpoint temperature. This results in less fuel
    burned and increase in the system’s efficiency potentially up to 98%. Converting to
    propane also uses a fuel source with less carbon emissions which can potentially improve
    the carbon footprint as well as the indoor air quality of the school.
    A consideration for the implementation of condensing boilers is the requirement of low
    return water temperatures (typically between 80 and 120 degrees F) for the system to
    operate at peak efficiency. The lower water return temperature (less than 130 degrees F)
    allows the exhaust gases to condense properly and transfer heat to the water. This means
    that end use equipment such as radiators, unit heaters and cabinet unit heaters, must be
    sized to maintain the heating requirements of the space and have the return water
    temperature required. SP+A recommends considering this when replacing the end use
    equipment such as the perimeter baseboard.
    SP+A recommends replacing equipment such as the hot water pumps with variable speed
    pumps. New pumps can optimize the system efficiency by reducing the likelihood of
    failures in the system. Variable speed pumps can change their flow depending on the
    heating load of the building and save energy.
    SP+A recommends insulating the entire hot water system in accordance with current CT
    Energy code.
