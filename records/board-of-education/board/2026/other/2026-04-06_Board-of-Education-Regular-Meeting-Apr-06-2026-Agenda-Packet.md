A.   Discussion: High School Vision Committee Presentation
     Marc Salerno, Chair of the High School Vision Committee, presented an
     update
     on Phase One of the feasibility study conducted by Silver Petrucelli
     Architects. The presentation provided an overview of the history and existing
     condition of the building, the potential timeline if the BOE moved forward
     with the study, and facility implications with scheduled capital projects. The
     second phase will analyze and provide recommendation if the high school
     building should be rebuilt, renovated, or built new.
     Board members engaged in discussions on the study findings, impact
     on the town, and how to approach the issue strategically. The board
     will vote on the approval of Phase 2 during the April 6th meeting.
     highschoolstudy-v1.pdf


B.   Discussion: Proposed Security Strategy or Deployment of Security Personnel
     Superintendent Newton and board members discussed and reviewed the plan
     for the Special Meeting scheduled for March 12th. Discussions included
     communications on feedback received thus far and how security personnel is
     one aspect of a comprehensive security plan (focusing on mental health and
     connection) in the district.

C.    Discussion/Action: Non-lapsing Account
     Catherine Steel shared the discussions of the FFO Committee on the non-
     lapsing account; the committee is proposing .75% would be the maximum
     contribution per year, with a cumulative amount of 1.25%. Board members
     engaged in discussions on possible uses and limits of the funding, and
     board policies set for account.

     MOTION:
     Motion to approve to limit the annual deposit of .75%, based on previous
     year’s budget, to the non-lapsing account with a cumulative total of 1.25%
     of the preceding fiscal year's approved budget

     D.    Discussion: District Repair Improvement Project - DRIP
           Superintendent Newton shared information on the new state grant
           program that is focused on funding school facility projects or
           improvements. The grant does not require the district to seek approval
           from the town to utilize that funding but must meet state requirements.
           The grant also allows the funding to be rolled over to be saved for a
           larger or costly project. This year East Lyme has been granted 124,000.
           Dr. Lund led discussions on facility priorities that have been deferred
           and possible uses for the grant, which will continue once the funding
           has been distributed.
           DRIP_FAQ.pdf
