C. Public Comment
Tim Dowd, 66 East Pattagansett Road, is a Science teacher at ELHS. Mr. Dowd thanked the BOE for their work in producing the budget, but shared concerns about impacts on personnel, stating this the second time in 4 years that staff cuts are being discussed. Mr. Dowd requested the Board consider this approach and the negative effect it has on morale and hiring/retention practices. Mr. Dowd wished to reiterate that the school district does not waste taxpayer money; teachers and staff are continually asked to do more with less resources and that teachers need the support of the Board.
2. DISCUSSION/ACTION ITEMS – FINANCE/FACILITIES/COMMUNITY
A. 2025-26 Budget Discussions (BOE Goal 3)
Chair Eric Bauman shared an overview of the budget process for the 2025-26 school year and that the BOE initially adopted a budget with a 6.72% increase over this fiscal year. Once the BOE presented the budget to the BOF, the BOF reduced the BOE adopted budget by 1.5 million after deliberations, which reflects a 4.22% increase over this year’s existing budget.
Leigh Gianakos
confirmed that the Public Hearing next week, on April 28, will provide an opportunity for the Board of Finance to receive resident feedback and that it is possible the BOF will change the initial reduction based on that feedback.
Superintendent Newton shared a
presentation
with a summary of proposed cuts from each building and area that would fulfill the budget reduction. Superintendent Newton reiterated the approach to the proposed reductions were taken with care and thought to each school building, and shared that student needs, existing class sizes, instructional leader feedback, fiscal responsibility, and teacher certifications were all considered in making these cuts. Each building submitted their non-staff-related budget reduction in preparation for this meeting. Superintendent Newton stated that all efforts are being made to reduce the student impact and minimize the individual loss of employment.
Vivek Purohit
asked if the proposed 9
th
grade PSAT removal would result in scholarship opportunities being lost; Principal Kydd would confirm for the Board.
Jill Carini
asked if reaching out to the parent community for donation support would be helpful. Superintendent Newton shared he is hesitant to solicit financial support from families, outside of taxpayer dollars, to fund the budget.
BOE members and administrators had thorough discussions regarding the negative implications and effects of the budget reductions, including: supporting social emotional growth; running student clubs/activities with minimal and reduced supplies; possible staff rotations based on resignations or retirements; discussed potential retirement incentives, and movement within schools to balance staffing levels
Jill Carini
appreciates the administrator’s feedback and opinions on how best to support the schools and thinks student loss should be a focus
Alisa Bradley
wants the Board to closely look at student impact and show class sizes with the reductions, to ensure they’re well informed.
Bill Derry
wants to ensure that it is clear to families what these changes will look like and the repercussions of this cut.
Leigh Gianakos
agreed and requested that the information is tangible so that the public can be well informed and encourage voting within the community.
Esteban Garcia
is concerned that the proposed staff cuts will never be readded to the budget and the trickledown effect for long term future student experience.
Jaime Barr Shelburn
believes cuts of this magnitude will lose folks permanently once these positions are eliminated and that materials not funded by the BOE will end up being paid for by community members as a second tax.
Vivek Purohit
finds the caseload numbers concerning if the counselor position is removed and spoke of their essential role for high school students.
Superintendent Newton will send a letter to families regarding budgetary impacts. The Board agreed to schedule an additional meeting to discuss the budget on May 6, 2025.
3. DISCUSSION/ACTION ITEMS – OTHER
A. Board of Education June Retreat (BOE Goal 2)
Eric Bauman
began the discussion of retreat planning and is hoping through discussion to ensure they’re covering the right topics to support the district, and the concept of a three-year financial plan based on existing contracts to anticipate future cost savings.
Alisa Bradley
is in favor of the retreat and using a 3-year lens to plan for future planning with horizon costs and changes and strategically planning to fit in educational growth in the district.
Cate Steel
asked about the possibility of future budget freezes and believes there should be a dedicated staff member to solicit grants, find supplemental funding and offset costs and expenditures, utilizing alumni to grant fund as a revenue stream.
After discussions, Chair Bauman shared the Board plan to hold the retreat following a Regular Meeting in June.
B. Board of Education 2024-25 Goals Review (BOE Goal 3)
The Board
reviewed the progress
of this year’s goals Pathways Programming, Culture & Climate Progression, High Level Instructional Practices, Assessment, Growth/Development for Students, and District marketing/Branding/Social Media Strategy highlighting changes and growth through this year and the connection and cultivation of educational growth within the district.
Assistant Superintendent Spaziano believes the work in climate and culture has been impactful and informed and anchored the school improvement plans. Staff are committed to the work that involves the school community and have been working to integrate it into the operational foundations, utilizing survey data based on feedback and data received. Mrs. Spaziano shared the budget implications that can affect the work and progress made this year.
The Board will plan goals for the next year over the summer and finalize by August.
