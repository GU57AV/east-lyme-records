Move to approve the proposed 2020/2021 Healthy Food Certification (HFC) Statement-Healthy Food Option.
The Pursuant to C.G.S. Section 10-215f, the board of education or governing authority certifies that all food items offered for sale to students in the schools under its jurisdiction, and not exempted from the Connecticut Nutrition Standards published by the Connecticut State Department of Education, will comply with the Connecticut Nutrition Standards during the period of July 1, 2021, through June 30, 2022. This certification shall include all food offered for sale to students separately from reimbursable meals at all times and from all sources, including but not limited to school stores, vending machines, school cafeterias, culinary programs, and any fundraising activities on school premises sponsored by the school or non-school organizations and groups.

B: Proposed 2021/2022 Healthy Food Certification (HFC) Statement - Healthy Food and Beverage Exemptions
Move to approve the proposed 2020/2021 Healthy Food Certification (HFC) Statement-Healthy Food and Beverage Exemptions
The board of education or governing authority will allow the sale to students of food items that do not meet the Connecticut Nutrition Standards and beverages not listed in Section 10-221q of the Connecticut General Statutes provided that the following conditions are met: 1) the sale is in connection with an event occurring after the end of the regular school day or on the weekend; 2) the sale is at the location of the event; and 3) the food and beverage items are not sold from a vending machine or school store. An “event” is an occurrence that involves more than just a regularly scheduled practice, meeting, or extracurricular activity. For example, soccer games, school plays, and interscholastic debates are events but soccer practices, play rehearsals, and debate team meetings are not. The “regular school day” is the period from midnight before to 30 minutes after the end of the official school day. “Location” means where the event is being held.

C. East Lyme High School Baseball Field
Superintendent Newton provided information on previous repairs and costs to the East Lyme High School baseball field from 2011-2012. The baseball field is currently having draining issues and grub issues.
Christian Lund, Director of Facilities provided an update:
-Multiple core ground samples were pulled from various areas of the field and are currently being tested. This will provide us with knowledge of what we are dealing with for soil composition and why the field will not drain properly in various areas.
-There is an issue that we have found with grubs that have impacted the field this year.  All pesticide treatments were conducted on their usual yearly schedule, and the baseball field received its grub treatment as it was supposed to.
-It is not recommended that we attempt to reseed over areas that are in poor condition as grass seedlings will most likely not take hold or grow.  Additionally, there is no irrigation system on the field to support necessary watering (another issue that needs to be addressed if grass remains moving into the future).
-Resodding the outfield was also not recommended at this time due to the fact that it would cost over $60K and most likely not take given the continued drainage issues and concerns with the soil composition that we are currently testing.
Action Steps:
-We will review the soil sample analysis and discuss this with the BOE once it has been completed.
-We are working to identify a possible short-term fix and a long-term fix that must happen so our children can get back to playing on what should be a field of the highest quality.
-We are in the process of researching possible options such as Dodd Stadium and/or the USCGA as a possible location for our kids to play a night game or two under the lights if our field remains unusable this season.
