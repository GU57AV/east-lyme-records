Chair Eric Bauman recognized the newly painted mission statement on the wall behind him and thanked the art students for their efforts.
Mr. David Fasulo introduced Mrs. Mary Turano, ELHS Science teacher, and thanked her for her dedication and efforts towards the EMT course at the high school. In addition to maintaining the significant certifications needed to be an instructor of the course, she has helped foster partnerships with local school districts to allow students outside of East Lyme to take the EMT course on a tuition basis, which directly impacts the community. Under Mrs. Turano’s leadership, students are prepared to pass the EMT state examination. Mrs. Turano shared her story and involvement over the years as an EMT instructor and thanked her students and the district for their support of this impactful program. Mr. Fasulo presented Mrs. Turano with a Viking Valor award.
Mr. Bauman shared his sentiments on the upcoming retirement of ELHS Principal Deb Roselli-Kelly. Mr Bauman shared that Mrs. Kelly is incredibly well respected and the high school has grown exponentially under her leadership. Not only has her impact on students and staff has been positive, but she developed a leader within Mr. Kydd who will take her place as Principal in January.
Superintendent Newton thanked Mrs. Kelly for her dedication and leadership at ELHS and that it has been a pleasure to work with her. Mrs. Kelly reflected on her over twenty years at East Lyme High School and how her initial position as a part-time counselor and how her roles have evolved over the years. ELHS shared she has been blessed to work with remarkable people and feels confident transitioning to retirement with Mr. Kydd as her replacement.

7. DISCUSSION/ACTION ITEMS – INSTRUCTION
A. Discussion/Information: Draft Artificial Intelligence District Guidelines (BOE Goal 3)
Superintendent Newton and Assistant Superintendent Spaziano initiated discussions about the draft Artificial Intelligence District Guidelines. As this topic is rapidly evolving, these guidelines are not yet being considered as policies, feedback was received from several committees and focus groups in the district for feedback on key takeaways and feasibility on implementation.
Board members engaged in discussions with their opinions on the subject and support of this draft being used within the district. Members also asked questions about how it is utilized in the classroom and discussed how Artificial Intelligence can affect learning within East Lyme Public Schools and how to be a collaborative partner is not a replacement of the actual work in AI and the tools will be monitored within the classroom setting. Faculty feedback will be sought after January.
Mrs. Shelburn and Mrs. Bradley discussed the shared materials from the AI session at the CABE/CAPSS convention that contains broad and high-level guidance to maintain as a starting point and utilizing readiness checklists for districts to use in assessing staff for AI understanding. Mrs. Shelburn requested that the word “music” be incorporated into the language as a form of AI.
Mr. Bauman shared he is in full support of the guidelines but is concerned that the document is not accessible for the broader reader. He would like to create a FAQ document summarizing the broader AI themes and structure for general questions. Dr. Purohit shared that he believes that AI technology is developing and stressed the importance of having regular conversations on this subject to ensure that the AI technology is necessary and serves the right purpose.
B. Discussion: Board of Education 2024-25 Goals Review (BOE Goals 2)
Mr. Bauman initiated the review of the
Board Goals
for the 2024-25 school year. Board members engaged in discussions on their goals as the year is underway. Board members acknowledged that some of the measurables were “stretch goals” and may not be achieved, but that it is not reflective of actual district growth. Mrs. Shelburn shared that the Department of Defense may be a good area to research grant funding for Pathways Programming.
Mr. Derry reflected on the high instructional practices goal and shared that the percentage amount for the assessment rating reflects a significant expectation of growth. Mr. Bauman stated that if the Board goals appear to go off track, it can become an agenda item per member request, otherwise the goals will be revisited in March by the board. Mrs. Bradley requested that notes and comments from this meeting are retained in a different color to archive progress made during the next time they are reviewed.
