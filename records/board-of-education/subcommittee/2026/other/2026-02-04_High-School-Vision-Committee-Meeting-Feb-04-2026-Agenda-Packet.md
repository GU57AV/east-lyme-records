EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
In January of 2025, Silver Petrucelli + Associates (SP+A) was retained by East Lyme Public Schools to
conduct a facility condition study of the existing East Lyme High School building located at 30
Chesterfield Road in East Lyme CT. The purpose of this initial Phase 1 study is to assess the current High
School architectural, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and fire protection conditions, in
conjunction with an existing space needs assessment. This initial Phase 1 study coupled with a future
Phase 2 site and building expansion study will aid the Town in determining the feasibility of renovation,
expansion and/or replacement of the existing High School building.

All the Phase 1 inspections were visual in nature and did not entail selective demolition to investigate
structures behind walls or hard ceilings. Visual inspections were performed by SP+A’s staff of architects
and M/E/P/FP engineers. Michael Horton Associates (MHA) provided a structural assessment. SP+A did
not engage the services of elevator, food service or acoustics consultants or civil or environmental
engineers for this Phase 1 study.

Field visits were conducted by SP+A and MHA from July through September of 2025. The assessment
was conducted through several detailed on-site investigations and included discussions with the ELHS
facilities staff, administration and select department heads as well as access to original construction
documents, reports, studies, and records supplied by ELPS.

This report addresses the existing building conditions and includes visual observations, code compliance
and recommendations. The reports refer to locations within the main Wings of the building and
corresponding photographs.

The code review included in this report references the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code as required
for existing buildings. The majority of the building was constructed under dated and less stringent codes.
This review identifies scope of work mandated for existing buildings and identifies thresholds that
trigger greater degrees of conformity with codes for new construction. An emphasis has been placed on
review of the facility to meet current Americans with Disabilities Act which is met with the state
requirements in the ICC A117.1 Accessible and Usable Building & Facilities.

Scope Exclusions:
This Phase 1 assessment includes the East Lyme High School building located at 30 Chesterfield Road
and does not include the town’s attached indoor pool facility, surrounding fields or parking.

Please note that the scope of this assessment did not include a Hazardous Building Materials Survey.

Building History

East Lyme High School building square feet summary:

    ▪    1967 Construction                   102,293 sf
    ▪    1973 Construction                   75,400 sf
    ▪    1999 Construction                   59,794 sf
        Total                               237,487 sf
The current East Lyme High School building is divided into the central A and B two-story Academic
Wings, the east D Music Wing, and the west Athletic Wing (see diagram on page 5). The original 102,293
sf 1967 High School building consisted of the D Wing, the south section of the A Wing, and the North
side of the Athletic Wing. In 1973 the addition of 75,400 sf was added to the original school consisting of
a two-story classroom addition to the north side of the A Wing, and a south gymnasium addition to the
Athletic Wing (see photo below). In 1999 a 59,794 sf two-story B Wing was added to the south of the
original A Wing. During the 1999 B Wing addition, the A, D, and the Athletic Wings were renovated.

The A Wing lower floor consists of the main office administration, entrance commons/cafeteria and
kitchen, nurse’s suite, toilet rooms, custodial spaces, special education classrooms, art rooms,
technology classrooms and shops.

The A Wing upper floor includes the Media Center, special education classrooms/offices, counseling,
attendance/behavior, social workers, IT, custodial, storage, toilet rooms and academic
classrooms/offices for English, foreign language and social studies.

The B Wing upper floor contains math and science classrooms, supporting offices, faculty toilet rooms, a

The one floor D Wing contains the auditorium with stage, a band classroom, orchestra/choral

Existing Conditions Observations and Recommendations
A Wing Interior: (see photos IA for reference)
Despite the age of building, the A Wing has been well maintained. There are minor issues as a result of
its age. Finishes are in overall good condition, be it the original ones or replaced/renovated ones. There
are minor cosmetic damages due to everyday use. There are some cracks in the CMU walls (see
structural assessment). Most corridors and rooms have been recently painted. Some ceiling tiles have
minor damage and/or sagging due to roof leaks.

Classrooms
The classrooms are in good condition. Most classrooms have limited natural lighting due to the limited
number of windows. Most classrooms have received new floors and are in good condition. Most
classrooms have newer markerboards. General cosmetic upgrades of interior finishes recommended.
Toilet Rooms
The Toilet Rooms throughout the A Wing have been designated for either Student use or Faculty Use.
The condition of the finishes and fixtures are well maintained and in fair condition. There are some
missing floor tiles, and some toilet partitions are showing wear. General cosmetic upgrades of interior
finishes is recommended.
Corridors & Stairwells
The corridors are in good condition. Newer VCT floors have been installed in some areas while the
original tile is found in other corridors. Sections of the older floor tile has been replaced with other
sections that are still delaminating. Walls have been painted. There are walls with a brick finish which
are in good condition.
Lockers within the corridors are showing age; most show some minor damage or paint
chipping/scratches. Staff reports lockers are not utilized.
Stairwells are in good condition. There is minor wear on the concrete steps. Handrails and guardrails are
in fair condition and have been painted multiple times with flaking visible. General cosmetic upgrades of
interior finishes is recommended.
Offices
The administration offices are in good condition. The built-in millwork is showing wear as well as the
carpet. Door frames have some minor wear. Most offices do not have exterior windows. General cosmetic
upgrades of interior finishes is recommended.
Elevator
The elevator appears to be original to the building. The size of the elevator limits accessibility and capacity.
It has been stated that the elevator has continual issues and needs maintenance on a frequent basis. The
elevator is overdue for replacement/recapitalization.
Servery & Kitchen
The Servery and Kitchen spaces are in fair condition. Most of the ceiling tiles and grid show wear. The
limited number of light fixtures provide low levels of light. CMU walls are in good condition and have
been painted recently. It is recommended to install new flooring, ceiling tiles and updated lighting.

Media Center
The Media Center space is underutilized but in good condition. Recent renovations to perimeter spaces
have created additional work and classrooms. The space continues to function with original finishes,
millwork and furniture. There have been staff comments on poor lighting and glare from the adjacent
atrium space clerestory windows. General cosmetic upgrades of interior finishes is recommended.
Doors and Door Hardware
Some of the original doors and door hardware were replaced as part of the 2000 renovation, however,
there are numerous original doors that do not close properly and numerous doors with original
hardware that should be replaced due to age, condition and code compliance.
B Wing Interior: (see photos IB for reference)
The two-story academic science and math B Wing was added to the original school in 1999 and
therefore is in the best overall condition. Interior finishes throughout the B Wing classroom, toilet room
corridors, stairways and support spaces are in overall good condition. There are some minor paint
scratches on the more utilized doors. Floors, walls, and ceilings are clean and in good condition. Minor
ceiling tile replacement is recommended in areas of previous roof leaks.
D Wing Interior: (see photos ID for reference)
The D Wing music classrooms and performing arts auditorium, seating and stage spaces are original to
the building’s 1967 construction. Minor renovations and repairs have been carried out over the last 59
years. Overall, the interior finishes are in fair condition including the auditorium seating. There are
minor cosmetic damages due to everyday use. Most corridors and rooms have recently been painted.
Ceiling tiles have minor damage, and some are sagging. General cosmetic upgrades of interior finishes is
recommended.
Auditorium
There is a lack of remote control or easily adjusted stage lighting which is difficult to use. It is
recommended to install new lighting with remote operation. The wood stage floor finish is in poor
condition. It is recommended to repaint and/or resurface. The auditorium lighting and sound systems
are obsolete. It is recommended to replace it with new. The folding partitions that were designed to
separate the upper seating into lecture spaces are not used and are in poor condition. It is
recommended, if these auxiliary spaces are to be utilized, the folding partitions be replaced. See space
study for additional information on the auditorium use.


Athletic Wing E Interior: (see photos IE for reference)
Locker Rooms and Toilet Rooms

The staff of Michael Horton Associates, Inc. (MHAI) visited East Lyme High School at 30 Chesterfield
Road in East Lyme, CT on August 4th, 2025, to perform a visual inspection of the structural condition of
the existing facility with the Silver Petrucelli & Associates team.

The building was unoccupied at the time of inspection. The inspection was comprised of visual
observations from finished grade at the exterior and the various floor levels at the interior. Observations
were limited due to in-place finishes. No destructive testing or exploratory work was performed as part
of this investigation. Within this report, MHAI addresses the general structural condition (interior and
exterior) of the building by Wing to identify any deficient areas observed with supporting representative
photographs. No analysis work to determine the load capacity of the structure was performed as part of
this evaluation.

Existing Conditions Observations
The original 1967 A Wing and D Wing building and subsequent north A Wing is a two-story structure.
The first-floor structure is a cast in place concrete slab on grade. The second floor and roof structures
are a cast in place concrete waffle slab supported by a cast in place concrete frame consisting of
reinforced concrete columns, beams and spandrels. Concrete masonry walls are present throughout the
interior and exterior, with a brick veneer at the exterior.

The Auditorium and Gymnasium roof structures consist of metal roof deck supported by structural steel
framing (joists and wide flange beams). The roof structures of the one-story athletic wing spaces (locker
rooms, offices, etc.) consist of a concrete waffle slab. MHAI was not able to review most of the framing
due to the presence of in place finishes.

 The 1999 B Wing addition is constructed of a structural steel frame. The supported floor system is a
cast in place concrete slab over a metal floor deck supported by structural steel framing. The roof
structure is a metal roof deck supported by structural steel framing. Concrete masonry walls are
present throughout the interior and exterior, with a brick veneer at the exterior. The B Wing
Atrium/Commons area is an open steel frame with tube steel trusses.

investigations and subsequent repair, as previously mentioned at A Wing, to determine the extent of the

Various degrees of deterioration and movement were observed in the brick veneer and mortar joints.

Summary
Overall, the original structure constructed in 1967 and 1973 were observed to be in fair condition. The
structural concrete frames exhibit signs of stress as noted. We would suggest further investigation, both
at the exterior and exposing structural elements at the interior, to determine if this cracking is pervasive
throughout the structure. All spalls and cracks should be exposed to remove deleterious concrete,
repaired and sealed. In particular, the exterior brick veneer at the Athletic and D Wings should be
further investigated as they show signs of separation from the cavity wall from possible water
infiltration.

 It was noted that sections of the brick veneer wall in the D Wing did in fact delaminate and collapse
sometime in the 2010-2015 timeframe. We suggest investigating the areas mentioned herein in a
reasonable timeframe to prevent further deterioration of said areas. We believe the brick façade is
vulnerable as well as the supporting lintels, particularly at the D Wing and should be investigated for
stability.

The other deficiencies observed are considered to be minor in nature and are common with a structure
of this age. We would suggest a complete review of the exterior by a masonry waterproofing and
restoration contractor to repoint, repair and waterproof all joints and cracks to prevent further water
infiltration and mitigate any further deterioration of the exterior.

Most of the electrical equipment and devices in East Lyme High School are original with some major

The building is completely sprinklered with the exception of the north corridor of the A Wing. There are
exposed standpipes in the stairwells of the two-story classroom Wings. There are three (3) separate six-

The building was first sprinklered in 1967, which served the auditorium, classrooms in the A Wing and
the north gymnasium. The original system is approximately 59 years old. Sprinkler heads over 50 years
old are required to be replaced per NFPA 13. Schedule 40 black steel pipes were used. There does not
appear to be any issues.

The building was fully re-sprinklered in 1999 with the major renovation and addition of the B Wing.
There were few areas in the A & B Wings where sprinklers were omitted. The newer system is
approximately 27 years old. As stated above, sprinkler heads over 50 years old are required to be
replaced by code.

An original (1967) six-inch fire service enters the building in the D Wing in a sprinkler room near the
stage. There is a backflow preventer where the service enters the building to protect against cross
contamination. There are two sprinkler zones, which is required due to the size of the school, as each
zone is a maximum of 52,000 square feet per zone. Shutoff valves are protected with tamper switches to
ensure valves are not accidentally turned off and to flow switches to determine if flow is occurring and
to monitor which zone is flowing.

Two newer six-inch fire service lines were added with the renovations and additions in 1999. One enters
the building through the Natatorium building and the other into the B Wing near the curved exterior
entry canopy. Both are zoned and have a backflow preventer where the services enter the building to
protect against cross contamination. The exterior canopy is not required to be sprinklered as it is
constructed with noncombustible materials.

There are exposed standpipes in the stairwells. The auditorium stage has two exposed standpipes on
both sides of the stage to manually fight fires for any stage equipment or decorations as required by
code. There are no dry sprinkler systems in the school and are not required by code.

The sprinkler system is piped with schedule 40 black steel pipe with cast iron threaded or grooved
fittings and joints. Sprinkler heads in unfinished ceiling areas are exposed pipe with upright type heads.

The building is mostly sprinklered with concealed type and semi-recessed sprinklers.

The sprinkler system piping and devices are tested regularly as per NFPA 25. There are no reported
issues, except for a leaking pipe in the D Wing sprinkler room D1-A (photo SP01). The condition of the
sprinkler system is satisfactory. Generally, sprinkler coverage meets the requirements of NFPA 13
Standard for the Installation of Sprinkler Systems. Some areas in a portion of the A Wing noted above
are missing sprinkler coverage which is a code violation.
