BOE Policy 5117 Students: School Attendance Zones
This policy was brought back to the Policy Committee for a second review.  Inter-district requests will be granted under extreme circumstances only.   The approval letter from the Superintendent will state that inter-district approvals are granted for one-year only.
The Policy Committee recommended the following changes:
Add “socio-economic” on line 7 under “Criteria”.
Remove Item #2 under “Inter-District Transfer” relating to child care services.
The revised policy will be brought forward to the Board of Education for a First Reading on December 17, 2018.
BOE Policy 5144.1 Students: Physical Restraint(s)/Seclusion
Kimberly Davis informed the committee that the State of Connecticut has added new regulations regarding the use of physical restraint and seclusion and that these new regulations need to be included in our policy and regulation by the end of January 2019.
Kimberly Davis made the recommendation to replace our current BOE policy and regulation with Shipman and Goodwin’s suggested policy and regulation.   Amy Drowne suggested including coaches as employees under the regulation.
Kimberly Davis also noted that any staff member is welcomed and encouraged to take part in the annual restraint training that is offered.
The current policy will be revised and brought forward to the Board of Education for a First Reading on December 17, 2018.
BOE Policy 1250 Community Relations: Visits to School
This policy was brought to the Policy Committee for a first review.
Jeffrey Newton noted that some school districts in Connecticut are having issues regulating school visits during the school day.  In an effort to minimize classroom disruptions and daily visits, Jeffrey Newton suggested modifying our current policy, last revised in 1993, to something similar to the Waterbury Public School’s current policy.
Amy Drowne noted that for security purposes, all visitors are checked in by the security guard and are escorted to their destination.  Classrooms are called notifying teachers of arriving visitors.
The committee agreed that the District should continue to encourage visitations to planned events during the day and after-school activities, but not daily visits to classrooms and/or lunchrooms that disrupt students learning time.
The suggested revisions will be brought back to the Policy Committee for a second review.
