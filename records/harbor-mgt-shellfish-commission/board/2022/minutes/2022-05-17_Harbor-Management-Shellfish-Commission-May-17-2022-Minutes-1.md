       With respect to eelgrass, item 5 on page 5 of the Guide to Marine
Aquaculture Permitting in Connecticut states, "aquaculture activity in any eelgrass
bed, including harvest, is strictly prohibited". The guide states that the United
States Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) requires that following conditions be
regularly implemented: aquaculture gear will not be located over or within 25
feet of submerged aquatic vegetation (SAV), routine lease activity will not take
place within 25 feet of SAV, every year a visual inspection for SAV will take place
at low tide at the beginning of the growing season. ln addition, the Connecticut
Aquaculture Mapping Atlas shows the location of know eelgrass beds. Map 3 was
generated from the mapping atlas.

      As the permitting process progresses, leases are analyzed to ensure there
will be no disturbance of SAV, interactions with protected species will be
minimalized, and to determine the impact on human activity.
    With respect to shellfish population, the state relies on local Shellfish
Commissions for that information.



4. Specific methodology & timing of growing and harvesting.

      The type of gear to be used will be cages on bottom using sunken lines with
anchors on each end and a buoy on one end. The size of the cages will range in
size (Table 1). Seed oysters will be set out at the start of growing season and
mature oysters will be harvested when they reach market size. Cleaning of
biofouling organisms by power washing will be performed as necessary
(approximately every 3 - 6 weeks). Tumbling oysters will be performed as
necessary. All operations will be completed shipboard.

                                  DEFINITTON OF AQUACULTURE
                                  Aquaculture is defined in the Connecticut General Statutes (Sec. zz-rc) as "the controlled rearing
                                  cultivation and harvest of aquatic plants and animals in land-based and marine-based culture systems,
                                  tanks, containers, impoundments, floating or submerged nets or pens and ponds." Aquaculture is
                                  governed by the Connecticut General Statutes, and is subject to other municipal, state and federal
                                  regulations. Relevant statutes and regulations can be found in section 6.


                                  AUDIENCE
                                  This guide is intended for those seeking authorization for:

                                         ) A new commercial operation or facility
                                         ) A change to an existing commercial operation or facility
                                         ) A non-commercial project (e.g. research, education, habitat restoration, stock enhancement)
                                            that includes use of aquaculture organisms, gear, facilities and/or planting and cultivation
                                            methods

 CONSIDERATIONS                          sale of a cultivated organism. The final product form dictates where the organism can be grown

                                    2. Use native species and strains only. The introduction and transfer of non-native marine
                                         species poses a risk of transporting competitors, predators, parasites, pests and diseases which
                                         can compromise the aquaculture operation and the marine environment. The use of non-native
                                         species is strictly prohibited in open water. Native species may be transplanted within Long lsland
                                         Sound and its tributaries with the appropriate license. Restrictions on import of native species

                                         molluscan shellfish for direct human consumption is only allowed from harvest areas classified as
                                         "Approved" and "Conditionally Approved" areas that are in "Open" status. Harvest of seaweed for
                                         direct human consumption is only allowed from harvest areas classified as "Approved" that are in
                                         "Open" status. View the Aquaculture Mapping Atlas to identifl/ harvest area classifications.

                                    4. Avoid to the greatest extent possible, and minimize potential interactions with
                                        protected species. Certain marine species (e.g. mammals, turtles, birds) and habitats (e.g.
                                        eelgrass) are protected by state and federal law. Restrictions may be placed on projects proposed
                                        in proximity to the approximate locations of endangered, threatened and special concern species
                                        in natural communities in Connecticut, as defined in the Natural Diversity Data Base maps. View
                                        them on the Aquaculture Mapping Atlas. State or federal agencies may also request changes to the
                                        project design or configuration to address potential impacts to these resources.

                                   5. Avoid activity in protected habitats (e.g. eelgrass, salt marsh). Aquaculture activity in any
                                        eelgrass bed, including harvest, is strictly prohibited.

                                        (b) routine lease activity including gear maintenance, washing etc. shall not occur within z5 feet of
                                        the edge of SAV beds;

                                        (c) every year at the onset of the growing season (May 15 through June r5) that gear will be placed

                                        the gear area (see Joint Federal Agenry SAV Survey Guidance - Tier r), at low tide, for eelgrass.
