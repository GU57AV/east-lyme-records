    Note Tobey Russ of Three Belles Marina explained that Three Belles Marina, DEEP, Save the River/Save
   the Hills, and Friends of Oswegatchie Hills Nature Preserve are working together on a project called
   "Opening a Gateway."

    Note The goal is to make the historically private marina more accessible to the public and give people who
   don't normally get out on the water an opportunity to do so. The project also involves buoys in the water,
   with DEEP being contacted regarding the buoy placement.

    Note It was detailed how an existing document, called “Opening a Gateway,” identifies 17 points of interest
   along the Niantic River. The document was originally developed with help from local organizations and
   DEEP (see attachment #6 above.)

    Note The proposal is to place lobster-style buoys approximately 10–15 feet from the shoreline at these
   points of interest.
   The buoys would:
   *Identify interesting and important locations along the river.
   *Allow Three Belles to offer guided and self-guided kayak tours.
   *Give people a better understanding of the river and its history and natural resources.
   *Help educate the public about the importance of protecting the Niantic River watershed.
   *Help connect the various organizations working to preserve the river and the Oswegatchie Hills.
   *Provide a useful reference for police and emergency personnel. If someone is lost or needs help on the
   river, officers could be given a buoy number or location to help identify where the person is.

    Note Mr. Russ reported that the project has received support from numerous groups and officials,
   including:
   *Friends of Oswegatchie Hills.
   *Save the River/Save the Hills.
   *East Lyme Police.
   *David Crocker, Waterford Harbor Master.
   * Steve Albert, East Lyme Harbor Master.
   *East Lyme Conservation of Natural Resources Commission.
   *Niantic River Watershed Commission.
   *Harbor Management Commissions in both towns.

    Note The group has deliberately tried to involve as many community organizations as possible before
   moving forward.

    Note A significant part of the discussion involved determining whether a permit or approval from DEEP is
   required. Mr. Albert thought the buoys might be treated similarly to lobster buoys, meaning the Harbor
   Management Commission might not have regulatory authority over them and a formal permit might not be
   necessary.

    Note Members agreed that they did not want to proceed without confirming the requirements. It was
   pointed out that because the buoys would be located in a federally maintained navigation area, the group
   should contact the appropriate navigation authority even if a formal permit is not required. The goal is to
   make sure the correct agency has approved or reviewed the proposal.

5-2. Ex-Officio

    Note Mr. Deeble shared that the town is moving forward with signing a new police union contract. Mr.
   Cunningham has been involved in negotiating the agreement, which is his third contract negotiated with
   public employees.

    Note There were also brief updates concerning:
   *Variances involving the noise ordinance.
   *Parks and Recreation doing additional weed maintenance along Main Street.

    Note Mr. Deeble also discussed the town's plans to install PFAS filtration systems at two wells.
   Federal drinking-water regulations are expected to become more restrictive, lowering the allowable PFAS
   levels. The town is currently below the existing threshold, but the new, lower limits could require additional
   treatment. The town is therefore trying to get ahead of the change by installing large filtration systems-
   described as essentially “gigantic Brita filters.”

    Note The Commission and Mr. Deeble discussed how PFAS levels could affect the town's ability to obtain
   additional water during a drought. In the past, the town had been able to pump water to a facility in
   Waterford for storage, which could then be used during the summer or during drought conditions. The town
   can no longer send its water there because of PFAS levels.

    Note Until the PFAS levels are reduced, the town is essentially on its own for its water supply, although
   there may be opportunities to obtain water from other sources during a severe drought. There are no
   guarantees, however, depending on how serious the summer drought becomes.
   The wells discussed were identified as:
   Wells 1A and 6, near the Little League field.
   Well 4A, behind Town Hall.

    Note There was also discussion about potential groundwater contamination and other possible sources of
   contaminants, but Mr. Deeble acknowledged that they do not have enough information yet.
   The town is currently conducting studies, and decisions about additional steps will be made once those
   studies are completed.

   generally not intended to function as a transient or guest mooring.
   The Commission discussed the possibility of creating a “use it or lose it” policy, similar to policies used in
   some other harbors.

    Note .The idea would be to notify mooring holders who are not actually using their moorings and suggest
   that they give up the space if they no longer have a boat or are not regularly using it.
   Mr. Albert suggested that he and Mr. Berger could prepare a letter toward the end of the season explaining
   the situation.
   The letter could remind owners that:
   *Moorings are intended for their assigned boats.

   *Owners who are no longer using their moorings may be asked to give them up.

   necessary.

    Note The Commission also revisited an earlier discussion about limiting the size of boats that can use
   certain moorings to 32 feet. There are currently a few larger boats that are grandfathered under the existing
   rules. It was clarified that grandfathering applies to the specific boat, not permanently to the mooring
   owner.
   For example, if someone has a grandfathered boat larger than 32 feet and then gets rid of that boat, a
