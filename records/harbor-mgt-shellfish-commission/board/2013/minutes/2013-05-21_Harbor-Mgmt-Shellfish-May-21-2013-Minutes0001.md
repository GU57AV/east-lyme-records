3. PUBLIC DELEGATIONS. Mr. Riozzi, of 22 North Pine Street, Niantic,
Connecticut, stated within the State of Connecticut , in a federally controlled channel, the speed
limit is designated as six miles per hour. He suggested posting signage that the speed limit is six
miles per hour. It is his understanding that the new bridge construction would include the proper
replacement of those signs. He requested that this Commission and the Board of Selectmen take
the necessary steps to ensure that a sign be placed stating only six miles per hour in the channel
as quickly as possible. On Memorial Day Weekend there will be many boats up and down the
river. All the fixed marker pilings in the channel at one time had six mile per hour signs. Over
the years some markers have fallen off. He requested that this Commission update those markers
stating six miles per hour. It appears that red No. 10 (a floating buoy) is missing. If you are

needs to replace that marker.

The Smith Cove channel markers are in disrepair. It is difficult to see them. Markers
need to be replaced and upgraded. The day markers in Smith Cove need to be taken care of.

With the new bridge there is a much better flow of water and less congestion. Since the
bridge is higher and much wider, he anticipates more transient boats. We have a nice Town but
no place to come ashore. These boaters are unable to go downtown. He would like the
Commission to speak to the Board of Selectmen about the possibility of talking to marinas about
the possibility of renting a dock to have a place to bring in a dingy in so boaters can be aware of
the nice things our town offers.

Mr. Landers stated Fred Grimsy is applying for a Long Island Sound Grant to install a
large sign. We have discussed this at a number of meetings. We are working on details for
signs. John Wilson has a computer program that allows us to see the sign as it will be. We have
been working with him.

4. REPORTS

A. Shellfish. Mr. Landers reported Shannon Kelly of DABA has asked for assistance
from this Commission. There are a number of sampling sites that they are required to sample
after .25" of rainfall within four days. Mr. Dinsmore reported the water is gray at Black Point.
Five samples were taken from every station after .25" of rainfall. The Commission was asked if
this Commission will pay for the Warden. Mr. Dinsmore will obtain a quote from WELSCO.

Mr. Theiler stated he has harvested in Niantic for a number of years, and now needs to
consider whether it is worth renewing his lease. One inch is probably not worthwhile for
commercial. The decision on sampling changed to 1". Mr. Landers stated it is not as attractive
as commercial ground.

Mr. Murin asked what would we gain by having these additional tests? Mr. Landers
replied within samples it is hard to evaluate. Mr. Thieler agreed to speak to Shannon Kelly and
report back to this Commission.

Mr. Dinsmore stated the fees are paid from East Lyme and Waterford. Mr. Dinsmore
will also speak with the Chairman of WELSCO.

Mr. Landers added there was harvesting in the polluted area. Thirty bushels were
replanted.

Ms. Johnston stated a few years ago Mr. Schutt and she agreed to collect samples.

B. Harbor Mr. Hitchery reported he has received 250 paid moorings. There are 12
outstanding. He has had no response after three notifications to these individuals. He plans to
send another notification, and if he does not receive a response by June 15, he will assume they
are not renewing.

Mr. Hitchery reported the Harbor Master's boat will be in the water this Saturday. He has
submitted bids for the Harbor Master mooring. He asked that a decision be made tonight.
