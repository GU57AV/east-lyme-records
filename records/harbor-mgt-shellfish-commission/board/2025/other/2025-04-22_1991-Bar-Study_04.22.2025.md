l                                                INTRODUCTION
        The Town of East Lyme is actively planning for the wise use and conservation of its waterfront
        resources. It is doing so in response to pressures for use of these resources and in response to increasing
I       awareness of the ecological values and recreational opportunities they provide. Following passage of
        the Connecticut Coastal Management Act, the East Lyme Planning Commission prepared a Municipal
        Coastal Program to guide coastal area land-use and protection of the Town's coastal resources. This
I       program led to the Coastal Area Development Plan adopted by the Planning Commission in 1982 and
        incorporated into a revised Town-wide Plan of Development in 1987.

I       In 1986, the Board of Selectmen appointed a Harbor Management Commission (HMC) with the power
        and duty to prepare a Harbor Management Plan to guide activities in the Town's marine area and protect
        the Town's marine resources. The Town's Harbor Management Plan was completed by the Commission
I       in 1990 and approved by the Connecticut Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) and
        Department of Transportation (DOT) in September of 1991. The Harbor Management Plan will be
        presented for Town adoption later this year.

        Both the Coastal Area Development Plan and the Harbor Management Plan address conditions
        throughout the Town's entire coastal area - from the Niantic River on the east to the Four Mile River
        on the west. In addition, both plans focus attention on the narrow strip of land known as the "Bar" at
        the mouth of the Niantic River. (See Figure 1.) The Bar separates the Niantic River from Niantic Bay
        and includ es right-of-ways for Connecticut Route 156 and Amtrak's northeast corridor rail line. The
        Bar also contains the western approaches to the Route 156 and railroad bridges that span the Niantic
        River, as well as the "railroad beach" on Niantic Bay, and privately owned land between the two bridge
        approaches. The DOT has constructed a new Route 156 bridge over the River (the new bridge opened
        in May of this year), and the old bridge is scheduled to be removed in the fall of 1991.

        The Bar has been the focus of public attention for a number of reasons. The Coastal Area Development
        Plan identified the opportunity for expanded public use of the Bar, including the railroad beach and the
        shoreline between the bridg es, and suggested development of the area as a multiple-use public
        recreation facility. In 1989, the Town's Economic Development Commission (EDC), interested in
        pursuing opportunities for stimulating local businesses, developed a conceptual plan for developing a
        public boat launching area on the Bar. Much attention over the past several years has also been focused
        on the DOTs bridge replacement plans and the effect of those plans on future use of the Bar and on
        navigation in the Niantic River channel.

        The Harbor Management Commission identified the Bar, along with the adjacent navigation channel
        and bridges, as one of four major harbor management "sub-areas" within the overall East Lyme Harbor
        Management Area. (See Figure 2.) The HMC developed general policies as well as more specific
        management guidelines that apply to this "Bar and Bridge Harbor Management Area." These policies
        and guidelines - included in the East Lyme Harbor Management Plan - will help to establish the
        future use and character of the Bar.

    such a study. This grant was combined with Town funds provided by the Economic Development
    Commission to enable the HMC, with important assistance from the EDC, to carry out the study and
    prepare recommendations for public use of the Bar.

     Throughout the study, the HMC sought input from the general public, Town officials, state agencies,

    National Railroad Passenger Corporation (Amtrak); owners of private land on the Bar; the Blackhawk
    group fishing operation; and others. In addition , concerned citizens and officials were provided
    opportunities to express their views regarding the study during the Harbor Management Commission's
    regular monthly meetings.

              The Harbor Management Commission also held several special meetings during the course of the
              study. A public meeting was held on March 14, 1990 to review conditions on and near the Bar, and
              a second public meeting was held on July 24, 1990 to discuss alternative development concepts. A
              meeting with representatives of the Town of East Lyme, the DEP, and the DOT took place on
              August 30, 1990 to review the alternative development concepts relative to·DOT s plans for bridge
              and highway construction. A similar meeting was held with representatives of the Town, the DEP,

   I          and Amtrak on October 5, 1990. Following those meetings, the HMC formulated a preliminary
              water access plan for the Bar, and a third public meeting was conducted by the HMC on May 7,
              1991 to present the plan and receive comments on it.
.. I          This report presents the Harbor Management Commission's recommended water access plan for the
              Bar and summarizes the different steps undertaken by the Commission to study the Bar and prepare
              the recommended plan. To prepare the plan, the Commission: 1) analyzed existing conditions in
              and around the Bar; 2) assessed opportunities and constraints affecting future use and development
              of the Bar; 3) prepared and analyzed alternative use and development concepts; and 4) formulated
              short and long-term recommendations (guidelines) for establishing water access facilities on the Bar.

              The water access plan is presented in Part Four (copied on gray paper) of this report, and is based on
              the following basic goals which received public support throughout the course of the study.

              •   THE TOWN SHOULD PURSUE DEVELOPMENT OF A WATER-ORIENTED, MULTIPLE-USE PUBLIC RECREATION
                  AREA ON TIIE BAR.


              •   THE TOWN SHOULD SUPPORT TIIE DEVELOPMENT OF FACILillES FOR TIIE AREA'S COMMERCIAL
                  FISHERMEN ON TIIE BAR.


              •   POTENTIAL ADVERSE ENVIRONMENTAL IMPACTS ASSOCIATED WITH NEW FACILillES AND USES ON TIIE
                  BAR SHOULD BE AVOIDED OR MINIMIZED.


             •    POTENTIAL ADVERSE IMPACTS ON PUBLIC SAFETY AND NAVIGATION ASSOCIATED WITH NEW FACILillES
                  AND USES ON TIIE BAR SHOULD BE AVOIDED OR MINIMIZED.


             The Harbor Management Commission will work with the Board of Selectmen and other Town
             commissions and organizations to achieve these goals through implementation of the
             recommendations contained in the water access plan. A first step will be for the Board of Selectmen
             to accept the plan, and for the plan's goals and recommendations to be established as Town policy
             - by incorporation in the Plan of Development and Harbor Management Plan, for example, and

 -I          ultimately into the Town's capital planning program. Available funding sources must also be pursued,
             including funds available from the State of Connecticut for acquisition of land and provision of public
             water access facilities.

     J       Implementation of the water access plan will not occur overnight and the total cost of realizing all
             of the plan's recommended components would be substantial. Implementation, however, can
             proceed in phases and in accordance with the availability of funds. Also, since the plan at this time
             is not a detailed design or construction blueprint, it can be modified over time as implementation
             of its various components proceeds. As such, the plan provides the flexibility necessary to ensure
             that future uses and water access activities on the Bar proceed in a manner to maximize public
             benefits.

                                  PART ONE:
                 SUMMARY OF CONDITIONS ON AND AROUND THE "BAR"
       The Bar is a narrow strip of land slightly more than one mile long when measured from Smith Street
       in the Village of Niantic (Town of East Lyme) to the edge of the narrow channel that connects the
       Niantic River with Niantic Bay. The Bar is bounded on the north and east by the Niantic River, on the
       south by Niantic Bay, and on the west by the commercial area of the Village of Niantic. Ownership of
       the Bar is divided among the State of Connecticut, the National Railroad Passenger Corporation
       (Amtrak), and private interests. Connecticut Route 156 and the Amtrak rail line run parallel to each
       other along most of the Bar. On the eastern part, the highway and railroad right-of- ways diverge slightly
       before crossing the Niantic River channel into the Town of Waterford. A wedge of privately owned
       land (referred to in this report as the "Bailey parcel") is found between the highway and the railroad.
       Land owned by Amtrak also includes the sandy beach (known as the "railroad beach") bordering on
       Niantic Bay. (Property ownership on the Bar is shown in Figure 1-1.)

      A dramatic change in the overall character of the Bar has recently occurred as a result of ongoing work
      by the Connecticut Department of Transpor tatio n (DOT) to reconstru.ct Route 156 in this a rea. This
      work has included construction of a new Route 156 bridge over the Niantic River. (Figures 1-2 and 1-
      3 show conditions before and during bridge construction.) The new Niantic River bridge is of
      significantly higher elevation than the old span and a short distance to the south. The new bridge and
      its elevated approaches were opened in May of 1991. Demolition of the old bridge and completion of
      landscaping, sidewalks, and other associated improvements should be accomplished by the end of
      1991. Once all construction is comple ted, the old bridge approaches oo the Bar and on the Waterford
      side of the channel will be abandoned by the DOT and available for public use.

      As part of the bridge and highway construction project, the DOT is providing two public parking areas
      - one on the Bar and one on the Waterford side of the Niantic River channel. In addition, construction
      of Town boat slips, docking space and a public park across the channel from the Bar have been
      incorporated into the bridge construction plans in Waterford. (Figure 1-4 shows some conditions that
      wiJI exist following completion of all bridge construction work as currently planned. Figure 1-5 shows
      various postconstruction distances and dimensions.)

      In the past several years, much of the waterfront on the Waterford side of the Niantic River near the
      Bar has undergone significant change. New commercial docks and boat service facilities, including a
      large dry-rack storage building and charter fishing facilities, have been constructed.

      Currently, the three principal uses of the Bar are the Route 156 roadway, the Amtrak railroad, and
      support facilities, including parking, for the Black Hawk group fishing boat that is docked in the Niantic
      River channel on the eastern edge of the Bar. Other recreational uses include passive recreational uses
      such as walking on the railroad beach along Niantic Bay. (See Figure 1-6.) Fishing also takes place
      on the Bar - fishermen cast into the Niantic River from the rip-rap approach to
- j   the old highway bridge, from just north of the railroad bridge over the Niantic River, and from just
      south of the railroad bridge as the channel empties into Niantic Bay. While Amtrak has not encouraged
