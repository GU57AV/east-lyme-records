Re: Waterford - East Lyme Shellfish Commission
Dear Mr. Nickerson:

You have requested our advice regarding the power of the Waterford — East
Lyme Shellfish Commission ("“WELSCO”) to grant leases for the taking of shellfish from
the waters of the Niantic River.

WELSCO’s enabling statute, Conn. Gen. Stat. §26-287 provides, in pertinent
part, that WELSCO may “license such taking [of shellfish] and, in connection therewith,
adopt reasonable regulations and fix license and permit fees.” Among the fundamental
principles of statutory construction are that the legislature was aware of what it was
doing when enacting a statute, that each word in the statute must be given effect, and
that the statute cannot be interpreted to authorize an action which the statute does not
expressly authorize. Applying these principles to WELSC-O’s enabling statute, it ie
noteworthy that the word “license” appears twice in the same sentence describing _
WELSCO’s powers and the word “lease” appears nowhere in the statute. It is evident
that the legislature was aware of what it was doing when it authorized only licenses, and /
not leases, because most statutes pertaining to shellfish commissions authorize these
commissions to grant leases. Here, the absence of the word “lease’ is significant,
inasmuch as it differentiates WELSCO’s power from the powers of most shellfish
commissions.

There is a basic distinction between a license and a lease. A lease grants to the
lessee the exclusive right to make use of a defined area for a specified period of time,
and enables the lessee to exclude others from the leased premises. A license is more
