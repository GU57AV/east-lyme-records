3. Public Delegations

A. Mark Castellucci, from the U.S. Army, Camp Niantic, made a presentation regarding the U.S.
Army's plan to establish a shoreline force protection exclusion zone that will run parallel with the
shoreline of Camp Niantic, and will go out 250 feet into the water, which is the minimum Safe
Distance Zone (SDZ), for an approximately 300,000 square feet, total.

Discussion ensued regarding the concerns of the Commissioners, in terms of its permanency, how
the residents of Niantic are going to react to this exclusion zone, if buoys adhere to tackle
regulations, impact on roads in the surrounding areas, as well as negatively impacting the access to
shell fishing grounds. Commissioner Landers also wants the U.S. Army to make sure that it's
consistent with the Harbor Management Plan.

B. Timothy Londregan presented his proposal, as promised, for the Type II aquiculture farm.

4. Reports

A. Shellfish. Commissioner Kanter says that the river and the bay are closed, due to the 1.3 inches
of rain received the other night. Area B in the river is closed. DABA said there were too many
boats still located in the river, creating high concerns of discharging in the area. If the number of
boats decreases, B may open up. Area F is open.

B. Harbor Master. Mr. Morris says that they received the rain gauge adapter today, and they have
started to hook it up. By tomorrow afternoon, they will start sending out some emails. He was
pretty impressed with the display screen.

The plan is to do a 2-year sign-up, at a cost of $60 ($30 per year) for opening it up, so you can go
to the weather station and look at the screen. It is off of the rain gauge at Dispatch in Flanders.
He assures there should be no limitation on the numbers of emails, which will be triggered at 1
inch of rain.

He's getting in gear to start the mooring process. Commissioner Hitchery ordered new stickers,
which have already come in. Commissioner Johnston questioned why the three moorings haven't
been hauled off, that are located off of Black Point. Mr. Morris indicated he's been waiting on the
Commission to approve their removal, and states that there are actually five that are red stickered
and unidentified that he would like to haul off. It is apparent that these have been abandoned. The
total cost would be $495.

MOTION: Commissioner Johnston moved to haul off the five moorings, with funding
provided by the Commission. This was seconded by Commissioner Mountzoures. The
motion carried. (6-0-0)

Commissioner Landers touched on the discussion that took place regarding the new Harbor
Management Plan, highlighting the fact that the numbers are to be removed, in terms of size and
vessels, and that capacities in areas will be upon consultation with the Harbor Master, as well as
specific designations.

Regarding Three Belles, beyond sending a letter, the Commission hasn't done anything further.

D. Ex Officio. Mr. Seary advises that the work on the boardwalk continues, and that they don't
