1. PRELIMINARY BUSINESS

Chairman Mountain called the Commission on Aging Regular Meeting of March
11, 2013 to order at 6:00 p.m.

a. Pledge of Allegiance. The Pledge of Allegiance was observed.

b. Delegations. There were no delegations.

c. Approval of Meeting Minutes — January 14, 2013. Mr. Mountain asked for
additions, deletions or corrections to the Commission on Aging Meeting Minutes of

presented. Seconded by B. B. Smith.

2. STATUS REPORT

a. Transportation. As a result of a recent blizzard, a mirror had to be repaired at
the Town Garage. In addition, strong winds damaged the door of a vehicle and was
also repaired at the Town Garage.

Senior Services' part time driver will be on vacation for the month of April.
Substitute drivers will drive for him.

On Thursday the Town is connecting its water with the City of New London. A
bus will be available to take residents to view it from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.

In next month's packet Mrs. Wilson will include a revised version of the brochure.
She has been informed that some individuals that are on the bus are either not
taking care of their personal hygiene, using strong perfume or after shave lotion. She
further discussed another individual who made individuals uncomfortable by his
conversation. She would like to stress to them the importance of consideration for
others.

Within the last few months she has had individuals taking the bus with limited
vision. The drivers are uncomfortable about transporting them. One individual delayed
the bus by not being on time, because of limited mental capacity. Ms. Smith asked if
there is a manual that outlines an individual's ability to ride the bus for the safety of the
driver and persons using the bus? Ms. Wilson replied there is currently a policy. Mary
Broga felt Ms. Wilson has every right to enforce rules. Ms. Hardy felt if someone is
unable to ride the bus independently, they should have a companion riding the bus with
them.

(1) Enrichment Trips. The following enrichment trips have been planned: March
13 to New London Country Club on Great Neck Road and March 27 to the Log Cabin in
Clinton; on April 11 to Newport Playhouse & Cabaret to see "Spread it Around" for a trip
fee of $75 per person; April 27 to New York City on your own; May 2 to Emerson Place
to the World's Largest Kaleidoscope and then to Stucki Embroidery Works to tour the
factory. The trip fee is $92 per person; May 14 to Federal Hill or "Little Italy" in
Providence, Rhode Island for a trip fee of $71 per person; May 30 to Fenway Park Tour
and Boston Duck Tour for a trip fee of $79; June 13 to Theater by the Sea to see
"Nunsence"; June 18 for a Whale Watch from Boston, Massachusetts on a high speed
catamaran with free time at Quincy Market afterward; June 27 to Hyde Collection
Museum in Glen Falls, New York for an exhibition of Georgia O'Keefe's works of the
Lake George area, with a guided tour of Saratoga included. The trip fee is $67; on July
7 to Boston Pops Orchestra with Special Guest Vince Gill for a matinee performance at
Tanglewood. Lunch is included. The trip fee is $137.

(2) Day/Overnight Trips. The following overnight trips/cruises have been
planned: April 19 to May 1, 2013 for The Best of Portugal including the Islands. Prices
begin at $3,969; May 28-29, 2013 to see Shoji Tabuchi Show. It includes a tour of
Saratoga Springs and a sightseeing cruise on Lake George. The cost is $322 for
double occupancy; October 4-9, 2013 to Charleston, Savannah and the Jekyll Island.
This trip includes a motor coach and airfare. It includes passes to the 37th Annual
Charleston Home & Garden Tour. Prices begin at $2,229 and January 9-19, 2014 fora
Hawaii Cruise Tour on NCL's Pride of America. This is seven day cruise with seven
shore excursions. Two nights at the Marriott Waikiki Beach. Prices start at $3,999 per
person.

c. Programs.
(1) Senior Nutrition Program. Ms. Wilson reported with federal and state budget
cuts, there may be changes to the Senior Nutrition Program. She informed the
Commission two volunteer Meals-on-Wheels drivers will be leaving in May.

Monday Bistro is well attended. No reservations are required, and the food is
good. The cost ranges from $3 to $5.

TVCCA is having a fundraiser on April 6 at Ocean Beach at the Port and
Starboard Banquet Center for $45. There will be a Silent Auction.

(2) Senior Center Programs-Ongoing. Ms. Wilson reported the AARP Tax Aide
Program for East Lyme and Waterford are unable to take any more appointments.
Individuals are being referred to Ledyard.

Chair Yoga is filled. Pickle Ball had twenty-five participating on Saturday. Our
Healing Touch instructor has had surgery, and this program will be cancelled until she
has recovered.

St. Patrick's Day Picnic is scheduled for March 20.

Ms. Wilson stated it is the twenty-fifth anniversary of naming the Senior Center
Nicholas Parahus. She would like to put together a summary of Mr. Parahus'
involvement in the Senior Center. Ms. Broga stated he was very active. He was
Chairman of the Senior Citizens Commission.

(3) Senior Center Programs — New. Ms. Wilson informed the Commission she
has a volunteer who would like to give Chinese watercolor instructions.

Beginner Bridge will begin this week for nine weeks.
Indoor Bocci will start in April.

Drug Take Back will be held on April 27 from 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. at the East Lyme
Police Station.

A Matter of Balance Class will begin in April. This class will be held for four
weeks.

A Program for a Living Will will take place in April.

The Shred or not to Shred Program will be held prior to Shred Day.

d. Budget - General Fund, Revolving Account. The budget Is doing fine.
e. Revenues - General Fund, Revolving Account. Revenues are as expected.

3. OLD BUSINESS

a. Budget Preparation for Fiscal Year 2013-2014. Ms. Wilson reported she has
made a change to the 2013-2014 budget, because the union contract has been settled.
The total request will be $238,751.

She met with First Selectman Paul Formica and does not have to attend the
Board of Selectmen Budget Hearing.

b. Mayors for Meals. Town leaders will deliver meals so that they can see how
the Meals-on-Wheels Program works. Ms. Wilson has extended an invitation to them.

c. Other Pertinent Business. B. B. Smith reported she called a business in
Windham regarding the kiln, and they indicated to her they are not willing to post an ad.

Ms. Wilson will contact the school system regarding the kiln.
4. NEW BUSINESS

a. Criminal Background Checks on Volunteers. Ms. Wilson feels that Criminal
Background checks are necessary for individuals volunteering to deliver meals-on-
wheels. She contacted the Department of Safety and was informed it takes 4-6 weeks
and costs $50.

She spoke to Dave Putnam, Director of Parks and Recreation, and he informed
her he uses SCCI. It handles a national background check which takes 48 hours for a
cost of $18.50.

b. Medical Transportation Grant Renewal. Ms. Wilson reported this grant has
been received for seven years. East Lyme has joined Waterford, New London, Groton,
Stonington and Ledyard. Each Town allows residents to have a limited amount of rides
for medical appointments only during each fiscal year. We are asking if Lyme and Old
Lyme would like to be included.

c. Other Pertinent Business. There was no discussion of other pertinent
business.
