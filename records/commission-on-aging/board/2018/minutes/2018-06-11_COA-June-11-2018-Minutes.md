NEW BUSINESS

a. Chair Yoga Issues. Chair Yoga is in a very small room. The cost is $24 for 30 classes that
must be used within a year. Because of the size of the room and that people purchase
passes, there is no way to determine how many people will show up for any given class. This
has caused problems because too many people are showing up for the class and the room is
overloaded. As of July 1, 2018, the pass system will be eliminated for this program. At this

time, no new registrations will be allowed until all current users have finished their pass. As
of July 1%, the current users will have to register for either the 9:30am class or the 10:30am
class so there will be some control on the size of the class. Once the passes are finished, the
class will be opened up again.

b. Pass Classes vs. Session Classes. During the FY18/19, all classes using the Pass system will
be evaluated. It is very likely that all pass classes will go to session classes.

c. Accepting Credit Card Payments. The Senior Center started using Parks and Recreation
mobile credit card machine on a daily basis. Up until May 30", the receipts were given to
Parks and Recreation for that department to deposit. This has proven problematic and the
Senior Center will now handle those deposits.

d. New Registration Software. Ms. Wilson is not happy with the new registration software.
Rec Track does not handle transportation requests. She is looking into 3 programs and will
have quotes available for the July meeting.

f. Update on Medicare Savings Plan. Ms. Wilson reported the State of Connecticut wanted to

cut programs for the Medicare Savings Plan. The Medicare Savings Program is a national
program but Connecticut used higher income limits so more people could qualify for the
benefit. The State of Connecticut paid the Part B premium for Medicare that is usually taken
out of an individual’s Social Security check. This program also either paid the premium for a
Part D prescription insurance plan or helped to subsidize it. The State of Connecticut
planned to scale back this program by using the Federal income limits. This caused a huge
outcry and as of July 1, the State of Connecticut plans to maintain the income limits
currently in place.
