i. Public Hearing

Mr. Goeschel recapped the last Planning Meeting stating that the Public Hearing for 26 Industrial Park
Road had been opened, with no action taken and rescheduled for this evening to accommodate Mr.
Pfanner. Mr. Schuch reopened the Public Hearing, Robert David Pfanner of J Robert Pfanner &
Associates came forward and provided the Commission with three handouts for review (Exhibit A,
Exhibit B, and Exhibit C). He summarized both the application as well as his report (Exhibit A), for waiver
requests. Presently there is an existing lot with a small building on it, and the Applicant would like to cut
the lot into two separate lots. Lot 1 will be comprised of the existing building and will be 72,200 square
feet while lot 2 will be 51,100 square feet. They are proposing to construct a 4,500 square foot building
on lot 2 with parking along the road frontage and loading areas on both sides of the building.

Mr. Pfanner explained that the Subdivision Regulations require that the Applicant contend with drainage
onsite and they have constructed two dry basins or rain gardens for this purpose. One is located in front
of the property and one is located in the back; any overflow would end up in the Pattagansett River. He
noted that they are asking for a waiver for sidewalks for several reasons; the property is isolated,
located in an industrial park, no sidewalks exist within the park with the exception of Stop & Shop and
the presence of a sidewalk would have to be constructed on the side of the road that would not disturb

1|Page

the brook. Such a sidewalk would be a sidewalk to nowhere, piecemealed together. Mr. Pfanner asked
the Commission if they had any questions. Ms. Bengtson asked how the drainage maintenance will be
enforced and Mr. Pfanner stated that it could be accomplished through a deed restriction but that it is
currently on the Plan, which serves the same purpose. Ms. Bengtson asked that it be noted that she
would prefer that it also be outlined through a deed restriction. She asked what the existing building is
currently being utilized for and the Applicant stated that the building is empty but that prior to that it
had been used for the manufacture of heating elements and resistors.

Mr. Schuch thanked Mr. Pfanner for his presentation and asked Mr. Goeschel to reacquaint the
Commission with the part of the Subdivision Regulations which allow the Commission to grant such
waivers. Mr. Goeschel called attention to his memorandum, which is recorded in the December 2, 2014
Meeting Minutes, noting Section 4-12 and the criteria for granting such waivers. During this discussion
he observed that the POCD encourages pedestrian walkways where permissible but pointed out that the
Charter requires that the Town handle the maintenance for all sidewalks which is a great expense. He
added that he would agree that there are no sidewalks within the industrial park and that it would be a
sidewalk to nowhere.

Mr. Schuch and Mr. Balantic discussed the so-called sidewalks to nowhere on Route 161, noting that it is
piecemeal work done in good faith; the hope and intent is that these pieces will eventually be
connected. Mr. Balantic observed that these sidewalk pieces show up as development opportunities
present themselves and should not be dismissed as sidewalks to nowhere. He added that the area in
question is utilized as a point of travel and if we say yes now, we are setting a precedent. Mr. Schuch
stated that he questions why someone would want a sidewalk in a light industrial area but that the
presence of Stop & Shop and the school on the other side serve as the perfect counterpoint to that
question.

Mr. Pfanner stressed that this is a small area and if the sidewalk were constructed, it would be a small
piece. All of the other areas that would need sidewalks to connect in would be the burden of the Town.
He pointed out that funding is always an issue and that the Town obviously prioritizes when selecting
sidewalk location. He finds it difficult to believe that the Town would choose sidewalks for a light
industrial area when so many more desirable and necessary locations exist. He illustrated his point by
bringing attention to the complete absence of sidewalks around the middle school and elementary
school. He added that he does not believe sidewalks are necessary in this area.

Mr. Schuch stated that the Applicant is asking for six separate waivers and asked that the Commission
review them and ask any questions they might have. Mr. Goeschel said that a soil report is not needed
for this area and that only a limited percentage exists for light industrial; to maximize this potential, no
open space is desired. Mr. Schuch asked in terms of that traffic study, how many daily trips are
anticipated for this site. Mr. Pfanner stated that it’s a manufacturing outfit, not retail and that ten trips a
day would be on the high side. Mr. Goeschel observed that the middle school encompasses grades 6"
thru 8™ and questioned how many children would actually walk to school and/or utilize Industrial Park
Road. Mr. Pfanner noted that light industrial is not an area that we want to encourage people to walk
through; it is an area where we want to generate business but keep separate from the public.

Mr. Schuch questioned whether an archeological survey might be necessary. He acknowledged that he is
not a historian and does not have that expertise. Since it’s not a timely or expensive process he is always
inclined to have the Applicant contact the State to determine whether it is an area of interest. Ms.

2| Page
Schwartz and Mr. Covino stated that they were okay with foregoing an archeological survey. Mr.
Balantic highlighted the portions of the POCD related to sidewalks for the Commission. Mr. Norman
Peck asked the Commission if he would be permitted to comment on the archeological survey. Ex-Officio
Roseann Hardy confirmed that as a member of the Zoning Commission Mr. Peck is not allowed to
comment. However, since he serves on the Historical Society he can speak in that capacity. Norman
Peck, President of the East Lyme Historical Society stated that a tremendous amount of historical
activity has occurred along the bay and the Niantic River in the northern end of town. Very rarely has
anything been found along the freshwater brooks. In his opinion the State Archeologist would pass on
performing a survey for this site.
