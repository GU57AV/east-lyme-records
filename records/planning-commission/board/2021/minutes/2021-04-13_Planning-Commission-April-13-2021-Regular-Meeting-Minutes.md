         B. Bruce Dasinger re: Darrows Pond
             Bruce Dasinger, 12 Stone Cliff Drive addressed his concern about proposed plans by the East
             Lyme Park and Recreation Dept. for active use of the 100 acres of the Darrow Pond property
             owned by the town. He described the history of the parcel and explained the property is 300 total
             acres, 200 are preserved for open space and 100 acres for town use. B. Dasinger is concerned
             about species that are at risk, especially the New England Cottontail (NEC) rabbit. He stated the
             area in which the town is proposing a disk golf course and mountain bike trails would be
             detrimental to the habitat of the NEC rabbit. When the property was originally bought and divided
             for open space and town use it was with the understanding that if the town was to propose any use
             it would consult with the open space committee for their comments and feedback.
             Lisa Wahle with the Wildlife Management Institute and CTDEEP described the shrub and thicket
             habitat where NEC's live, as well as birds that are at risk. She informed the members that in2006
             the NEC's were considered as a candidate for protection under the endangered species act. In 2015
             the USFWS determined the NEC's did not meet the criteria for endangered species but the efforts
             to protect the habitat and the NEC need to continue to ensure their viability. She stated the area on
             the town-controlled portion of Darrow Pond preserve is prime habitat for the rabbit.
             B. Dasinger thought that there was approximately 50 acres of shrub land and 25 acres of dense
             thicket in the town portion of the property.
             M. Salerno said there was a Park and Rec subcommittee to evaluate what would be a suitable use
             for the property and at that point they would come to the BOS for final approval of the proposal.
             There would be a referral to the Planning Commission.
             M. Williams said that often, once it gets to the stage where the Planning Commission makes
             commen[ the process has proceeded and a lot of effort has gone into the plaruring. She would like
             to get updates on the progress of the plans. G. Goeschel said he is a member of the subcommittee
             and will keep the Commission updated.
