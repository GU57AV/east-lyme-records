zoning enforcement officer or the defendant. lnstead, the defendant asserts that, because such label was

zoning enforcement officer therefore has the power to deny a permit to buitd on the parcet.

*758 The defendant's proposition fails in at leasttwo regards. First, the regulations did not require an open space

parcet, rather, the regulations merely required the developer to atlocate a parcel as open space on the subdivision
ptan submitted forthe application. The commission could then decide whetherthe open space parcel should be
required. See East Lyme Subdivision Regs. (Rev. to June 5, 1967), 5 3.5. ln this case, the commission was sitent on
the matter, so it cannot be assumed that the commission eventua[[y required the parcetto remain open space.
Further, the developers twice offered to deed the parcel to the town but the town denied both offers. Had the
town truly wished to ensure that the parcet would remain "open space" indefinitely under the regulations, it
could have accepted title to the property. Second, the defendant's argument faits because, even if the regulations
actual[y required the commission to designate the parcel as open space before approving the subdivision
apptication, the designation of "recreation area" did not fall within the expticit confines of the regulations.
lnstead, the regulations atlowed the commission the discretion to mandate that a developer provide [and to the

Subdivision Regs. (Rev. to June 5, 1967), 5 3.5. The regutations made no mention of the commission's power to
require the developer to set aside land for a "recreation area." Thus, because the commission tacked the
authority to require the developer to designate a "recreation area," it fottows that the zoning enforcement officer
and the defendant could not deny the plaintiff a buitding permit for the parcel on the basis of its original
"recreation area" designation on the subdivision plan.

                                                                                                                                                   *759
The defendant's second ctaim on appeal, namely, that the court erred in determining that the town was
required to accept titte to the property in order to effectuate the recreation area designation, also fails. The
defendant's argument is based on a misguided reading of the cou rt's decision. Atthough the court stated that the

designation, but rather on the illegatity of the action taken by the zoning enforcement officer and the defendant
in denying the plaintiff a buitding permit. Accordingty, the court did not err, and the defendant's second claim
fa ils.


We conclude that the court properly sustained the ptaintiff 's appeat and remanded the case to the defendant
with direction to **75 reverse the ruling of the zoning enforcement officer.
